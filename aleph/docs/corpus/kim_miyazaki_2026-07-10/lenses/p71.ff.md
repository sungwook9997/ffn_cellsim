# FF lens — p71

**Paper:** Muresan, Sun, Yadav, Tabatabai, Lanier, Kim J.H., Kim T., Murrell — "F-actin architecture determines constraints on myosin thick filament motion." *Nat. Commun.* 13:7008 (2022). DOI 10.1038/s41467-022-34715-6.

**One-line for FF:** Branched (Arp2/3) vs linear/bundled (formin mDia1) cortex-like actin geometry, at *constant total actin*, decides whether myosin-II thick filaments contract-and-transmit stress or get **sterically caged** — and the paper's own agent-based Langevin/Brownian-dynamics model (Taeyoon Kim lab; the physics FF re-implements in Warp) shows **volume exclusion (VE) is the dominant caging mechanism**, not crossbridge kinetics. This is a direct architecture→active-stress-generation validation battery for FF's cortex + Arp2/3 + myosin-minifilament stack, and a strong lever on our open γ-generation and network-contraction problems: *contractility is architecture-gated, and our LJ excluded-volume term is exactly the knob that gates it.*

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Volume-exclusion (steric caging) of the myosin minifilament is a first-class active-stress determinant (FF: myosin + excluded-volume LJ)
The single strongest FF takeaway. In the Kim agent-based model, toggling the repulsive VE force between all cytoskeletal elements ON reproduces the experimental caging (myosin MSD drops, sub-diffusive), and OFF removes it — **at identical R_Arp2/3, identical crossbridge kinetics** (Fig 8c, "with VE" filled vs "w/o VE" hollow circles). Controls (Suppl. Fig 15): a bound+VE motor that *walks* has MSD ≈ a *non-walking* motor — i.e., steric confinement, not force-dependent unbinding, sets the ceiling.
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF already has LJ-repulsive excluded volume ON (CLAUDE.md worked example: "EV-off → LJ repulsive on from Phase 1"), but we have never *treated it as a load-bearing determinant of myosin contractility* — it's been a stability/overlap-prevention term. This paper says the steric term is the physics that decides whether the Arp2/3 lamellipodium can generate stress at all. Our lamellipodium (Arp2/3 70°±35°) + myosin minifilament should reproduce caging emergently; if it doesn't, our VE radius / mesh density is wrong. This directly informs the **network-contraction & γ-generation open problems**: a dense branched region should *not* contract, and if our cortex over-contracts it may be under-resolving VE.
- **Actionable:** make the myosin minifilament a spatially-extended, VE-participating body (see M2), not a point force-dipole, so steric caging can emerge.

### M2 — Myosin as a multi-arm, spatially-extended bipolar minifilament with a real backbone length (FF: myosin module)
The model motor = **64 arms** on a backbone of **equilibrium length L_M ≈ 1.30 µm (1302 nm)**; each arm independently binds F-actin, walks toward the barbed end, unbinds at force-dependent rate. Caging strength scales with L_M: sweeping L_M = 42 / 126 / 294 / 630 / 1302 nm, **larger L_M → more inhibition; small L_M → little inhibition** (Fig 8d). A large thick filament is geometrically harder to thread through a branched mesh.
- **Why it beats current FF:** aligns with our ratified Stam–Hocky multi-head bipolar minifilament (CLAUDE.md: reject "single 2-head spring (AFINES)"). This paper gives the quantitative *why*: the minifilament's **finite extent** is the load-bearing feature (an idealized force dipole/point cannot cage). If FF's myosin is effectively short or point-like, we lose the entire architecture-dependence. The L_M sweep is a ready-made FF sensitivity test.
- **Note:** SKMMII (this paper) ≈ 20 molecules/thick filament vs NMMII ≈ 300 (Discussion). Our cortex is NMMII — *bigger* filaments → *more* susceptible to caging. So the caging effect this paper measures is a **lower bound** for our physiological NMMII cortex.

### M3 — Architecture-dependent thick-filament speed law: v ∝ 1/L in linear, size-independent+suppressed in branched (FF: myosin walk kinetics)
In formin/linear networks thick-filament speed correlates negatively with its own length (v ∝ ~1/L, Fig 7a) — consistent with **crossbridge-number-limited** sliding (more arms engaged on a longer filament → more drag/stall). In Arp2/3/branched networks speed is **size-independent and strongly reduced** — geometry, not crossbridge count, is rate-limiting.
- **Why it matters for FF:** a diagnostic. Emergent FF myosin velocity should show v∝1/L in bundle/filopodium contexts and lose that dependence (with an overall speed drop) when embedded in the Arp2/3 lamellipodium. If our myosin walks at the same speed regardless of local architecture, our steric coupling to the surrounding network is missing.

### M4 — Cofilin de-branching converts a caged (gel) network into a contractile one (FF: turnover/fragmentation channel)
Adding **cofilin 790 nM** to a 74 nM Arp2/3 network debranches it → myosin **speed ↑ ~7-fold** (τ<15 s), MSD exponent **0.35→0.77**, and nematic order ⟨n⟩ significantly up (p=0.0045) (Fig 6). Debranching removes steric constraints and lets filaments align into contractile bundles.
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF's known-open "filament turnover/fragmentation" problem currently has no *architecture-remodeling* channel. This is the mechanistic content: severing/debranching is not just recycling monomer — it **changes the network's contractile competence** by lowering connectivity/branch density and raising alignment. A cofilin-style debranch/sever operator (remove Arp2/3 branch junction and/or sever near it) is the fine-grained mechanism, and the 7× speed / 0.35→0.77 exponent shift is its acceptance target. Beats a lumped "turnover rate" because the *outcome is emergent alignment + contractility*, not a tuned recycling constant.

### M5 — Membrane tethering of nucleators attenuates contractility (FF: cortex–membrane coupling)
His-tag↔Ni-NTA-lipid anchoring of nucleators (and hence the network) to the bilayer **attenuates** contraction (Fig 2). A network pinned to the membrane resists collapse to asters.
- **Why it matters for FF:** we run cortex under membrane containment. This says the **membrane–cortex anchor density is a contractility modulator** — relevant to whether our cortical γ over- or under-generates. A fully anchored cortex should contract less than a free network; our physiological baseline must set anchor density, not leave it free.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### Simulation force set (Kim agent-based, the FF-cognate model), p.13 + p.2060
Cylindrical segments with harmonic **bending + extensional (stretch) + torsion** potentials, plus a **repulsive volume-exclusion** force between *all* elements, integrated by an over-damped **Langevin/Brownian-dynamics** equation. Motor arms bind F-actin, walk toward the barbed end, and **unbind at force-dependent (Bell-type) rates**. FF already has all four potential classes + BAOAB; the mapping is 1:1. (Explicit spring constants + the Bell prefactors are in Supplementary Table S1, **not in this PDF** — flag: fetch SI or use FF's existing Ferrer/Bell values.)

**Symbol → FF state map:**
| paper symbol | meaning | FF state |
|---|---|---|
| cylindrical segment (bending κ, stretch, torsion) | actin/motor/Arp2/3 element | FF filament bead-rod segment + angular bending κ + stretch modulus |
| VE repulsive contact force | steric confinement | FF LJ-repulsive excluded-volume term (radius = key knob) |
| motor arm, walks to barbed end | one myosin head | FF Stam–Hocky minifilament head |
| 64 arms, backbone L_M | minifilament extent | FF myosin minifilament head-count + backbone length |
| force-dependent unbinding | Bell/catch off-rate | FF Bell-Evans `k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b)` (already in FF) |
| z-wall repulsion | bilayer boundary | FF membrane containment / substrate wall |

### Anomalous-diffusion observables (the FF read-out battery), Eqs (2),(3),(6),(8)
Port these as **FF myosin-tracking diagnostics** on emergent trajectories:
- **Translational MSD:** `4·D_T·t^α = (1/N)·Σ_i ⟨(r(0) − r_i(t))²⟩`  (Eq 2). α<1 caged, =1 diffusive, >1 directed/contractile.
- **Rotational MSD:** `2·D_θ·t^α = (1/N)·Σ_i ⟨(θ(0) − θ_i(t))²⟩`  (Eq 3). θ = long-axis tilt to xy-plane.
- **Non-Gaussian parameter (2-D):** `NGP(τ) = 3⟨r⁴(τ)⟩ / (5⟨r²(τ)⟩²) − 1`  (Eq 6). 0 = Gaussian; large = trapped/rare-event.
- **Local MSD slope:** `α(τ) = d ln⟨Δr²(τ)⟩ / d ln τ`  (Eq 8).

### Two-point stress-transmission correlation, Eqs (10),(11)
`D_{αβ}(r,τ) = ⟨Δr_α^i·Δr_β^j·δ[r − R^{ij}(t)]⟩_{i≠j}`; normalize → `C_rr`; fit `C_rr ~ 1/r^α` over r = 3–20 µm. **α≈1 ⇒ continuum-like force transmission (contractile, linear network); no assignable 1/r ⇒ non-transmitting (caged, branched).** This is a fine-grained FF metric for whether our cortex transmits tension across the cell — directly relevant to γ-generation.

### Macroscopic stress-relaxation constitutive (ablation), Eq (12)
Kelvin–Voigt: `ε(t) = (σ/E_v)(1 − e^{−t/τ_v})`, `τ_v = η_v/E_v`. Use as the **acceptance fit for an FF virtual laser-ablation / cut test**: sever a cortex band, fit retraction strain, extract τ_v; branched region should show **no retraction** (no accumulated tension).

### Nematic order (architecture read-out), Eq (1)
`⟨n⟩ = 2⟨cos²θ − ½⟩` over 3.5 µm windows vs 8 neighbors. FF observable for local F-actin alignment; couples to contractile competence (M4: debranching raises ⟨n⟩ → contractility).

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce

**FF scenario "architecture-gated contractility" (drop-in):**
1. Build a thin **quasi-2D cortex-like slab** (native filament resolution) nucleated from a bottom surface (mimics bilayer growth → denser lower half; the Kim "biased" seeding). Domain ~4×4×1.5 µm, periodic in x,y, repulsive wall in z. Hold **total actin constant**.
2. Two architecture arms at equal actin:
   - **Linear/bundled:** formin (mDia1) elongation + bundling crosslinkers → aligned/entangled filaments (83 nM sparse-high-curvature bundles vs 830 nM dense-isotropic).
   - **Branched:** Arp2/3 70°±35° dendritic gel (R_Arp2/3 sweep 0.001→0.01).
3. Add myosin minifilaments (64-arm, L_M≈1.3 µm, seeded near the dense −z surface to maximize network contact) and let them walk.
4. **Toggle VE ON/OFF** (the decisive experiment): measure myosin MSD and network strain both ways.
5. **Cofilin arm:** apply a debranch/sever operator to the branched network → re-measure (M4).
6. **Virtual ablation:** cut a band, fit Eq (12) retraction.

This is a clean, self-contained FF validation harness that exercises filament κ, Arp2/3 branching, formin elongation, myosin minifilament, crosslinkers, excluded volume, and turnover — all at once, against measured numbers.

**Caveat on scale:** run the *conclusion-grade* version at native cortex resolution per the HARD rule; the 4×4×1.5 µm in-vitro box is a **dev-scale smoke harness**, not the native full-cell cortex. The mechanism (VE gates contractility) must be reconfirmed on the native `--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules` cell before it's reported.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

1. **Purified in-vitro vs living cortex.** This is a reconstituted skeletal-muscle-myosin (SKMMII) network on a lipid bilayer at pH 8.5 (raised to suppress spontaneous nucleation), with methylcellulose crowding — not a turgor-pressurised MCF7 cortex. **Adapt, don't copy:** port the *mechanism* (VE gates contractility; architecture sets caging) and the *dimensionless observables* (MSD exponents, C_rr exponent, VE-toggle direction), but keep FF's physiological baseline (65 Pa·s cytoplasm, resting turgor, NMMII isoform, native crosslinker set). The absolute strain/τ_v numbers are in-vitro references, not MCF7 setpoints — use them as behavioral bands, never as fit targets (no-fitting rule).

2. **Isoform mismatch (SKMMII ~20 vs NMMII ~300 molecules/filament).** Our cortex uses NMMII (bigger minifilaments, higher duty ratio for IIB). Per M2's L_M law, bigger filaments cage *more*, so the measured caging is a **lower bound** for FF. Do not down-tune FF myosin extent to match SKMMII — set L_M to the physiological NMMII value and expect *stronger* architecture-dependence.

3. **Quasi-2D thin slab vs 3-D spherical cortex.** The assay is 0.56–1.63 µm thick on a flat bilayer; our cortex is a closed 3-D shell. The z-density gradient ("biased" seeding, myosin near the dense surface) is an assay artifact of bilayer nucleation — in FF, cortex density is set by the native build, so seed myosin per our physiological distribution, not near a −z wall.

4. **SI-gated constants.** The explicit VE radius, segment spring constants, and Bell unbinding prefactors are in Supplementary Table S1, absent from this PDF. Do **not** fabricate them. Keep FF's existing Ferrer link stiffness / Bell-Evans / Hill values; if a quantitative VE radius is needed for the caging test, flag "fetch p71 SI" to PI rather than tuning a magic number.

5. **Cofilin operator is a remodeling channel, not a monomer sink.** Implementing M4 as a lumped "turnover rate" would miss the point — the physics is *branch removal → connectivity drop → alignment rise → contractility onset*. It must act on explicit Arp2/3 junctions/filaments so the 7× speed and 0.35→0.77 exponent shift *emerge*. Watch for over-severing destabilizing the native cortex (structural-stability rule).

6. **GSER modulus assumes thermal-only (Eq 9).** `|G*(ω)| ≈ k_BT/(π·a·⟨Δr²⟩·Γ[1+α])` ignores active/polymerization stress — so any FF G*(ω) computed from bead MSD in an *active* network is only comparable to the paper's ~1–10 Pa passive-consistent estimate, not to the active regime. Use it as a passive cross-check only.

7. **Native-scale reconfirmation is mandatory.** The 4×4×1.5 µm box is a dev harness; every finding (especially the VE-toggle result) must be reconfirmed on the native `--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules` full-compartment cell before being reported or acted on (HARD rule, PI 2026-07-09).
