# DCM lens — p42

**Paper:** Jung, Murrell & Kim, *F-Actin Fragmentation Induces Distinct Mechanisms of Stress Relaxation in the Actin Cytoskeleton*, ACS Macro Lett. 2016, 5, 641–645. DOI 10.1021/acsmacrolett.6b00232.

**One-line verdict for DCM:** This is a sub-grid, purified-network, agent-based (Taeyoon-Kim) rheology paper — its filament severing/buckling physics lives *below* the DCM surface mesh. It is **primarily an FF-engine paper**, not a DCM paper. For DCM it contributes **one genuinely useful conceptual/constitutive lever**: the cortex should relax stress *irreversibly and only across strain reversals* (a plastic/remodeling term), not as a reversible elastic spring — which is exactly the structural argument DCM already needs for the open **loose→compact aggregate compaction RATE** problem (T1-rearrangement-limited, not σ-pull-limited). Everything else is a validation-band datum for the FF cortex, not a DCM force.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

Realistically **one** mechanism, plus one caveat-mechanism. Keep expectations low — nothing here replaces a DCM force wholesale.

### M1 (primary) — Cortex constitutive law: add an *irreversible, reversal-gated* plastic relaxation term to γ, distinct from reversible viscous relaxation
- **DCM module:** actomyosin-cortex surface tension γ / membrane–cortex viscoelastic response (the `cortex` compartment's stress response and its surface-viscosity term).
- **Change:** DCM currently carries the cortex as a lumped tension γ = ΔP·R/2 with a viscous (rate-dependent, *reversible*) relaxation via surface viscosity η_s. This paper shows that in the real actin network there are **two orthogonal, non-interfering relaxation channels**:
  1. **Reversible / within-cycle** relaxation — ACP unbinding in the load-bearing (~45°) "supportive framework"; self-heals via rebinding; relaxes stress *during* the current stiffening ramp, **not** across cycles. → This is the analogue of DCM's existing viscous η_s term (recoverable).
  2. **Irreversible / across-cycle** relaxation — F-actin severing of buckled (~135°) filaments *outside* the framework; **no rebinding**; contributes ~zero relaxation to the current ramp but relaxes stress **only when strain reverses sign** (bidirectional loading required; unidirectional shows ~0 relaxation, Fig 2d).
- **Why it beats what DCM does now:** DCM's cortex has only a recoverable viscoelastic response. A cell that is cyclically loaded (e.g. during confinement pulsing, repeated aspiration, or aggregate compaction where neighbours re-orient the interface) should exhibit **history-dependent, ratchet-like softening** that a Kelvin–Voigt/Maxwell tension law cannot produce. Adding a plastic remodeling term (γ that *decrements* on load reversal and does not fully recover) is the fine-grained-honest way to represent cortical actin turnover/severing at the continuum level. It is the mechanistically-correct upgrade over "reversible spring + dashpot."
- **Structural payoff for the OPEN compaction-rate problem:** the memory note `project-dcm-compaction-turgor-blocked` records that aggregate-σ compaction is a *drag-limited mechanical end-state* (~100 s), while real compaction is 24–48 h and needs **T1 rearrangement escalation**, not more σ-pull. This paper is the clean microscopic precedent for that exact distinction: **reversible pulling relaxes nothing across cycles; only an irreversible topological remodeling event (severing ↔ T1 neighbour swap) relaxes accumulated stress and lets the geometry advance.** Use it as the *mechanistic justification* that the compaction rate-limiter must be an irreversible, reversal-/rearrangement-gated event — not to set a number.

### M2 (caveat-mechanism, do NOT bolt on naively) — Do not treat cortex relaxation as strain-rate-independent
- The paper shows **both** channels are stochastic ⇒ relaxation **decreases as strain rate increases** (Figs S3a–d). Any DCM plastic-γ term must therefore be rate-dependent (a rate, not a per-step probability), or it becomes a magic number that violates grid-invariance.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

Only two equations in the Letter are usable at DCM scale; the harmonic/severing kinetics (eqs S8/S9) live in the SI and are FF-scale, not DCM-scale.

### Eq (1) — cyclic stress-drop metric → a DCM cortex-fatigue observable
```
drop in stress = (σ₁ − σ₂) / σ₁ × 100   [%]
```
- **Map to DCM:** σ₁, σ₂ = cortical membrane stress (or measured cortical tension γ_eff, or peak reaction force in an aspiration/AFM DCM scenario) at the **first** and **second** identical loading peaks of a cyclic protocol. This is a *measurement protocol*, not a force — port it as a DCM diagnostic to quantify any plastic-γ term you add. **Sanity-gate use:** DCM with a *purely reversible* cortex must return drop ≈ 0 under bidirectional cycling; a nonzero, amplitude-increasing drop only appears once M1's irreversible term is on. That is a clean sign/behavior gate.

### Eq (2) — Euler buckling threshold (context only; NOT a DCM node/face force)
```
F_buckle ∼ π² · κ_{b,A} · r_{0,A} / L_c²          F_buckle ∝ L_c⁻²
```
- κ_{b,A} = actin bending stiffness, r_{0,A} = actin segment rest length, L_c = cross-linking span. **This is a filament-scale (FF) law** — it sets *when* a sub-grid filament buckles/severs. **DCM has no L_c and no discrete filament**, so this equation cannot be evaluated on DCM state. Its only DCM use is to *rationalize* the constitutive form of M1: the plastic softening rate should grow with the amount of cortical material outside the instantaneous load-bearing framework (the compressed fraction), because that fraction is what buckles→severs. Do not attempt to inject F_buckle onto mesh nodes — that would be a category error (DCM = surface continuum, meters; FF = filaments, µm; see memory `reference-dcm-meters-ff-microns`).

**Net:** nothing here becomes a new node/face force in DCM. Eq (1) becomes a diagnostic; eq (2) is FF-only context.

## 3. PARAMETERS to set

**None are directly settable as DCM config fields** — every numeric parameter in this paper is either purified-network geometry (L=3 µm cubic domain, R_ACP ratio, ⟨L_f⟩) or a filament-scale force. They **do not supersede any DCM default.** Listed here only as validation bands (see §5) and to be explicit that adopting M1 must NOT smuggle in these numbers as tuning constants:

| symbol | value | units | role for DCM |
|---|---|---|---|
| strain amplitude | 10–50 (0.5 max) | % | protocol knob for a cyclic-loading DCM scenario (§4) |
| F_buckle @ L_c=300 nm | 4.1 | pN | FF-scale only; NOT a DCM parameter |
| high-load ACP threshold | ≥ 8 | pN | FF clutch/xlink; NOT a DCM parameter |
| supportive vs buckled orientation | ~45° / ~135° | deg | FF orientation-partition diagnostic; NOT DCM |
| peak shear stress | ~100–150 | Pa | FF network-rheology validation band, not a DCM setpoint |

Any plastic-γ rate introduced by M1 must be **derived** (from cortical actin turnover time / severing rate at the physiological operating point), not read off this paper. If no physiological turnover datum exists → surface to PI rather than borrow a purified-gel number. (Do NOT KB-register these purified-network numbers as MCF7 cortex parameters.)

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

The paper's own geometry (3 µm periodic cube, bulk shear) is **not** a DCM scenario — it's a bulk-rheology box, not a closed cell. But the *loading protocol* is directly reusable as a DCM cortex-fatigue test:

- **Cyclic-deformation DCM scenario (new):** take the native full-compartment MCF7 cell at its physiological baseline (`--from-resting`, turgor Π₀, γ interphase 0.15 mN/m, nucleus, microtubules — never stripped/coarse per the HARD rule), then apply a **cyclic macroscopic deformation** — e.g. sinusoidal parallel-plate compression or sinusoidal micropipette-aspiration pressure — at amplitudes 10–50% and a low rate. Measure cortical tension / reaction force at successive peaks.
  - **Bidirectional vs unidirectional contrast (the paper's decisive control):** run both a *reversing* cycle (compress↔stretch) and a *one-sided* cycle (compress→relax→compress). The paper's signature is **relaxation only under reversal**. A DCM cortex with M1's plastic term should reproduce that asymmetry; a reversible cortex will not. This is the cheapest possible test that the added mechanism is behaving.
- **Amplitude sweep:** stress-drop should rise monotonically with amplitude (Fig 2b) — a qualitative gate, not a fit.

These are *dev/validation scenarios* for the constitutive upgrade; they are not production physiology.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

Because DCM does not resolve filaments, the paper's absolute numbers are **FF/TAG targets, not DCM targets.** The DCM-level (qualitative/behavioral) targets that a cyclic DCM run *should* reproduce once M1 is on:

1. **Reversal asymmetry (primary behavioral gate):** bidirectional cyclic loading → measurable cortical-tension drop across cycles; unidirectional → negligible (~0–5%) drop. (Fig 2b, 2d.)
2. **Amplitude monotonicity:** stress-drop increases with strain amplitude, 0 → up to ~70% as amplitude → 50%. DCM should show the *trend*; the magnitude depends on the (derived) cortical turnover rate, not on this gel. (Fig 2b, 4b.)
3. **Rate dependence sign:** relaxation *decreases* as loading rate increases (Figs S3). DCM plastic-γ must respect this sign.
4. **No-remodeling null:** a DCM cortex with only reversible viscoelasticity returns ~0 cyclic drop — the control that proves the new term is what produces the effect.

**FF/TAG-only bands (record in KB against FF cortex, not DCM):** peak shear stress ~100–150 Pa at 50% strain (R_ACP 0.032–0.1); F_buckle = 4.1 pN at L_c = 300 nm; ~45°/~135° orthogonal orientation split; exponential cross-linking-distance distribution 0.1–2 µm.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Regime mismatch (severe):** purified, cross-linked F-actin gel in a periodic box under bulk shear — **not** a living cell, no membrane, no turgor, no myosin-generated prestress (the authors explicitly defer motor-generated internal stress and (de)polymerization to future work). Our DCM baseline is the *opposite*: a pressurized, myosin-active, membrane-enclosed MCF7 cell at its physiological operating point. Do **not** import this paper's stress magnitudes or rates as MCF7 cortex values.
- **Sub-grid to DCM:** severing/buckling is a filament event with no representative on the DCM surface mesh. The only honest DCM use is a *coarse-grained constitutive consequence* (irreversible reversal-gated softening), never a per-node buckling force. Trying to put F_buckle on mesh nodes is a category/unit error.
- **Physiological-baseline rule:** the paper's networks *start unloaded and are driven to failure*; DCM must instead start at the resting turgor/γ operating point and apply cyclic perturbation **from** that baseline. Any plastic-γ term must be OFF-effect at the resting setpoint (no spurious softening of a quiescent cortex) and only activate under supra-threshold cyclic deformation.
- **Magic-number trap:** the plastic-relaxation rate MUST be derived from a cortical actin turnover/severing timescale at physiological conditions, not tuned to hit the ~70% drop or borrowed from R_ACP/⟨L_f⟩ here. If that datum is missing → HALT and surface to PI (same posture as the γ-floor "no MCF7 input" situation).
- **This is chiefly an FF paper:** the highest-value port (mechanistic severing via curvature-dependent stochastic scission eq S9, Bell ACP unbinding eq S8, Euler F_buckle for filament stability) belongs to FF, and the SI (with the full parameter table + eqs S8/S9) should be obtained for that. For DCM, keep scope to M1 + the cyclic-loading diagnostic.
