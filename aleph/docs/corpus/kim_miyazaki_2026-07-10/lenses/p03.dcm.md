# DCM lens — p03

**Paper:** Kim, Gardel & Munro (2014), *Determinants of Fluidlike Behavior and Effective Viscosity in Cross-Linked Actin Networks*, Biophys J 106(3):526–534. DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2013.12.031.

**One-line for DCM:** DCM's cortex is a *lumped* surface tension γ with no honest viscous/plastic flow law; this paper hands us the **emergent constitutive law the coarse cortex should obey** — turnover-set effective viscosity (η_eff ∝ 1/turnover) with a **biphasic yield-stress creep** (linear-viscous below σ_th≈1 Pa, stress-independent plastic flow above). That yield-limited, turnover-rate-set plastic-flow form is the natural mechanistic template for the **loose→compact aggregate compaction RATE** open problem and for cortical-flow / surface-viscosity closures — but note this is a passive, myosin-free, purified in-vitro network, so it feeds DCM's *rheology closure*, not its tension generation.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cortex surface viscosity η_s SET BY TURNOVER (supersede the constant surface-viscosity default)
DCM's membrane/cortex compartment currently carries a lumped, hand-set "surface viscosity." This paper shows the cortex effective viscosity is not free: **η_eff ∝ 1/k⁻** (turnover rate), collapsing across actin density C_A, cross-link ratio R, filament length ⟨L_f⟩, and turnover mode onto one law (Eq. 6). WHY it beats the status quo: it removes a magic number — the cortical viscosity becomes *derivable* from the cortex actin turnover rate the FF engine already models (treadmilling rate 5.4·n s⁻¹), instead of a tuned constant. This is grid-invariant and mechanistic (satisfies the Magic-Number Block).

### M2 — Biphasic YIELD-STRESS creep law for the cortex surface (new constitutive form)
Below σ_th≈1 Pa the cortex is linear-viscous (η_eff const, ε̇ ∝ σ); above σ_th it flows at a **stress-independent plastic rate ε̇ = k⁻·Δε_avg** for ~2 decades of stress. WHY it matters: DCM presently has no yield behaviour in the cortex — driving stress just scales the response. A yield-stress (Bingham-like) cortex is the physically-correct closure and directly gives a **plastic rearrangement rate that is turnover-limited, not drag-limited** — precisely the missing ingredient in the compaction-rate problem (real 24–48 h compaction is a slow plastic flow set by turnover, not the ~100 s drag-limited σ-pull DCM currently produces).

### M3 — Compaction / T1-rearrangement RATE = turnover-limited plastic flow (attack the open problem)
Memory record `project-dcm-compaction-turgor-blocked` states aggregate-σ compaction is only a mechanical *end-state*, and the true rate needs "cadherin T1 rearrangement escalation." This paper supplies the RATE law for exactly that regime: above yield, macroscopic irreversible creep proceeds by a **cycle of load-pathway formation → tension buildup → turnover release**, with net rate ε̇ ∝ turnover rate and independent of the driving stress. WHY it beats current DCM: it explains *why* compaction is slow and *what* sets the timescale (turnover k⁻, not the pull magnitude), giving a principled, non-fitted way to set the DCM cortical/junctional plastic-flow rate. Escalating T1 rearrangement should be gated by a **turnover clock**, not by increasing σ.

### M4 — Nonaffine load-bearing acknowledgement (reject naive linear-viscoelastic cortex)
~5% of filaments carry ~99% of the load; stress relaxes through discrete percolating pathways, not uniformly. WHY it matters for DCM: it is a mechanistic *warning* — a single-Maxwell / uniform-viscous surface will mispredict relaxation spectra. For the lumped surface this argues for the yield-stress form (M2) rather than a linear dashpot, and it tells us any DCM cortical-flow closure derived from FF must average over a broad, heavy-tailed pathway-lifetime distribution.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

### E1 — Master effective-viscosity law (Eq. 6) → cortex surface constitutive law
```
η_eff · n = η_eff⁰ · max(σ, σ_th) / σ_th        (η_eff⁰ ≈ 35 Pa·s, σ_th ≈ 1 Pa)
```
Map to DCM: σ → in-plane cortical tension increment (deviatoric surface stress, N/m divided by cortex thickness h to get Pa, or keep everything as 2D surface stress τ_s = σ·h). `n` → cortex turnover scale factor, i.e. treadmilling rate ω_t = 5.4·n s⁻¹. Rearranged for the DCM cortex node/face update, the **effective 3D cortex viscosity** is
```
η_eff(σ) = (η_eff⁰ / n) · max(σ, σ_th)/σ_th ,   with n = ω_t / 5.4 s⁻¹
```
and the **surface viscosity** used on the mesh is η_s = η_eff · h with cortex thickness h ≈ 1 µm (the paper's slab thickness): η_s⁰ ≈ 35 Pa·s × 1 µm ≈ **3.5×10⁻⁵ Pa·s·m ≈ 35 µN·s/m** at n=0.1's reference turnover.

### E2 — Toy-model creep rate (§Toy model, Eq. 8 analysis) → DCM cortical/junction plastic-flow kernel
```
Linear   (σ < σ_th):   ε̇ = k⁻ σ / E₀                 with E₀ = n_L K
Plastic  (σ > σ_th):   ε̇ = k⁻ · Δε_avg               (independent of σ)
Yield:                 σ_th ≈ E₀ · Δε_avg
```
Map to DCM: ε̇ → local surface (or junction-interface) strain rate of a mesh patch; k⁻ → cortex/junction turnover off-rate (per-node, = ω_t for treadmilling, or cadherin cluster off-rate for the junction); E₀ → local cortex 2D elastic modulus (from K_A / area-elasticity for the surface, or junction cluster stiffness); Δε_avg → average per-pathway strain increment before turnover. This is the exact form to install as a **turnover-gated plastic strain-rate** on the cortex face-strain integrator and on the cadherin-junction interface: below yield it is a viscoelastic relaxation, above yield it advances at a fixed turnover-set rate.

### E3 — Segment drag (Eq. 4) — reference only, NOT ported to DCM
```
ζ_i = 3π η_m r_c,i (3 + 2 r₀,i/r_c,i) / 5
```
Stokes cylinder drag; this is FF/Cytosim-level (per-filament) and does not map onto DCM nodes. Listed so the DCM engine does *not* accidentally import filament drag as a surface drag — DCM node drag is derived separately from cytoplasm η≈65 Pa·s.

### E4 — Force autocorrelation lifetime (Eq. 7) — diagnostic, not a force law
```
R(τ) = ⟨(F(t)−µ)(F(t+τ)−µ)⟩ / std(F)² ,   decay slope ∝ n
```
Use as the *definition of the turnover clock* that sets k⁻ in E2: the pathway-tension autocorrelation time τ_c ≈ 1/(turnover-set rate). Lets DCM read its cortex plastic-flow rate directly off the FF pathway-lifetime measurement rather than fitting.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce (DCM scenarios)

### P1 — Uniaxial cortex creep test (direct DCM validation scenario)
Reproduce the paper's protocol on a DCM cortex patch: a thin mesh strip (their 5×5×1 µm, PBC in x,y during equilibration), pin the −x edge nodes, apply a **constant tensile surface stress σ** on the +x edge held by a PD-feedback boundary, and record ε(t) for 100 s. Sweep σ ∈ [0.1, 100] Pa and turnover n ∈ [0.01, 1]. DCM observable: creep-rate ε̇ (slope of the quasi-constant window) and η_eff = σ/ε̇. This is a clean, self-contained rheology gate for whatever cortex surface-viscosity / yield closure DCM installs.

### P2 — Turnover sweep for the η_eff ∝ 1/n law
Fix σ=10 Pa, sweep n=0.01–1, confirm η_eff ∝ n⁻¹ (Fig 2C) and that at n=0 there is **no long-term creep, only an initial elastic jump** (Fig 1C). Direct test that the DCM cortex plastic flow shuts off when turnover→0 (a fluid↔solid transition gate).

### P3 — Stress-decomposition diagnostic
Split total surface stress into elastic σ_e and viscous σ_v (their σ_v = drag on the load-connected network / area). Above σ_th, σ_v saturates while σ_e keeps rising ∝ σ (Fig 1E). DCM can reproduce this partition on the cortex surface as a validation that the yield transition is in the right place.

### P4 — Aggregate compaction re-timed by turnover (the payoff scenario)
Apply E2's plastic-flow law to the N≈400 aggregate: with cortex/junction turnover setting k⁻, the compaction should proceed at a **turnover-limited rate independent of the aggregate-σ magnitude above yield** — the test being whether a physiological turnover (ω_t ~ 0.1–1 s⁻¹ cortex, slower junction remodeling) reproduces a 24–48 h compaction timescale instead of the current drag-limited ~100 s. This is the concrete way to bring this paper's mechanism onto the open problem.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Passive, myosin-free, purified network.** This is reconstituted actin + α-actinin-like ACPs with *no active contraction*. It gives the cortex's **viscous/plastic flow response**, NOT tension generation. Do not use it to set γ or resolve the γ-floor problem — it is orthogonal (rheology closure, not active stress). The physiological-baseline rule is respected as long as we install it as the *surface-viscosity / yield closure on top of* the already-pressure-borne, myosin-active cortex, not as a replacement for active tension.
- **In-vitro α-actinin, not living MCF7 cortex.** Cross-linker identity, density, and turnover differ from a live cortex. σ_th≈1 Pa and η_eff⁰≈35 Pa·s are at C_A=20 µM/R=0.02/⟨L_f⟩≈0.9 µm; they **must be rescaled** to the DCM/FF cortex operating point via Eq. 6's collapse (they rise with C_A, R, ⟨L_f⟩). Copying the literals would be a magic number and would fit to the wrong regime.
- **3D bulk slab, not a curved 2D shell.** The 5×5×1 µm slab is bulk-3D with 1 µm thickness; DCM's cortex is a curved 2D surface. The 3D↔2D conversion (η_s = η_eff·h, τ_s = σ·h) uses h≈1 µm and is only valid where the cortex thickness is well-defined — flag curvature/thickness coupling for the membrane–cortex closure.
- **No turgor, no membrane, no nucleus.** The creep test is a bare network under uniaxial tension; DCM must apply the closure *from the physiological baseline* (turgor-pressurised, membrane-coupled), i.e. superpose the yield-stress plastic flow on the pre-tensioned shell rather than starting from the paper's force-free slab.
- **Turnover mode idealized (steady treadmilling).** Real cortex also severs/bursts; the paper shows creep matches when per-monomer turnover + length + density are matched, so DCM can use its own turnover rate as k⁻ — but the mapping k⁻ ← FF turnover measurement must be made explicitly, not assumed equal to 5.4·n.
- **Nonaffine broad relaxation spectrum.** A single dashpot will mispredict; the yield-stress form (M2) is the minimum honest closure. Do not collapse to a linear Maxwell for convenience.
