# DCM lens — p27

**Paper:** Kim, Zhang, Iyer-Pascuzzi, Staiger, Kim (Taeyoon Kim lab). *Agent-Based Computational
Modeling of the Stochastic Dynamic Behavior of Actin Filaments Recapitulates the Homeostatic
Cortical Array in Plant Epidermal Cells.* Cytoskeleton 2026; 83:407–426. DOI 10.1002/cm.70014.

**One-line verdict for DCM:** LOW direct relevance. This is a fine-grained, agent-based
Brownian-dynamics model of *individual* actin filaments (segments + Arp2/3 + reaction kinetics)
in a **plant** epidermal cortex — the exact lineage of our **FF** engine, not DCM. It has no
membrane/turgor/junction/surface-tension physics, no mammalian/MCF7 numbers, and its
polymerization/depolymerization are explicitly **force-independent**. DCM is a closed-surface
continuum cell. The paper is a strong FF resource (see the FF lens); for DCM it offers only
(a) one transferable *mechanochemical-coupling template* (curvature-dependent turnover), (b) a
conceptual sub-grid "cortex-as-turnover-steady-state" picture that could later ground γ
generation, and (c) FDT/overdamped-Langevin cross-checks DCM already satisfies. Everything below
is flagged for what is honestly portable vs. what would violate our physiological-baseline / no-
magic-number / native-MCF7 rules if imported naively.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

Ranked by honest DCM value. Most are weak; I am not going to inflate them.

### 1.1 Curvature-dependent surface remodeling (the ONE real transferable idea) — MODERATE
- **Paper mechanism:** severing rate is mechanochemically gated by *local bending angle*:
  `k_sev = k0,sev · exp(λ_sev · θ)` (Eq. 9). k0,sev is tiny and λ_sev is "quite high," so a
  *straight* filament essentially never severs, but any process that **bends** the filament
  (crosslinker, motor buckling, myosin XI) sharply accelerates disassembly. Turnover is
  **emergent from mechanics**, not a flat rate.
- **DCM module to touch:** the **remesh** operator and the **T1-rearrangement / cortex-turnover**
  open problem (aggregate compaction RATE, currently drag-limited). Also membrane–cortex coupling.
- **Specific change:** make the *local mesh/cortex remodeling propensity* a function of local
  surface curvature (discrete mean/bending curvature at a node/face) via the same
  Arrhenius-in-angle form: `rate_remodel(node) = r0 · exp(λ · |κ_local − κ_rest|)`, where
  κ_local is the discrete curvature DCM already computes for the bending force κ_m. High-curvature
  cortex regions (a forming furrow, a bleb neck, a T1 contact edge) turn over faster — exactly the
  physics that lets a real cortex *rearrange* rather than only elastically strain. This gives a
  mechanistic, curvature-gated escalation of rearrangement to attack the "aggregate-σ compaction
  is drag-limited, needs T1 escalation" open problem WITHOUT a lumped time-constant.
- **Why it beats what DCM does now:** DCM's remesh is currently geometric/quality-driven and its
  T1 rearrangement is an open item with no mechanistic trigger. A curvature-gated rate makes
  cortex remodeling *emerge from the deformation field* — same spirit as our HARD "fine-grained
  mechanistic over lumped" rule — instead of a hand-set rearrangement timescale.
- **Caveat (do not skip):** the paper's law is for a single filament's severing; porting it to a
  *continuum* curvature is an analogy, and λ, r0 must be **derived** (from a cortex turnover
  half-life datum), not tuned to make compaction land on 24–48 h. If no MCF7 datum exists, surface
  to PI — do NOT fabricate λ. This is a candidate mechanism, not a ready parameter.

### 1.2 Cortex-as-turnover-steady-state → a substrate for γ generation — WEAK/CONCEPTUAL
- **Paper mechanism:** cortex density is the *balance* of assembly (nucleation + barbed-end
  growth, formin ×3) against disassembly (severing + capping + pointed-end depoly). Inhibiting
  the two disassembly channels (Fig. 6, PTI mimic) makes F-actin density and length climb until
  the monomer pool is depleted; the array is a self-limiting homeostatic steady state at ~500 s.
- **DCM relevance:** DCM's biggest open problem is **γ generation** — where cortical tension
  comes from mechanistically. This paper is the sub-grid picture of the network that *produces*
  the cortex whose tension DCM lumps into γ. In principle a future two-scale coupling could let a
  local F-actin *density* field (from an FF-style turnover balance) set a spatially varying
  γ(x) = f(ρ_actin, ρ_myosin) on the DCM surface, instead of a single scalar γ.
- **Why it (might) beat now:** DCM currently imposes γ ≈ ΔP·R/2 (pressure-borne, spatially
  uniform). A density-linked γ would let tension *redistribute* (advect/dilute) as the cortex
  remodels — needed for furrow-localized tension in division and for the myosin-modulated γ story.
- **Honest caveat:** this paper does NOT compute tension at all, is **plant** at C_A = 1 µM (an
  admitted ~40–50× underestimate of real actin), and has **no myosin** (myosin XI excluded). So it
  gives the *scaffolding logic*, not any number DCM can set γ from. Treat as design inspiration for
  a future FF→DCM density hand-off, not an adoptable DCM force.

### 1.3 Overdamped-Langevin node integration + FDT thermal noise — ALREADY-HAVE / cross-check only
- Eqs. 1–4 are the standard inertia-neglected Langevin scheme DCM's Warp integrator already
  embodies at the node level. Nothing new. If DCM ever wants explicit **thermal** node noise
  (currently effectively deterministic at 7.5 µm scale), Eq. 3's FDT normalization
  `⟨FᵀFᵀ⟩ = 2 k_B T ζ δ_ij / Δt` is the correct amplitude — but at cell scale thermal forces are
  negligible vs. turgor/cortex, so this is a validation cross-check, not a mechanism to add.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

Only two are worth writing down for DCM; the rest are FF-native.

### 2.1 Curvature-gated remodeling law (adapted from Eq. 9)
Paper (per-filament severing):
```
k_sev = k0,sev · exp(λ_sev · θ)              (Eq. 9)
```
DCM continuum adaptation (per-node cortex remodeling / local remesh-T1 propensity):
```
r_remodel(i) = r0 · exp( λ · |H_i − H0| )
```
Symbol map to DCM state:
- `θ` (filament bend angle) → `H_i` = discrete mean curvature at surface node/face *i* (DCM already
  evaluates this for the bending force with modulus κ_m); `H0` = rest curvature (≈ 1/R for the
  resting sphere).
- `k0,sev` → `r0` = baseline cortex turnover rate at rest curvature (MUST come from an MCF7 cortex
  turnover half-life, e.g. FRAP recovery τ; not a free knob).
- `λ_sev` → `λ` = curvature sensitivity (derive from how turnover scales with local strain; surface
  to PI if no datum).
- Output `r_remodel` gates the probability that a local edge undergoes a T1 flip / a cortex patch
  resets its rest length — i.e. it drives the *rate* term the aggregate-compaction problem lacks.

### 2.2 Fluctuation–dissipation amplitude (Eq. 3) — cross-check for optional node noise
```
⟨ F_iᵀ(t) · F_jᵀ(t) ⟩ = (2 k_B T ζ_i δ_ij / Δt) · δ
```
Symbol map: `ζ_i` → DCM per-node drag (DERIVED from cytoplasm η ≈ 65 Pa·s, our existing
`node_drag`); `Δt` → DCM timestep; `k_B T` = 4.142×10⁻²¹ J. Use only to size thermal noise IF ever
added; at R = 7.5 µm this term is ~orders below turgor/cortex forces — expect it to be inert.

### 2.3 NOT to port (FF-scale, listed so they are not mistaken as DCM levers)
Eq. 2 cylindrical-segment drag `ζ_i = 3πμ r_c (3 + 2 r0/r_c)/5`; Eqs. 5–7 harmonic
stretch/bend/torsion of *segments*; Eq. 8 one-sided inter-filament repulsion. These are
filament-level and belong to FF, not the DCM surface mesh.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

The paper's setups are 2D-thin periodic actin arrays, not cell-shape scenarios — mostly **not**
usable as DCM cell protocols. What marginally maps:

- **Homeostatic steady-state protocol (Fig. 3):** run to a *dynamic* steady state (~500 s) and
  measure at 1000 s+, averaging ≥5 seeds. **Adopt the discipline, not the geometry:** any DCM
  cortex-turnover model (§1.1/§2.1) should likewise be reported at a *turnover* steady state with
  seed-ensembled spread — matches our "seed-robustness ensemble before 'spontaneous'" lesson.
- **Perturbation-sweep design (Figs. 4–11):** knock a single mechanism up/down and read the
  emergent density/length response (severing, capping, Arp2/3, formin, profilin). If DCM adds the
  curvature-gated remodeling law, mirror this: sweep `λ` (curvature sensitivity) and confirm
  monotone, physically-sensible remodeling response *before* trusting it for compaction rate.
- **NOT usable:** the 20×20×1 µm PBC slab, the z-wall repulsion, the 1 µM concentration domain —
  these are cortical-array geometry, orthogonal to a closed 7.5 µm cell surface.

No aspiration / AFM / confinement / division / durotaxis protocol is present in this paper — it is
purely a free cortical array. So it contributes **no new DCM scenario geometry**.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

**Essentially none for DCM.** Every measured number is a filament-kinetics observable in a plant
cortex, not a DCM cell observable (tension, shape index, division force, contact angle, compaction,
flow). For the record, the paper's targets (poly 2.2 µm/s, depoly 0.2 µm/s, filament length
~9.9 µm, severing 0.01 events/µm/s, nucleation 1.2×10⁻⁴ events/µm²/s, 99% profilin-bound, ~36
capped ends/400 µm²) are **FF acceptance-oracle values (plant)**, and are handled in the FF lens.

The only DCM-adjacent qualitative target worth remembering:
- **Cortex is a turnover steady state reached in ~500 s and self-limits via monomer depletion.**
  Any future DCM density-linked γ / curvature-remodeling model should reproduce *a bounded,
  homeostatic* cortex density rather than runaway growth — a sanity behavior, not a number.

Do NOT KB-register any of these as MCF7 cortex validation values.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

1. **Plant, not mammalian.** Arabidopsis hypocotyl epidermal cortex ≠ MCF7 cortex. Turnover
   phenomenology (no treadmilling; severing-dominated disassembly; profilin-throttled de novo
   nucleation) is plant-specific. Importing any rate as an MCF7 DCM parameter violates the
   physiological-baseline rule.
2. **Fine-grained ≠ DCM scale.** This is a filament-agent model; DCM is a continuum surface. The
   §1.1/§2.1 curvature-gated remodeling law is an **analogy** lifting a per-filament angle law to a
   continuum curvature — defensible in spirit but must be validated at native full-compartment DCM
   scale, never adopted as-is.
3. **Force-independent kinetics.** Polymerization/depolymerization here do NOT respond to load —
   the opposite of what a mechanistic cortex-tension generator needs. Only severing is
   mechanochemically coupled (via θ). So the paper cannot ground a *tension-generating* γ law; it
   can only inspire a *turnover/remodeling* rate.
4. **C_A = 1 µM efficiency compromise.** Self-declared ~40–50× under real actin; every density,
   length, and count is an admitted underestimate. No absolute density is transferable.
5. **No myosin, no crosslinkers, no membrane, no turgor, no junction.** The compartments DCM cares
   about are all absent. The paper explicitly defers myosin XI and ACPs to future work.
6. **No-magic-number trap.** The tempting move — set λ so curvature-gated remodeling makes
   aggregate compaction hit 24–48 h — would be exactly the gate-fitting our HARD rule forbids. λ
   and r0 must be derived from an independent MCF7 cortex turnover datum, or surfaced to PI. If we
   cannot derive them, this mechanism stays a *proposal*, not a production change.

**Adaptation path that respects the rules:** treat §1.1 as a *candidate mechanistic trigger* for
the open T1/remesh escalation, prototype it at native + full-compartment DCM scale, derive its two
constants from a mammalian cortex-turnover half-life (FRAP τ) rather than the plant severing rate,
and only then test whether curvature-gated remodeling supplies the missing compaction *rate*.
Everything quantitative in this paper stays in the FF lane.
