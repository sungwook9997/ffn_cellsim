# DCM lens — p46

**Paper:** Gupta, Nam, Yim, … **Taeyoon Kim**, Chaudhuri. *The nature of cell division forces in epithelial monolayers.* J Cell Biol 2021, 220(8):e202011106. DOI 10.1083/jcb.202011106.

**Why this paper is unusually on-target for DCM:** its computational core (Fig. 7 b–e, Fig. S4 b–f) is a **triangulated closed-surface-mesh membrane under global volume + surface-area conservation with dihedral bending and overdamped Langevin/Brownian dynamics** — i.e. the *same SimuCell3D/DCM architecture we run*, authored by **T. Kim** (DCM/agent-BD lineage). It is effectively a published DCM used to answer one question: *how much axial elongation does cytokinetic-ring constriction produce under near-volume-conservation?* Answer: **~5% volume loss ⇒ ~60% axial elongation.** That is precisely the division/cytokinesis module DCM lists as an open problem, delivered as mechanism + equations + an acceptance oracle. It also cleanly settles a modeling question for us: **elongation does NOT require the interpolar spindle** — ring constriction + the enclosed-volume constraint alone produce it — so DCM's division module can be built from the constraints it *already has*, no spindle mechanics needed.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — DCM **cell-division / cytokinetic-ring** module: ring = prescribed equatorial-band node constriction (NEW module)
DCM currently lists "cell division / cytokinetic ring" as open. This paper gives the minimal mechanistic recipe that reproduces real elongation:
- Select the closed set of mesh nodes lying within a **1-µm-wide equatorial band** about the chosen division axis (band width ∝ R).
- **Displace those nodes inward toward the division axis at constant speed v_c**; each node **freezes once its distance to the axis < 0.5 µm** (the ring "closes"). This is a *kinematic* boundary condition, not a potential — no ad-hoc ring line-tension constant needed (no magic number).
- The rest of the surface responds through the **existing** DCM constraints: enclosed-volume κ_v pushes the poles outward as the waist pinches (volume conserved ⇒ mass redistributes axially), membrane bending κ_m and area K_A smooth the neck.
- **Why it beats what DCM does now:** DCM has no division mechanism; a naive "split the mesh" is geometric and unphysical. This is fully mechanistic (ring pinch + volume incompressibility), uses only DCM state, and is validated to give the correct 60% elongation. It also means we do **not** need to build interpolar-spindle microtubule pushing to get elongation (the paper shows spindle perturbation only *partially* affects it; ring inhibition abrogates ~60%). Big scope win.

### M2 — Enclosed-volume constraint is the *elongation engine* — validate/tune κ_v against the 5%→60% coupling
The paper's central finding is a constraint statement about our own Uv (Eq. 7): near-incompressibility converts equatorial pinch into axial extension. DCM already runs turgor/enclosed-volume (Π₀, κ_vol). **We should treat "5% volume loss ⇒ ~60% elongation, and ~40% volume loss to abolish elongation" as a direct acceptance oracle for our volume-conservation stiffness.** If our κ_vol is too soft, ring pinch will leak volume and under-elongate; too stiff and it over-elongates / destabilizes the solver. This gives a physics target to *validate an existing DCM parameter*, not a new knob.

### M3 — Surface-area constraint with a **membrane reservoir** (soft, one-sided-permissive area law)
The paper explicitly notes area is NOT conserved like a lipid vesicle: sim area **grows +22–23%** during cytokinesis because cells recruit **membrane reservoirs** (Gauthier 2011; Raucher & Sheetz 1999). Our DCM membrane K_A currently penalizes area change symmetrically. Adopt an **asymmetric / soft area law** whose stiffness is small enough to permit reservoir-fed area growth during large shape change (they swept κ_a = 1e-4/1e-5/1e-6 N/m; softer κ_a → more linear, less elongation). This directly bears on our known-open **membrane–cortex coupling / bleb** items too: a membrane that can pay out reservoir area is the prerequisite for blebs and for large-deformation division without spurious area-lock artifacts.

### M4 — **Node-area-proportional drag** ζ_i = λ·A_i (fine-grained drag law)
DCM currently sets per-node drag from cytoplasm viscosity η (a lumped per-node γ). This paper uses the more fine-grained **ζ_i = λ A_i** — drag proportional to the instantaneous polygon area each node subtends (Eq. 3) — so drag tracks local mesh coarseness and stays grid-consistent under remesh. This is the mechanistic option over a uniform per-node γ and makes the drag **grid-invariant** (a node covering more surface drags more), which matters because our division/remesh changes local node density. Consider migrating DCM's drag to ζ_i = λA_i with λ derived from η (λ = η/δ for membrane thickness δ, or calibrated to the same total surface drag) so it is derivable, not tuned.

### M5 — **Dead-band extensional edge spring** (slack window) for remesh-tolerant edge elasticity
Their edge law (Eq. 5) is a **piecewise spring with a zero-force slack window** [r0,L=0.05 µm, r0,H=1.25 µm]: edges roam freely inside the band, linear restoring force only outside. This is a cheap way to let the mesh accommodate large local stretch/compression (as at the cytokinetic neck) without spurious edge forces or constant remeshing. Adopt as an option for the DCM membrane edge term during division, with r0,L/r0,H scaled to our target edge length.

### M6 — Division is neighbor-transmitted: single isolated cell generates **no** consistent elongation traction
Mechanistic constraint for **aggregate (N≈400) DCM**: the dividing cell pushes *outward* and the force is transmitted to substrate via the **adjacent cells' adhesions**; a single cell dividing in isolation shows no elongation traction (Fig. 5 d,e). So a DCM division scenario is only physiologically meaningful **inside the confluent aggregate** (neighbors present), consistent with our "native + full compartment" rule. It also predicts adjacent-cell compression + increased cell–cell boundary curvature — observables our aggregate already resolves.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (mapped to DCM state)

| # | Paper equation | DCM mapping |
|---|---|---|
| **(1)** Overdamped Langevin: `F_i − ζ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_i^T = 0` | DCM node force balance; F_i = Σ(volume + area + bending + contact + cortex-tension + cytokinesis-BC forces) on node i. Confirms our inertia-free BD/BAOAB choice. |
| **(2)** FDT: `⟨F_i^T(t) F_j^T(t)⟩ = (2 k_B T ζ_i δ_ij / Δt) δ` | Thermal-force variance for DCM's stochastic term; ties noise amplitude to the **per-node ζ_i** and Δt. Use if/when DCM runs finite-T membrane fluctuations. |
| **(3)** Drag: `ζ_i = λ A_i` | DCM per-node drag → make proportional to node polygon area A_i (see M4). λ derived from η, not fit. |
| **(4)** Euler update: `r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (1/ζ_i)(F_i + F_i^T)Δt` | Their integrator is explicit Euler; DCM's IPC/large-dt + BAOAB is *stronger* — keep ours, this is the reference. |
| **(5)** Dead-band extensional: `U_s = ½κ_s(r−r0,L)²  [r<r0,L]; 0 [r0,L≤r≤r0,H]; ½κ_s(r−r0,H)² [r>r0,H]` | DCM membrane **edge** spring with slack window (M5). r = edge length; κ_s = edge stiffness. |
| **(6)** Bending (dihedral): `U_b = κ_b[1 − cos(θ − θ_0)]`, θ_0 = 0 | **Exactly DCM membrane bending κ_m**: θ = dihedral between adjacent faces, θ_0 = 0 (flat rest). Forces distributed to the 4 nodes of the two triangles. Confirms our bending form. |
| **(7)** Volume: `U_v = κ_v (V − V0)² / (2V0)`, V by signed-tetrahedra sum | **Exactly DCM enclosed-volume / turgor constraint**. V0 = target volume; κ_v = volume stiffness (↔ our κ_vol / bulk modulus behind Π₀). This is the equation M1/M2 exercise. |
| **(8)** Area: `U_a = κ_a (A − A0)² / (2A0)` | **DCM membrane area-elasticity K_A** (global form). A0 = target area; κ_a ↔ K_A. Make soft/asymmetric for reservoir (M3). |

Cytokinesis driver is **not** an equation — it is the kinematic band-node BC of M1 (constant-speed inward displacement, freeze at 0.5 µm from axis).

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce as DCM scenarios

**P1 — Single-cell cytokinesis (core DCM division test).** Sphere R (10 µm in paper; use our MCF7 R=7.5 µm), full compartments ON. Select 1-µm equatorial band, drive band nodes inward at v_c toward axis, freeze at 0.5 µm. Measure **axial elongation = (leftmost−rightmost extent)/initial diameter** vs. **volume change**. Reproduce: 5% vol loss → ~60% elongation; ~40% vol loss → ~0 elongation (Fig. 7 d,e; S4 e,f). Also record area change (+22%, Fig. S4 d) and time-to-plateau (~250 s, Fig. S4 c).

**P2 — κ_v / κ_a sensitivity sweep.** Repeat P1 across volume-conservation strengths and κ_a ∈ {1e-4,1e-5,1e-6} N/m; produce the elongation-vs-volume-change family (Fig. S4 e) to place our stiffnesses.

**P3 — Three-stage division sequence** (for full DCM division lifecycle): (i) **mitotic rounding** — cell rounds up generating net inward cell–matrix stress / tensile cell–cell stress; (ii) **division elongation** — ring pinch → outward axial, strongly anisotropic (perp stress <25% of axial); (iii) **postdivision spreading** — daughters re-spread, uniform outward stress. Useful as a scripted DCM scenario mapping to real cell-cycle stages.

**P4 — Aggregate division (N≈400).** Divide one cell inside the confluent DCM aggregate; measure **adjacent-cell area strain (negative, along axis only)** and **cell–cell boundary curvature increase** (M6). Confirms division force is neighbor-transmitted and reproduces the universal in-vivo signature.

**P5 — Division-force estimate (cross-check).** From adjacent-cell deformation in P4, back-out the applied force and compare to the FEM-inferred **300–500 nN** (Fig. 5 h,i).

## 6. RISKS / caveats and how to adapt

- **Cell type + geometry:** MDCK / MCF10A epithelial cells in a **flat 2D-confined monolayer** on ~1 kPa gel; our DCM is a single **MCF7-like** cell (R=7.5 µm) / small 3D aggregate. The *cell-intrinsic* coupling (ring pinch + volume incompressibility → elongation, Eqs. 5–8) is geometry-agnostic and ports directly. The *stress-anisotropy* and *neighbor-transmission* results assume the epithelial-monolayer confinement — apply those only in the aggregate scenario (P4), not to a lone cell.
- **Model is membrane-ONLY (stripped) — do NOT copy that.** The paper's BD model is a bare membrane shell with volume+area constraints; it has **no cortex tension γ, no cytoplasm viscosity η, no nucleus, no gravity**. Under our HARD physiological-baseline / full-compartment rule we adopt only the **mechanism** (M1 ring BC, Eqs. 5–8, M4 drag) into our **full-compartment, from-resting, native-scale** cell — cortex/cytoplasm/nucleus/turgor stay ON at physiological values. Their R=10 µm ≈ 20-µm mitotic MDCK; rescale band/stop/edge lengths to our R=7.5 µm.
- **v_c and κ_s, κ_b are not published** (Table S2 not transcribed in text). **Do not invent them to hit 60% elongation** (would be fitting-to-outcome / magic number). Derive v_c from the real cytokinesis duration (ring closes over ~minutes), κ_v from near-incompressibility, and treat 60% as the *emergent* check — if it doesn't emerge at physiological κ_v, that is a finding to surface, not a knob to turn.
- **Explicit Euler + no contact:** their integrator is plain Euler with a single free membrane (no cell–cell contact in the sim). Our IPC/large-dt + node-face penalty is strictly stronger; keep ours. The ring-node freeze-at-0.5µm can create a near-zero-radius neck that stresses the solver — cap neck radius / lean on remesh + IPC.
- **300–500 nN is force transmitted through neighbors, read via a separate FEM** (adjacent-cell continuum, E=1 kPa, ν=0.38), not the dividing cell's own cortex output. Use as an order-of-magnitude cross-check on our aggregate P5, not as a per-node force law.
- **Substrate stiffness:** monolayer experiments used ~1 kPa; single cells needed 7.43 kPa to divide. If DCM ever couples division to a substrate, this stiffness dependence is a caveat (soft-gel confinement matters), but our current single-cell/aggregate DCM has no compliant substrate, so it's informational only.
