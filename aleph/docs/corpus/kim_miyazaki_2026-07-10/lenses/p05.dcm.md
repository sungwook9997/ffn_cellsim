# DCM lens — p05

**Paper:** Matsuda, Li, Brumm, Adachi, Inoue, Kim (2019) — *Mobility of Molecular Motors Regulates Contractile Behaviors of Actin Networks*, Biophys. J. 116:2161–2171, DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2019.04.018.

**One-line honest verdict for DCM:** This is a *fine-grained agent-based* actomyosin model (Kim-lab Cytosim-like Brownian dynamics of cylindrical F-actin/ACP/motor segments) — the native home of the **FF engine, not DCM**. For DCM (surface-mesh continuum) it is **not a parameter source** and offers **no equation to port to the mesh level**. Its value to DCM is *conceptual/architectural*: it tells us **what emergent variable controls the cortical surface tension γ that DCM currently prescribes as a lumped scalar**, and it gives a **connectivity criterion** (two-arm/antiparallel bridging vs. one-arm/drag-anchored pulling) that maps directly onto why DCM's *aggregate-σ compaction* is drag-limited and why *cadherin T1 rearrangement* (not more σ) is the real rate lever. Treat everything below as **motivation and cross-scale sanity targets**, sourced from a purified/quasi-2D-slab regime that must NOT be back-fit into DCM's physiological baseline.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt (DCM module → specific change → why it beats current)

DCM cannot ingest a filament-level motor model without violating its own coarse-graining charter (γ is a *surface* field, not a bath of explicit motors). So the "adopt" here is **structural insight for the FF→DCM upscaling contract**, not a new DCM force. Ranked by usefulness:

1. **Cortex tension γ generation (module: `cortical_tension` / γ-floor problem) — reframe γ as MOBILITY-gated, not just density×force.**
   DCM's open γ-floor problem historically framed the deficit as *force-magnitude / density* limited (½·n2D·f·ℓ). This paper adds a **third axis DCM ignores: motor mobility ζ_M**. The active tensile force a motor exerts is *not* fixed by its stall force — for a **one-arm (myosin-I-like, membrane-anchored) motor it is set by how much drag anchors its reaction force** (Fig 6a, "rope on rough vs. slippery surface"), and it is **biphasic in mobility** (Fig 2d). For a **two-arm (myosin-II thick-filament-like) motor bridging antiparallel F-actin, force is mobility-INDEPENDENT** (Fig 6b). *Why this beats current DCM:* it explains that the cortical tension DCM tries to generate is dominated by the **two-arm/antiparallel population** (myosin-II minifilaments), whose contractile output is **connectivity-limited, not drag-limited** — so the correct FF→DCM γ closure should scale with *antiparallel-bound motor count × per-motor force*, and is *insensitive* to the cytosol viscosity knob DCM would naively expect to matter. This is a guardrail against "tune η to fix γ."

2. **Aggregate compaction RATE (module: aggregate-σ + cadherin junction / T1) — connectivity, not viscous pulling, sets contraction; drag-limited pulling is exactly the wrong lever.**
   The paper's central dichotomy is the microscopic twin of DCM's open compaction-rate problem. One-arm motors that rely on **drag as a reaction anchor** produce **many small clusters and saturate** — i.e. drag-limited, slow, order-dependent aggregation. Two-arm motors that **bridge two filaments** contract robustly and monotonically. Mapping up: DCM's *aggregate-σ pull* is the "one-arm, drag-anchored" mode (mechanical end-state only, ~100 s drag-limited — matches memory note `project-dcm-compaction-turgor-blocked`), whereas real 24–48 h compaction is a **connectivity-rearrangement (bridging → T1 neighbor exchange) process**. *Why it beats current:* independent literature confirmation that **escalating σ (a drag-pull) will not fix the rate** — you need the junction to *re-bridge new neighbors* (T1), the continuum analog of a two-arm motor finding a fresh antiparallel pair. Directly supports the PI-scoped decision to build **T1 rearrangement escalation** rather than more aggregate-σ.

3. **Cell division / cytokinetic ring (module: division / contractile ring — currently open) — node MOBILITY as a ring-assembly gate.**
   §Discussion (p.9): fission-yeast cytokinesis nodes = membrane-anchored multi-head (two-arm-like) motors whose **mobility is deliberately suppressed** by ER–PM contacts (ref 47). *Increasing* node mobility *inhibits* proper ring assembly (premature actin clustering). *Why useful for DCM's future cytokinetic-ring module:* the ring line-tension should be modeled as an **equatorial band of low-mobility, high-connectivity contractile elements**; the physiological correct baseline is *anchored/low-mobility* nodes, not free motors. Concretely: when DCM adds a cytokinetic-ring line-tension γ_ring on the equatorial faces, it should be applied to an **anchored band** (membrane-coupled) and be **connectivity-driven**, reproducing that too-mobile → no ring.

4. **Membrane–cortex coupling (module: membrane_cortex coupling) — myosin-I as the mobility bridge.**
   The paper's *one-arm motor = myosin-I anchored to the membrane, drag set by membrane surface viscosity* is exactly the physical object at the DCM membrane–cortex interface. Its mobility (hence its tension contribution) is set by **membrane surface viscosity η_s**, a DCM membrane field DCM already carries but does not couple to γ. *Adopt as insight:* the membrane's surface viscosity is a **legitimate physiological modulator of the cortical-tension source term** — not an inert bending/area shell.

*(No item here introduces a lumped proxy in place of a mechanism; each is a cross-scale mapping that keeps the mechanism where it belongs — in FF — and only informs DCM's closure/baseline choices.)*

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

**Honest finding: none port directly.** Every governing equation is a *segment-level* Langevin/agent law, not a surface-field law. Transcribed for completeness with the (non-)mapping to DCM state:

- **Segment Langevin (overdamped):** `ζ · dr/dt = F_ext + F_bend + F_rep + F_thermal`. *DCM map:* this is the FF per-node equation; DCM's node EOM is the *surface-mesh* analog `ζ_node · dx_node/dt = F_area(K_A) + F_bend(κ_m) + F_tension(γ) + F_pressure(Π) + F_contact + F_thermal`. The paper does NOT give any of the DCM surface terms — no K_A, κ_m, γ, Π law. **Do not port.**
- **Bell's law ACP off-rate (Eq., ref 34):** `k_off = k_off,0 · exp(F·λ / k_BT)`. *DCM map:* this is the **cadherin catch/slip junction** off-rate DCM already uses (Rakshit KU-4.2); the paper reuses it for cross-linkers. Confirms the functional form DCM already has — **no change**, cross-check only.
- **PCM motor kinetics (Erdmann–Schwarz refs 35–36):** `k_{w,M}(F), k_{u,M}(F)` both ∝ k₂₀, both *decrease* with load (catch-bond), stall ∝ 1/k₂₀. *DCM map:* purely sub-grid; DCM has no explicit motors. Feeds the **FF** engine's motor module, not DCM. The only DCM-level residue is the *scalar* it ultimately determines — the cortical tension γ — which DCM sets by γ = ΔP·R/2 (osmoregulation) and the interphase/mitotic 0.15/1.5 mN/m targets. **No mesh equation.**
- **Heterogeneity Q_A (Eq. 1):** grid-SD of actin density. *DCM map:* not a mechanical law; could be repurposed as a **cortex-density-uniformity diagnostic on the mesh** (SD of γ or of node cortical density over surface patches), analogous to DCM's existing "check UNIFORMITY not V/V0" rule for confluent aggregates. Optional diagnostic only.

**Net:** 0 equations to port as physics; 1 (Bell's law) confirmed-already-present; 1 (Q_A) optionally reusable as a surface-uniformity metric.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce as DCM scenarios

The paper's geometry is a **10×10×0.1 µm PBC slab** — a periodic thin-film, *not* a closed cell. It is **not directly a DCM scenario** (DCM is a closed surface enclosing a volume). Usable protocol ideas, adapted:

1. **Cytokinetic-ring assembly scenario (for the future DCM division module):** reproduce the *qualitative* node-mobility gate — impose an equatorial contractile band on an anchored (low-mobility, membrane-coupled) set of faces and show a ring *forms and constricts*; then raise the band's effective mobility (weaken membrane anchoring) and show the ring *fails to assemble / clusters prematurely* (paper's ref-47 result). This is a mechanism-level acceptance scenario, not a fit.
2. **Cortex-uniformity vs. contractility diagnostic:** apply the Q_A-style surface-density SD (§2) as a pass/fail uniformity check on a single DCM cell's cortex under increasing γ — a healthy cortex stays uniform; excessive local contraction should surface as SD growth (analogous to the paper's clustering). Diagnostic scenario, reuses DCM's existing "uniformity not V/V0" audit.
3. **Aggregate compaction contrast (N≈400):** run the existing aggregate-σ scenario and contrast a *drag-pull-only* run vs. a *T1-rearrangement-enabled* run; the paper predicts the drag-only mode saturates (small clusters) while the connectivity/rearrangement mode compacts monotonically — a direct qualitative check that T1 (not more σ) is the rate lever.

*(The purified motility-assay vs. reconstituted-gel and lipid-bilayer-viscosity in-vitro verification protocols on p.8 are FF/experimental, not DCM.)*

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

**No direct DCM observable is validated by this paper** (no tension in mN/m, no shape index, no division force, no compaction fraction, no contact angle). The measured numbers are per-filament/per-motor and belong to FF/TAG. Recorded so DCM does not mis-borrow them:

- Per-motor-arm tensile force: **~8–13 pN** (Fig 2a one-arm, Fig 4a two-arm). *FF motor-force target; NOT a DCM γ.*
- F-actin average speed: **0.02–0.08 µm/s** (Fig 2c/4c). *FF retrograde/pulling-speed target.*
- Unloaded walking velocity: **140 nm/s (ref k₂₀) → ~2 µm/s (max k₂₀)**. *FF motor calibration.*
- Steady-state onset of scalar metrics: **t ≈ 20 s** (force/%bound/speed plateau; Fig S2). *Cross-scale sanity: a cortex mechanical relaxation reaching quasi-steady in ~10s of seconds is consistent with DCM's ~100 s mechanical-saturation observation for σ-compaction — a soft consistency check, not a target.*
- **Qualitative trend targets that DCM should NOT contradict:** (a) two-arm/antiparallel contraction is **mobility-insensitive** → DCM's γ closure must not make cortical tension strongly depend on cytosol viscosity; (b) drag-anchored contraction **saturates into small clusters** → DCM aggregate-σ compaction saturating at ~100 s is expected, not a bug; (c) contraction is **biphasic in ATP (k₂₀)** (ref 49, and this paper) — a future FF cross-check, not DCM.

**DCM's own validation anchors remain unchanged and paper-external:** γ_interphase ≈ 0.15 mN/m, γ_mitotic ≈ 1.5 mN/m, γ = ΔP·R/2, Π₀ ≈ 40 Pa, η ≈ 65 Pa·s, R ≈ 7.5 µm.

## 6. RISKS / caveats (regime mismatch + how to adapt without breaking the baseline)

1. **Wrong engine.** This is a *filament-level* model; its natural consumer is FF. **Risk:** importing pN motor forces or ζ_M into DCM's surface config as if they were γ/η. **Adapt:** keep all numbers in FF; DCM takes only the *conceptual* connectivity/mobility framing above.
2. **Purified proteins, not living MCF7.** F-actin, ACP, myosin in a reconstituted quasi-2D gel; the authors explicitly state (p.4) "parameter values … are not always physiologically relevant" and are swept over unphysical ranges to expose trends. **Risk:** treating any swept value as a physiological setpoint → violates the physiological-baseline HARD rule. **Adapt:** use only trends, never the sweep magnitudes; set DCM baselines from MCF7 literature (existing config), not from here.
3. **2D slab vs. 3D closed cell.** 10×10×0.1 µm PBC film with repulsive z-walls has no enclosed volume, no turgor Π, no membrane area-elasticity — none of DCM's defining physics. **Risk:** the geometry is not a DCM scenario. **Adapt:** only the *cytokinetic-ring anchoring* and *uniformity-diagnostic* ideas transfer, re-expressed on the closed mesh.
4. **No actin turnover; planar-motor assumption.** Authors flag both as simplifications (p.9). **Risk:** the paper's clustering/aggregation timescales are lower bounds and not comparable to DCM's turnover-bearing cortex. **Adapt:** use for direction-of-effect only.
5. **Drag coefficients admittedly un-estimable in cells.** Authors (p.9) say cytoplasm and membrane viscosities are method-dependent and giant-vesicle η_s ≠ real-cell η_s. **Risk:** borrowing ref-52 membrane viscosity into DCM. **Adapt:** if DCM needs a membrane surface viscosity to couple to γ, **surface to PI for an MCF7 value; do not import the vesicle number.**

### Bottom line for DCM
Low direct impact, moderate architectural value. The paper contributes **zero new mesh equations and zero DCM parameters**, but supplies two well-grounded guardrails the DCM roadmap already needs: (1) **connectivity/rearrangement — not viscous drag-pull — sets contraction rate**, reinforcing that aggregate-σ is a mechanical end-state and **T1 rearrangement** is the correct compaction-rate lever; and (2) **cortical tension is mobility-gated at the membrane**, so the γ source term should couple to membrane surface viscosity and be dominated by the two-arm/antiparallel (myosin-II) population — a sanity constraint on any future FF→DCM γ closure. Its real home is the FF engine.
