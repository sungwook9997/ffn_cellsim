# DCM lens — p67

**Paper:** Lee, Pelz, Ferrer, Kim, Lang, Kamm (2009) *Cytoskeletal Deformation at High Strains and the Role of Cross-link Unfolding or Unbinding.* Cell. Mol. Bioeng. 2(1):28–38. DOI 10.1007/s12195-009-0048-8.

**One-line verdict for DCM:** This is a single-molecule / fine-grained filamin crosslink paper (belongs to FF). For the **surface-mesh DCM engine it is weak/indirect** — DCM has no explicit crosslink DOF. The one genuinely DCM-scale lever it offers is a **force-dependent, angle-dependent rupture law for the cell–cell cadherin junction** (already a catch-bond cluster in DCM), which touches the OPEN T1-rearrangement/compaction-rate problem. Everything else is context or FF territory. **Relevance to DCM: 2/5.** Do NOT invent a lumped cortex "strain-softening" surface law from this — that would violate the mechanistic/no-magic-number rules and the paper gives no surface-level constitutive equation.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Junction rupture: add a shear/torsion angle-knockdown to the cadherin catch-bond off-rate (**the one real DCM lever**)
- **DCM module:** cell–cell junction interface (`dcm/` cadherin catch-bond cluster, Rakshit KU-4.2; load-sharing clusters + contact-age maturation).
- **Change:** the paper's Fig. 5a shows the crosslink rupture force is roughly **constant below a ~45° pulling angle and drops sharply above it** — i.e. when the bond is loaded in shear/torsion instead of pure tension it ruptures at much lower force. DCM currently loads its junction by the scalar normal/tangential force between node-face pairs and (as far as the catch-bond law goes) treats the load as an effective tension magnitude. Add an **angle-resolved off-rate**: decompose the junction force on each cadherin cluster into normal (tension) and tangential (shear) components relative to the two membrane surfaces, and let the effective bond-loading angle θ modulate the Bell/catch-bond off-rate so that shear-dominated loading (θ ≳ 45°) accelerates unbinding.
- **Why it beats what DCM does now, and why it matters:** during **loose→compact aggregation and T1 neighbour-exchange**, junctions between cells that are sliding past one another are loaded predominantly in **shear**, not tension. A tension-only off-rate keeps those junctions artificially long-lived, which is exactly the regime where DCM's compaction is stuck (drag-limited, no genuine T1 escalation — see MEMORY `project-dcm-compaction-turgor-blocked`). A shear-weakened off-rate makes sliding junctions rupture-and-rebind preferentially, which is the mechanistic seed of emergent T1 rearrangement — the paper's finding that shear/torsion drops rupture force is the physical justification for it. This is fine-grained (per-cluster force decomposition), not a lumped tissue σ.

### M2 — Two-barrier (loading-rate-dependent) energy landscape for the junction off-rate
- **DCM module:** same junction catch/slip-bond law.
- **Change:** Fig. 5b + Table 1 show the rupture force rises with **ln(loading rate) with two distinct slopes** — evidence of two energy barriers (an inner and a suppressible outer barrier). If DCM's cadherin junction is currently a single-barrier Bell/Rakshit form, this paper (together with the α-actinin Ferrer 2008 sister paper already in the KB) is the literature support for a **two-barrier landscape** so that junction lifetime has the right loading-rate dependence across the slow (physiological, hours) vs. fast (perturbation) regimes.
- **Why:** DCM's real target dynamics span 24–48 h aggregate compaction (very low effective loading rate) up to fast mechanical perturbations. A single-barrier off-rate cannot have the correct lifetime in both regimes; a two-barrier form can. This is a mechanism-fidelity upgrade, not a tuning knob.

### M3 — (context only, NOT a DCM change) unfolding vs. unbinding distinction
- The paper's headline — crosslinks can **unfold** (reversible, ~28 nm Ig sawtooth, refolds on unload) OR **unbind** (irreversible until rebind) at *similar* critical force (57 vs 70 pN) — is a **cortex/crosslinker remodeling picture**. For DCM this is context: it explains *why* a cortex has both a recoverable-compliance branch (unfolding) and a plastic-remodeling branch (unbinding). **Do not** encode this as a DCM surface constitutive law — DCM has no crosslink DOF and the paper provides no surface-mesh equation. This belongs in FF. Flag it only so a future DCM cortex-plasticity effort knows the two-branch source.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (to the junction, not the surface elasticity)

### E1 — Bell force-dependent off-rate (junction unbinding)
```
k_off(F) = k_off0 · exp( F · x‡ / (k_B T) )        (Eq. 1)
```
- **Map to DCM:** `F` = tension carried by one cadherin cluster on a node-face junction pair; `x‡` = transition-state distance; `k_off0` = zero-force off-rate; `k_B T` = 4.11 pN·nm at 37 °C. This is the same Bell form DCM's junction already uses — the paper supplies **filamin** numbers, which are a crosslinker, NOT cadherin, so use them only as a **sanity range / oracle for the crosslinker used in FF**, not as the cadherin junction parameters (see Risks R1).

### E2 — Barrier height from spring constant and transition distance
```
ΔG = ½ · κ_m · x‡²                                  (Eq. 2)
```
- **Map to DCM:** `κ_m` = molecular spring constant of the bond, `x‡` = transition-state distance. Lets you cross-check that any junction off-rate parameters you adopt correspond to a physically sane barrier (a few k_BT), rather than a number tuned to pass a gate. Useful as a **magic-number-block guard** on junction parameters.

### E3 — Dynamic-force-spectroscopy two-slope scaling (implied)
```
F_rupture ∝ (k_B T / x‡) · ln( r / r0 )   with a slope change between two barriers
```
- **Map to DCM:** `r = dF/dt` = loading rate on the junction cluster. Supports the two-barrier off-rate of M2 and tells you the *sign and functional form* of how junction lifetime should shorten as loading rate rises.

### E4 — Angle knockdown (mechanism M1, phenomenology from Fig. 5a)
The paper gives no closed form, only the empirical fact: rupture force ≈ constant for θ < ~45°, then falls. Port as a **monotone shear-weakening factor** on the effective loaded tension, e.g. resolve the junction force into normal `F_n` and shear `F_s`, and feed an effective force `F_eff = F_n + w·F_s` (with `w` derived from the ~45° threshold, not fitted) into E1. **The 45° threshold itself is a derivable geometric datum** (the angle at which shear equals tension in the two-filament crosslink geometry) — it is grid-invariant and NOT a magic number, which is what makes it admissible.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

### P1 — Bead-drag-through-network (Figs. 6–7) → NOT a DCM scenario
The reconstituted-gel bead-drag assay (1.2 µm bead pulled at 20 µm/s through a crosslinked F-actin gel, force-vs-displacement with discrete rupture drops) is a **fine-grained network probe** — it is the FF/Cytosim scenario, not a surface-mesh DCM one. DCM has no explicit gel to drag a bead through. Note it for FF; skip for DCM.

### P2 — Junction shear-vs-tension pull (adapted from Fig. 5a) → usable DCM micro-test
Build a **two-cell DCM contact** and load the shared cadherin junction at controlled angle: pull the two cells apart along the interface normal (θ=0, pure tension) vs. slide them tangentially (θ→90°, pure shear), measuring the junction force at which the cluster de-coheres. This is the DCM-scale analogue of the paper's angle-dependence experiment and is the **validation protocol for mechanism M1**. It uses the native two-cell full-compartment build (no stripping).

### P3 — Loading-rate sweep on the junction (adapted from Fig. 5b) → usable DCM micro-test
Separate a two-cell junction at several separation speeds (loading rates spanning the physiological-slow to perturbation-fast range) and confirm the de-cohesion force rises with ln(rate) — validates the two-barrier off-rate of M2.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **R1 — Filamin ≠ cadherin (wrong molecule for the DCM junction).** Every force/off-rate number here is for the **actin crosslinker filamin A**, a cytoskeletal ABP. DCM's cell–cell junction is a **cadherin** adhesion, anchored to Rakshit KU-4.2. Porting filamin's 70 pN / k_off0=0.115 s⁻¹ into the cadherin junction would be a category error and a KB-citation-integrity violation. Only the **dimensionless mechanisms** (angle knockdown shape, two-barrier landscape) transfer; the cadherin *magnitudes* must stay on their own literature anchor. Adopt M1/M2 as functional forms, keep cadherin parameters as-is.
- **R2 — In-vitro purified, room-temperature, non-physiological loading rate.** The assays use purified proteins, RT, and 400–2000 pN/s loading (5 µm/s stage) — orders of magnitude faster than physiological junction loading (24–48 h compaction). Per the **physiological-baseline HARD rule**, do NOT initialise or calibrate DCM junction dynamics at this fast regime; use the **low-loading-rate branch** (Ferrer 2008, k_off0≈0.087 s⁻¹, the sister paper already in the KB) as the physiological anchor, and treat the fast-regime numbers as the perturbation limb of the two-barrier form only.
- **R3 — Fine-grained-network scale, not surface-mesh scale.** The bead-drag and single-molecule assays live at the filament/crosslink scale — this is **FF's domain**. Trying to force a lumped cortex "strain-softening" surface constitutive law out of this paper would (a) invent a magic-number continuum law the paper never states and (b) violate the mechanistic-over-lumped rule. Keep the DCM take narrow: junction off-rate shape only.
- **R4 — Shear/torsion decomposition on a triangulated interface is nontrivial.** M1 needs a well-defined normal/tangential split of the junction force at each node-face cluster; on a remeshing surface this must be grid-invariant (defined from the interface geometry, not mesh edges) or it becomes a resolution-dependent artifact. Validate grid-invariance before using it in a conclusion (native + full-compartment, per HARD rule).
- **R5 — Two-barrier form must not become a fitting vehicle.** Adopt M2's two-slope landscape only with barrier parameters that satisfy E2 at physically sane ΔG (few k_BT); never tune the barrier to make a compaction-rate gate pass. If the physiological cadherin barrier is unknown, surface to PI rather than borrowing filamin's.
