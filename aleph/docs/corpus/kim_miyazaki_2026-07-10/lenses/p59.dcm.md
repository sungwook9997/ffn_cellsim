# DCM lens — p59

**Paper:** Miyazaki, Chiba, Eguchi, Ohki, Ishiwata (2015) *Cell-sized spherical confinement induces the spontaneous formation of contractile actomyosin rings in vitro*, Nature Cell Biology 17(4):480–489, DOI 10.1038/ncb3142.

**One-line for DCM:** A bottom-up reconstitution that gives DCM a *mechanistic, parameter-complete recipe for the cytokinetic-ring / division module* — equatorial furrow placement from confinement + bending energy, a contraction-rate ∝ perimeter constriction law with a filament-length-set critical perimeter, constant-volume constriction, and a duty-ratio-squared effective-myosin law that ties cortex tension generation to motor kinetics. This is the strongest single-paper anchor for the currently-open **cell division / cytokinetic ring** problem, and secondarily informs **confinement response** and **cortical-tension γ generation**.

Caveat up front (governs all six sections): this is **purified rabbit skeletal-muscle actomyosin in water-in-oil droplets**, not a living MCF7 cortex. Numbers transfer as *mechanism form* and *order-of-magnitude anchors*, not as literal MCF7 setpoints. Every value below is flagged for how it maps under the physiological-baseline rule.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cytokinetic-ring / division module: equatorial furrow line-tension constriction (NEW module)
DCM currently lists "cell division / cytokinetic ring" as an *open problem*. This paper is a complete minimal spec for it. The mechanism DCM should adopt:
- A **contractile ring = a great-circle band of elevated cortex tension** on the mitotic (rounded) surface mesh. Not a separate object — it is a spatially-localized boost of the existing cortex γ on the ring of faces intersecting the division plane.
- **Placement is emergent, not imposed:** the ring localizes at the **equator (max-radius great circle)** because a semiflexible bundle (or, at DCM's continuum level, a tension band) minimizes bending/curvature energy at the largest radius (Fig. 2e). DCM should place the furrow by an energy-minimization over candidate planes, reproducing R_ring/R_droplet ≈ 0.96–0.97 (ring hugs the periphery, Fig. 2c).
- **Constriction law:** the ring shrinks with velocity proportional to its own perimeter above a critical perimeter, at **constant ring volume** (bundle thickens as radius shrinks). This is the constitutive law to port (Eq. below).

**Why it beats DCM now:** DCM has no division mechanism at all; the alternative "impose a shrinking constraint radius" is a lumped proxy. This gives a *mechanistic line-tension law* whose rate constant and critical cutoff are measured, and whose furrow placement is emergent from the same cortex-bending energy DCM already carries (κ_m). It respects the "one mechanism, fine-grained" rule: the furrow is a modulation of the existing cortex tension field, not a bolt-on force.

### M2 — Duty-ratio-squared effective-tension law for cortex γ generation
The unifying result [myosin]_eff = [myosin]_bulk × (duty ratio)² (Eq. 1) says only motors with **both heads simultaneously bound** produce inter-filament sliding/force, so the force-generating motor density scales as the *square* of the per-head duty ratio. DCM's cortex γ (and the open **γ-generation** problem, where active γ floors ~400–530× under band) currently treats myosin density/force as a scalar. Adopting the duty-ratio-squared gating means: the *active* fraction of bound cortex myosin that actually contributes to tension is (duty)²·[bound], not [bound]. This is a mechanistic correction that DCM's γ estimator can carry, and it explains why nominal density overestimates force.

**Why it beats DCM now:** it converts "how much myosin" into "how much *doubly-engaged* myosin," which is the physically load-bearing quantity. It is derivable (probability of two independent bound heads) — not a magic number — and it directly bears on the γ-floor deficit (part of the deficit may be that the *engaged* fraction is (duty)²-suppressed relative to the total).

### M3 — Cell-sized spherical confinement boundary condition + persistence-length criterion
The paper's confinement BC (a purely steric spherical wall, no chemistry — Supp. Fig. 2b,d) and the criterion R_cell ≲ L_p for a filament to behave as an equatorial elastic rod give DCM a clean **confinement-response scenario** and a size-vs-persistence-length coupling. At DCM's continuum cortex level this becomes: a tension band localizes to the minimal-curvature great circle, and under external compression re-orients to the plane *perpendicular to the compression axis* (Fig. 2g: rings go parallel to the coverslips). DCM can reproduce this reorientation as a confinement-response validation.

**Why it beats DCM now:** DCM has confinement listed as open. This gives a concrete geometry (spherical wall of radius R, or two-plate gap h = 4.44 µm) and a *predicted response* (equatorial → plate-parallel furrow reorientation) to validate against.

### M4 — Constant-volume constriction bookkeeping (remesh constraint)
During constriction the ring conserves actin volume: radius shrinks, bundle width thickens (Fig. 4e). For DCM this is a **remesh/mass-conservation constraint** on the furrow band — the cortex material swept out of the shrinking circumference is redistributed into the thickening band, not deleted. This keeps the furrow physically conservative and prevents the "vanishing cortex" artifact.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port

**E1 — Effective (force-generating) myosin density (Eq. 1, "central unifying law").**
```
[myosin]_eff = [myosin]_bulk × (duty ratio)²
```
Map to DCM cortex: let ρ_myo be the bound cortex-myosin areal density (per face, from the γ estimator), r_duty the per-head duty ratio. The *tension-generating* density is ρ_eff = ρ_myo · r_duty². Feed ρ_eff (not ρ_myo) into the active-tension term of γ. r_duty is a motor property (see P-table): myosin-II heads 0.04–0.05, myosin-V 0.5–0.7.

**E2 — Semiflexible bending energy / equatorial placement (elastic-rod criterion).**
```
E_bend = (κ/2) ∫ (1/R_local)² ds,     κ = k_B T · L_p
```
Confinement criterion: a filament/tension-band behaves as an elastic rod and localizes at the equator when **R_cell ≲ L_p**. Map to DCM: κ here is the *filament* bending stiffness (k_B T · L_p); at the surface-mesh level the corresponding quantity is the cortex bending modulus κ_m already in DCM. The furrow-placement rule = minimize Σ_faces (κ_m/2)(2H)²·A_face over candidate division planes → picks the max-radius great circle (lowest mean curvature H for a given band). This is the mechanistic reason R_ring/R_droplet → 0.96.

**E3 — Constriction-velocity ∝ perimeter with critical cutoff (Fig. 4f / Supp. Fig. 3d).**
```
dP/dt  = −k_c · (P − P*)      for P > P*,   else 0
```
where P = 2πR_ring is the ring perimeter, P* ≈ 4.5 µm (main) / 4.90 µm (SI) is the critical perimeter below which the ring cannot contract, and the **rate per unit perimeter** k_c ≈ 0.39 µm min⁻¹ per µm (main) / 0.31 (SI). Equivalently the linear constriction speed of the furrow radius is v = (k_c/2π)(P − P*). Map to DCM: this is the furrow-band constitutive law — the tangential contraction speed of the division-plane ring of nodes, gated below P*. P* is *set by the mean filament length* (≈5.4 µm) — at DCM's coarse scale, set P* from the cortex filament/segment length, NOT tuned to a gate.

**E4 — Constant-volume constriction (Fig. 4e).**
```
V_ring ≡ π R_ring · (π d²/4) = const
```
R_ring = furrow radius, d = bundle (band) width. As R_ring shrinks, d thickens to conserve V_ring. Map to DCM: mass/area-conservation constraint redistributing swept cortex area into the band thickness during remesh (M4).

**E5 — Ring-orientation observable (Methods).**
```
θ = sin⁻¹(h_ring / R_ring)
```
h_ring = Z-extent (bottom-to-top) of the ring, R_ring = ring diameter. θ ≈ equatorial for spherical confinement, plate-parallel for compression. Map to DCM: a division-plane-orientation diagnostic to score confinement response.

**E6 — Two-Gaussian band cross-section (Supp. Fig. 3c) — a measurement fit, port only if DCM emits synthetic cross-sections.**
```
F(x) = A₁ exp[−2(x−x₁)²/d₁²] + A₂ exp[−2(x−x₂)²/d₂²] + B
```
Use for extracting band width d from a DCM density cross-section to compare to E4.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

**S1 — Cytokinetic-ring / division scenario (primary).**
Start from a **rounded (mitotic) full-compartment cell at physiological baseline** (turgor Π₀, cortex γ_mitotic ≈ 1.5 mN/m, membrane, nucleus present). Nucleate a furrow band by boosting cortex tension on the equatorial great circle chosen by E2 energy-minimization. Drive constriction by E3 at constant volume (E4). Observables: R_ring(t), P(t), band width d(t), R_ring/R_droplet, θ. Success = furrow lands at equator (ratio 0.96), constricts at ~0.39 µm/min per µm perimeter, halts near P* if filament-length-limited, or R_ring→0 for complete division when motor density is escalated (Fig. 6: 44% complete at 23 µM HMM + 2 µM full-length myosin + 2 µM α-actinin).

**S2 — Spherical confinement / size-scan scenario.**
Vary R_cell from ~2 to ~20 µm; measure ring-formation probability. Target: monotonic decrease → 0 near R*~15 µm (≈L_p). Establishes the size↔persistence-length coupling as a DCM confinement-response gate. (Our MCF7 R=7.5 µm sits safely below R*.)

**S3 — Two-plate compression scenario.**
Confine the cell between plates at gap h = 4.44 µm. Predicted DCM response: furrow reorients from equatorial to **plate-parallel (perpendicular to compression axis)**, and R* rises to ~20 µm. A clean directional confinement-response validation.

**S4 — Motor-escalation / complete-constriction scenario.**
Stepwise raise effective cortex-myosin density (via E1: raise bound fraction or duty²) through the paper's 5-stage life cycle: polymerize → ring form → slow (weak) contraction → fast (strong) contraction → disassembly. Reproduce the bimodal weak/strong split (strong-contraction fraction 22% at 25 µM HMM) and the ATP-depletion-triggered slow→fast transition. This is the mechanistic driver for going from a *stalled* furrow to *complete* division.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

1. **In-vitro purified vs living MCF7 (biggest gap).** Rabbit *skeletal-muscle* myosin-II, purified actin, no membrane trafficking, no RhoA/formin/spindle signalling. DCM's cortex is non-muscle myosin-IIA/B on a living cell. **Adapt:** port the *laws* (E1–E4) and dimensionless targets (R_ring/R_droplet, k_c which the authors themselves say matches in-cell rings), but re-anchor duty ratio, ATPase and any tension magnitude to non-muscle-myosin / MCF7 sources before production. Do not KB-register the droplet γ magnitudes as MCF7 setpoints.

2. **The ring is a filament bundle; DCM cortex is a continuum tension field.** The paper's ring is a discrete semiflexible bundle whose width d is a physical bundle. At DCM's surface-mesh scale the furrow is a *band of elevated tension*, not a resolved bundle. **Adapt:** map d and P* onto the coarse cortex segment length; keep P* derived from filament/segment length (no tuning). Flag that constant-volume bookkeeping (E4) is the continuum stand-in for bundle thickening.

3. **Boundary is a steric water/oil wall, not a membrane+adhesion.** The confinement here has no adhesion, no traction, no lipid–actin coupling (myosin was even tail-truncated to avoid lipid interaction). DCM cells have membrane–cortex coupling and (in aggregates) cadherin junctions. **Adapt:** use S2/S3 confinement only as a *geometric* response test; do not import "no boundary interaction" into a scenario where membrane–cortex coupling is the physics of interest.

4. **Depletion (methylcellulose/PEG) bundling is an in-vitro crutch.** Ring assembly is helped by an artificial depletant, absent in cells. **Adapt:** ignore the depletion force for DCM (it is a reconstitution convenience); the physiological bundler is α-actinin/filamin (already in DCM's crosslinker vocabulary). Note the paper shows α-actinin alone *suppresses* formation unless balanced by myosin remodelling — a useful crosslinker-vs-motor balance constraint, not a magic number.

5. **P* and disassembly are filament-length / turnover artifacts of a closed reconstitution.** In cells, actin turnover replenishes the ring so constriction can complete; here it stalls at P* or disassembles (no depolymerization-driven completion — constriction is phalloidin-insensitive). **Adapt:** DCM's furrow should complete via continued cortex remodel/turnover (S4 motor escalation → 44% complete), not stall at P*. Treat P* as the *no-turnover* floor, and completion as requiring the physiological remodel that the droplet system lacks.

6. **No dynamical simulation in the paper — all "equations" are scaling/analysis laws.** There is no integrator, timestep, or force field to parity-check against. **Adapt:** E1–E5 are constitutive laws and observables, not a numerical oracle; validate DCM against the *measured numbers* in §5, and cross-check the emergent ring physics against the fine-grained FF engine (which can resolve the actual filament bundle) rather than against this paper's non-existent code.

---

**Bottom line for DCM:** This paper's highest value is as the **mechanistic + parameter-complete blueprint for the cytokinetic-ring/division module** (currently open): emergent equatorial furrow placement from cortex bending energy (E2), a measured constriction law dP/dt = −k_c(P−P*) at constant volume (E3/E4), and a duty-ratio-squared effective-tension law (E1) that also feeds the open γ-generation problem. Secondary value: concrete spherical-confinement and two-plate compression scenarios (S2/S3) for the open confinement-response problem. Our R=7.5 µm MCF7 sits inside the ring-forming regime (< R*~15 µm), so the transfer is regime-valid.
