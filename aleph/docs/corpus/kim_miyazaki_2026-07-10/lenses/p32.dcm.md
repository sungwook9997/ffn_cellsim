# DCM lens — p32

**Paper.** Jung, Li, Chaudhuri, Kim (2020), *Nonlinear Elastic and Inelastic Properties of Cells*, J. Biomech. Eng. 142(10):100806, DOI 10.1115/1.4046863. **Type: invited REVIEW** (Taeyoon Kim lab; Chaudhuri co-author). No new data, **no transcribable governing equations** in the running text — physics is conceptual/relational. Its value to DCM is (a) it is almost exactly the DCM design spec (enumerates the compartments a surface-mesh cell must carry and the emergent whole-cell behaviors it must reproduce), and (b) it hands over a set of secondary validation numbers with primary provenance. Treat every number as **secondary/quoted** (TAG-register against the primary ref, never this review).

Bottom line: this paper does not give DCM a new force law to port, but it names four inelastic behaviors DCM currently does not reproduce (superelastic cortex plateau, two-regime nuclear stiffening, cytoplasmic poroelasticity, viscoplastic irreversible offset) and, crucially, tells us the **mechanistic origin** of each — which is what lets DCM add them fine-grained rather than as a lumped Maxwell element (the review's own central criticism of continuum models).

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Superelastic cortex: tension plateau via fluidization + IF backstop  (γ-generation lever)
- **DCM module:** actomyosin cortex surface tension γ.
- **Now:** γ is pressure-borne, `γ = ΔP·R/2` (constant along the surface, set by osmoregulation). No stretch dependence.
- **Change:** make γ an *area-strain-dependent* constitutive law with a **tensional plateau**. Mechanism (Latorre 2018 [8], §4.1.2): as the cortex is stretched its finite actomyosin is diluted → it fluidizes (elastic→fluid transition above a critical tension), so tension *stops rising* and stays flat over huge areal strain (up to ~1000% area, plateau σ ≈ 1–3 mN/m in the epithelial dome). Intermediate filaments act as a **strain-hardening backstop** that (i) caps deformation and (ii) restores shape on unloading (reversible = superelastic).
- **Why it beats current DCM:** DCM's constant γ cannot reproduce the large-reversible-deformation regime cells use for confinement/transmigration, and gives no mechanistic ceiling on cortical tension — exactly the open "γ generation / γ-floor" problem. A fluidization plateau *is* a physiological ceiling on γ that emerges from constituent depletion, not a tuned cap.
- **Fine-grained framing (respects hard rule):** implement as cortex-density-coupled γ — γ rises with areal strain until surface actomyosin areal density drops below a fluidization threshold, then plateaus; an IF/keratin backstop term re-stiffens at very high strain. Density is already tracked in the FF cortex; DCM can carry an areal-density field per face.

### M2 — Nucleus: two-regime strain-stiffening (chromatin soft → lamin-A load-bearing)
- **DCM module:** nucleus (stiffer inclusion, E_nuc 1–10 kPa).
- **Now:** single linear-elastic modulus.
- **Change:** two-regime response (Stephens 2017 [104], §4.1.1): **chromatin dominates below ~3 µm extension** (soft), **lamin A becomes the load-bearing element above ~3 µm** (strong stiffening). Model as a soft bulk (chromatin) + a stiff shell that engages only at high extension (lamin-A nuclear-envelope mesh) — plus a **compressed-lamina reserve**: the lamin network sits pre-compressed and can expand ~2× in area before it load-bears (Dahl 2004 [105]).
- **Why it beats current DCM:** a single modulus cannot both let the nucleus squeeze through a constriction (needs the soft/low-strain regime + the 2× area reserve) *and* backstop the cell at extreme deformation (needs lamin-A stiffening). This is the mechanistically correct nucleus for the confinement/transmigration scenario.

### M3 — Poroelastic cytoplasm (short-time relaxation via fluid efflux)
- **DCM module:** cytoplasm (η ≈ 65 Pa·s lumped viscosity + turgor/enclosed-volume Π₀).
- **Now:** single-phase viscous + volume constraint.
- **Change:** biphasic solid+fluid (Moeendarbary 2013 [98], §4.4). Stress relaxes by water flowing through the cytoskeletal mesh; the response is **exponential at short time (poroelastic, t < ~0.05 s) then power-law (viscoelastic) at long time** ([101]). Governed by a poroelastic diffusion coefficient (see Eq. E1) set by mesh pore size and cytosol viscosity, giving a water-flow relaxation timescale **0.1–10 s** ([98]).
- **Why it beats current DCM:** DCM's single lumped η cannot produce the correct *rate-dependent* AFM/aspiration relaxation; poroelastic drainage is the physiological origin of short-time force relaxation and of the volume changes under indentation. Note this must **reconcile with, not double-count**, the existing enclosed-volume constraint — poroelastic drainage *is* the mechanism by which the enclosed volume relaxes under load rather than being rigidly conserved.

### M4 — Viscoplasticity: irreversible strain offset from bond rupture / turnover  (compaction-RATE lever)
- **DCM module:** cell–cell cadherin junction, aggregate-σ compaction, remesh, T1 rearrangement (open).
- **Now:** aggregate-σ compaction is drag-limited and reaches only a mechanical end-state; the real 24–48 h loose→compact rate needs cadherin T1-rearrangement escalation (per project memory).
- **Change:** the review's viscoplasticity section (§4.3, Fig. 6(a)–(c), [147]) frames this precisely: cell response = reversible viscoelastic (Boltzmann superposition of two power-law creeps) **plus an irreversible plastic offset that grows during load and stays flat during relaxation**, arising from **force-dependent crosslinker unbinding and F-actin turnover** (and, in the nucleus, chromatin realignment [107]). The plastic component scales ~linearly with force.
- **Why it beats current DCM:** this is the mechanistic identity of the "escalation" DCM needs — compaction is an *irreversible (plastic) rearrangement*, not an elastic-with-drag relaxation. The junction should carry a plastic-strain state variable that accumulates from cadherin/crosslinker rupture-and-rebind (already the Rakshit catch-bond machinery) — i.e. T1 rearrangement is the plastic-yield event, and its rate is set by force-dependent bond turnover, NOT by drag. This gives a physiological compaction clock without tuning drag.

### M5 — Power-law (soft-glassy) rheology, not single-time Maxwell
- **DCM module:** whole-cell viscoelastic response (creep / G′,G″).
- **Change:** creep and modulus follow a **power law** J(t)~t^β, G*~ω^β — *no single relaxation time* (§4.2, [126,127,130]). The review's headline criticism of continuum lumped models is that a spring+dashpot cannot reproduce G′/G″ frequency dependence; DCM's advantage (discrete cortex/filament turnover) is exactly what produces power-law behavior, so DCM should **report G′(ω),G″(ω)** as an observable and confirm power-law exponents rather than fitting a Maxwell time.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (relational only — this review prints no numbered laws)

**E1 — Poroelastic diffusion coefficient / relaxation timescale** (§4.4):
```
D_p ≈ E_solid · ξ² / η_fluid          τ_p ≈ L² / D_p
```
Map to DCM: `E_solid` = effective cytoskeleton/cortex elastic modulus; `ξ` = cytoskeletal **mesh pore size** (inter-node / inter-filament spacing on the surface mesh); `η_fluid` = cytosol viscosity (distinct from the 65 Pa·s *effective* cytoplasm η — here it's the drainage fluid); `L` = deformation length scale (indenter/aspiration radius). Target τ_p in **0.1–10 s** ([98]) with exp→power-law crossover ~0.05 s ([101]). Feeds a new `cytoplasm.poroelastic` block.

**E2 — Young–Laplace aspiration tension** (§3, [57]) — DCM measurement protocol, already the way DCM reads γ:
```
ΔP_asp = 2 γ (1/R_pipette − 1/R_cell)
```
Map: `γ` = cortex surface tension DCM state; `R_pipette`, `R_cell` = geometry. Use as the **γ read-out** in the aspiration scenario (§4).

**E3 — Entropic-spring / bending→extension stiffening** (§4.1.1, [93], conceptual): filament force–extension diverges as end-to-end distance → contour length; a network is soft while filaments bend, stiff once they extend. DCM-level consequence: the **cortex differential modulus rises with prestress/strain** (Fig. 3(d): K′ ≈ 1→~100 Pa above σ_crit). Port as a strain-stiffening γ–area tangent modulus, not as an explicit filament law (that's the FF side). Myosin prestress means the cortex sits already in the extension (stiff) branch at baseline — consistent with the physiological-baseline rule.

*(No Maxwell/Kelvin/SLS ODEs are recommended for adoption — the review explicitly argues these lumped forms fail on G′/G″; DCM should get viscoelasticity from discrete turnover, M4/M5.)*

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce (DCM scenarios)

1. **Micropipette aspiration + Young–Laplace (E2).** Pressure-difference sucks a tongue of the mesh into a pipette of radius R_p < R_cell; measure aspiration length vs ΔP over time → γ, apparent modulus, and (M3) the poroelastic short-time relaxation. Canonical DCM γ read-out. (§3, Fig. 2(c).)
2. **AFM indentation, rate-swept.** Spherical/sharp tip on the node-face contact; run **stress-relaxation (constant depth)** and **force–depth** ramps. Targets: moderate stiffening + loading/unloading hysteresis (Fig. 7(d): 0→~70 pN over 0→0.4 µm); nucleus makes it stiffen earlier (Fig. 8(b,c): 0→600 pN over 0→8 µm). Sweep loading rate to expose M3 poroelastic rate dependence. (§3, §5.)
3. **Whole-cell uniaxial microplate stretch.** Cell adhered top+bottom plate, oscillatory length change, force from plate deflection (<200 nm precision). Reproduces strain-stiffening (Fig. 3(a-c)) and creep-to-rupture (Fig. 5(a)). DCM: prescribe boundary node displacement, read reaction force.
4. **Pressurized dome (superelasticity, M1).** Pressurize a cell/patch (3D-TFM analog); plot γ vs areal strain and confirm the **plateau** (Fig. 3(e,f)). Direct test of M1 fluidization law. (E=3 kPa substrate BC.)
5. **Microfluidic constriction passage (confinement / transmigration).** Push the cell through a channel narrower than its diameter; passage velocity ↓ with stiffer cytoskeleton/nucleus and higher viscosity (Fig. 8(e), DPD [182]). Tests M2 nucleus two-regime + M3. This is DCM's **confinement-response** open problem in a validated protocol.
6. **Isolated-nucleus AFM, rate sweep 1→16 µm/s.** Peak force rises with loading rate (Fig. 6(e), [159]) — isolates M3 nuclear poroelasticity from the cortex.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **This is a review, not a primary source.** Every number is quoted from a cited paper. TAG-register each against its **primary** ref (e.g. σ_plateau→Latorre 2018 [8], crossover→Hu 2019 [101], nucleus 3 µm→Stephens 2017 [104]) and confirm the `verdict=OK` before citing in a deliverable — never cite this review as the datum.
- **Cell-type / scale mismatch on the σ magnitudes.** The superelastic σ ≈ 1–3 mN/m is an **epithelial-monolayer DOME (aggregate/tissue) tension**, and the 2× lamin reserve is **Xenopus oocyte** — not single MCF7. Do **not** overwrite the MCF7 single-cell cortex γ (interphase 0.15, mitotic 1.5, KB-anchored). Adopt the **mechanism** (fluidization plateau + IF backstop) and let the plateau magnitude emerge from MCF7 actomyosin density; the dome number is an order-of-magnitude bracket, not a setpoint. Likewise most rheology data are fibroblast/chondrocyte/myoblast — use as form/shape targets, magnitudes are cell-type-specific.
- **2D-substrate vs 3D-ECM.** The review's own closing gap (§6): properties measured on 2D or bare cells differ from cells in 3D ECM. DCM's confinement/durotaxis scenarios should prefer the 3D-relevant protocols (constriction passage, dome) and treat 2D AFM/microplate numbers as bounds.
- **New compartments (IF/keratin, lamin backstop) = PI-gated scope.** M1/M2 imply adding intermediate-filament and lamin-A elements DCM does not currently have. Under the physiological-baseline rule, MCF7 (epithelial) *does* carry keratin IF and lamin A, so turning them ON is legitimate — but this is a compartment addition, surface to PI, don't bolt on a tuned spring.
- **Poroelastic double-counting.** M3 must reconcile with the existing enclosed-volume constraint and the 65 Pa·s lumped η — poroelastic drainage is the *mechanism* of volume relaxation, so replacing (not stacking on top of) the rigid volume constraint under load is the correct move; keep the resting Π₀≈40 Pa baseline.
- **No magic numbers.** σ_plateau, the 3 µm crossover, τ_p, and β must enter from their primary sources (or be derived from cortex density / mesh pore size / cytosol viscosity via E1) — never tuned to make a gate pass. The review gives ranges (0.1–10 s, β 0.18–0.31), so pin the specific value to the closest-matching primary condition and record provenance.
- **The review offers zero portable force law.** Do not expect an equation drop-in; the deliverable here is the *mechanistic identity* of DCM's four missing inelastic behaviors plus a validation-target table, which is exactly what turns a lumped fix into a fine-grained one.
