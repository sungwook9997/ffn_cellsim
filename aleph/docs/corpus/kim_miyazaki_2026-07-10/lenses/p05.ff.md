# FF lens — p05

**Paper:** Matsuda, Li, Brumm, Adachi, Inoue, Kim (2019) "Mobility of Molecular Motors Regulates Contractile Behaviors of Actin Networks." *Biophys. J.* 116:2161–2171. DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2019.04.018.

**Why this one matters to FF:** This IS the FF engine's own physics lineage. It is the Kim-lab Cytosim-class agent-based Brownian-dynamics model — cylindrical-segment F-actin with extensional + bending stiffness (persistence length anchored), α-actinin-class ACP cross-linkers with **Bell's-law** force-dependent off-rate, and myosin motors driven by the **Parallel Cluster Model (PCM)** catch-bond cross-bridge cycle with excluded volume. Same vocabulary as `aleph/laws/`. The novel lever it introduces — **motor mobility, set by an explicit motor drag coefficient ζ_M, plus one- vs two-arm motor connectivity** — is directly portable into FF's Stam–Hocky minifilament module and is a first-principles mechanism for the cortical-tension γ / network-contraction open problems. Caveat: the deep numeric parameter tables (S1/S2 — absolute k_s, k_b, PCM's 5 rates, dt, segment lengths) live in the SI which is NOT in the provided text; several values below are ratios/reference values only and need an SI follow-up before they become magic-number-block provenance.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Motor MOBILITY as an explicit, first-class DOF (myosin module). ★ top lever
Current FF Stam–Hocky minifilament is (effectively) either a free bead in the cytosol drag or rigidly anchored. This paper shows the myosin **body drag coefficient ζ_M** is a *distinct control variable* from the head-actin kinetics, and it changes contractile outcome by up to the full dynamic range. Add a per-motor translational drag `zeta_M` on the minifilament backbone/bare-zone node (separate from head binding drag and from cytosol Stokes drag), physiologically set by *where the motor lives*: free thick filament in cytosol → low ζ_M; membrane-anchored / cortical / cytokinesis-node myosin → high ζ_M. **Why it beats current FF:** FF currently has no principled way to represent membrane-tethered vs cytosol-free myosin; today that's a lumped on/off. This makes anchoring an emergent mechanical resistance, not a switch — consistent with the fine-grained-over-lumped hard rule.

### M2 — One-arm vs two-arm motor CONNECTIVITY (myosin module).
The paper's central dichotomy: a motor that can grab only ONE F-actin (myosin-I-like) needs viscous drag as a *reaction anchor* to pull (contraction is **biphasic** in mobility, peak at intermediate ζ_M); a motor that bridges a PAIR of antiparallel F-actins ("two ropes, both hands") pulls them together with NO drag anchor, so contraction rises **monotonically** with mobility. FF's Stam–Hocky bipolar minifilament is inherently the two-arm (multi-head, both ends) case → FF should reproduce the *monotonic* branch. Adopt the explicit **antiparallel vs parallel binding classification** of each engaged head pair (dot product of the two host-filament tangents < 0 → antiparallel) as a diagnostic, and optionally add a **one-arm myosin-I clutch** (single-head, membrane-tethered) as a new motor species for the cortex/lamellipodium. **Why:** gives FF a mechanistic myosin-I for endocytosis/membrane-cortex adhesion contexts and a built-in sanity check that its bipolar minifilament sits on the correct (monotonic) mobility branch.

### M3 — Stall force / affinity tied to a single ATP-dependent unbinding rate k₂₀ (myosin module, PCM).
Rather than independently prescribing stall force, walk rate, off-rate, the paper drives ALL of them from one ATP-dependent head unbinding rate k₂₀ via PCM: `k_w,M ∝ k₂₀`, `k_u,M ∝ k₂₀`, **stall force ∝ 1/k₂₀**, both k_w and k_u *decrease under load* (catch bond). This gives FF a single physiological ATP knob that co-varies velocity and force self-consistently — beats FF independently tuning stall and off-rate (which risks a magic-number). Adopt k₂₀ as the myosin config's primary ATP-state parameter.

### M4 — Contraction is drag-rate-limited over ~100 s, not instantaneous (validation/turnover insight).
Scalar metrics (motor force, %bound, F-actin speed) plateau by **t ≈ 20 s**, but morphological heterogeneity (clustering/contraction) keeps evolving to **t = 100 s**, limited by network elasticity + inter-filament repulsion. This is an independent in-vitro corroboration of the DCM finding that mechanical contraction saturates on ~100 s (drag-limited), NOT the hours-long cadherin timescale — useful cross-engine consistency datum for FF's contraction runs.

### M5 — (explicitly-NOT-adopted, flag) No actin turnover in this model.
The paper deliberately omits severing/capping/branching. FF already has barbed-end growth + should keep Bell-Evans crosslink turnover; do NOT copy this simplification. Note the companion Kim paper (ref 29, Li–Biel–Kim 2017 *Soft Matter* "Buckling-induced F-actin fragmentation modulates contraction") IS the turnover/fragmentation mechanism — cite it as the follow-up source for FF's fragmentation channel, not this one.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### L1 — Overdamped Langevin per segment (already FF's form; confirms parity)
```
ζ · dr/dt = F_ext + F_bend + F_rep + F_thermal
```
ζ = segment drag; F_actin & F_ACP drags fixed, **ζ_M** (motor) is the swept control. Maps to FF's constrained BAOAB force balance; ζ_M → new per-minifilament drag term on the backbone node.

### L2 — Extensional (stretch) spring — per element type
`F = k_s (r − r₀)`, with distinct `k_{s,A}` (actin), `k_{s,ACP}` (crosslinker), `k_{s,M}` (motor). Maps directly to FF's bead-spring stretch modulus per bond type. Absolute values in SI Table S1 (not in text — SI follow-up needed).

### L3 — Bending (flexural) angular harmonic
`U_bend = ½ k_b (θ − θ₀)²` with `k_{b,A}`, `k_{b,ACP}`; `k_{b,A}` set to give actin persistence length **L_p = 9 µm** at reference. Maps to FF actin bending κ. ⚠️ **Supersession note:** FF currently uses ℓ_p ≈ 17 µm (canonical actin). This paper uses 9 µm (Isambert 1995, ref 57 — the lower end, reflecting bound-nucleotide/regulatory-protein softening). Do NOT blindly overwrite FF's 17 µm; flag as an alternative-source range 9–17 µm and keep FF's value unless PI re-anchors.

### L4 — ACP unbinding: Bell's law (matches FF exactly)
`k_off = k_off,0 · exp(F·λ / k_BT)` (ref 34, Bell 1978). Identical to FF's Bell-Evans crosslinker off-rate `k_off0·exp(F/F_b)` with `F_b = k_BT/λ`. Confirms FF's crosslinker kinetics scheme; source for λ is Ferrer 2008 (ref 60, already FF's α-actinin rupture-force anchor per MEMORY).

### L5 — Motor active kinetics: Parallel Cluster Model (PCM), Erdmann–Schwarz (refs 35,36) — PORT candidate
3 mechanochemical states, 5 rates. Walking `k_{w,M}(F)` and unbinding `k_{u,M}(F)` both **∝ k₂₀** and both **decrease with load F** (catch bond, refs 37 Guo–Guilford, 38 Thomas). **Stall force ∝ 1/k₂₀.** Each arm binds one actin site at rate `40·N_h s⁻¹`. Maximal per-motor force `= N_h × stall_force × n_arms`. FF currently uses Hill (1938) force-velocity + duty ratio for the Stam–Hocky heads. PCM is a *more mechanistic, ensemble-cluster* alternative that natively produces the catch-bond load dependence and the ATP-coupled stall/velocity trade-off. **Decision for FF:** either (a) keep Hill but add the catch-bond load-suppression of the OFF rate (currently likely Bell-Evans slip), or (b) adopt PCM wholesale for the head ensemble. PCM's 5 rates are in SI Table S2 (needs SI follow-up).

### L6 — Motor drag law
`F_drag = ζ_M · v`. Reference `ζ*_M = 8.10×10⁻⁸ kg/s`. This is the new FF parameter.

### L7 — Antiparallel two-arm force (mechanistic crux, no new equation, geometric rule)
A minifilament head pair on filaments with tangents t₁, t₂: if `t₁·t₂ < 0` (antiparallel) the pull needs no external drag anchor; if parallel it does. FF gets this *for free* from explicit two-ended minifilament geometry — this paper explains WHY FF's bipolar myosin should contract efficiently even for low-drag (cytosol-free) motors.

### L8 — Heterogeneity metric Q_A (portable FF observable)
```
Q_A = (1/2N_G) [ Σ_{i=1..N_G} std_j(ρ_A^{i,j}) + Σ_{j=1..N_G} std_i(ρ_A^{i,j}) ]
```
ρ_A^{i,j} = # actin segments in grid cell (i,j); N_G = 20 grids/direction. Averaged SD of filament areal density across rows & columns. Directly implementable as an FF cortex-clustering / condensation diagnostic (symmetry-breaking, buckling-condensation open problem).

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenario)

**Scenario "p05-mobility-slab":** thin quasi-2D actomyosin slab.
- Domain **10 × 10 × 0.1 µm**, PBC in x,y; repulsive walls in z. The 0.1 µm z-thickness is *deliberately* thin so filaments at similar (x,y) interact regardless of z — mimics a cortex (100–200 nm, Salbreux ref 42) with minimal volume exclusion.
- Self-assemble F-actin (nucleation + polymerization) to ⟨L_f⟩ = 1.5 µm, L_p per §3; add ACPs at R_ACP = 0.1 (α-actinin/filamin-class, Bell off-rate); add motors at R_M = 0.8 at random (x,y,z).
- A fraction of motor arms bind without walking; after the network forms, motors start walking. Two-phase init = physiological-baseline-compatible (form network at operating point, THEN activate).
- Sweep ζ_M (5 decades) × k₂₀ (0.25–32×) as 2D heatmaps; run to 100 s; sample v every 2 s.
- **FF adaptation to native/full rule:** run this as a *dev-scale in-vitro parity test* only (it is purified reconstituted geometry, coarse by design). Any conclusion (e.g. "FF bipolar myosin is on the monotonic branch") must be **reconfirmed on the native full cortex** (`--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules`, MCF7 R=7.5 µm) before it's reported — per the HARD native-scale rule. The slab is a mechanism-isolation harness, not a production cell.

**Second scenario "p05-motor-anchoring":** on the native cortex, set ζ_M high for a membrane-anchored myosin sub-population vs low for cytosol-free thick filaments, and measure the effect on γ generation / clustering. This is the physiologically-relevant use of M1.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Purified in-vitro, not living cortex.** Reconstituted slab with no membrane tension, no turgor, no nucleus, no microtubules. Use only as a mechanism-isolation harness; every finding reconfirmed on native full-compartment MCF7 cortex before reporting (HARD rule). Do NOT KB-register slab numbers as cortex parameters.
- **2D thin slab (0.1 µm) vs FF 3D cortex.** The z-thinness suppresses volume exclusion by construction — a modeling convenience. FF's native cortex has real 3D excluded volume (LJ on); expect quantitatively different clustering. Adopt the *mechanism* (mobility → contraction branch), not the slab geometry, for production.
- **ζ_M is not calibrated to a cell.** Authors are explicit: cytoplasm viscosity (refs 50,51) and membrane surface viscosity (ref 52) are uncertain by orders of magnitude, so ζ_M ↔ real motor drag mapping is "quite challenging." Treat ζ*_M = 8.10×10⁻⁸ kg/s as a *reference*, not a physiological anchor. For FF's physiological-baseline rule, derive the membrane-anchored motor drag from the actual MCF7 membrane/cytosol viscosity FF already uses (η ≈ 65 Pa·s cytoplasm), NOT from this paper's reference — surface to PI if no membrane-viscosity datum exists.
- **L_p = 9 µm conflicts with FF's 17 µm.** Isambert-1995 lower-end value (bound-nucleotide/regulatory softening). Keep FF's 17 µm; log 9–17 µm as the literature spread. No silent overwrite (no-fitting rule).
- **No actin turnover here.** Do not import this simplification; FF keeps growth + Bell-Evans turnover, and should take fragmentation from the companion ref 29 (Li–Biel–Kim 2017) instead.
- **N_h > 1 (8 / 4) is a coarse-graining** of head ensembles; it slightly inflates processivity & stall vs a single head. FF's Stam–Hocky already models multiple explicit heads — prefer FF's explicit-head count over importing N_h as a lumped multiplier; use N_h only to cross-check the max-force scaling `N_h × stall × n_arms`.
- **PCM adoption cost.** PCM's 5 rates (SI Table S2) are not in the provided text. Porting PCM wholesale is a larger change than augmenting FF's existing Hill+duty-ratio heads with catch-bond off-rate load-suppression. Recommend the incremental catch-bond augmentation first; full PCM only if head-ensemble stochasticity proves needed — and only after retrieving SI Table S2.
