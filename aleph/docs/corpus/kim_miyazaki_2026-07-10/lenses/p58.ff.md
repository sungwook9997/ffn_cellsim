# FF lens — p58

**Paper:** Negi, Sakamoto, Ienaga, Miyazaki, Maeda. *Myosin-Driven Advection and Actin Reorganization Control the Geometry of Confined Actomyosin Gel.* Nano Lett. 2025, 25, 17979–17987. DOI 10.1021/acs.nanolett.5c02558.

**One-line for FF:** Xenopus-extract actomyosin (F-actin + Arp2/3 + myosin II) confined in cell-sized semiclosed microwells self-organizes by myosin-driven *contractile advection* into a dense cluster; the confinement boundary shape is transferred into the gel shape via inward + upward actin flow. Measured emergent numbers (inward flow v = 1.20 ± 0.41 µm/s, wave period T = 101.0 ± 7.9 s) and the phenomenological split — **contractility (Pe) sets cluster SIZE, polymerization rate sets cluster SHAPE** — are the useful, transferable content. The model itself is a *continuum* 2D active-fluid/phase-field, i.e. the LUMPED opposite of FF; we do NOT adopt its equations as runtime, we adopt its *emergent targets* and its *mechanistic decomposition* as validation for FF's native-scale actomyosin cortex/network.

**Bottom line for FF impact:** MODERATE. This is exactly the FF physical regime (explicit actin + Arp2/3 branching + myosin II minifilaments contracting under confinement), but the paper's model is continuum, so it yields **validation targets and a scenario**, not new force laws. The single most valuable thing is a clean, purified, quantitative benchmark for **myosin-driven advective flow speed (~1 µm/s)** and **pulsatile/periodic contraction (~100 s period)** that FF's Stam–Hocky myosin + turnover should reproduce as an emergent observable — a direct test of the open FF problems "motor-driven flows," "network contraction & stress relaxation," and "buckling/condensation symmetry-breaking."

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

The paper is continuum, so nothing here replaces an FF force law. What it does is *pin down which FF mechanisms, run together, must produce which emergent behavior*, and it isolates the causal roles cleanly enough to be a mechanism-level acceptance test.

1. **Myosin (Stam–Hocky minifilament) → emergent contractile advection / inward flow.** The paper's central mechanism is that myosin-generated contractile stress drives a bulk *advective* mass flux of F-actin (Pe = advective/diffusive time-scale ratio). FF already has explicit bipolar minifilaments walking toward barbed ends with Hill force–velocity; the adopt-item is to **treat "net inward actin flow speed" as a first-class emergent FF observable of the myosin module**, benchmarked to v ≈ 1.2 µm/s in a purified crosslinked network. Beats current FF practice only in that we currently have no purified-network flow-speed acceptance target for the myosin module; this supplies one.
   - *Why it matters:* FF's open "motor-driven flows" and "crawl speed (grid-drag)" problems both hinge on whether the myosin module produces the right *bulk transport* velocity, not just per-head force. A network that generates correct per-motor force but wrong collective flow reveals a connectivity/turnover/drag bug — this benchmark exposes it.

2. **Turnover (polymerization source + depolymerization sink) as a SHAPE/steady-state controller, not just a maintenance term.** The paper shows depolymerization sink `k_d ρ` + surface-biased polymerization source `k_p` are what let the contracting network reach a *steady* cluster instead of collapsing to a point, and that tuning `k_p` (via CytoD/VCA) changes the emergent *shape*. FF currently has barbed-end growth + fragmentation as turnover; the adopt-item is to make FF's **turnover balance a tested determinant of the emergent contracted-state geometry** (does more polymerization → larger, rounder-vs-more-faceted condensate?), reproducing the CytoD→rounder / VCA→larger ordering.
   - *Why it beats current FF:* FF turnover is presently justified only by single-filament length distribution (KB-3.18) and ratchet growth (KB-3.6). This paper adds a *network-level* consequence — turnover sets the contracted steady state — giving turnover a second, emergent validation hook.

3. **Contraction as active *condensation/coarsening*, not just tension generation.** The mechanism "network contracts to a dense cluster with continuous inward flow" is the reconstituted analog of FF's open "network contraction & stress relaxation" and "confinement-driven self-organization." Adopt the *phenomenon* as an FF scenario: an initially space-filling native actomyosin network under confinement should **condense** with a measurable contraction rate and a final packing, with myosin activity as the driver. This is the mechanistic content behind DCM's aggregate-σ compaction work too, but here at FF (filament) resolution.

4. **Surface-biased nucleation (Arp2/3 preferentially at the boundary).** The model sets `k_p^bulk / k_p^surf = 0.5` — polymerization is ~2× faster at the confinement surface because "actin polymerization begins at the boundary after the initial contraction" (refs 16, 18). For FF, this maps to **spatially biasing Arp2/3 nucleation / barbed-end growth toward the membrane/confinement surface** rather than uniform-in-bulk. Beats a spatially-uniform nucleation assumption; it is also physiologically correct for a cortex (nucleation is membrane-templated). Treat as an optional, physiologically-motivated nucleation-localization for confined FF runs — NOT a magic number (the value 0.5 is model-internal and must not be registered as physical).

**NOT adopted (explicitly reject as lumped):** the active-fluid stress `σ_act ∝ ρ`, the phase-field boundary, the Péclet/`k_p^surf` nondimensional control knobs. These are the coarse-grained *proxy* mechanisms the FF architectural rule forbids at runtime. FF gets its active stress from explicit Stam–Hocky heads + Bell-Evans crosslinker kinetics; the boundary from explicit membrane containment + turgor. Use the continuum model only to predict *what the emergent field should look like*, as a cross-check oracle.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

**Direct verdict: none of the paper's equations port into FF as runtime.** They are continuum PDEs (lumped), which the hard rule forbids as mechanism. Transcribed here only as the **cross-check oracle** FF's emergent fields should be compared against, with symbol→FF-state mapping.

**Eq 1 — force balance (active fluid velocity):**
```
v = ∇²v + λ ∇(∇·v) + ∇f ,     λ = 1 + η_b/η   (set 1/3)
```
- `v` ↔ FF: the coarse-grained *number-density-weighted mean velocity* of actin beads on a grid (compute from FF particle velocities by binning) — the observable to compare, not a state to integrate.
- `∇f` (density-dependence of active stress) ↔ FF: emergent divergence of the myosin stress tensor computed from explicit minifilament head forces (virial stress on a grid). This is how you *measure* σ_act from FF to compare to the model's `f`.
- `λ = 1/3` ↔ FF: sets bulk/shear viscosity ratio of the cytosol; FF uses explicit LM/BAOAB drag + cytoplasm viscosity (η ≈ 65 Pa·s MCF7). Do not import 1/3; it's an in-vitro extract value.

**Eq 2 — actomyosin mass conservation (advection–diffusion + turnover):**
```
∂ρ/∂t + Pe ∇·(ρ v) = ∇²ρ + k_p − k_d ρ + ε_ρ ∇²(δE/δρ)
```
- `ρ` ↔ FF: local F-actin bead number density (grid-binned) — the field to compare against.
- `Pe ∇·(ρv)` ↔ FF: advective flux; in FF this is *emergent* from myosin-driven transport, not a prefactored term. Measuring `Pe_eff` from FF = (measured advective transport time)/(measured diffusive time) is a derived diagnostic, legitimately comparable to the paper's Pe ≈ 30–120 range.
- `k_p` (source) ↔ FF: barbed-end polymerization / Arp2/3 nucleation rate (per unit volume). FF has explicit polymerization; `k_p` is its coarse-grained rate.
- `k_d ρ` (first-order sink) ↔ FF: depolymerization / severing rate. FF has explicit fragmentation/turnover; `k_d` is its coarse-grained first-order rate. NOTE: first-order linear sink is the *lumped* form; FF's mechanistic turnover (cofilin-like severing, pointed-end depoly) is the fine-grained version and is preferred — only the *aggregate rate* is compared.
- `ε_ρ ∇²(δE/δρ)` ↔ FF: no-flux boundary regularizer; FF gets no-flux from explicit membrane containment. Not ported.

**Eq 3 — phase-field boundary:**
```
∂ϕ/∂t = D_ϕ ∇²ϕ − Γ_ϕ U′(ϕ)
```
- Pure numerical boundary device. FF's confinement is an explicit membrane + turgor + wall potential. **Not ported.**

**Péclet definition (useful as a derived diagnostic):**
```
Pe = (ζΔμ)₀ / (D γ)
```
- `(ζΔμ)₀` = contractile stress per F-actin due to myosin ↔ FF: computable from Σ(head forces)/(filament cross-section) in the native network.
- `D` = actin diffusion constant; `γ` = friction/drag coefficient ↔ FF per-node drag.
- Use: compute an *effective Pe* from FF's own emergent quantities as a one-number summary of "how advection-dominated is my myosin-driven flow," compare to the ≈50 the paper uses to match ~1 µm/s flow. This is a legitimate derived cross-check, not a fit.

**Kinetic schemes to reproduce (emergent, not prescribed):** periodic/pulsatile contraction with period T ≈ 101 s. In FF this should EMERGE from the myosin-bind/turnover competition (the paper cites Kumar–Bois–Jülicher–Grill pulsatory active fluids, ref 31, and the Krishna-Keren size-dependent steady→wave transition, ref 18). If FF's native actomyosin under confinement produces a contraction *wave/pulse* at ~10²s scale, that is strong emergent validation of the coupled myosin+turnover modules.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenario)

**Scenario name (proposed):** `ff_confined_actomyosin_condensation` — a purified-network analog to test myosin-driven flow + turnover + confinement self-organization at native FF scale.

- **Network composition:** F-actin (explicit bending beams, ℓ_p ≈ 17 µm) + Arp2/3 branches (70°±35° angle-harmonic) + Stam–Hocky myosin II minifilaments + α-actinin/filamin crosslinkers (Bell-Evans off-rate). This is exactly FF's cortex/lamellipodium element set — no new module needed.
- **Confinement geometry:** cell-sized well. Native FF operates at MCF7 R = 7.5 µm; the paper's D = 350 µm is ~23× larger (extract droplet, not single cell). **Adapt:** run confinement at FF's native cell radius (7.5 µm) — do NOT scale FF up to 350 µm (that would explode Nc and violate native-scale practicality). Test the *phenomenology* (boundary shape → condensate shape, contraction to a cluster) at our scale, using shaped confinement boundaries: circle, semicircle (W/D = 1), square. Report shape-transfer as an emergent result; only the *direction* of the roundness/quartic trends need match, since absolute size is scale-dependent.
- **Initial condition:** space-filling isotropic network at physiological density (KB-3.18 ~100/µm² areal cortex density) inside the confinement, at the physiological operating point (turgor ON, cytoplasm η ≈ 65 Pa·s, membrane ON) — per the physiological-baseline HARD rule. Myosin ON at physiological engaged fraction from t=0.
- **Boundary bias (optional, physiological):** localize Arp2/3 nucleation toward the membrane (surface-biased, mirroring k_p^surf > k_p^bulk) — this is the real cortex geometry and is mechanistically justified, not a fit.
- **Drive:** let myosin contract the network; measure inward flow field (bin FF bead velocities → PIV-analog), contraction extent, and final condensate shape.
- **Perturbation arms:** (a) ↑ myosin engaged fraction (CalA analog) → expect smaller/faceted; (b) ↑ nucleation (VCA analog) → expect larger; (c) ↓ polymerization (CytoD analog) → expect rounder-not-smaller.

**Validate at NATIVE scale + full compartments** (HARD rule): the conclusion run must be `--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules` equivalent inside the confinement, all compartments ON. A coarse network will condense/collapse from too-few-filaments and masquerade as the mechanism — reject coarse for any conclusion.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for FF observables

Register these as FF emergent-behavior acceptance targets (measured, DOI-verified — candidate SourceEvidence rows; the model knobs are explicitly excluded per §3):

1. **Inward myosin-driven flow speed:** FF native actomyosin network under confinement should produce a coherent inward advective flow of **v ≈ 1.2 µm/s** (order-of-magnitude, purified network). This is the primary myosin-module bulk-transport target — distinct from single-motor velocity and from crawl speed. Directly probes the open "motor-driven flows" problem.
2. **Contraction pulsatility / wave period:** emergent periodic contraction at **T ≈ 100 s** (10² s scale). Probes coupled myosin+turnover "network contraction & stress relaxation" and the steady→wave transition (Krishna-Keren ref 18).
3. **Contraction extent / packing:** network condenses to a dense cluster occupying **A_c/A_μ ≈ 0.10–0.15** of the confinement area, thickness ~invariant (~10 µm at their scale; at our scale expect a fixed fraction of confinement height). Contraction should be **volume/mass-controlled, not shape-controlled** (cluster area ∝ well volume, not well shape) — an emergent invariance FF should reproduce.
4. **Shape transfer (direction only, scale-adapted):** boundary asymmetry transfers to condensate: circle→round (R≈1), semicircle→crescent (R↓ to ~0.25–0.3), square→square→star under ↑contractility (quartic 0.069→0.088). FF need only reproduce the *sign/ordering*, not absolute R at 350 µm.
5. **Mechanistic causal split (the key qualitative acceptance test):** **contractility controls SIZE (↑myosin → smaller), polymerization controls SHAPE (↑nucleation → larger; ↓polymerization → rounder, not smaller).** FF perturbation arms (§4) must reproduce this ordering. This is the strongest, cleanest FF validation the paper offers — a double-dissociation of two mechanisms.
6. **Corner/flat-side stress concentration:** myosin force perpendicular-to-surface abruptly changes direction at corners → actin accumulation/outgrowths at corners; flat sides are marginally stable. An FF native network in a square/semicircle confinement should show emergent corner accumulation — a spatial-stress validation.
7. **Centering:** contractile network centers within the confinement in ~83% of cases (circular). FF condensate should center emergently (connects to the FF centering/tug-of-war literature, Sakamoto ref 16).

## 6. RISKS / caveats

1. **Continuum vs fine-grained (the core mismatch).** The paper's *model* is exactly the lumped active-fluid proxy the FF architectural rule forbids as runtime. Risk: importing Eqs 1–3 or Pe/k_p^surf as FF mechanism would violate the hard rule. **Mitigation:** use them ONLY as a cross-check oracle for emergent fields; keep FF's explicit minifilaments/crosslinkers/turnover as the mechanism. Never register Pe, k_p^surf, k_p^bulk/k_p^surf, λ, Δ as FF parameters (they are nondimensional model knobs).
2. **Purified extract vs living cortex.** Xenopus egg extract has *excess free G-actin* and NO monomer-conservation (paper explicitly drops it); a living MCF7 cortex has finite monomer pool + membrane + nucleus + microtubules. Risk: FF's turnover with a bounded monomer pool will behave differently from the source-unlimited extract. **Mitigation:** run FF with the physiological (bounded) monomer/G-actin pool per the physiological-baseline rule; expect the *trends* to match but not the unbounded-growth regime. Do not "unlock" monomer conservation to match the extract.
3. **Scale mismatch (350 µm droplet vs 7.5 µm cell).** The assay is a ~350 µm confinement, ~23–47× our MCF7 cell. Flow speed and period may be scale-dependent (the paper itself notes size-dependent steady→wave transitions, ref 18). Risk: forcing FF to a 350 µm well to match R/area absolutely would blow up Nc and break native-scale practicality. **Mitigation:** reproduce the *phenomenology and mechanistic ordering* (§5.4–5.5) at native cell scale; treat v≈1.2 µm/s and T≈100 s as order-of-magnitude targets, not exact.
4. **2D model, 3D reality (the paper's own caveat).** Their model is 2D and produces an instantaneous cluster; real F-actin is advected *upward* (Z) and accumulates slowly under the top glass — an intrinsically 3D flow the 2D model can't capture (diffuse cluster outlines in experiment). FF is 3D, so FF is *better positioned* than the paper's own model here — but it means the paper's simulated shapes are only qualitative. **Mitigation:** compare FF to the *experimental* numbers (v, T, R, A_c/A_μ), not to the paper's simulated fields, where the two diverge.
5. **Semiclosed top boundary is an assay artifact.** The upward flow + top-glass accumulation is specific to the semiclosed microwell (Hg/H≈0.067) and vanishes with an oil-seal (loses shape control). This is NOT a cortex mechanism — it's a confinement plumbing detail. **Mitigation:** do not build the top-gap accumulation into FF as a cell mechanism; use the in-plane contraction/flow only.
6. **Species/isoform.** Xenopus egg non-muscle myosin II + embryonic Arp2/3, vs FF's MCF7/mammalian parameterization. Rate constants (duty ratio, stall force) differ by isoform. **Mitigation:** keep FF's mammalian myosin parameters; use this paper only for the emergent flow/period targets, which are relatively isoform-robust order-of-magnitude quantities. Do not retune FF myosin to hit v=1.2 µm/s (that would be fitting) — treat a mismatch as a diagnostic to investigate (drag, connectivity, turnover), per the no-fitting rule.
7. **TAG/KB registration.** Register v=1.20±0.41 µm/s, T=101.0±7.9 s, R-vs-W/D law, A_c∝volume, quartic 0.069→0.088 as *measured* SourceEvidence (DOI 10.1021/acs.nanolett.5c02558). Flag Pe/k_p^surf/λ as **model-internal, NOT physiological setpoints** — do not register as Parameters.
