# FF lens — p63

**Paper:** Taeyoon Kim (2015), "Determinants of contractile forces generated in disorganized actomyosin bundles," *Biomech Model Mechanobiol* 14:345–355. DOI 10.1007/s10237-014-0608-2.

**Why this paper is unusually high-value for FF:** it is essentially a sibling FF engine. Same physics FF is built on — every actin filament a bending beam (harmonic stretch + bend, persistence length), explicit two-armed cross-linkers with Bell force-dependent off-rate, geometrically-realistic bipolar myosin minifilaments with force-velocity/stall from a stochastic head model, excluded volume, overdamped Langevin. Kim is the same lineage as our Cytosim reference and the Ferrer-2008 ACP constants already live in our KB. So its numeric outputs are near-drop-in **acceptance oracles** for an FF actomyosin-bundle contraction test, and several of its mechanistic choices directly address FF open problems (cortical-tension γ generation, network contraction & stress sustainability, buckling symmetry-breaking, crosslinker-as-clutch friction).

---

## 1. MECHANISMS TO ADOPT

### 1.1 Crosslinker-as-molecular-clutch: the ACP density/kinetics ratio governs *sustained* tension (the γ-floor lever)
**FF module:** crosslinker (α-actinin/filamin) + myosin coupling.
**Change:** Treat ACP density and off-rate not as a passive connectivity knob but as the **friction clutch that lets motors reach stall and hold tension**. Kim's central result: motors *alone* can build 1–2 nN but only at absurd density (R_M ~ 0.8–1), inefficiently (χ ≈ 0.005 = 0.5 % of summed stall forces) and transiently (tension collapses < 5 s as the network loses percolation by flowing to the clamps). Adding ACPs at R_ACP = 0.032 lets **32× fewer motors** (R_M = 0.025) hold the *same* ~2 nN, now sustained ~100 s (S ≈ 1) at χ ≈ 0.15 (~40× the ACP-free lower limit).
**Why it beats current FF:** Our γ-floor problem (active myosin tension ~530× under band even at native 38000 filaments; see MEMORY `project-gamma-floor-likely-deficit`) has been chased as a *motor force-magnitude* deficit. Kim shows the missing lever is often **crosslinker-mediated friction**, not motor force: without sufficient reversible crosslinking, motors slide off barbed ends and dissipate elastic energy instead of building sustained tension. FF should test whether its cortex crosslinker density/off-rate sits in the low-R_ACP "unsustained" regime. This is a mechanistic (not a fitting) route into the floor.

### 1.2 F-actin buckling as the tensile/compressive symmetry-breaker
**FF module:** filament (bending beam) + measurement of segment-wise axial force.
**Change:** Adopt the buckling picture explicitly: net *tensile* (contractile) bias emerges because filament segments **between crosslink points buckle under compression** (Euler beam, Eq. 7) but bear full load in tension — asymmetry ⇒ net contraction. Verified in Kim by a control: raise κ_b,A 100× (persistence length 9 µm → ~0.9 mm) and compressive forces build up, F_max and S drop.
**Why it beats current FF:** FF already has bending κ and ℓ_p≈17 µm — so buckling can EMERGE natively with zero new mechanism, which is exactly the mechanistic-over-lumped ideal. This directly informs FF's open "buckling/condensation symmetry-breaking" and "network contraction" problems: contraction should be an emergent consequence of buckling + motor walking, not a lumped contractile stress. Gives a falsifiable native-scale check (κ_b sensitivity of contraction).

### 1.3 No artificial barbed-end stall — motors slide off (turnover-limited processivity)
**FF module:** myosin (Stam–Hocky minifilament) walk/unbind logic.
**Change:** Do NOT clamp motors at the barbed end. Kim explicitly rejects the "complete stall at barbed end" assumption (Borau 2012, Dasanayake–Carlsson 2013) as artificial: myosin heads readily slide off in vitro motility assays (Lowey 1993; Umemoto–Sellers 1990). In his model a motor arm reaching the barbed end simply walks off via the next event. Consequence: most motor arms are in *consistent turnover*, and f_M is predominantly < 1 pN (far below the 4.5 pN stall) unless crosslinker friction holds the filament.
**Why it beats current FF:** If FF pins/soft-stalls motors at barbed ends it will over-generate tension and mask the real friction-limited physics. Sliding-off is the mechanistic default and it couples the γ output to crosslinker density (mechanism 1.1) rather than to a motor clamp.

### 1.4 Substrate rigidity → biphasic tension (rigidity sensing) — for the adherent FF cell
**FF module:** clutch / substrate boundary (FA anchoring), cortex-tension readout.
**Change:** Tension is biphasic in substrate stiffness E: proportional to E below a transition, flat above (transition ~300 Pa here for a bundle; ~1000 Pa for the Borau 2012 3D network). Mechanism: a stiff substrate stops the filament being dragged, so motors reach stall after a *short* walk; a soft substrate lets filaments glide and motors slide off before stalling.
**Why it beats current FF:** FF's adherent/crawl line (MEMORY `project-ff-crawl-mechanism`, `project-ff-adherent-a3-a1`) needs a mechanistic rigidity-sensing prediction. This gives one and a validation curve.

### 1.5 Crosslinker bond-type (catch vs ideal vs slip) sets the stability/density trade-off
**FF module:** crosslinker off-rate law.
**Change:** Recognize that a *catch* crosslinker (λ = −λ*) stabilizes a bundle at ~150× lower crosslinker density than a slip bond (S→1, F_max plateau at R_ACP ≈ 0.0002 vs slip's ≈ 0.01–0.04) via a "reverse sorting" process — high-load ACPs become effectively permanent. Slip bonds instead produce the F_max ∝ R_M^0.6 power law and the destabilizing "sorting" that dissipates energy at high k0. This maps onto our existing catch/slip infrastructure (cadherin KU-4.2 Rakshit; α-actinin slip Ferrer).
**Why it matters:** FF can pick crosslinker bond-type per protein mechanistically and the paper predicts the *qualitative* consequence — a testable emergent difference in tension sustainability at fixed density.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES TO PORT

### 2.1 ACP (crosslinker) force-dependent unbinding — Bell slip with compression cutoff (Eq. 6)
```
k_u,ACP = k0_u,ACP · exp( λ_u,ACP · |F_s,ACP| / k_B T )   if  r ≥ r0,ACP   (arm stretched)
k_u,ACP = k0_u,ACP                                          if  r < r0,ACP   (arm compressed)
```
- `F_s,ACP = ∇U_s,ACP` = extensional force on the ACP arm spring (only the stretch component gates unbinding).
- `λ_u,ACP` = bond compliance (length). λ = 0 → ideal bond; λ = +λ* → slip; λ = −λ* → catch.
- **Map to FF state:** `F_s,ACP` = magnitude of FF crosslinker-bond stretch force (already computed for the harmonic link); `k_B T` = FF thermal energy; the `r < r0` branch = **don't accelerate off-rate under compression** — a detail FF should replicate (a compressed crosslinker isn't being ruptured). FF's current Bell-Evans k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b) maps with F_b = k_B T/λ.

### 2.2 Myosin walk/unbind — catch-bond ensemble (PCM), force only on stiff arm spring
- Heads modeled as **catch bonds**: k_w,M and k_u,M *decrease* with higher force (opposite to ACP). Only `F_s,M4 = ∇U_s,M4` (the stiff right-angle spring) feeds the kinetics.
- Arm binding rate = `40·N_h s⁻¹` (single head 40 s⁻¹ × N_h heads/arm).
- Two-spring arm: soft `κ_s,M3` holds r0,M3 = 13.5 nm gap (filament↔backbone); stiff `κ_s,M4` holds right angle (r0,M4 = 0).
- Walk step ≈ 7 nm toward barbed end; slide off at barbed end.
- **Map to FF state:** FF's Stam–Hocky heads → replace/parametrize the per-head binding at 40 s⁻¹ and per-arm at 40·N_h; force-velocity/stall (below) supplied by whatever ensemble law FF uses (Hill in FF vs PCM here — see risks). The "only stiff-spring force gates kinetics" is a clean rule FF can adopt so lateral/soft compliance doesn't spuriously change duty.

### 2.3 Binding-spot lever rule (Eqs. 4, 5) — force redistribution to nodes
```
spot position:   r = [ y·r_P + (r_A − y)·r_B ] / r_A
force split:     F_i^B = F_i·(r_A − y)/r_A ,   F_i^P = F_i·(y/r_A)
```
- y = distance of the binding spot from the barbed endpoint; r_A = instantaneous segment length; r_B, r_P = barbed/pointed endpoint positions.
- **Map to FF state:** if FF binds motors/crosslinkers at continuous positions along a bead-rod segment (not only at nodes), this is exactly how to distribute the resulting force onto the two bead nodes. Useful if FF adds sub-segment binding resolution (7 nm spot spacing).

### 2.4 Euler buckling threshold per segment (Eq. 7)
```
F_seg^buckle ≈ π² · κ_b,A · r0,A / L_seg²
```
- L_seg = filament length between adjacent crosslink points; κ_b,A = bending stiffness; r0,A = segment length.
- **Map to FF state:** a diagnostic FF can compute per inter-crosslink span to *predict* which spans buckle and confirm the emergent contraction is buckling-driven. Not a force law to impose — an observable to verify.

### 2.5 Overdamped Langevin / Euler update (Eqs. 1–2)
```
F_i − ζ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_i^T = 0
r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (1/ζ_i)(F_i + F_i^T) Δt ,   Δt = 2.3×10⁻⁶ s
```
FF uses constrained Leimkuhler–Matthews BAOAB (higher-fidelity than forward Euler) — keep BAOAB; the value here is the **time-step scale** (2.3 µs for 140 nm segments with these stiffnesses) as a CFL sanity anchor.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL TO REPRODUCE (as an FF scenario)

**Scenario "FF-BUNDLE" — clamped disorganized actomyosin bundle, contraction against elastic substrate:**
1. **Geometry:** rectangular box 5×5×20 µm. Place N=50 actin filaments length 9 µm, aligned along z, on an x,y grid at 27 nm spacing → bundle thickness ~135 nm.
2. **Sever to distributed lengths:** deactivate PBC in z; sever filaments crossing z boundaries → ⟨L_f⟩ ≈ 6.5 µm (gives length polydispersity, aligns with FF's exponential length distribution philosophy).
3. **Clamp both ends** to a linear-elastic substrate E = 5000 Pa; 11 barbed + 11 pointed ends clamped per z boundary. Boundary area A = 25 µm².
4. **Pre-assembly (t = −100 → 0 s):** filaments held fixed while motors self-assemble and bind (no walking) and ACPs bind/crosslink. Reference densities R_M = C_M/C_A = 0.025, R_ACP = C_ACP/C_A = 0.032.
5. **Release (t = 0):** filaments free; motors walk per force-velocity. Run to 100 s.
6. **Sweeps:** R_M ∈ [10⁻³, ~1], R_ACP ∈ [10⁻⁴, 0.1], k0_u,ACP × [0.1, 32], bond type {catch, ideal, slip}, crosslink angle {parallel, anti-parallel, mixed}, κ_b,A ×100, filament length, substrate E.

**Adaptation to FF native-scale rule:** this in-vitro bundle is a *component test*, not the cell. Run it as an FF **validation harness** (like an oracle cross-check), clearly labelled non-authoritative for cell conclusions. Any γ-generation conclusion drawn from it must then be reconfirmed on the native `--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules` full cell before being reported (HARD rule). The bundle scenario is legitimate as a *mechanism isolation* test where the native cell is the confounded system.

## 6. RISKS / CAVEATS

1. **Purified in-vitro bundle ≠ living cortex.** This is a clamped, preassembled, dense linear bundle with 3 components (actin/ACP/myosin), no membrane, no turgor, no ECM, no actin turnover ("we ignored actin dynamics"). Use it as a **mechanism-isolation validation harness**, never as a cell conclusion. Reconfirm any γ/contraction finding on the native full-compartment cell (HARD rule).
2. **k0_u,ACP 0.115 vs KB 0.066 s⁻¹.** Both cite Ferrer 2008 — a loading-rate-dependent single-molecule measurement. Do NOT overwrite the KB's 0.066 with Kim's 0.115. Surface the discrepancy to PI; likely different loading-rate points on the same Bell curve. Same for F_b = k_BT/λ ≈ 39.7 pN.
3. **ℓ_p = 9 µm here vs FF 17 µm.** Kim's actin is softer than FF's canonical value. Keep FF at 17 µm unless PI ratifies; note that the *buckling* conclusions (mechanism 1.2) are ℓ_p-sensitive, so re-derive the buckling threshold at FF's stiffness rather than importing Kim's F_max drop numbers.
4. **PCM vs Hill.** Kim's motor kinetics come from the Parallel Cluster Model (Erdmann–Schwarz), not Hill (1938) which FF uses. Don't swap FF's Hill law for PCM; instead treat PCM's *outputs* (v0 ~550 nm/s, stall ~4.5 pN, catch-bond force-softening of k_w/k_u) as targets FF's Hill+minifilament should reproduce. The catch-bond softening of the walk rate under load is a mechanistic detail worth checking FF has.
5. **Forward Euler, Δt = 2.3 µs.** Kim uses forward Euler; FF's BAOAB is strictly better — keep it. Only borrow the time-step *scale* as a CFL sanity check for 140 nm segments at these stiffnesses.
6. **Coarse-graining knobs are conveniences, not physics.** N_h=8, N_a=20, N_head=N_a·N_h/4, κ_ACP "reasonable values" — never import these as if physiological or tune them to hit a gate. FF should set head count from real myosin-II minifilament stoichiometry.
7. **Molar ratios R_M, R_ACP are concentration ratios, not FF's areal densities.** Map through concentration→count carefully before comparing to FF's cortex densities (KB-3.18 ~100/µm²); the *scaling laws and χ/S/efficiency* transfer, the raw R values do not.
8. **Sample size.** Kim ensembles only 2–4 sims/condition — FF should run larger seed ensembles (per our seed-robustness lesson) before claiming a scaling exponent like R_M^0.6.
