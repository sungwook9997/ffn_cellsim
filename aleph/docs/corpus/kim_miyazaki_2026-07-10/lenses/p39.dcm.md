# DCM lens — p39

**Paper:** Slater, Li, Indana, Xie, Chaudhuri, Kim — "Transient mechanical interactions between cells and viscoelastic extracellular matrix," *Soft Matter* **17**, 10274–10285 (2021). DOI 10.1039/d0sm01911a.

**One-line relevance to DCM:** The paper's cell IS a DCM-style object — a membrane + always-contractile actomyosin cortex (zero-rest-length springs) that pulls on a surrounding viscoelastic fiber matrix through permanent focal-adhesion-like harmonic links. It is directly useful to DCM as (a) the **cortex-tension → external-traction interface** template, (b) a clean **σ ∝ r⁻¹ (2-D) / r⁻² (3-D) analytic validation** for any DCM-in-ECM run, and (c) a **tensional-homeostasis / stress-relaxation** target set. It is NOT useful for the cell's *internal* compartment physics (membrane K_A, bending, nucleus, cytokinesis) — those are absent here. This overlaps and extends the project's existing DCM⊗ECM-vs-Kim 1/r work.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cell→ECM traction interface: permanent FA harmonic link cortex-node → nearest fiber (DCM `contact`/`ecm` module)
The cell exerts NO body force on the matrix; all cell→ECM force is transmitted through explicit **permanent** harmonic springs (Eq. 1) between cortex surface nodes and fiber ends. DCM currently couples to ECM through node-face penalty / traction terms that are more lumped. **Why it beats DCM-now:** it is the fine-grained, mechanistic force-transmission path — cortical tension does not "leak" into the matrix as a continuum stress; it is carried by discrete FA bonds whose count/stiffness set the traction. This is the mechanistically-correct way to wire the DCM cortex to an FF-style collagen network for the durotaxis / long-range-sensing scenarios. Maps cleanly onto the existing DCM⊗ECM bridge.

### M2 — Radial stress diagnostic σ(r) and its dimensionality signature (DCM `validation`/observable)
Measure σ(r) by summing the **radial component** of all fiber+crosslinker chain forces crossing a cylinder (2-D) / sphere (3-D) of radius r and dividing by that surface's area. A **fully transmitted** steady stress then decays as **σ ∝ r⁻¹** in 2-D quasi-slab geometry and **σ ∝ r⁻²** in 3-D, purely because the transmitting surface area scales as r (2-D) or r² (3-D). **Why:** this is a *derivable, grid-invariant* self-check (no fitting) that tells you whether a DCM cell's contraction has actually reached the boundary vs. been absorbed locally. Our DCM is native-3-D, so the target is **r⁻²**; the paper's r⁻¹ is the 2-D-slab counterpart and confirms the 1/r work already in the KB (project-dcm-ecm-kim-1r-gap: MID slope −1.21 ≈ −1 in the clean band).

### M3 — Emergent matrix viscoelasticity from crosslinker turnover, NOT a lumped modulus (DCM/FF `ecm` module)
Matrix stress relaxation is produced mechanistically by force-dependent unbinding of discrete transient cross-linkers (Eq. 2, Bell slip-bond), never by a viscoelastic continuum modulus. **Why it beats a lumped Maxwell/Kelvin ECM:** it honors the project HARD rule (fine-grained over proxy). For DCM this is the correct way to give the surrounding ECM its 24–48 h stress-relaxation timescale — as an emergent property of bond turnover, which is the same lever DCM needs for the loose→compact aggregate-compaction RATE problem (cadherin T1 rearrangement is the intra-aggregate analog of this inter-fiber crosslink turnover).

### M4 — Counter-intuitive coupling: stronger contraction → FASTER stress relaxation (mechanistic insight, not a new force)
Higher cortical activity k_s,c raises peak stress AND accelerates relaxation, because the higher bond loads drive more slip-bond unbinding. **Why it matters for DCM:** warns that in any DCM run coupling an active cortex to a turnover-limited interface (ECM crosslinks OR cadherin junctions), raising the active tension does NOT monotonically raise the sustained load — a design caution for the compaction-rate escalation work.

### M5 — Plastic / irreversible remodeling as a validation behavior (DCM `ecm` observable)
When the cell is removed, the matrix barely retracts (>~most deformation is irreversible/viscoplastic). **Why:** gives DCM a concrete plasticity target for the ECM side and distinguishes elastic (recoverable) from plastic (turnover-locked) remodeling — a behavior our elastic-only ECM cannot show and should reproduce once crosslink turnover (M3) is in.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port

### Eq. 1 — Focal-adhesion / cortex-node → fiber harmonic link (permanent)
```
U_s,fc = ½ · k_s,fc · (r_fc − r_0,fc)²
```
- `r_fc` → distance between a **DCM cortex surface node** and the attached **ECM fiber-end particle**.
- `k_s,fc` → FA-link extensional stiffness → new DCM config field `ecm.fa_link_k` (force/length).
- `r_0,fc` → FA rest length; MUST be set > membrane thickness so the link is exempt from membrane–matrix excluded-volume repulsion → `ecm.fa_link_r0`.
- Permanent (no unbinding) in this paper — DCM should keep it permanent for the traction interface unless FA maturation is later added (paper flags FA maturation as its own limitation).

### Eq. 2 — Bell's-law slip-bond crosslinker off-rate (ECM viscoelasticity origin)
```
k_ub = k_ub,0 · exp( x_ub · |F_s,xl| / (k_B T) )    if r ≥ r_0,xl   (tension)
k_ub = k_ub,0                                        if r <  r_0,xl   (compression)
```
- `|F_s,xl|` → tensile force on the ECM crosslinker spring (DCM/FF ECM node-pair).
- `k_ub,0` → zero-force off-rate → `ecm.xl_kub0`.
- `x_ub` → Bell force-sensitivity length → `ecm.xl_xub`.
- `k_B T` → 4.1×10⁻²¹ J at 300 K.
- `r_0,xl` → crosslinker rest length; compression branch clamps at baseline rate.
- **Note for DCM cell-cell junctions:** this is a **slip** bond. DCM's cadherin junction is a **catch** bond (Rakshit KU-4.2) — do NOT overwrite it with this. This Eq. 2 belongs to the ECM crosslinker layer, not the cadherin interface. (The mechanistic pattern — force-dependent off-rate governing an emergent macroscopic relaxation — is shared, but the sign of the force dependence is opposite.)

### Implied — cortex zero-rest-length contractile spring
```
U_cortex = ½ · k_s,c · r²        (r_0 = 0  ⇒ always contractile)
```
- Maps to DCM cortex tension. In DCM the cortex is a **surface tension γ** on the mesh (γ = ΔP·R/2, pressure-borne), which is the physiologically-preferred, area-based form. This paper's zero-rest-length edge-spring is the **discrete-edge equivalent** of γ: γ ≈ k_s,c per unit contour length. Do NOT replace DCM's γ with edge springs — γ is the better (area/tension-native) formulation; use k_s,c only to *cross-map* the paper's contraction sweep onto a γ range for validation (see §5).

### Implied — stress observable (port to 3-D)
```
σ_radial(r) = [ Σ radial-component of chain spring forces crossing surface at r ] / A(r)
2-D slab:  A(r) ∝ r      ⇒  σ ∝ r⁻¹   (paper)
3-D:       A(r) ∝ r²     ⇒  σ ∝ r⁻²   (DCM target)
```

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce (DCM scenarios)

### S1 — Single contractile cell in a fiber matrix (traction / long-range-sensing scenario)
1. Build DCM cell (membrane + cortex, physiological baseline: turgor Π₀, γ, η) at domain center, R≈7.5 µm.
2. t ∈ [0,200 s]: assemble the surrounding ECM fiber network + transient crosslinks (Eq. 2) + form permanent FA links (Eq. 1) between cortex nodes and nearest fiber ends.
3. t = 200 s: turn on cortical contraction (in DCM: ramp γ to mitotic-level activity, or apply the k_s,c-equivalent).
4. Run ~1 h simulated; record σ(r,t), fiber displacement, net local deformation, non-uniformity.
5. **3-D DCM version uses a spherical shell of ECM (not the 1-µm slab)** → expect σ ∝ r⁻².

### S2 — Irreversibility / plasticity probe
Reach the contracted steady state, then **delete the cell** and continue integrating; measure outward retraction of fibers vs. the original inward displacement. Target: little retraction (plastic).

### S3 — Myosin-off control (validation anchor)
Same as S1 but cortex activity = 0 (blebbistatin/ML-7 analog). Target: remodeling collapses to near-zero — a clean null that DCM must reproduce.

### S4 — Covalent-crosslink (tTG) control
Add a fraction of **permanent, non-turnover** ECM crosslinks (no Eq. 2 unbinding). Target: reduced remodeling and slower relaxation vs. all-transient.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Cell type + dimensionality mismatch.** This is a **3T3 fibroblast** (strongly contractile, spread, protrusive) in a **collagen-I** gel; the simulation is a **quasi-2-D 1-µm slab** (σ ∝ r⁻¹). Our DCM is a rounded **MCF7-like epithelial** cell in **3-D** (σ ∝ r⁻²). Port the *mechanism and the r⁻ⁿ diagnostic*, but do NOT transplant fibroblast-scale contraction magnitudes as MCF7 physiological values. MCF7 cortical tension is weak (interphase γ≈0.15 mN/m); use §3/§5 numbers as *fibroblast-regime cross-checks*, not MCF7 setpoints.
- **Cortex model altitude.** The paper's cortex is a 1-D chain of zero-rest-length edge springs; DCM's cortex is an area-based surface tension γ on a closed mesh (physiologically preferred, pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2). Keep γ; use k_s,c only to *cross-map* the contraction sweep. Do not regress DCM to edge springs — that would be a coarser, less mechanistic formulation.
- **Slip vs catch bond.** Eq. 2 is a **slip** bond for **ECM crosslinkers**. Our cell-cell **cadherin junction is a catch bond** (Rakshit KU-4.2). Applying Eq. 2 to the junction would corrupt maturation physics. Confine Eq. 2 to the ECM crosslinker layer.
- **No cell interior physics.** The paper has no nucleus, no membrane K_A/κ_m, no cytoplasm viscosity beyond drag, no division, no protrusion (authors list these as limitations: continuous unregulated contraction, no protrusion, no FA maturation, no permanent fiber elongation/mechanosensitivity). So this paper contributes ONLY to the cortex→ECM traction/validation side — it does not touch DCM's open γ-generation, cytokinesis, bleb, or membrane–cortex coupling problems.
- **ESI-gated parameters.** k*_s,c, C*_f, R*_xl, ζ, Δt, κ_b are in ESI Table S1 (absent). Retrieve the ESI before KB-registering any of the four sweep reference values. Physiological-baseline rule: when instantiating the ECM scenario, set crosslink turnover ON at k_ub,0=1×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ (not a convenient zero) and cortex at its real MCF7 tension, not the fibroblast peak.
- **Overlap with existing KB.** The r⁻¹/r⁻² result and the Kim-lab fiber-ECM mechanism are already partly in the KB (project-dcm-ecm-kim-1r-gap, DOI-verified 1/r recovery). This paper *corroborates* that line rather than opening a new one — net-new DCM value is the plastic-remodeling target (M5) and the explicit permanent-FA-link transmission template (M1).
