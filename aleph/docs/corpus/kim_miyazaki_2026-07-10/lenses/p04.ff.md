# FF lens — p04

**Paper:** Yu, Li, Murrell, Kim (2018) "Balance between Force Generation and Relaxation Leads to Pulsed Contraction of Actomyosin Networks," *Biophys. J.* 115:2003–2013. DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2018.10.008.

**One line for FF:** This is a sibling of the FF engine — an explicit-segment Brownian-dynamics actomyosin network (Kim lab, same lineage FF already borrows Ferrer link-stiffness from). It hands FF two ready-to-port mechanisms it currently lacks — (a) **treadmilling turnover as force-relaxation** (barbed-polymerize / pointed-depolymerize at matched rates) and (b) **angle/buckling-dependent F-actin severing** — plus the concrete demonstration that FF's open problems "network contraction & stress relaxation," "turnover/fragmentation," and "buckling/condensation symmetry-breaking" are ALL controlled by the balance between motor force generation and these two relaxation channels. Relevance to FF is high but with one hard caveat: this is a purified quasi-2D network, so numbers are architectural/qualitative anchors, not physiological cortex targets.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — F-actin treadmilling turnover as an explicit force-relaxation channel (turnover module — NEW)
- **What:** polymerization = add one segment at the **barbed end only**; depolymerization = remove one segment at the **pointed end only**; rate constants set EQUAL (k₊,A = k₋,A) so the filament treadmills at fixed mean length while monomers cycle. Nucleation from a single polarity-seed segment in a random direction feeds new filaments; the ratio k_{n,A}/k_{t,A} sets mean length ⟨L_f⟩.
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF already has barbed-end *growth* (ratchet, KB-3.6) but no matched pointed-end depolymerization → filaments only elongate; there is no monomer recycling and no dissipative relaxation of motor-built stress. This paper's central quantitative result is that **turnover monotonically relaxes actomyosin stress and prevents runaway aggregation** (Fig. 1b: heterogeneity Q_A ↓ monotonically as k_{t,A} 15→240 s⁻¹). This is EXACTLY the missing dissipation channel behind two FF open problems: "network contraction & stress relaxation" and the DCM-side finding that aggregate-σ compaction has no physical rate limiter. Treadmilling turnover is the mechanistic rate limiter.
- **Beats the lumped alternative explicitly:** the paper contrasts its treadmilling against the "remove-a-whole-filament-at-a-fixed-rate" turnover of Belmonte-Nedelec 2017 and Hiraiwa-Salbreux 2016 and calls the whole-filament version "much less physiological" (breaks many crosslinks at once → artificial disassembly). This is the same fine-grained-vs-lumped call FF's HARD rule mandates — port the segment-resolved treadmilling, NOT a filament-lifetime Poisson removal.

### M2 — Angle-dependent, buckling-induced F-actin severing (turnover/fragmentation module — NEW)
- **What:** each internal actin segment carries a severing propensity that rises exponentially with the **local bending angle** (sum of the two junction angles at the segment's ends, θ_{s,A}). When a segment severs, the filament splits into two fragments. Severing preferentially fires where filaments are most buckled — i.e. inside contracting clusters where motors have crushed the network.
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF has κ-bending beams that CAN buckle under motor load but has no fragmentation response — a buckled filament just stores elastic energy indefinitely. In reality (Murrell-Gardel 2012, PNAS 109:20820, cited ref 14; Vogel 2013 eLife membrane-bound) buckled cortical filaments **sever**, which is the local, selective force-relaxation that lets large contracted clusters disassemble on physiological timescales. Without it, FF contraction is irreversible (the paper's Fig. S4a: at negligible severing, clusters are permanent). This directly addresses FF's "buckling/condensation symmetry-breaking" and "network contraction & stress relaxation" open items and is the fine-grained mechanistic analog of what the DCM cadherin work needed (a real disassembly driver, not a frozen end-state).
- **Key structural finding to reproduce:** **both** channels are required. Severing alone (no treadmilling) irreversibly shortens filaments → network can't rebuild large structures (Figs. S6/S7). Treadmilling recycles the short severed fragments back into fresh filaments. FF must implement them together, not either-or.

### M3 — Parallel-Cluster-Model (PCM) multi-head motor force-velocity + force-dependent unbinding (myosin module — CROSS-CHECK / candidate refinement)
- **What:** myosin thick filament = backbone + 4 arms × 8 heads; each arm binds at constant rate, walks toward the barbed end at a rate that **decreases with tensile load and stalls above the stall force**, and unbinds at a rate that here is taken **inversely proportional to load** (Erdmann-Schwarz PCM, refs 24/25). Note this unbinding sense is *opposite* to a slip bond.
- **Why relevant to FF:** FF uses Stam-Hocky bipolar minifilament + Hill (1938) force-velocity + a duty ratio. The PCM is an alternative small-ensemble stochastic treatment of the SAME 32-head geometry (4×8) with an ensemble-level force-velocity that emerges from head-level catch/slip statistics. It is NOT a replacement for FF's Hill/Stam-Hocky choice (which is the ratified mechanistic option), but it is a **cross-check oracle** for FF's emergent minifilament force-velocity curve and a source for the load-dependent *unbinding* law if FF's current minifilament unbinding is under-specified. The "walking rate → 0 above stall, unbinding ∝ 1/F" scheme is directly transcribable (Sec. 2 below).

### M4 — Connectivity-controlled contraction regime map (crosslinker + filament-length modules — VALIDATION SCENARIO)
- **What:** contraction extent is non-monotonic in connectivity, tuned interchangeably by ACP density R_ACP or mean length ⟨L_f⟩: **three regimes** — (i) low connectivity → weak/no contraction or many tiny clusters; (ii) intermediate → single large cluster, maximal contraction/heterogeneity/motor persistence; (iii) high → interconnected bundles that resist contraction (network too elastic).
- **Why relevant to FF:** FF's crosslinker (α-actinin/filamin, Bell-Evans off-rate) density and its exponential length distribution (1–10 µm) jointly set cortical connectivity. This paper predicts FF should see a **maximum in contractility at intermediate crosslinker density / filament length**, not a monotone one — a concrete falsifiable prediction for FF's native cortex and directly relevant to the γ-floor open problem (too-high connectivity → elastic lock-up → suppressed active tension; too-low → motors can't transmit force → floored tension). The intermediate optimum is a candidate γ-generation sweet spot.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### Eq. 1 — Angle-dependent severing rate (PORT to FF turnover kernel)
```
k_{s,A} = k⁰_{s,A} · exp( θ_{s,A} / λ_{s,A} )
```
Symbol → FF state map:
- `k_{s,A}` — per-segment severing rate [s⁻¹] → attempted each FF turnover substep with prob 1−exp(−k_{s,A}·Δt_turnover).
- `k⁰_{s,A}` — zero-angle base severing rate constant [s⁻¹]. Very small (see §3); the angle exponential does the work.
- `θ_{s,A}` — **sum of the two bending angles at the segment's two junctions** [deg]. In FF this is directly `angle(seg_{i-1},seg_i) + angle(seg_i,seg_{i+1})` from the existing κ-bending beam junctions — no new state needed, it's already computed for the bending force.
- `λ_{s,A}` — angle insensitivity [deg]; larger λ ⇒ severing waits for a larger bend. Sets the buckling-severity threshold.
- **Action on fire:** delete the segment (or break the bond), producing two independent filaments; the freed short fragment then depolymerizes via M1's pointed-end channel.
- **Selectivity result to reproduce:** at the physiological window >50% of severing events occur INSIDE contracting clusters (Fig. 5c) — a built-in check that FF's severing is buckling-selective, not uniform.

Note the paper flags Eq. 1 as empirically motivated (fit to McCullough 2011 cofilin-flexibility data, ref 30) with "no rigorous physical basis," and states results are **insensitive to the specific severing model**. So FF may substitute a curvature-threshold or a stored-bending-energy criterion (e.g. sever ∝ exp(E_bend/E_scale)) without loss — pick whichever is grid-invariant and satisfies the magic-number block. The **angle-sum θ** form is preferred because it's already on hand from the bending beam.

### Treadmilling kinetics (PORT to FF turnover module)
- Barbed-end polymerization rate constant `k₊,A`; pointed-end depolymerization `k₋,A`; **impose k₊,A = k₋,A** → dynamic equilibrium; the common value = turnover rate `k_{t,A}`.
- Segment length quantum = **140 nm** in this model (their coarse-grain); FF should use its own segment length, only the RATE is transferable.
- Mean length control: `⟨L_f⟩ ∝ k_{n,A} / k_{t,A}` (nucleation-to-turnover ratio). FF already sets length via an exponential distribution, so use this only to sanity-check that turnover holds ⟨L_f⟩ stationary (Fig. S3c: ⟨L_f⟩ stays ~1 µm, k_{t,A}-independent).

### Motor force-velocity / unbinding (PCM — CROSS-CHECK against FF Hill/Stam-Hocky)
- Walking rate: monotone decreasing in per-head tensile load, **→ 0 at/above stall force** (same qualitative shape as Hill).
- Unbinding rate: **inversely proportional to applied force** (PCM convention here; contrast with Bell-Evans slip which INCREASES off-rate with force). Use this only as an alternative-scheme comparator; do NOT overwrite FF's ratified catch/slip minifilament unbinding without a parity study.

### Bell's-law ACP unbinding (CONFIRMS FF's existing crosslinker law)
- ACP off-rate rises exponentially with tensile force (Bell 1978, ref 23): `k_off = k⁰_off · exp(F·x_β/k_BT)`. This is exactly FF's current Bell-Evans crosslinker off-rate `k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b)` — **the paper corroborates FF's design choice**; the explicit constants live in this paper's Table S1 (SI, not in main text — flag for retrieval if FF wants Kim-lab numeric x_β / k⁰_off to cross-check against Ferrer 2008).

### Analysis observables (PORT as FF diagnostics)
- Ensemble motor speed (Eq. 2): `⟨v_M(t)⟩ = (1/N_M) Σ |r_{i,M}(t) − r_{i,M}(t−1)| / (1 s)`. Sample at 1 s to average thermal noise.
- Radial distribution g(r) (Eq. 4): `g(r) = P(r)/(2πr·δr·ρ_a)`, r ∈ [50 nm, 1 µm], δr = 50 nm; g≈1 homogeneous, peaked = clustered.
- Motor persistency (Eq. 5): grid-averaged velocity autocorrelation `⟨cosφ⟩ = ⟨v(t)·v(t+τ)/|v||v|⟩`, τ=1 s; →1 signals coherent contraction.
- Heterogeneity Q_A: SD of per-grid actin-segment density on N_G×N_G grid, N_G=10–20. All four are cheap FF post-processors on existing particle positions.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenario)

**Scenario "pulsed-contraction reconstitution" (quasi-2D thin slab):**
- Domain **10 × 10 × 0.1 µm**, periodic in x,y; repulsive walls in z (thin-film cortex proxy). Self-assemble at t=0 from F-actin + ACP + motor — physiological operating point from t=0 (no relaxed null start).
- Fixed: C_A = 60 µM, R_M = 0.04, 4×8 motor heads.
- Three-knob sweep to map regimes: k_{t,A} (turnover), R_ACP or ⟨L_f⟩ (connectivity), (k⁰_{s,A}, λ_{s,A}) (severing).
- **Protocol targets to hit in order:**
  1. **Turnover suppression:** at R_ACP=0.02, ⟨L_f⟩=1.6 µm, sweep k_{t,A}=15→240 s⁻¹ → heterogeneity Q_A should decrease monotonically; at k_{t,A}=15 one large cluster forms, at 240 homogeneous.
  2. **Connectivity optimum:** at k_{t,A}=30, sweep R_ACP 0.0025→0.16 (and separately ⟨L_f⟩ 0.7→5.0) → max contraction/persistence at INTERMEDIATE value; bundles at high.
  3. **Weak pulses (balance):** R_ACP=0.01, ⟨L_f⟩=1 µm, k_{t,A}=70 s⁻¹ → small nascent clusters, lifetime <50 s (too small vs in-vivo — expected).
  4. **Strong pulses (add severing):** R_ACP=0.02, ⟨L_f⟩=1 µm, k_{t,A}=30, add Eq.1 severing with k⁰_{s,A} in 10⁻⁵⁰–10⁻³⁰ window → large clusters that fully disassemble ~100 s, matching in-vivo size/duration.

**Native-scale adaptation (FF HARD rule):** this quasi-2D 10×10 µm periodic box is a dev/oracle scenario, NOT the authoritative basis. Any conclusion (e.g. "turnover sets γ," "severing enables disassembly") must be **reconfirmed on the native full cell** — `--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules`, MCF7 R=7.5 µm, full compartments — before it is reported. Use the thin-slab periodic scenario only to validate the two new kernels in isolation, then wire them into the native cortex.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

1. **Purified quasi-2D vs living 3D cortex.** 10×10×0.1 µm periodic thin film with self-assembled network ≠ MCF7 cortex under turgor with membrane containment, nucleus, microtubules. The regime map (three connectivity states, pulse windows) is architectural guidance; the numeric pulse sizes/durations are the *in-vivo* targets (refs 36) not the *in-vitro* sim outputs. Adapt by porting the two kernels (turnover, severing) and re-running in the FULL native compartment stack, per HARD rule — never conclude from the thin slab.

2. **Severing law is phenomenological + carries magic numbers.** k⁰_{s,A} ∈ 10⁻⁵⁰–10⁻³⁰ s⁻¹ and λ_{s,A} are fitted, not derived; the authors themselves say "no rigorous physical basis" and "results insensitive to the model." FF's no-magic-number rule forbids importing these. Adapt: implement severing from a physical curvature/stored-bending-energy fracture criterion, validate against the *observable* (buckling-selective, >50% intra-cluster; ⟨L_f⟩ stationary), and surface the calibration to PI rather than tuning k⁰ to make a pulse appear.

3. **PCM motor unbinding sense conflicts with FF's ratified catch/slip.** PCM here uses unbinding ∝ 1/F (slower off at higher load), which is neither pure Bell-Evans slip nor the catch-bond FF adopts for cadherin/integrin. Do NOT overwrite FF's Stam-Hocky/Hill minifilament with PCM; use PCM only as an independent force-velocity cross-check oracle.

4. **Turnover rate magnitude.** k_{t,A}=15–240 s⁻¹ is a *segment* turnover rate in a 140-nm-grain model; it is NOT directly FF's barbed-growth rate. Map by matching the emergent whole-filament turnover time (⟨L_f⟩·segment/k_{t,A}) and the physiological cortical actin turnover timescale (~10–60 s filament lifetime), not by copying 30 s⁻¹ into FF's per-segment kernel. Physiological-baseline rule: set turnover to the real cortex value from t=0, not a convenient default.

5. **RhoA biochemistry absent.** The paper deliberately omits the RhoA oscillator (its whole point: pulses can be purely mechanical). For FF this is a feature — it means turnover+severing alone can produce contraction dynamics, no signaling module required. But if FF later needs *spatially patterned* pulses (apical constriction), a RhoA-like myosin-activity modulator is still missing (the paper flags it as future work). Keep FF's version mechanistic (myosin recruitment/activation field) if added.

6. **Both channels required — don't half-adopt.** Implementing severing without treadmilling degrades FF into progressively-shortening filaments that can't rebuild structure (Figs S6/S7); treadmilling without severing gives irreversible large clusters. FF must land BOTH kernels together for the contraction/relaxation balance to be physical.

---

**Bottom line for FF:** two concrete new kernels (segment-resolved treadmilling turnover; buckling-selective severing) that plug directly into FF's existing bending-beam + minifilament + Bell-Evans-crosslinker stack, close the "network contraction & stress relaxation" and "turnover/fragmentation" open problems mechanistically, and come with a falsifiable regime map + in-vivo validation targets (2–4 µm / 40–100 s pulses, 0.6–1.8 µm/s motor speed, intermediate-connectivity contractility maximum). Import the *mechanisms and observables*, derive the *rates* physically (avoid the paper's fitted severing constants), and confirm on the native 38000-filament cortex.
