# DCM lens — p14

**Paper:** Miyazaki, Laboni & Kim 2025, "Reconstitution of actomyosin networks in cell-sized liposomes reveals distinct mechanical roles of cytoskeletal organization in membrane shape remodeling." bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.05.18.654456.

**One line for DCM:** A cell-sized (8–20 µm) triangulated deformable-membrane model with per-triangle area conservation + global volume conservation, coupled to an active actomyosin interior, that produces **blebbing** via two clean, analytically-derived mechanisms (cortex **detachment** vs cortex **rupture**). This is *exactly* DCM's surface-mesh + enclosed-volume + cortex-tension paradigm, and it hands DCM (a) a directly-portable membrane-mesh potential set, (b) a Bell-kinetics detachment law for membrane–cortex coupling, and (c) the Young-Laplace cortex-tension closure DCM already relies on — with numbers.

The paper's own DCM-analog is Taeyoon Kim's agent-based membrane (Supplementary Note 2 §3), so the membrane physics maps 1:1 onto our surface mesh. Caveat throughout: it is in-vitro purified F-actin + smooth-muscle myosin in a liposome, not a living MCF7 — the *membrane/geometry/tension* transfers cleanly; the *material identity* of the interior does not (see §6).

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Membrane–cortex coupling as an explicit, breakable Bell-kinetics catch of discrete linkers (→ DCM bleb / membrane-cortex decoupling module)
**What DCM does now:** membrane and cortex are effectively co-located; there is no explicit, load-dependent membrane↔cortex *linker* population that can peel. CLAUDE.md lists "membrane–cortex coupling" and "bleb" as OPEN.
**Paper's mechanism (Eqs 4–8, S1–S13):** a surface density ρ_H^b of actin-bound membrane linkers (His-α-actinin ↔ Ni-NTA lipid; the DCM analog is ERM/talin-class linkers) obeys force-loaded Bell kinetics. Internal pressure ΔP loads each bond with force f = ΔP/ρ_H^b. When ΔP reaches a fold-bifurcation critical value ΔP\*, the bound population collapses (no steady state) → the membrane peels off the intact cortex → **bleb by Detachment**. Above a coupling threshold the cortex instead **Ruptures** (successive severing of the load-bearing network at fixed tension).
**Why it beats what DCM has:** it makes blebbing and membrane–cortex decoupling *emergent and mechanistic* (load-dependent unbinding of a real linker population) rather than a lumped "detach when stress > X" switch — squarely on the CLAUDE.md fine-grained-over-proxy rule. And it is analytically closed (Eq. 7 gives the tension threshold), so it is a validation gate, not a tuned event.
**DCM implementation:** add a per-node (or per-membrane-vertex) scalar field `rho_link_bound` living on the surface mesh, integrated by Eq. 5 each step, with the local normal cortex↔membrane restoring force switched off where `rho_link_bound → 0`. The load f uses the *local* Young-Laplace ΔP already computed from the enclosed-volume constraint.

### M2 — Two-mechanism bleb selection driven by two orthogonal control ratios R_C, R_X (→ DCM cortex/junction config axes)
**Mechanism:** the *mode* (no-deformation → weak → single bleb → multiple blebs) and the *initiation mechanism* (Detachment vs Rupture) are set by two independent dimensionless ratios:
 - **R_C = coupling / actin** (membrane–cortex linker density) — the *primary* lever for deformation magnitude and mode.
 - **R_X = crosslinker / actin** (network connectivity) — sets the rupture threshold, barely shifts the mode.
Low R_C / high R_X → Detachment; high R_C / low R_X → Rupture (Fig 4, Fig 7d).
**Why adopt:** gives DCM two *derivable, grid-invariant* config knobs (concentration ratios, not tuned forces) that reproduce a whole phase diagram — exactly the kind of non-magic parameterization the hard rules demand. Maps to DCM as (R_C) membrane-linker areal density and (R_X) cortex crosslink density — both already physical fields in our cortex module.

### M3 — Cortex Rupture by deterministic tension-threshold severing → local, self-amplifying cortex failure (→ DCM cortex-integrity / bleb-nucleation)
**Mechanism (Fig 6h, S8):** where local network tension is highest and local motor density is highest, filaments sever at F_sev; load redistributes to neighbours, which then exceed threshold → catastrophic cascade → a hole in the cortex → bleb. Local motor density in the bleb region ≈ 4 N_M/µm² vs 2 in merely-deforming regions; local tension ≈ 200 vs 100 pN/µm².
**Why:** DCM currently has no cortex-failure mode; this is a mechanistic nucleation site for blebs and for confinement/aspiration rupture. At the DCM (mesh) scale we don't resolve single filaments, but we can carry a per-face cortex-tension and a critical areal-tension γ_C^{R\*}(R_X) above which that face's cortex-tension contribution drops (locally releasing the constraint) — a coarse-grained image of the severing cascade, gated by the analytic threshold, not a tuned number.

### M4 — 2D-shell vs 3D-fill cortex geometry as a first-class configuration (→ DCM cortex builder)
**Mechanism (Fig 5, 7):** a thin 2D cortical shell (network confined to 0.9·r → r, 27.1% of volume) gives single blebs; a 3D volume-spanning network gives **multiple blebs** over a wide window because randomly-oriented *bulk* myosin generates inward antagonistic forces (Fig 7p–r). Bulk (cytoplasmic) contractility is qualitatively different from cortical contractility.
**Why for DCM:** validates that DCM's cortex-as-surface-shell is the *right* idealization for single-cell interphase morphology, and flags that any bulk-cytoskeleton term (microtubule/IF, stress fibers) will change the multiplicity of protrusions. Concretely: shell fraction 27.1% and the 2D/3D dichotomy are a builder switch and a caveat.

### M5 — Deterministic bleb positioning by *local* R_C / R_X patterning → programmable polarity without biochemical cues (→ DCM polarity / durotaxis scenario)
**Mechanism (Fig 8):** locally perturbing R_X (crosslinker) positions the bleb with 100% efficiency; local R_C with 50%. Cell-scale polarity emerges purely from mechanical network heterogeneity.
**Why:** a clean DCM scenario for emergent front/back polarity and, by extension, a mechanical substrate for durotaxis/confinement-directed protrusion — mechanistic, no signaling model required.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

Symbol map to DCM state: R = local mean cell/mesh radius; γ_c = cortex surface tension (DCM `gamma_cortex`, the pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2 field); γ_m = bare membrane tension (DCM membrane area-elastic tension ~K_A·(A−A₀)/A₀); ΔP = trans-membrane pressure from the enclosed-volume/turgor constraint (DCM `Pi`, `k_vol`); ρ_H = total membrane-linker areal density; ρ_H^b = *bound* linker density (new per-vertex field); ρ_A = actin binding-site areal density on the cortex; k_BT = thermal energy; r₀ = Bell characteristic bond distance; K_m^{2D} = k_off⁰/k_on 2D affinity.

**Eq. 2 — Young-Laplace with split tension (DCM pressure closure, verbatim):**
 ΔP = 2(γ_m + γ_c)/R − 2γ_{m0}/R₀
DCM already sets ΔP from the volume constraint; this is the consistency relation between DCM's `Pi`, membrane tension and cortex `gamma`. Use it as a *gate*, not an input.

**Eq. 3 — near-threshold reduction:** ΔP ≃ 2γ_c/R (since in silico γ_c ≫ γ_m at onset). → In DCM, when cortex tension dominates, internal pressure is fixed by cortex tension alone. Direct check on our γ=ΔP·R/2 osmoregulation closure (it is the *same* equation, solved for γ_c: γ_c = ΔP·R/2).

**Eq. 5 — Bell-loaded linker kinetics (the new field equation for M1):**
 dρ_H^b/dt = k_on (ρ_H − ρ_H^b)(ρ_A − ρ_H^b − ρ_N^b) − k_off⁰ · ρ_H^b · exp[(r₀ / k_BT)(ΔP / ρ_H^b)]
Integrate this per membrane vertex with local ΔP. Detachment = loss of steady state (fold bifurcation) → release the local normal membrane↔cortex spring.

**Eq. 6 / S13 — critical detachment pressure (analytic gate):**
 ΔP\* ≃ 0.7 · (k_BT · ρ_H / r₀) · ln(ρ_A / K_m^{2D})

**Eq. 7 — critical cortical tension for detachment (the headline DCM gate):**
 γ_C^{D\*} ≃ 0.35 · (k_BT · ρ_H · R / r₀) · ln(ρ_A / K_m^{2D})
Maps every symbol to DCM state: multiply the Bell/linker parameters (ρ_H, r₀, K_m^{2D}) by local mesh radius R to get the cortex-tension at which the DCM membrane should bleb. This is a *closed-form validation oracle* for a DCM bleb run — no fitting.

**Eq. 8 — scaling:** γ_C^{D\*} ∝ R_C (since R_C ≃ ρ_H/ρ_A). Detachment threshold rises linearly with membrane-linker density. Rupture counterpart: γ_C^{R\*} increases with R_X, independent of R_C.

**Membrane-mesh potentials (Supplementary Note 2 — port directly to DCM surface mesh):**
 - **S8, volume:** U_V = ½ κ_V (V − V₀)² → DCM `k_vol` enclosed-volume constraint. κ_V = 1.0×10³ N/m².
 - **S9, per-triangle area:** U_A = ½ κ_A (A − A₀)² → DCM per-face area-elasticity K_A. κ_A = 1.0×10⁻³ N/m, A₀ = 2.12×10⁻¹⁵ m².
 - **S6, bending on dihedral angle:** U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ₀)², θ₀ = 0 → DCM membrane bending κ_m. κ_b,MB = 2.4×10⁻¹⁹ N·m.
 - **S5, edge extension:** U_s = ½ κ_s (l − l₀)² → DCM edge stiffness. κ_s,MB = 1.0×10⁻⁴ N/m, l₀ = 70 nm.
 - **S10, self-repulsion / node-face:** U_r = κ_r (r₁₂ − r₀)²·[½ + ⅓(1 − r₁₂/r₀)⁴], r₁₂ < r₀ = d_MB → DCM node-face/self-contact penalty (Ericson). κ_r,MB = 5.1×10⁻³ N/m, cutoff 50 nm. Note this *steepened* half-harmonic (the ⅓(…)⁴ term) is a stiffer, better-behaved contact kernel than a plain half-harmonic — worth comparing against DCM's current penalty.
 - **S11, cortex↔membrane attractive coupling:** U_a = ½ κ_a (r₁₂ − r₀,a/2)², r₀,a/2 ≤ r₁₂ < r₀,a → the DCM membrane-cortex normal spring that M1's Bell law switches off. κ_a = 1.0×10⁻³ N/m; force ≈ 30 pN at dissociation range.

**Overdamped update (S1–S4)** matches DCM's overdamped/implicit stepping; the per-node cylindrical drag S3 is filament-specific (FF), not needed for DCM mesh nodes.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce as DCM scenarios

- **G1 — Cell-sized spherical confinement + volume-conserved bleb:** spherical membrane, diameter 8 µm (native-cheap) or 16 µm (validated); 2D cortex in the outer 10% shell (0.9·r → r, 27.1% of volume); cortex assembled with membrane *frozen*, then membrane released and myosin activated. **Reproduce in DCM:** build our 7.5 µm cell, pre-tension cortex at rest (`--from-resting`), then ramp cortex tension → observe bleb. This is a native, full-compartment scenario (membrane + cortex + enclosed volume + interior viscosity) — no compartment stripped.
- **G2 — Temporally-gated contractility ramp:** cortex assembly completes *before* contraction (ZIPK gating; actin 90% polymerized ~10 min, myosin 90% phosphorylated ~40 min). **DCM:** turn cortex γ up on a ramp after the mesh has equilibrated — never activate contraction on an un-relaxed build (matches our `--from-resting` discipline).
- **G3 — R_C / R_X phase-diagram sweep:** sweep membrane-linker density (R_C) and cortex-crosslink density (R_X) → map no-def / weak / single-bleb / multi-bleb and Detachment/Rupture regions (Fig 5, 7). **DCM:** a 2-axis controlled sweep of the M1 linker density and cortex crosslink density; gate = matches the analytic phase boundary (Eqs 7–8), not a tuned target.
- **G4 — Local patterning → deterministic bleb site (Fig 8):** impose a local R_X (or R_C) depletion patch → bleb nucleates there. **DCM:** heterogeneous cortex field → emergent polarity scenario; substrate for confinement/durotaxis directionality.
- **G5 — 2D-shell vs 3D-fill contractility:** run cortex-only (shell) vs bulk-filled interior contractility → single vs multiple protrusions. **DCM:** informs whether adding a bulk (microtubule/cytoplasm) active term changes protrusion multiplicity.

## 6. RISKS / caveats (and how to adapt without breaking physiological-baseline)

- **R1 — In-vitro purified, not living MCF7.** Interior is purified F-actin + smooth-muscle myosin + α-actinin only; no membrane reservoir/exocytosis, no PIP2/ERM native linkers, no microtubules/IF, no nucleus, no adhesions. The **membrane/geometry/tension/pressure physics transfers**; the **interior material identity does not**. Adapt: port the *membrane-mesh potentials, Young-Laplace closure, and Bell detachment law* (mechanism-level), but keep DCM's own physiological interior (η≈65 Pa·s, nucleus, turgor Π₀≈40 Pa) — do NOT import the minimal purified interior as our baseline.
- **R2 — Membrane linker is His-α-actinin↔Ni-NTA, an engineered defined coupling**, not the native talin/ERM/PIP2 system. The Bell *form* (Eq. 5) and the R_C axis are transferable; the specific K_d=1.4 µM and r₀ are the artificial linker's, so for MCF7 re-anchor ρ_H, K_m, r₀ to ERM/talin literature values (surface to PI if unknown — do not default). Flag as a parameter needing a living-cell datum, not a magic number.
- **R3 — κ_b,MB = 2.4×10⁻¹⁹ N·m (~58 k_BT) is a composite membrane+cortex bending stiffness**, far above bare lipid (~20 k_BT). If DCM already carries cortex bending separately, do not double-count — treat this κ_b as "membrane mesh at cortex-coarse-graining," or split it. This is a real mis-mapping risk.
- **R4 — Blebbing here is driven at high, near-mitotic contractility and by an assembly protocol (frozen membrane → release).** DCM must reproduce it *from the physiological resting baseline* (`--from-resting`, cortex pre-tensioned, then ramp), never from a floppy/zero-turgor shell — otherwise the bleb is an initialization artifact (the exact 2026-06-04 saturation failure). The paper's own "membrane frozen during assembly then released" is compatible with our resting-checkpoint discipline; mirror it.
- **R5 — Explicit Euler, Δt = 1.15×10⁻⁴ s, μ = 8.6 Pa·s** is a fine-grained (filament) setup. DCM uses IPC large-dt on the mesh; the parameter *values* (κ's, F_sev) are scale-honest but the *integrator/step* is not portable — keep DCM's implicit stepping. The κ set is stated at native filament scale; verify grid-invariance when mapping per-triangle A₀ to our remesh resolution (κ_A and A₀ co-vary).
- **R6 — 8/16 µm liposomes are slightly larger than our 7.5 µm MCF7 and monodisperse-ish (19.9±7.56 µm experimentally).** Scale V₀ ∝ R³, A₀ ∝ (edge length)², and the Eq. 7 threshold ∝ R when moving to 7.5 µm. Minor, but do it explicitly rather than reusing 8 µm numbers raw.
- **R7 — This is a bleb/protrusion paper, NOT an aggregate-compaction paper.** It does not touch the loose→compact 24–48 h cadherin-T1 open problem. Do not over-read: its cortex-tension corroboration helps γ-generation and bleb/membrane-cortex modules, and nothing about it addresses aggregate compaction RATE.
- **R8 — bioRxiv preprint, not peer-reviewed** (CC-BY). Cite as preprint; the individual *underlying* data (F_sev Tsuda 1996, l_p Isambert 1995, stall Erdmann-Schwarz, K_d co-sed here) are the citable anchors — verify each DOI's `verdict=OK` before KB registration.
