# DCM lens — p48

**Paper:** Kim & Kim 2025, "The mechanism of dynamic steady states in lamellipodia," PLOS Comput Biol 21(10):e1013572, DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013572.
**Engine fit (honest):** This is a **filament-resolved Brownian-dynamics** lamellipodium model — the FF engine's exact paradigm, NOT surface-mesh continuum. For **DCM** it is peripheral. There is no closed triangulated cell, no enclosed volume, no membrane/nucleus, no cadherin cell–cell junction, no turgor. What DOES transfer to DCM is at the level of *sub-continuum force laws that DCM already lumps*: (i) the force-dependent adhesion-clutch off-rate (Bell slip-bond, Eq S10) that DCM's node-face/junction layer uses; (ii) the **contractile-force-vs-adhesion-friction competition** that sets a surface flow speed — the same physics DCM expresses through cortex tension γ and node drag; (iii) a turnover-keeps-the-surface-homogeneous principle that maps onto DCM remesh; (iv) an elastic-substrate stiffness datum for durotaxis scenarios. Treat everything below as **cross-check / optional-enrichment**, not a core DCM datum. Relevance to DCM ≈ 2/5.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt (mapped to a DCM module + why it beats current)

**M1 — Adhesion clutch off-rate: keep DCM on a force-dependent Bell law, use this as a slip-bond cross-check.**
DCM module: **contact / cadherin catch-bond junction** and the node–substrate FA clutch (for adherent single-cell scenarios, `--from-resting`). The paper's nascent-FA link uses a pure **Bell slip-bond** (Eq S10): off-rate rises exponentially with link tension, but only under extension (r ≥ r₀,C). DCM already uses Rakshit KU-4.2 catch-bond for cell–cell and Bell-Evans generally — so this does NOT supersede our catch-bond (cadherin is catch, this FA link is slip). *Why it still matters:* it confirms the **functional form** DCM's slip-type substrate FA clutch should use, and it gives the "friction plateau" behavior — total substrate force saturates because links unbind faster as they load (Fig 5G, 6G plateau at ~0.2–0.25 nN). DCM's adherent-cell traction should show the same **force-dependent plateau**, not unbounded growth. Beats a linear/Metropolis proxy for the FA off-rate.

**M2 — Cortical/retrograde flow as an emergent contraction-vs-friction balance (a validation lens for DCM cortex advection).**
DCM module: **actomyosin cortex tension γ + cortical surface flow**. The paper's central result: retrograde flow speed = f(myosin contractile force − adhesion frictional resistance). Flow rises monotonically with motor density R_M to ~45–50 nm/s (Fig 5F) and falls with FA area A_FA to ~2 nm/s (Fig 6F); above A_FA>0.4 flow is frustrated. *Why relevant:* DCM currently treats γ as a scalar surface tension with no explicit **advective redistribution** of cortical material. If/when DCM adds cortical surface flow (membrane–cortex coupling, bleb, division-furrow ingression), the steady-state flow speed must emerge from exactly this competition — contractile γ-gradient pull vs. adhesion drag — and land in the **~20–50 nm/s** band. This is the physical anchor for a future `cortex_flow` term, not something to bolt on now.

**M3 — Turnover keeps the surface homogeneous (maps onto DCM remesh + a homogeneity gate).**
DCM module: **remesh / surface quality control**. In the paper, buckling-induced severing (Eq S9) + pointed-end depolymerization at the rear *disassemble the myosin-compacted actin arc* so density stays homogeneous — measured as coefficient-of-variation (CoV) of density along y. Too little disassembly → CoV ~1.1 (arc piles up, motors trapped, flow stalls); intermediate → CoV ~0.2–0.3 (homogeneous, fast flow) (Fig 3E, 4E). *DCM analog:* remesh + node redistribution keep the triangulated surface uniform; without it, contraction piles nodes and stalls. **Adopt the CoV-of-surface-density metric** as a DCM diagnostic: compute CoV of local mesh node/area density; a healthy contracting DCM cortex should keep CoV low (~0.2–0.3), a stalled/over-compacted one spikes. This is a cheap new observable, not a new force.

**M4 — Substrate as an elastic triangulated mesh with a single effective stiffness (durotaxis scenario scaffold).**
DCM module: **substrate BC for adherent / durotaxis runs**. The paper reduces a triangulated substrate to one lumped **κ_eff ≈ 2.4×10⁻⁴ N/m** (Mair 2023), tunable via edge spring/bend and mesh spacing. For a DCM durotaxis scenario this is the right abstraction level: a compliant node-anchored substrate with a controllable effective stiffness, letting DCM adhesion links feel substrate compliance. Beats a rigid-wall substrate (which cannot express durotaxis).

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

**Eq S10 — Bell slip-bond off-rate (DCM node–substrate FA clutch, and cross-check for the slip branch of the cadherin junction):**
```
k_u,C = k⁰_u,C · exp( λ_u,C · F_s,C / k_B T )   if r ≥ r₀,C
      = k⁰_u,C                                   if r <  r₀,C
```
Symbol map → DCM: `F_s,C` = adhesion-link spring force = k_link·(r − r₀) on a DCM contact/junction bond; `r₀,C` = bond rest length (node-face penetration threshold / cadherin equilibrium); `k⁰_u,C` = zero-force off-rate; `λ_u,C` = force sensitivity (compliance / k_BT, units 1/force). Port as the **slip component** of DCM's per-bond break test each step. (DCM's cadherin uses the Rakshit catch form; this is the complementary slip form for substrate FAs.)

**Eq S9 — angle/buckling-dependent severing (concept only, → DCM remesh trigger):**
```
k_sev = k_{0,sev} · exp( λ_sev · θ )
```
Symbol map → DCM: θ = local surface bending angle at a mesh edge/node. DCM does not sever, but this motivates a **curvature-triggered remesh/relaxation weight**: where the cortex buckles sharply (high θ, e.g. over-compacted furrow), increase local remesh/relaxation rate — the continuum analog of "buckled material is preferentially turned over." Do NOT implement as literal severing; use as a curvature-adaptive remesh heuristic (still must be derivable/grid-invariant — no tuned constant).

**Force-balance backbone (Eqs S1–S4) — already how DCM's overdamped Warp integrator works; use as a consistency check only:**
```
F_i − ζ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_i^T = 0     (S1, overdamped Langevin)
r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (1/ζ_i)(F_i + F_i^T)Δt   (S4, Euler)
⟨F_i^T F_j^T⟩ = (2 k_B T ζ_i / Δt) δ_ij δ       (S3, FDT)
```
Map → DCM: identical overdamped form; `ζ_i` = DCM per-node drag (currently derived from cytoplasm η=65 Pa·s). Confirms DCM's node drag / thermal-noise scaling is the same lineage. **Drag law S2** `ζ_i = 3πμ r_c (3 + 2r₀/r_c)/5` is a cylinder (filament) drag — NOT applicable to DCM's spherical-node drag; do not port.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce as DCM scenarios

**P1 — Adherent-cell traction saturation on a compliant substrate (directly runnable in DCM).**
Setup: DCM single cell `--from-resting` adhered to an elastic substrate of effective stiffness κ_eff≈2.4×10⁻⁴ N/m; FA links form within a defined basal contact zone with Bell slip-bond breaking (Eq S10). Sweep myosin/cortex activity (analog of R_M) and contact-zone size (analog of A_FA). **Expected DCM signature:** total substrate/traction force rises then **plateaus** (force-dependent FA unbinding), and cortical/basal flow *decreases* as contact-zone size grows — matching Figs 5G, 6F/G. This is a clean DCM validation of the traction-vs-adhesion clutch layer at continuum scale.

**P2 — Durotaxis substrate scaffold.** Use the κ_eff substrate abstraction (§1 M4) as the compliant-substrate BC; grade κ_eff across x to drive DCM durotaxis. The paper supplies the stiffness magnitude and the "flow falls with more/stiffer adhesion" trend to sanity-check direction.

**P3 — Homogeneity/CoV diagnostic on any contracting DCM cortex.** Not a new geometry — add the CoV-of-surface-density readout (§1 M3) to existing compaction/division runs to detect pile-up/stall.

*(Confinement, aspiration, AFM, cell–cell aggregate, division/cytokinetic-ring — this paper offers NO protocol for these; it has no closed cell.)*

## 6. RISKS / caveats (regime mismatch + how to adapt without breaking physiological-baseline)

- **Not our geometry.** 2D quasi-planar lamellipodium slab (5×2.5×0.1 µm), open network, no closed cell, no enclosed volume/turgor, no membrane/nucleus/cadherin. DCM is a closed 3D triangulated cell. → Use only the *force-law forms and adhesion/flow trends*, never import the geometry or claim it validates DCM shape/volume physics.
- **Slip vs catch.** The FA link here is a pure **slip-bond** (Eq S10). DCM's cell–cell interface is a **cadherin catch-bond** (Rakshit KU-4.2, PI-mandated fine-grained). Do NOT let this paper's slip form leak into the cadherin junction — it applies only to the substrate FA clutch. Keep the two distinct.
- **Deliberately sub-physiological actin.** C_A=250 µM (real 500–1000 µM), monomer-recycling closed pool, coarse r₀,A=140 nm, no ADP/ATP-actin/cofilin states, no FA maturation. These are FF-scale coarse-grainings — irrelevant to DCM but a reminder that the *quantitative* filament numbers are not physiological anchors. DCM must still initialize at its own physiological setpoints (η=65 Pa·s, Π₀=40 Pa, γ interphase 0.15 mN/m); this paper's substrate κ_eff is the only new physical value to consider, and only for adherent/durotaxis scenarios that are ON at real substrate compliance, not a null.
- **No cell type match.** Generic migratory-cell lamellipodium (fibroblast/keratocyte-like retrograde flow), not MCF7. The 20–30 nm/s flow band and 120 nm/s / 5.33 pN NMII numbers are cell-type-general enough to use as cross-check bands, but flag them as **non-MCF7** in any KB registration.
- **Do not KB-register as a DCM datum.** Per the "oracle is cross-check, not truth" rule, this paper's numbers enter DCM only as validation bands / scenario inputs, not as primary DCM parameters. Its strong home is the **FF** engine.
- **No magic numbers / no gate-loosening:** any curvature-adaptive remesh weight (§2, Eq S9 analog) must be derived from mesh geometry and grid-invariant — do not tune k0,sev/λ_sev-style constants into DCM.
