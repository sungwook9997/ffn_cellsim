# DCM lens — p26

**Paper:** Matsuda, Jung, … Kim, Yajima (2024) *Myosin-induced F-actin fragmentation facilitates contraction of actin networks.* Cytoskeleton 81(8):339–355. DOI 10.1002/cm.21848.

**Verdict up front — DCM relevance is LOW / INDIRECT (score ≈ 1.5/5).** This is a filament-level, in-vitro + agent-based Brownian-dynamics study of a purified actin/myosin-II/anillin network in a 20×20×0.1 µm quasi-2D slab. It has **no membrane, no cortex-tension continuum, no turgor, no nucleus, no cell-cell junction** — it lives squarely in the **FF engine** (fine-grained filaments), where its relevance is HIGH. For the DCM surface-mesh continuum, essentially nothing ports directly; every parameter is filament-scale. What DCM *can* take is one genuinely transferable **conceptual lever**: the paper is a clean demonstration that **contraction rate of a highly-connected network is set by irreversible connectivity-reduction (topology change), not by adding more motor force** — which is a direct physical analogy to DCM's open aggregate-compaction-RATE problem and its "cadherin T1 rearrangement escalation, not aggregate-σ" hypothesis. Below I separate the (thin) real levers from the (large) non-transferable body, per the physiological-baseline and mechanistic-fidelity rules.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### 1a. (PRIMARY, conceptual) Aggregate-compaction RATE — "irreversible topology reduction unlocks catastrophic contraction," supports the T1-rearrangement-escalation hypothesis
- **DCM module:** aggregate surface tension σ + cadherin catch-bond junction + the **open T1-rearrangement escalation** for the loose→compact compaction-RATE problem.
- **What the paper shows:** a heavily cross-linked network is **mechanically stable and does NOT contract** no matter how much motor force is present (Fig 1 RANLN=0.3; Fig 4 RANLN=0.24 stable to 2000 s). Contraction is unlocked only by an **irreversible connectivity-reduction event** — gelsolin severing (Fig 4) or tension-induced fragmentation (Figs 6–8). Once a few irreversible events fire, force redistributes onto the surviving connections, pushing them over threshold → **catastrophic (sharp, self-accelerating) area collapse** (Fig 8e; "catastrophic manner", p.13). ACP *unbinding* (reversible) gives only **gradual** contraction and stalls in long/high-connectivity networks because links re-bind (Fig 8e, Fig S19).
- **Why it beats what DCM does now / why it matters:** DCM's memory record already concludes that aggregate-σ compaction is a **mechanical end-state only** and is **drag-limited**, NOT rate-representative — real 24–48 h compaction "needs cadherin T1 rearrangement escalation, not aggregate-σ" (`project-dcm-compaction-turgor-blocked`, `project-dcm-ipc-large-dt`). This paper is independent, quantitative support for exactly that logic at the network level: **more contractile force does not set the rate; an irreversible topology-reduction cascade does.** The lever for DCM: model T1 rearrangement (neighbor-swap) as an **irreversible, threshold-gated, force-redistributing** event on the cadherin junction graph — and expect the RATE signature to be **catastrophic/self-accelerating once a connectivity threshold is crossed**, not the gradual drag-limited pull that aggregate-σ produces. The reversible-vs-irreversible contrast (ACP-unbind = gradual/stalls; sever = catastrophic) is the diagnostic to design the T1 term against.
- **Caveat (mandatory):** the paper's irreversible severing is at the *filament* level; DCM's analog is *junction-graph* topology (T1 neighbor exchange), which is legitimately irreversible-per-event but must remain reversible-in-population (cells re-adhere). Adopt the *dynamics signature* (threshold → force redistribution → cascade), not the filament numbers.

### 1b. (SECONDARY) Cortex as a connectivity-gated, stress-relaxing material — not a fixed γ
- **DCM module:** actomyosin cortex surface tension γ (currently a fixed interphase 0.15 / mitotic 1.5 mN/m field) and the γ-generation open problem.
- **What the paper shows:** network tension is **non-monotonic in crosslinker density** (Fig 1: contraction speed falls as RANLN/RACP rises; too many crosslinkers → zero contraction) and **biphasic in myosin turnover rate** (Fig 3: optimum ATP / optimum k₂₀≈50–60 s⁻¹; too fast → duty ratio collapses → no tension; too slow → no remodeling). Tension also has a **rupture ceiling** — beyond a load threshold the network sheds tension by breaking connections (stress relaxation).
- **Why it could matter:** DCM's γ-floor work (`project-gamma-floor-*`) treats the deficit as a force-magnitude limit. This paper says cortex tension is **not** a monotone function of "more myosin / more crosslink" — it has an interior optimum and a self-limiting rupture ceiling. If DCM ever moves γ from a set field toward an emergent cortex quantity, the **shape** (biphasic in turnover, non-monotone in crosslink, capped by a relaxation threshold) is the qualitative target. **This is a low-priority, forward-looking note** — do NOT retrofit it onto the current fixed-γ baseline without PI sign-off (it is a γ-generation redesign, not a parameter).

### 1c. (TERTIARY) Cytokinetic ring closure may stall without a topology-reduction term
- **DCM module:** cell division / cytokinetic ring (open problem).
- **What the paper shows:** anillin is a **contractile-ring** crosslinker; a highly-connected anillin network **cannot complete contraction on line-tension alone** and needs severing to finish. Ring closure ≈ maximally-connected contractile bundle.
- **Why it matters:** when DCM implements a cytokinetic-ring line-tension law, expect it to **stall near closure** if the ring is a permanently-crosslinked bundle — a disassembly/severing (connectivity-reduction) term at the ring may be required to reach full furrow ingression. Flag, not an implementation ask yet.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

**Honest answer: none of the paper's explicit equations port to DCM.** The functional forms (harmonic stretch U_ext=½k_s(r−r₀)², bend U_bend=½k_b(θ−θ₀)², volume-exclusion, Eqs S1–S3) live in Supporting Info (not in this PDF) and are **per-segment filament potentials** → they belong to FF, not the DCM surface mesh. The three main-text relations, and why they stay in FF:

1. **Bell's law (ACP off-rate):** `k_off = k_off,0 · exp(F·λ / k_BT)` (Bell 1978). DCM already has the equivalent at its own scale (cadherin catch-bond, Rakshit KU-4.2) — this is the FF crosslinker off-rate, **no new port to DCM**.
2. **Tension-fragmentation criterion:** `F_ext(segment) > F_frag = 500 pN ⇒ remove segment` (Tsuda 1996). Filament-scale severing; the *DCM analog is a junction-graph T1 trigger*, but the **500 pN number does NOT transfer** to a per-node/per-face force in the surface mesh — it is a monomer–monomer bond-rupture force, not a cell-cell or cortex-continuum threshold.
3. **Filament force-accumulation scaling (text p.12):** `F_filament ≈ N_bound_heads · f_stall`, worked as ⟨Lf⟩=2.6 µm (~370 monomers), RM=0.32 → ~60 heads, half bound → ~300 pN average (subset > 500 pN via Gaussian length spread). Purely FF (motor→filament load). The only *transferable idea* — not equation — is that **tension is heterogeneously distributed and a small tail of high-load elements sets the failure**, which for DCM means a T1-escalation rule should key off the **max-loaded junction**, not the mean.

→ **DCM equation ports: 0.** Symbol map (F→junction load, k_off→cadherin off-rate) already exists in DCM's cadherin module; this paper adds no new continuum-level equation.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

DCM-usable scenarios are thin, and all are **analogies**, not direct geometry ports (the paper's 20×20×0.1 µm periodic slab is a 2D network, not a cell):

- **"Stable-until-perturbed → catastrophic compaction" protocol (Fig 4 gelsolin trigger).** DCM analog: assemble an N≈400 aggregate that is compaction-**stalled** at physiological cadherin connectivity (the drag-limited plateau), then **enable the T1-rearrangement escalation** as the "gelsolin-like" connectivity-reduction trigger and measure whether compaction transitions from stalled → catastrophic. This is the cleanest DCM experiment the paper motivates: it operationalizes the compaction-RATE open problem as a trigger experiment.
- **Connectivity-density sweep (Fig 1).** DCM analog: sweep cadherin junction density / maturation and confirm the **non-monotone** compaction-rate signature (too few → no long-range force transmission → local only; too many → over-stabilized → stalls). Sanity target, run at native + full compartment only.
- **NOT reproducible in DCM:** the in-vitro flow-chamber, PIV area-decrease assay, single-filament rhodamine tracking — all filament-microscopy protocols with no DCM observable.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

Direct numeric targets: **none** (all the paper's numbers — 500 pN, 5 pN, µm²/s network contraction, events/s severing — are filament/2D-network observables with no DCM continuum counterpart). **Qualitative dynamical signatures** DCM's compaction/T1 machinery should reproduce:

| signature | paper source | DCM observable to match |
|---|---|---|
| **Catastrophic vs gradual area drop** — irreversible topology change → sharp self-accelerating collapse; reversible unbinding → gradual, stalls | Fig 8c,e; p.13 "catastrophic manner" | aggregate compaction curve should go **sharp/self-accelerating** once T1 escalation fires, vs the current gradual drag-limited pull |
| **More force ≠ faster once connectivity high** — stuck network unmoved by extra motor; only connectivity reduction unlocks it | Fig 1 (RANLN=0.3), Fig 4 | confirms DCM finding that aggregate-σ (more force) does not set rate; T1 (topology) does |
| **Non-monotone in crosslinker density** — contraction speed falls then vanishes as crosslink rises | Fig 1c,f | compaction rate vs cadherin density should be non-monotone (interior optimum) |
| **Failure keyed to the high-load tail, not the mean** — avg 300 pN but subset >500 pN fragments | text p.12 | T1 escalation should trigger on **max-loaded** junction, expect heterogeneous junction-load distribution |

**Explicit anti-target:** the in-vitro network reaches steady state in **<1 h** (p.6). **Do NOT use this as a DCM compaction-rate target** — real MCF7 aggregate compaction is 24–48 h and is set by cadherin T1 rearrangement, not purified-network severing (`project-dcm-compaction-turgor-blocked` scope note). Citing the <1 h number for DCM would violate the rate-citation ban already logged.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Wrong engine.** This is an FF paper (agent-based fine-grained filaments). Forcing it into DCM risks importing filament-scale numbers as continuum params — a category error of exactly the kind logged in `reference-dcm-meters-ff-microns` (FF K_A and DCM ΔP unit slips). Keep 500 pN / 5 pN / k₂₀ in FF; DCM takes only the *dynamical logic*.
- **In-vitro purified, not living MCF7.** Rabbit skeletal-muscle actin + non-muscle myosin II + anillin, no membrane/turgor/nucleus/adhesion. The physiological-baseline rule means DCM must **not** adopt the paper's zero-turnover, purified regime; the authors themselves note (p.15) that **in cells fast F-actin turnover suppresses the large-force buildup**, so tension-induced fragmentation is *less* important in living cells except in slow-turnover, tightly-crosslinked regions (e.g. cortex). So even the cortex-tension analogy (§1b) is a **minority mechanism** in vivo — flag before any γ-redesign.
- **2D quasi-slab, tiny domain.** 20×20×0.1 µm periodic; the authors explicitly downscale for cost and warn results are **qualitative, not quantitative** (p.14). No length-scale-faithful transfer.
- **Irreversibility mismatch.** The paper's severing is permanent (no annealing). DCM's T1 rearrangement is per-event irreversible but population-reversible (cells re-adhere) — adopt the cascade signature, not literal permanence.
- **Anillin kinetics unknown** (p.14) — even at the source scale the crosslinker rates are uncertain; not a citable parameter source.
- **Torsion/undertwist omitted** (p.14) — the actual molecular severing mechanism (tension→undertwist→bond destabilization) is not modeled; the 500 pN rule is a phenomenological stand-in, reinforcing that it must not be treated as a fundamental transferable constant.

**Net:** one strong conceptual lever (irreversible topology reduction → catastrophic contraction ⇒ supports T1-escalation over aggregate-σ for the compaction-RATE problem), two weak forward-looking notes (biphasic connectivity-gated cortex tension; ring closure needs a severing term). Zero portable equations, zero portable parameters. Real payoff for this paper is in the FF lens.
