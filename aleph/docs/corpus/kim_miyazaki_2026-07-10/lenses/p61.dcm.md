# DCM lens — p61

**Paper:** Linsmeier, Banerjee, Oakes, Jung, Kim, Murrell (2016) — "Disordered actomyosin networks are sufficient to produce cooperative and telescopic contractility." *Nat. Commun.* 7:12615. DOI 10.1038/ncomms12615.

**One-line orientation for DCM:** This is a 2D in-vitro biomimetic cortex + an **isotropic active-viscoelastic-gel continuum model** + an agent-based (Kim-lab) simulation. The *agent-based* half belongs to FF. The **continuum active-gel half is the DCM-relevant part**: it is exactly a surface/continuum-level description of a locally-activated contractile cortex, and it gives (a) a mechanistic *time-evolution law* for active cortical stress, (b) a validated dimensionless regime (active stress ≈ elastic modulus), (c) cortical-flow validation numbers, and (d) two localized-activation protocols (disk cap, ring) that map onto DCM's open **cell-division / cortical-polarity / confinement** problems. Magnitudes are in-vitro 2D purified — usable as *form/timescale/scaling* oracles, NOT as MCF7 magic numbers.

Honest scope: DCM's cortex today is a whole-cell surface tension γ (static, or pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2). This paper does not hand DCM a better *magnitude* for γ. It hands DCM (1) a **temporal buildup law** for γ when myosin is switched on, (2) a **spatial-heterogeneity / localized-activation** description that DCM currently lacks, and (3) **cortical-flow observables** to validate against once localized activation exists.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cortex tension: add an active-stress *buildup* law (temporal ramp of γ on activation) — **DCM module: `dcm` cortex tension γ**
Paper's core continuum ingredient (Methods, Eq below): active contractile stress accumulates as σₐ(r,t) = σ₀(r)·(1 − e^(−t/τₐ)). DCM currently sets γ instantaneously (or ties it to ΔP·R/2). Whenever a DCM scenario *switches on / up-regulates* myosin (mitotic entry γ 0.15→1.5 mN/m; a polarity cap; a local contractile patch), the tension should **relax exponentially to its target with a characteristic time τₐ**, not step-jump.
**Why it beats current DCM:** step-jumping γ is a numerical convenience that injects an unphysical impulse into the overdamped mesh (shock, remesh churn). The exponential buildup is the mechanistically-correct approach to the new operating point and matches the measured strain lag/linear/plateau phases (P1/P2/P3, ~300 s to saturate). It also respects the physiological-baseline rule cleanly: **baseline γ stays at its resting operating point; the ramp governs only the perturbation FROM baseline** (e.g. interphase→mitotic transition).

### M2 — Localized cortical activation (contractile cap / equatorial band) as a first-class surface field — **DCM module: cortex γ made a per-face scalar field γ(x)**
The paper's whole point is *spatial* control: a circular active core (radius ξ) inside a passive medium. DCM's cortex tension is currently a single global scalar. Promoting γ to a **per-face field** with an activated patch (spherical cap of faces at elevated σₐ, surrounded by passive faces at baseline γ) is the minimal mechanistic upgrade that unlocks **division (equatorial ring), polarity/durotaxis (front-rear cap), and confinement-response asymmetry**.
**Why it beats current DCM:** a global γ can never produce the retrograde/convergent cortical flow, the boundary-localized velocity, or the center-peaked stress that define real cortical mechanics and cytokinesis. This paper is the clean, minimal template (isotropic, no anisotropy needed) for doing it at the continuum level.

### M3 — Cytokinetic ring = ring-activation pattern — **DCM open problem: cell division / cytokinetic ring**
Fig 1f shows an explicit **ring activation pattern** producing an annulus of contraction. This is the direct in-vitro analog of the cytokinetic furrow: a band of elevated cortical tension around the equator. DCM's division is listed as open; this paper gives the *contractility protocol* (ring of high σₐ in a passive cortex) and the flow signature it should produce (convergent radial flow into the ring), which is what a DCM cytokinetic-ring line-tension model must reproduce.
**Why:** it grounds the ring not as an ad-hoc line tension but as a spatially-patterned instance of the *same* active-stress field used everywhere else — one mechanism, spatially modulated (consistent with the project's "one junction / one category" philosophy).

### M4 — Active-vs-elastic *regime* selection (E/σₐ dimensionless number) — **DCM cortex ↔ membrane K_A / κ_m balance**
Telescopic, cell-like contraction emerges only when **active stress ≈ elastic modulus** (E/σₐ ~ O(1); telescopic slope tuned by E/σₐ ∈ {0.04, 0.45, 2.3, 4.5}). For DCM this is a *sanity constraint*: the cortical active tension γ must be co-scaled with the membrane area-elasticity K_A and bending κ_m so the cortex sits in the balanced regime — too stiff (E≫σₐ) and contraction/flow is suppressed; too soft and it collapses. This is a **grid-invariant, derivable dimensionless check**, not a tuned constant.
**Why it beats current DCM:** gives a principled way to check that DCM's γ, K_A, κ_m are mutually consistent rather than independently set — a Magic-Number-Block-compliant relation.

### M5 — Bending/buckling is NOT required for cortical contractility → **architectural validation of DCM's surface-continuum cortex**
Fig 6: telescopic contraction is retained even when F-actin persistence length is raised 20 µm → 2.6 mm (buckling suppressed). Only *total active-motor content* matters. This is a strong, direct justification for DCM's design choice: **DCM does not resolve individual filaments or their buckling and it does not need to** — a continuum/surface active-stress term captures the emergent cortical contractility. Use this as the citable defense for the cortex coarse-graining.
**Why:** pre-empts the objection "you must resolve F-actin buckling (Murrell-Gardel 2012) to get contractility" — this paper's own agent model refutes it.

### M6 — Crosslink stiffening attenuates contraction (filamin) — **DCM cortex crosslink density ↔ effective E**
Filamin-A crosslinking (RC=0.035) cut strain 2.4→0.3 and strain rate ~0.015→0.002. Mechanistically: raising passive network stiffness E (relative to σₐ) attenuates contractile strain/rate while preserving the telescopic *form*. For DCM this maps to: the cortex's effective elastic modulus (crosslink density) is a **modulator of how much the active γ actually deforms the surface** — relevant to the γ-floor / "cross-bridge stiffness" open thread (see MEMORY γ-floor entries).

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

### E1 — Active-stress buildup (Methods, the paper's only closed-form governing law)
```
σₐ(r, t) = σ₀(r) · (1 − e^(−t / τₐ))
```
- σₐ → DCM active cortical tension contribution, applied per cortex face (or as an added line/surface tension). Units N/m (surface) or N/m² if kept as a 2D stress ×thickness.
- σ₀(r) → target/plateau active tension = spatial activation profile. In DCM: σ₀ = γ_active on **activated faces**, 0 (baseline) elsewhere. For mitotic global up-regulation, σ₀ = (γ_mitotic − γ_interphase) uniformly.
- τₐ → active-stress accumulation timescale (buildup constant). **Value lives in Supplementary Table 1 (not in main text) — FLAG to fetch.** Empirical anchor from the measured strain course: saturation over P1+P2 ≈ 0→300 s ⇒ τₐ is O(100 s) (see §5).
- t → sim time since activation onset.

**DCM port:** `γ_face(t) = γ_baseline + Δγ_target · (1 − exp(−t/τₐ))` on activated faces. Overdamped mesh already handles the resulting node motion.

### E2 — Strain = divergence of displacement (analysis / observable)
```
ε = ∇·u
```
Port as a **surface divergence over the triangulated cortex**: for node displacements **u** on the mesh, ε per face = surface-∇·**u** (discrete divergence using face gradient operators). This is the DCM observable to quantify cortical *convergence* (contraction) vs the paper's ε.

### E3 — Strain rate = divergence of velocity (analysis / observable)
```
dε/dt = ∇·v
```
Surface divergence of node velocities → per-face contraction rate. The paper's ∇·v reaches a minimum (~−7×10⁻³ s⁻¹) at peak contractility; DCM's activated-cap run should produce a comparable convergent ∇·v signature.

### E4 — Hill cooperativity of strain vs myosin density (Methods / SI; standard sigmoid)
```
ε_max(ρ) = ε_sat · ρ^nH / (ρc^nH + ρ^nH),   nH = 11,  ρc = 0.56 µm⁻²
```
- ρ → local cortical myosin thick-filament areal density (µm⁻²).
- **Port with caution.** If DCM ever makes γ_active a function of a myosin-density field, this gives the *functional form* (sharp percolation-like threshold) — but nH=11 and ρc=0.56 µm⁻² are **in-vitro 2D purified constants and must NOT be hard-coded as MCF7 values** (would violate no-magic-number / no-fitting). Use only the *shape* (threshold cooperativity) qualitatively; surface the actual MCF7 threshold to PI if quantitative.

### E5 — Overdamped substrate-friction force balance (implicit, continuum)
```
active/elastic divergence  =  ζ · v      (drag ∝ velocity)
```
- ζ → substrate friction. **No literal DCM analog** (cortex sits on the membrane/cytoplasm, not a coverslip). Map ζ → **membrane–cortex drag + cytoplasm viscous drag (η≈65 Pa·s)** already in DCM's per-node drag. This is how DCM's existing overdamped Warp integrator already realizes the same balance — reassuring, and means the paper's flow numbers are drag-set (so match DCM's drag before comparing absolute speeds).

### E6 — Regime dimensionless group
```
E / σₐ  ∈ {0.04, 0.45, 2.3, 4.5}   (telescopic slope);   σrr / E  (normalized radial stress)
```
DCM analog: `K_A_eff / γ_active` (or bending κ_m vs γ). Keep O(1) for cell-like balanced contraction (§1 M4). Grid-invariant sanity check.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce (DCM scenarios)

### P1 — Localized disk-cap activation → cortical-flow scaling ("telescopic" test)
Activate a **spherical-cap patch of cortex faces** at elevated γ (via E1 ramp) on an otherwise-resting native full-compartment cell; sweep the cap angular radius (small/medium/large, analogs of ξ = 9 / 23 / 37 µm). Measure per-face ε, dε/dt, and the node velocity at the cap boundary.
**DCM payoff:** first mechanistic test of a *heterogeneous* cortex; validates against the telescopic law (§5) and seeds polarity/durotaxis (front-rear cap).

### P2 — Ring / equatorial-band activation → cytokinetic furrow
Activate an **annular band of faces around the cell equator** at elevated γ. Watch convergent flow into the band and furrow ingression.
**DCM payoff:** directly attacks the open **division** problem with a spatially-patterned instance of the same active-stress field (M3), rather than an ad-hoc line-tension bolt-on.

### P3 — Global up-regulation with ramp (interphase→mitotic)
On the native full cell at resting γ=0.15 mN/m, ramp γ→1.5 mN/m via E1 with τₐ. Confirm the shape change proceeds through a lag→linear→plateau strain course (not a shock).
**DCM payoff:** physiological mitotic-rounding onset done mechanistically; also a numerical-stability win vs step-jumping γ.

**All three respect the HARD native+full-compartment rule:** run on `--cortex-fil`-equivalent native mesh, `--from-resting`, all compartments ON; the activation patch/ramp is the *only* perturbation from the physiological baseline.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

Cross-check oracles (in-vitro measured + reproduced by the paper's continuum & agent models). Treat as **form / timescale / scaling** targets; absolute speeds are drag-set so only compare after matching DCM's ζ↔η drag.

- **Cortical contraction saturation time ≈ 300 s** (P1 0–100 s lag, P2 100–300 s linear, P3 >300 s plateau). A DCM ramped/cap run should saturate on this O(minutes) scale.
- **Strain rate 0.005–0.020 s⁻¹** (Fig 3b); telescopic dε/dt ≈ constant ~0.015–0.02 across cap sizes (Fig 4d).
- **∇·v minimum ≈ −7 to −8 ×10⁻³ s⁻¹** at peak contractility (~250–350 s) (Fig 2c).
- **Boundary flow speed 0.025 → 0.07 µm s⁻¹, INCREASING with activation radius** while mean strain/strain-rate stay ≈constant — the **telescopic law** (Fig 4c–e). This is the key scaling a DCM disk-cap sweep (P1) must reproduce: bigger cap ⇒ faster boundary flow, same mean strain rate.
- **Max mean radial velocity ⟨vr⟩ up to ~0.05 µm s⁻¹, peaking at r≈ξ (the cap boundary)** (Fig 2d).
- **Radial stress peaks at CENTER, decays to boundary** (σrr/E ~0.6→0, Fig 5c / Fig 2e). DCM's per-face stress in an activated cap should show center-peaked convergence.
- **Radial/tangential flow transition:** initially isotropic → predominantly radial within ~30 s (vr/|v| → ~0.75+) (Fig 2c inset).
- **Stiffening (crosslink) reduces contraction:** strain 2.4→0.3, rate 0.015→0.002 when E is raised (filamin) (Fig 5h,i) — M6 modulator direction check.
- **Bending-invariance:** contractility/telescopy retained as filament bending is suppressed (Fig 6) — validates DCM's non-filament-resolving cortex (M5).

## 6. RISKS / caveats (regime mismatch & adaptation)

1. **In-vitro purified, 2D-on-lipid-bilayer, NOT living MCF7, NOT 3D.** The whole assay is a flat quasi-2D actomyosin film on a coverslip-supported bilayer with *no volume constraint, no turgor, no nucleus, no membrane tension coupling.* DCM's cortex is a closed 3D surface enclosing a pressurized volume. → **Transfer only the *form* (buildup law E1, telescopic scaling, center-peaked stress) and *timescale* (τₐ, ~300 s), NEVER the absolute γ magnitude or ρc as an MCF7 value.** Baseline γ stays at DCM's physiological 0.15/1.5 mN/m.

2. **No F-actin crosslinker (myosin-only connectivity) in the base assay.** Connectivity/percolation is entirely myosin-mediated here (that's *why* nH=11, ρc=0.56 are so sharp). Living cortex has α-actinin/filamin crosslinkers → its cooperativity threshold differs. → Do **not** import ρc/nH into the runtime; if a myosin-density-dependent γ is ever wanted, surface to PI for a crosslinked-cortex / MCF7 threshold.

3. **Substrate friction ζ has no cell analog.** The paper's overdamped balance drains momentum into the coverslip. DCM's cortex drains into cytoplasm (η≈65 Pa·s) + membrane. → Map ζ → DCM's existing per-node cytoplasm/membrane drag; **match this drag before comparing absolute flow speeds** (0.025–0.07 µm/s are ζ-set and will not transfer numerically).

4. **Non-muscle myosin II in vitro at RM=0.085, blebbistatin-uncaged** — a controlled biochemistry, not the regulated NMII of a living cell. → composition ratios (RM, RC) are context, not DCM inputs.

5. **"Telescopic" magnitudes are strain-normalized in-vitro.** Fig 4c ε_max ~2.5–3.0 are huge because the 2D film freely condenses; a 3D turgor-pressurized DCM cortex cannot strain that far (enclosed-volume constraint resists). → Expect the DCM cap run to show the *scaling* (boundary velocity ∝ cap size) but **much smaller absolute strain** — that is correct physics, not a failure. Do not tune γ up to hit ε~2.5.

6. **Physiological-baseline compliance:** M1's ramp is explicitly a *perturbation from* the resting operating point (baseline γ pre-set at 0.15 mN/m, turgor Π₀ ON, η physiological). The τₐ ramp only governs the transition (mitotic entry / cap activation), so it does **not** reintroduce an unphysical zero-tension start. If Supp-Table-1 τₐ can't be fetched, DO NOT invent a value — surface to PI or bound it from the 300 s strain course and label non-authoritative.

---

**Bottom line for DCM:** moderate-value, mostly *architectural + protocol* rather than new magnitudes. The concrete wins are (i) the exponential active-stress **buildup law E1** with a saturation timescale (replaces step-jumping γ), (ii) promoting cortex γ to a **per-face field** enabling disk-cap and **ring/cytokinesis** protocols (P1–P3), (iii) a suite of **cortical-flow validation targets** (telescopic scaling, ∇·v min, center-peaked stress, ~300 s saturation), and (iv) the **bending-invariance result** that citably justifies DCM's non-filament-resolving continuum cortex. The cooperativity constants (nH=11, ρc=0.56) are in-vitro and stay out of the runtime.
