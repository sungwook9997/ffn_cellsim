# DCM lens — p72

**Paper**: Noji et al. 2025, "Protein design of two-component tubular assemblies similar to cytoskeletons," *Nat. Commun.* 16:6738 (DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-62076-3).

**Bottom line for DCM**: essentially nothing to adopt. This is a de novo protein-design + in-vitro reconstitution + cryo-EM + single-filament TIRFM paper. It contains **no membrane, no cortex, no turgor/osmotic, no cadherin junction, no cell-cell contact, no division, no confinement, no aggregate compaction** — i.e. none of the surface-mesh/continuum physics the DCM engine models. The single quantitative physical model in the paper (a 2D worm-like-chain persistence-length fit) lives at the single-semiflexible-filament scale, which is an FF (Filament-FEM) concern, not a DCM one. DCM operates on a closed triangulated cell surface; a 28–40 nm designed protein nanotube with Lp≈20 µm is not a DCM ingredient. Relevance score below is near-floor and I recommend NOT porting anything into the DCM engine from this paper.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt
**None applicable to DCM.**

Surveying every DCM module against the paper:
- **Membrane (K_A, κ_m, surface viscosity)** — paper has no lipid membrane; the "tube" is a hollow protein lattice, not a cell boundary. Nothing.
- **Actomyosin cortex / γ generation** — no myosin, no cortex, no active tension. The open γ-generation problem gets no lever here.
- **Cytoplasm viscosity / turgor Π₀** — no enclosed volume, no osmotic pressure. Nothing.
- **Nucleus inclusion** — nothing.
- **Contact / cadherin catch-bond junction / aggregate σ / compaction RATE** — the paper's assembly is coiled-coil heterodimer binding (M3L2/p66α, Kd≈268 nM, Tm≈35 °C) between purified protein subunits, salt- and temperature-tuned. It is NOT a cadherin catch-bond, not load-sharing, not a cell-cell interface, and gives no off-rate/force-dependence. It cannot inform the loose→compact T1-rearrangement problem.
- **T1 rearrangement / remesh / division / confinement / durotaxis / bleb / membrane-cortex coupling** — none appear.

The paper's one physics result (persistence length via WLC) belongs to the fine-grained filament engine (FF), where it is at best a weak cross-check for a *single actin filament's* bending stiffness — not a cell-surface quantity. It does not map onto any DCM continuum force.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh/continuum level
**None.** The only governing equation in the paper is the Kratky–Porod 2D worm-like-chain relation, Eq. (1):

  ⟨R²⟩ = 4·Lp²·[ 2·exp(−L / 2Lp) − 2 + L / Lp ]

- Symbols: ⟨R²⟩ = ensemble mean-square end-to-end distance of a thermally fluctuating filament confined to 2D; L = contour length; Lp = persistence length (κ = Lp·kB·T).
- **Mapping to DCM state: none.** DCM does not represent individual semiflexible filaments — the cortex is a continuum surface tension γ on a triangulated mesh, and membrane bending is a Helfrich κ_m on faces, not a filament Lp. There is no DCM node/face/γ/Π/η/K_A/κ_m symbol that this equation feeds. (It maps to FF, where an actin segment's Lp sets κ_bend, and it is already the WLC physics FF uses.)

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce
**None usable as a DCM scenario.** The paper's protocols are: mix two purified proteins in buffer (300 rpm, 100 mM NaCl, 40 °C, hours–days) → self-assembled nanotubes; cryo-EM single-particle/helical reconstruction; TIRFM of thermally bending single tubes under methylcellulose depletion. There is no confinement-of-a-cell, aspiration, AFM-on-cell, division, aggregate, or durotaxis setup. The "salt-exchange reversibility" (100→0→100 mM) is a protein-assembly assay, not a cell-mechanics BC.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables
**None.** The paper's measured numbers (Lp 19.7/12.5 µm; tube diameters 28.6–40 nm; length ~5 µm at 48 h; symmetry-class distribution C4–C10; salt-reassembly p-values) are all single-filament / lattice observables. **DCM emits cell-scale observables** — cortical tension, shape index, contact angle, division force, ring-closure rate, compaction fraction, aggregate surface tension. This paper reports none of those. (The only datum a DCM run could even superficially "reproduce" — actin Lp 9–20 µm — is not a DCM output at all.)

## 6. RISKS / caveats
- **Regime mismatch is total for DCM's purposes.** In-vitro purified de novo protein (not a living cell); no membrane, no active tension, no osmotic/turgor state; equilibrium thermal fluctuations of a rigid synthetic tube, not out-of-equilibrium actomyosin. There is no physiological cell operating point to initialize to, so the physiological-baseline rule cannot even be engaged — there is no compartment here.
- **Do not over-reach**: it would violate the mechanistic + no-magic-number rules to graft this synthetic tube's Lp (19.7 µm) or its coiled-coil Kd onto a DCM cortex/junction — they describe a different object at a different scale.
- **Where it is (weakly) legitimate**: only in the FF lens, and only as an independent citable cross-check for a *single actin filament's* persistence length (12.5 µm here; 9–20 µm literature) and the standard WLC formula. Even there it is a sanity datum, not a mechanism. For DCM it is out of scope; recommend no KB mechanism registration against the DCM engine.
