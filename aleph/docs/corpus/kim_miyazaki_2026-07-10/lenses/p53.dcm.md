# DCM lens — p53

**Paper:** Costache et al. 2022, *Rapid assembly of a polar network architecture drives efficient actomyosin contractility*, Cell Reports 39(9):110868. DOI 10.1016/j.celrep.2022.110868. (Robin lab experiment + Taeyoon Kim agent-based actomyosin model.)

**One-line for DCM:** This paper is primarily an FF-scale (filament-level) paper, but it hands DCM a *mechanistic, excitable, pulsatile* recipe for **cortical tension γ generation** — RhoA pulses → delayed myosin activation → transient contraction with a measured cortical-flow speed — which is exactly the open problem DCM currently papers over with a static uniform γ. Treat it as a **γ(x,t) surface-field driver + validation-target** source, NOT a parameter source for the mesh elasticity (those params are 140-nm filament segments, wrong scale for a surface mesh).

Honest scope: DCM impact is **moderate**. The Table S4 stiffnesses (κ_s,A, κ_b,A, ACP/motor arms) are filament-segment constants and do NOT map onto a surface-mesh K_A/κ_m — do not port them into DCM. What ports is the *kinetic timing law* of cortical contraction and its emergent-tension observables.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cortex γ generation: replace static uniform γ with an excitable RhoA-pulse field γ(x,t) (DCM module: `cortex` / cortical_tension)
**Now:** DCM sets cortical tension γ as a spatially uniform, time-constant scalar (interphase ≈0.15 mN/m, mitotic ≈1.5 mN/m), pressure-borne as γ=ΔP·R/2. The γ-generation problem is flagged OPEN — where does the *number* come from, and it is not spatiotemporally resolved.
**Paper's mechanism:** cortical contraction is not steady — it is a train of **stochastic RhoA pulses** (period ~30 s in the embryo, spatially localized 3–5 µm wide, 5–10 µm apart) that each recruit myosin after a **fixed delay d_M = 5 s** and hold it active for **τ_M = 15 s**, producing a transient local contraction and a cortical flow of **~0.3 µm/s** at peak.
**DCM change:** make γ a per-face (or per-vertex) surface field γ_i(t) driven by an excitable RhoA activator a_i(t): each stochastically-nucleated pulse raises myosin activity m_i(t) after delay d_M, held τ_M, and γ_i(t) = γ_0 + γ_myo · m_i(t). This gives DCM emergent, physiologically-timed cortical-tension heterogeneity and transient contractions instead of a hand-set scalar.
**Why it beats now:** (a) it is the *fine-grained mechanistic* option (RhoA→ROCK→myosin cascade with real delay/duration) over a lumped constant, per the HARD rule; (b) it makes γ *emergent from a driver* rather than a magic number tuned to hit 0.15/1.5; (c) it is the natural substrate for division (equatorial RhoA band → cytokinetic ring, see M3) and for bleb/pulse dynamics.

### M2 — Polar cortical architecture → anisotropic/oriented surface tension (DCM module: cortex tension tensor)
**Paper result (Figs 5–6):** a pulse builds a **polar "actin aster," barbed ends pointing radially OUTWARD** from the pulse center; this barbed-out polarity transmits myosin force *farther* and — critically — **prevents collapse into disconnected aggregates**, giving higher network-summed tensile force (up to ~3.7 nN) than the inverted (pointed-out) architecture, which instead polarity-sorts myosin to the center and *reduces* transmitted force.
**DCM change:** promote cortical tension from a scalar γ to a rank-2 **surface tension tensor** γ_αβ carrying an in-plane orientation/anisotropy per face, seeded by the local pulse polarity. Even a cheap version — a per-face "contractile efficiency" scalar η_c that scales how much local myosin converts to *transmitted* (long-range) tension vs *wasted* (local aggregation) — captures the paper's headline.
**Why it beats now:** DCM's own OPEN aggregate-collapse worry (loose→compact aggregate, motors self-disengaging) is the same failure mode the paper identifies as "collapse into disconnected aggregates." The paper says the fix is *network architecture/polarity*, not more force. A polarity/efficiency factor is a mechanistic lever DCM currently lacks.

### M3 — Division / cytokinetic ring as an equatorial RhoA band (DCM open problem: cell division)
**Paper:** the embryo's pulsed RhoA machinery *is* the same machinery that, when spatially patterned to the equator, drives cytokinesis; the paper explicitly contrasts pulsed cortical contraction (metazoan, barbed-out, 10–50 µm) with the fission-yeast cytokinetic-node search-capture (SCPR, pointed-out, 1–2 µm) as two geometries of the **same contractile output**.
**DCM change:** implement the cytokinetic ring as a *narrow equatorial band of elevated γ* driven by the same γ(x,t) machinery from M1 — a ring of faces whose RhoA activator is held ON, giving a line-tension that closes the furrow. Reuse d_M/τ_M kinetics for ring assembly timing.
**Why it beats now:** DCM has no division mechanism; this gives one that is continuous with the cortex module rather than a bolted-on furrow force.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (to the surface-mesh / continuum level)

### E1 — RhoA pulse waveform (simulation input, paper §STAR / Fig S5)
```
RhoA(t) ∝ sin⁶(π·t / T_pulse),   T_pulse = 30 s
```
Map: drives the activator a_i(t) of each cortical pulse in M1. Per-face, offset by the stochastic nucleation time of that face's pulse. Sharp (sin⁶) periodic spikes, not sinusoidal — matches the "rapid assembly" phenotype.

### E2 — Myosin-activation delay/duration relative to the RhoA pulse (Fig 6 sim protocol)
```
myosin m_i(t) = ON  for  t_pulse + d_M ≤ t ≤ t_pulse + d_M + τ_M
d_M = 5 s   (delay of myosin after RhoA)
τ_M = 15 s  (duration of myosin activation)
```
Map: the DCM cortical-tension update. γ_i(t) = γ_0 + γ_myo·m_i(t)·(polarity/efficiency factor from M2). γ_0 is the pressure-borne baseline γ=ΔP·R/2.

### E3 — Cortical contraction onset lag (Fig 3M — a timing validation constraint)
Contraction starts **~3 s after pulse initiation** (~6 s after particle accumulation), and elongating (force-building) formins arrive **~3.3 s BEFORE** recruited/inactive formins ("first-come-first-served" inversion). At the DCM surface level this fixes the *rise time* of γ_i(t) after a pulse: γ should ramp with a ~3 s lead before peak cortical flow, not step instantaneously. Encode as a first-order rise on m_i(t) with τ_rise ≈ 3 s.

### E4 — Filament-length law (informs cortex correlation length, NOT mesh elasticity)
```
L_filament = V_elong / (1/τ_actin + 1/τ_formin)
```
with V_elong ≈ 1.1–1.3 µm/s, τ_actin from turnover 0.05–0.15 s⁻¹, formin off-rate 0.11 s⁻¹ → **⟨L⟩ ≈ 6 µm**. For DCM this is only a *sanity anchor*: the cortical mechanical correlation length (~µm) is comparable to a coarse mesh edge at R=7.5 µm, so a single face already averages over ~one filament length — i.e. a surface-continuum γ is a defensible coarse-grain here. Do NOT port the filament stiffnesses; port the *conclusion that µm-scale continuum tension is legitimate*.

### E5 — Barbed-end half-life (self-consistency, Discussion)
```
t½ = ln2 / (k_on · [F]),   k_on = 0.029 nM⁻¹s⁻¹, [F] ≈ 10 nM  ⇒  t½ ≈ 3 s
```
Consistent with E3's ~3 s onset. Used only to justify the ~3 s timescales above; not a DCM state variable.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce (as DCM scenarios)

- **P1 — Pulsed cortex on a single cell.** Take the native full-compartment MCF7-like cell (R≈7.5 µm), initialize at the physiological baseline (γ_0=ΔP·R/2, Π₀≈40 Pa, η≈65 Pa·s), then drive the cortex with the M1 pulse field: stochastic pulses at period 30 s, width 3–5 µm patches, myosin delay 5 s / duration 15 s. Observe transient local contractions and cortical flow. This is the direct DCM analogue of the paper's Fig 6 slab, wrapped onto our closed surface.
- **P2 — Polar vs. apolar pulse (Fig 6 inversion).** Run P1 twice: (a) each pulse raises tension isotropically (apolar); (b) each pulse raises an *outward-polar* (barbed-out) anisotropic tension per M2. Compare summed transmitted cortical tension and whether the cortex forms disconnected high-tension aggregates. Reproduces the paper's central architecture result at surface scale.
- **P3 — Cytokinesis (M3).** Pattern the RhoA activator as a persistent equatorial band; measure furrow ingression / ring closure driven purely by the elevated-γ band. Division scenario DCM does not currently have.
- **P4 — Cell-cycle γ modulation.** The paper measures formin speed dropping >10% during cytokinesis and recovering in interphase (Table S1). At DCM scale this is the interphase↔mitotic γ_0 switch (0.15↔1.5 mN/m) already in DCM — P4 just adds the *temporal* profile (contraction competence drops at cytokinesis).

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Organism/scale mismatch.** This is the *C. elegans* early embryo cortex (cell 50×30 µm, cell cycle ~10 min, embryonic pulsatile RhoA), NOT a living MCF7 tumor cell (R≈7.5 µm). Pulse geometry (3–5 µm wide, 5–10 µm apart, period 30 s) is embryo-specific. **Adapt, don't copy:** use the *mechanism* (excitable RhoA→delayed-myosin→transient-γ) and scale the pulse widths/spacing to R=7.5 µm; treat the 30 s period and 0.3 µm/s flow as order-of-magnitude targets, and surface to PI before adopting an MCF7-specific pulse period (which may differ — do not tune the period to make a gate pass).
- **In-embryo cortex, not the DCM physiological baseline.** μ=0.86 Pa·s is the surrounding medium, not cytoplasm; keep DCM's η≈65 Pa·s. The pulse field is *additive on top of* the γ_0=ΔP·R/2 baseline — it must NOT replace or zero the resting turgor/tension. Per the physiological-baseline HARD rule, run P1–P4 from the full `--from-resting`-equivalent baseline with pulses layered on, never from a floppy unpressurised shell.
- **Filament-level model ≠ surface mesh.** The Kim agent-based model (Fig 6) is FF's domain. Its stiffnesses, Bell's-law ACP off-rate, PCM motor, and Δt are the wrong scale for DCM and must not be ported (see §3 exclusion list). DCM takes only the *phenomenology* (timing law, flow speed, polar-efficiency result). This keeps DCM at the single-cell fine-grained-surface scale rather than importing a tissue/continuum shortcut OR a sub-mesh filament model.
- **Polarity tensor is a genuine extension, not a free lunch.** M2 (anisotropic γ_αβ) is a real new state on every face and must be derived (from pulse geometry), grid-invariant, and not tuned — flag to PI as a cortex-module contract change before production, and validate the scalar-efficiency reduced form first.
- **No fitting to data.** The v_flow=0.3 µm/s and 3 s lag are *validation targets written before the run*, not fit parameters. If P1 misses them, surface the discrepancy — do not retune d_M/τ_M/T_pulse to hit them.
