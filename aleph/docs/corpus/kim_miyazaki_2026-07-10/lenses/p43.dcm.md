# DCM lens — p43

**Paper:** Kim, Hwang, Lee, Kamm (2009) *Computational Analysis of Viscoelastic Properties of Crosslinked Actin Networks*, PLoS Comput Biol 5(7):e1000439. DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000439.

**Honest framing (read first).** This is a *fine-grained Brownian-dynamics filament model* — explicit actin cylinder chains + explicit two-armed crosslinkers (ACPs) + steric repulsion + Langevin thermal noise. It is the methodological ancestor of the **FF** engine, not DCM. DCM is a closed triangulated **surface-mesh continuum**; there is no direct particle/bond to port. What DCM *can* mine is (a) the **cortical material law** this model emits — magnitudes of G′/G″, the prestress-stiffening exponent, the threshold prestress — which are sanity targets for our cortex `γ`/`K_A`/surface-viscosity fields; (b) a **first-principles justification for DCM's deterministic overdamped mesh dynamics** (thermal fluctuation shown negligible under physiological crosslink spacing); and (c) an **oscillatory-shear rheology protocol + modulus-extraction equation** we can wrap as a DCM validation scenario. DCM relevance is moderate-low; treat every number below as a cross-check oracle, never a runtime input. Note the paper's k_s,A is deliberately **1/40 of the real actin value** for compute efficiency — do not KB-register 1.69e-2 N/m as physiological.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cortex prestress-stiffening (the strongest DCM lever) → cortical-tension module
**What the paper shows:** a prestrained crosslinked network becomes dramatically more elastic. At γ=0.55 the storage modulus G′ is **~100× higher** than at γ=0 and becomes **nearly frequency-independent** (phase delay → 0, i.e. behaves as an elastic solid). Above a threshold prestress **τ₀ ≈ 0.1 Pa**, G′ follows a power law **G′ ∝ τ₀^0.85** (≈1 in matched in-vitro rheometry, ref [14]). The authors attribute the in-vivo cell modulus **G′ ~ 10³ Pa** to being in a *prestressed state* set by the actomyosin contractile apparatus.
**DCM change:** DCM already initializes the cortex at a physiological prestress via `γ = ΔP·R/2` (osmoregulation) — this paper is the mechanistic *validation* that a prestressed cortex should be far stiffer (superlinearly) than a relaxed one, and that its effective shear response should become elastic (low loss) as prestress rises. Add a **prestress-stiffening acceptance test**: sweep baseline cortical tension `γ` (equivalently baseline turgor `Π₀`), measure the cell's effective shear/indentation modulus, and check it rises **super-linearly (exponent ~0.85–1)** above a threshold, with the loss angle collapsing toward 0.
**Why it beats current DCM:** DCM currently treats `γ` as a tension setpoint but has **no declared expectation that stiffness scales with it** — the effective modulus vs. prestress curve is untested. This paper gives the exponent, the threshold, and the qualitative frequency-independence, turning "cortex is pretensioned" into a *falsifiable stiffening law*. It also directly reinforces the physiological-baseline HARD rule: a relaxed (γ≈0) cortex is not merely "less tense," it is **~100× too soft** — running from an unpressurised shell is quantitatively, not just cosmetically, wrong.

### M2 — Deterministic overdamped mesh dynamics is *justified*, not a shortcut → integrator / node dynamics
**What the paper shows (Fig 7C, Fig 9):** thermal fluctuation (the Langevin F^B term) has **no significant effect on G′** whenever the crosslink spacing l_c ≪ persistence length l_p. At physiological actin (l_p ~10–20 µm) and l_c = 0.393 µm (R=0.021), removing thermal noise leaves G′ essentially unchanged at **all** prestrains. Thermal effects only appear when l_p is knocked down to ≲3 µm (comparable to l_c) *and* prestrain is low. Conclusion: "a crosslinked F-actin network behaves essentially as a **deterministic overdamped system in a viscous medium**."
**DCM change:** none required — this is a *positive result* for DCM. It licenses DCM's choice to run the surface mesh **deterministically (no per-node Langevin noise)** at the cortical/continuum scale, because the coarse-grained cortex sits deep in the l_c ≪ l_p regime. Record this as the physical justification for the DCM integrator being noise-free, with the caveat that a *soft, low-tension* cortex (effective l_p ↓) is where the assumption would break — another reason to hold the physiological prestress baseline.
**Why it matters:** removes an open worry ("should the DCM mesh have thermal noise like FF?") with a cited, quantitative answer.

### M3 — "Supportive framework": cortex stress is carried by a percolating subset → cortex/aggregate heterogeneity caveat
**What the paper shows (Fig 10):** under prestrain, ~**25% of crosslinkers** (connecting ~**28% of filaments**), selected by highest ACP bending force, carry ~**70% of the total stress**; the load-bearing subset deforms **affinely** while the rest is nearly stress-free. Load localizes onto a percolating scaffold, not the bulk.
**DCM implication (caveat, not a build):** a homogeneous continuum `γ` field assumes stress is smeared evenly over the surface — this paper says real cortical stress is **strongly localized** onto a percolating substructure. For single-cell DCM this is a fidelity caveat (the effective-modulus test in M1 should expect heterogeneous, near-affine deformation, not uniform strain). For the **aggregate compaction** open problem it is conceptually aligned with our existing **load-sharing cadherin clusters** (junctions bear load collectively, a few carry most) — same "few elements carry the load" physics one level up. It argues *against* modeling compaction as a uniform aggregate-σ pull (consistent with the memory note that aggregate-σ is a mechanical end-state, not the real rate mechanism).

### M4 — Loss modulus G″ / phase delay → membrane surface-viscosity calibration
**What the paper shows:** the network is genuinely viscoelastic; G″ and the phase delay φ = tan⁻¹(G″/G′) are measured across frequency (Eq 10). Adding crosslinker lowers φ (more elastic); G″ shows a low-frequency uptick at high prestrain.
**DCM change:** DCM carries a **membrane surface viscosity**; this paper's G″ magnitudes and G″/G′ ratios (loss angle) are the observable to calibrate/validate that viscosity against, once M1's oscillatory-shear scenario exists. Currently DCM has no dissipation-side validation target.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (to the surface-mesh / scenario level)

**Eq 10 — modulus extraction from an oscillatory-shear DCM scenario (directly usable):**
```
|G*(f_s)| = |τ_zx| / |γ_x|
G′(f_s) = |G*| cos φ
G″(f_s) = |G*| sin φ
```
Map to DCM: impose a small sinusoidal shear (or indentation) on a confined/plated cell, sum the **reaction forces on the driven mesh nodes** → divide by contact-plane area → stress amplitude |τ_zx|; |γ_x| is the imposed strain amplitude; φ is the stress–strain phase lag extracted from the DCM force/displacement time series. Yields G′, G″ for a DCM cell as a whole-cell rheology observable. φ=0 ⇒ elastic, φ=π/2 ⇒ viscous.

**Eq 9 — medium/cytoplasm viscous stress add-on:**
```
τ_zx = η · dγ_x/dt
```
Map to DCM: the paper adds the surrounding-medium viscous stress on top of network stress. In DCM the analogous term is the **cytoplasm/medium drag** — η here is medium viscosity (~water); in DCM the interior is **η ≈ 65 Pa·s** and the exterior medium is separate. Use the *form* (viscous stress ∝ strain-rate) when assembling the total shear stress in an M1 rheology run; do **not** import η's water value (physiological-baseline rule → use the DCM cytoplasm η).

**Prestress-stiffening law (target relation, not a force law):**
```
G′ ∝ τ₀^0.85          (τ₀ > ~0.1 Pa; ≈ frequency-independent at high prestress)
```
Map to DCM: τ₀ ↔ baseline cortical prestress (from `γ = ΔP·R/2`); G′ ↔ DCM effective shear/indentation modulus. This is the quantitative acceptance curve for M1.

**Eq 11 — Mason segment-tracking microrheology (port with caution / mostly N/A):**
```
|G*(ω)| ≈ k_BT / [ π r_b ⟨Δr²(1/ω)⟩ Γ(1+χ(ω)) ] ,  χ(ω)=d ln⟨Δr²⟩/d ln t |_{t=1/ω}
```
DCM runs deterministically (M2), so there is **no thermal MSD** to feed this — it is inapplicable to the noise-free mesh. Flag only as the method FF would use; for DCM prefer the driven Eq-10 protocol.

*(Eqs 1–8 — Langevin, harmonic stretch/bend, steric repulsion, ACP arm geometry — are filament-level FF physics with no surface-mesh analogue; not ported to DCM.)*

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce as DCM scenarios

**P1 — Whole-cell oscillatory-shear rheology (the portable protocol).** Plate/confine a DCM cell, fix the basal nodes, drive the apical nodes with a small sinusoidal shear strain (paper: 5% superposed, ≤2% linear), sum reaction forces on driven nodes / contact area → stress, extract G′, G″ via Eq 10 across a frequency sweep. Run it at **several baseline cortical tensions** (M1) to trace the prestress-stiffening curve. This is a clean, new DCM validation scenario mirroring a bulk rheometer.

**P2 — Prestress ramp then differential modulus.** Paper's exact recipe: translate the top boundary at constant strain rate to a target prestrain γ ∈ {0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.55}, *then* superpose the small oscillation to get **differential** moduli (as in Gardel 2006 [14]). DCM analogue: pre-tension the cortex to a series of setpoints, then apply the P1 oscillation — reproduces the γ=0.55 → ~100× / frequency-independent result.

**P3 — Boundary handling to copy.** Fix all nodes within a thin basal layer (paper: bottom 70 nm), drive nodes within a thin apical layer, deactivate periodicity on the driven faces. For DCM this maps to the plated-cell contact BC already used, plus a rigid apical shear platen.

*(The network-generation, gelsolin length-capping, and 40 mm parallel-plate in-vitro rig are FF/experimental, not DCM scenarios.)*

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

1. **Prestress-stiffening curve:** DCM effective modulus G′ vs baseline cortical prestress τ₀ should be **flat below ~0.1 Pa**, then rise as **G′ ∝ τ₀^0.85** (accept 0.7–1.0). *(Fig 5B, 6E.)*
2. **Stiffening magnitude:** relaxed→physiologically-pretensioned cortex should stiffen **~100×**, with the loss angle φ → 0 (elastic, frequency-independent) at high prestress. *(Fig 5A.)*
3. **Frequency slope:** a well-crosslinked/prestressed DCM cortex's G′(f) log-log slope should sit in the **cell-like 0.15–0.3** band, not 0.75 (which is the un-crosslinked thermal-undulation signature — irrelevant to the continuum). *(Fig 3A.)*
4. **Absolute magnitude:** in-vivo cell G′ **~10³ Pa** as the order-of-magnitude a prestressed DCM cell should reach; passive/relaxed network ~O(1–10 Pa). *(text, Fig 9.)*
5. **Loss modulus:** G″/G′ (phase delay) as the target for DCM membrane surface-viscosity calibration; passive G″ ~O(1–3 Pa) at 10 Hz. *(Fig 3B, methods.)*
6. **Yielding sanity (from the caveat):** DCM cortex stress should **not grow unbounded** — real crosslinked networks drop stress beyond ~1–30 Pa; a DCM cortex that stiffens without any yielding/remodeling reproduces the paper's permanent-crosslink artifact and should be flagged.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Scale/representation mismatch (primary):** this is a µm³ box of explicit filaments; DCM is a whole-cell surface continuum. Nothing ports as a force — only as *material-law targets and a protocol*. Don't overclaim a "mechanism adoption"; M1/M4 are validation-driven calibrations of existing DCM fields.
- **k_s,A = 1/40 real, poison number:** the paper deliberately softens actin stretch stiffness 40× for compute. Any stiffness/modulus magnitude inherits this. The **exponents and thresholds** (0.85, 0.1 Pa, 0.15–0.3 slope) are robust and portable; the **absolute Pa values** (5 Pa at γ=0, etc.) are model-specific — use in-vitro/in-vivo anchors (10³ Pa cell, 1–30 Pa yield) for magnitude targets instead.
- **Permanent crosslinks → over-stiff, artificially high stress (no yielding).** The paper itself flags that no ACP unbinding makes stress "catastrophically" too high (>100 Pa vs 1–30 Pa real). DCM must **not** copy this: our cortex should yield/remodel (this actually argues for DCM's remesh + cadherin T1-rearrangement escalation as the physiological release valve). Treat the >100 Pa branch as what *not* to reproduce.
- **Passive, no myosin:** purified actin+crosslinker, no motors, no active tension generation. It cannot inform the **cortical-tension γ-generation** open problem (that needs engaged-NMII physics, which this paper lacks). It only informs how an *already-prestressed* cortex behaves — consistent with our known result that γ-generation is a force-magnitude/density question this paper does not touch.
- **Water viscosity, not cytoplasm:** Eq 9's η is water-like and HI is neglected (φ_actin ~0.1%). DCM must keep its physiological **η=65 Pa·s** interior; import only the *form* of the viscous-stress term, never the value.
- **In-vitro reconstituted gel, filamin/gelsolin — not MCF7:** cell type and crosslinker set differ. The transferable claims are the *generic prestress-stiffening scaling and thermal-fluctuation-negligibility*, which the authors argue are network-generic; the specific ACPᴮ/ACPᶜ magnitudes are not MCF7-anchored and must not be KB-registered as such.
- **Thermal-fluctuation-negligible has a floor:** valid only while the effective cortex l_p ≫ l_c. A pathologically soft / low-tension DCM cortex would leave that regime — another reason the physiological prestress baseline is load-bearing for the deterministic-dynamics justification (M2).
