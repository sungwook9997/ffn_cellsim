# DCM lens — p35

**Paper:** Li, Biel, Lomada, Yu, Kim — "Buckling-induced F-actin fragmentation modulates the contraction of active cytoskeletal networks," *Soft Matter* 2017, 13, 3213–3220 (DOI 10.1039/c6sm02703b).

**Bottom line for DCM (relevance ≈ 2/5, indirect-but-usable).** This is a Taeyoon-Kim agent-based Brownian-dynamics model of a *cortex-like thin actomyosin network* (explicit F-actin beams + ACP cross-linkers + myosin thick filaments). It is the FF engine's native physics, NOT a DCM surface-mesh/continuum object — it has no membrane, no turgor/osmotic pressure, no nucleus, no bending shell, no closed volume. **Nothing here ports as a new DCM force law verbatim.** What it *does* give DCM is (a) a clean, directly-reusable contraction/compaction observable (Eq. 2), (b) a mechanistic, sub-grid picture of *why* cortical tension γ and aggregate compaction behave non-monotonically — which is decision-relevant to two named DCM open problems (γ-generation; loose→compact aggregate RATE), and (c) a "single-focus vs multiple-foci" end-state classifier that maps onto DCM aggregate-σ compaction outcomes. Treat it as a **provenance/sanity source for DCM's lumped γ and σ**, and as a **cross-engine (FF→DCM) coarse-graining anchor**, not as a mechanism to graft.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

Ranked by genuine DCM applicability. Most are "informs / sanity-checks a lumped parameter," not "add a new fine-grained force" — because DCM deliberately lumps the actin network into γ and σ.

1. **Aggregate compaction end-state classifier: single-focus vs multiple-foci (Fig. 6A).**
   DCM module: aggregate surface tension σ compaction + `remesh`/T1 (`dcm/` contact/junction). The paper's central result is that a contracting active network settles into exactly three end states: (i) single focus, large contraction; (ii) single focus, negligible contraction (no buckling); (iii) *multiple foci*, small contraction (over-severing → connectivity loss). **Why it beats DCM-now:** DCM currently treats aggregate compaction as monotone in σ (bigger σ → more compact), but the σ-sweep already found a NON-MONOTONIC sweet spot at σ≈5 mN/m ([[project-dcm-ipc-large-dt]], memory). This paper supplies the *mechanistic reason* a monotone-σ picture is wrong: past a threshold, the contractile machinery *fragments* the connectivity that transmits the tension, so more drive → *less* net compaction and *more* separate foci. For a DCM aggregate of N≈400, "multiple foci" = the aggregate splitting into several compaction centers instead of one — a directly observable, gate-able failure mode of over-driving σ. **Adopt as:** a sanity gate on the aggregate-σ compaction run ("a physiological σ must give ONE focus, not fragment the aggregate"), not a new force.

2. **γ / σ generation is set by a motor–crosslink RATIO, not motor drive alone (Fig. 2C, Fig. 6B).**
   DCM module: cortical tension γ generation (open problem); aggregate σ. The paper's phase boundary is `critical κ_b,A ∝ R_ACP/R_M` and "large contraction requires R_M ≥ R_M^min AND R_ACP^min ≤ R_ACP ≤ R_ACP^max." **Why it matters to DCM:** DCM's γ-floor open problem ([[project-gamma-floor-likely-deficit.md]]) has been chasing a *force-magnitude* (motor density) lever. This paper says the *emergent* contractile tension of a cortex-like layer is a two-parameter object — you need enough motors AND an intermediate cross-linker density; too much crosslinking kills buckling and *reduces* tension. If DCM ever replaces its scalar γ with a sub-grid 2-parameter law (motor ratio × crosslink ratio) this is the canonical phase map. **Adopt as:** documentation/guardrail for any future γ-generation sub-model — γ is non-monotone in crosslink density; do not model it as monotone. (Do NOT wire a filament network into DCM to get this — that's FF's job; DCM keeps γ lumped.)

3. **Contraction quantified as fractional foci-area reduction (Eq. 2) — reuse as DCM's compaction observable.**
   DCM module: compaction metric in the aggregate driver + viz. See §2. **Why it beats DCM-now:** DCM has been criticized for the A/A0 tautology ([[project-dcm-fidelity-remediation]]) and for porosity as the compaction metric. Eq. 2's ξ = 1 − ΣπR_i(t)²/ΣπR_i(0)² is a *foci-radius-based* contraction extent that (i) is defined even when the aggregate fragments (sum over N foci), and (ii) gives an unambiguous [0,1] scale. It is a cleaner cross-engine-comparable compaction observable than porosity for the "did the N=400 aggregate compact to one center" question.

4. **Intermediate-stiffness optimum as a cautionary analogue for cortex bending κ_m.**
   DCM module: membrane/cortex bending κ_m. The result "largest contraction at *intermediate* bending rigidity" is a filament-level (κ_b,A, persistence length) statement and does NOT transfer to the DCM shell's κ_m directly (different object: beam L_p vs surface bending modulus). **Why flagged anyway:** it's a warning that "stiffer cortex" is not monotone-good for compaction — over-stiff resists compaction, over-soft loses structural integrity. This echoes the HARD rule finding that coarse (too-few-filament) cortex "collapses" from lost structural stability. Keep as conceptual caveat only; no κ_m change is licensed by this paper.

Explicitly NOT adopted for DCM: the curvature-dependent severing law (Eq. 1) — it is a per-filament fragmentation rate with no surface-mesh analogue; it belongs to FF. Grafting a "severing" onto DCM triangles would be an invented, non-physiological mechanism (a magic mechanism), forbidden.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

**Eq. 2 (network contraction extent) — PORT as DCM compaction observable.**

Paper form:
```
ξ(t) = 1 − [ Σ_i^N π R_i(t)² ] / [ Σ_i^N π R_i(0)² ]
```
Symbol → DCM state mapping (for an N≈400 aggregate):
- `N` = number of detected compaction foci (clusters of cell centroids), by the same density-threshold-then-merge auto-detection the paper describes (§Quantification). In DCM, run over **cell-mesh centroids** (one per cell), not actin endpoints.
- `R_i(t)` = mean distance from focus-i center to its member cell centroids at time t. Focus center = mean of member cell (x,y[,z]) positions.
- `ξ ∈ [0,1]`: 0 = no compaction, 1 = full compaction to point foci.
- Extend to 3D (DCM is 3D, this paper is quasi-2D): replace πR_i² area with (4/3)πR_i³ volume, or keep the projected-area form for a top-down silhouette to stay consistent with the DCM A/A0 = top-down-xy rule ([[feedback-aa0-topdown-area]]). **Recommend the top-down projected-area form** so it's directly comparable to existing DCM silhouette metrics and to this paper.

**Eq. 1 (severing rate) — do NOT port to DCM.** Transcribed for completeness:
```
k_s,A = k⁰_s,A · exp( θ_s,A / λ_s,A )
```
θ_s,A = sum of two adjacent bending angles at a point on an F-actin; k⁰_s,A = zero-angle severing rate constant; λ_s,A = angle sensitivity. This is a per-filament, angle-activated bond-breaking rate. There is no DCM node/face state it maps to (DCM faces don't "sever"; a torn membrane is a different, bleb-regime physics). Keep it in the FF lens only.

**Steady-state / rate definitions — reuse the protocol, not new physics:**
- `ξ̇(t) = dξ/dt`; steady state = first t where `ξ̇(t) < 0.1·ξ̇(0)`; `t_ss` = that time; **average compaction rate = ξ_ss / t_ss**. Port this exact steady-state criterion to the DCM aggregate driver so DCM's compaction-RATE (a named open problem) is measured with a literature-standard, non-arbitrary steady-state definition instead of a hand-picked stop time.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

1. **Circular cortex-like contraction footprint (Fig. 1B), R = 8 µm.** As a DCM scenario: a single cell's cortex layer initialized flat/circular then contracting to a focus is qualitatively the DCM "cortex compaction" demo at nearly the same radius (7.5 vs 8 µm). Reproducible as a DCM single-surface tension-driven rounding run; compare the t = 0/100/200 s snapshots (Fig. 1B) to a DCM cortex-γ relaxation trajectory for face-validation (are the timescales and the "contract to one center" behavior consistent?).

2. **Two-stage protocol: ASSEMBLE (no drive) → then DRIVE (turn motors on).** DCM analogue: build + relax the full-compartment cell/aggregate at physiological baseline FIRST (`--from-resting`), THEN switch on the active tension σ/γ. This matches the DCM HARD rule to start from the relaxed physiological baseline and perturb FROM it, and the paper's clean assemble-then-contract separation is a good template for the aggregate-σ compaction run's staging.

3. **Foci auto-detection pipeline (§Quantification, Fig. S1):** density map of member positions → coarsen to a 3D histogram → threshold for focus centers → merge adjacent → back-trace members to t=0 → per-focus R_i(t). Reproduce this exact pipeline in the DCM aggregate analysis on cell centroids to get N(t) and ξ(t). This gives DCM a principled "how many compaction centers did the aggregate form" measurement (the single-vs-multiple-foci classifier of §1.1).

4. **Steady-state stopping rule** (ξ̇ < 0.1·ξ̇₀) as the DCM aggregate run's termination/measurement criterion (§2).

Not reproducible / not applicable: aspiration, AFM, confinement, durotaxis, division — this paper has none of them.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

These are cross-engine (network→continuum) sanity targets. They validate that DCM's lumped compaction reproduces the *qualitative structure* of an explicit-network contraction. They are NOT MCF7-specific numbers and must not be registered as MCF7 KB gates — they are FF-lineage active-gel targets used here as coarse-graining anchors.

| DCM observable | target from p35 | condition | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| compaction extent ξ_ss (single focus) | > 0.50 ("large contraction") reachable; strong case → ~1 by ~200 s | sufficient drive, intermediate crosslink | Fig. 1B, 2B/C |
| number of foci N | 1 (single focus) in the physiological/adhesive regime; grows to ~10–11 only under over-drive | vary drive | Fig. 3C, 4C, 5C |
| compaction is NON-MONOTONE in "stiffness/drive" | max compaction at *intermediate*, not extreme, rigidity | with fragmentation active | Fig. 3D, 4D, 5D — corroborates DCM σ-sweep sweet spot σ≈5 mN/m |
| mechanical compaction timescale | steady state in ~100–500 s | active contraction | Fig. 1B, 2B — corroborates DCM ~100 s mechanical limit, NOT 24–48 h |
| over-drive → fragmentation | too much motor / too little crosslink → multiple foci + LOWER net ξ | low R_ACP/R_M | Fig. 5D, 6 — DCM gate: physiological σ must give ONE focus |
| phase boundary shape | critical rigidity ∝ (crosslink/motor ratio), independent of severing constant | — | Fig. 6B |

**Decisive DCM cross-check:** run the DCM aggregate-σ compaction sweep, compute ξ(t) via Eq. 2 on cell centroids, and confirm (a) a single focus at physiological σ, (b) ξ_ss > 0.5 achievable, (c) fragmentation into multiple foci if σ is pushed past its sweet spot. If DCM shows monotone compaction with no fragmentation ceiling, that's a flag that DCM's lumped σ is missing the connectivity-loss physics this paper isolates — surface to PI as a scope question (does DCM need a σ-saturation term?), do NOT tune σ to force the match.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

1. **Regime mismatch — purified reconstituted gel vs living MCF7 cell.** This is an in-silico reconstituted actin/myosin/ACP network (validated against Bendix 2008 actin + skeletal myosin II + α-actinin gels), NOT a living cancer cell. It has no membrane, no osmotic turgor, no nucleus, no cadherin adhesion, no ECM. Anything imported must respect the DCM physiological-baseline rule: DCM keeps its full compartment stack ON; p35's numbers are only *qualitative structure* (non-monotonicity, foci count, ~100 s timescale), never a replacement for MCF7 setpoints (η=65 Pa·s, Π₀=40 Pa, γ=0.15/1.5 mN/m, σ=5 mN/m).

2. **2D thin slab (0.1 µm) vs DCM 3D closed shell.** Contraction-to-foci in a 20×20×0.1 µm sheet is a 2D areal collapse; DCM compaction is 3D volumetric with an enclosed-volume constraint and turgor resisting collapse. Eq. 2 must be adapted (top-down projected area, per §2) and the "compact to a point" limit is unreachable in DCM because turgor/volume conservation floors it — so ξ_ss ≈ 1 is a 2D artifact, not a DCM target. Use ξ for *trend* and *foci count*, cap expectations on absolute magnitude.

3. **Motors have NO volume exclusion in this model (stated, p.3)** → artificially over-compact "very tight" motor aggregates. This inflates ξ_ss toward 1. DCM's node-face penalty + IPC *does* enforce excluded volume, so DCM should NOT reproduce ξ→1; a DCM aggregate hitting ξ→1 would indicate a contact-penalty failure, not success. Treat the paper's ξ≈1 as an upper bound the excluded-volume DCM must stay *below*.

4. **The headline mechanism (buckling→severing) is sub-grid for DCM and must stay in FF.** Grafting a severing/fragmentation rate onto DCM triangles would invent a non-physiological surface mechanism (forbidden: no magic mechanisms, fine-grained belongs to FF). The DCM-legitimate use is only the *consequence* (connectivity-loss → multiple foci → lower compaction) as an end-state classifier, with the mechanism resolved in FF.

5. **Parameters are molar ratios + ESI-only dimensional constants.** R_M, R_ACP, k⁰_s,A, λ_s,A, κ_b,A carry no DCM config field, and the dimensional spring/motor/severing constants are in the ESI (not in the provided text). Do not attempt to back out a DCM force constant from them — provenance is incomplete and the object is wrong. For a real DCM γ-generation sub-model, ingest the ESI + refs 14/15/16 through the FF lens first, then coarse-grain.

6. **"Optimal intermediate rigidity" does NOT license changing DCM κ_m.** It is a filament persistence-length statement (L_p 0.58–5 µm), not a surface bending-modulus statement. Keep κ_m at its MCF7-sourced value; use the non-monotone insight only as a caveat against "stiffer cortex is always better."
