# FF lens — p16

**Paper:** Yim, Patterson, Suter, Miller, Kim (2025), *Computational Modeling of Motor-Driven Extension of Microtubule Bundles in Axons*, bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.09.25.678704. Agent-based Brownian dynamics of a microtubule bundle (deformable cylindrical-element MTs + transient Tau-like cross-linkers + dynein-like motors) confined in a cylinder, extending a moving boundary against a resisting force.

**Framing for FF.** This is *not* an actin-cortex paper — it is MTs, dynein, and an axon. Its value to FF is that it is the **same modeling class and lineage as FF**: the Taeyoon-Kim agent-based cylindrical-element BD framework (Jung, Murrell & Kim 2015, ref 46), which is the sibling of FF's Cytosim-style fine-grained engine. It executes, at production scale, exactly the physics FF struggles with in its open problems — **filament buckling under motor compression, buckling threshold set by cross-linker spacing, and motor↔cross-linker friction competition** — and reports concrete emergent numbers. So it is primarily a **mechanism cross-check + validation-target + observable-library** source, and secondarily a direct porting source for two concrete pieces (segment-drag law, per-node curvature diagnostic). Because MT-specific parameters (l_p ≈ 1 mm, r₀ = 280 nm) do NOT transfer to the actin cortex, and because the native FF cell already carries a microtubule compartment (`--microtubules`), the one *directly biological* transfer is: if FF ever activates MT-MT dynein sliding, this is the force scheme to use.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Buckling threshold tied to cross-linker spacing (filament + crosslinker modules; HIGH value)
The paper's central mechanistic result: an MT's effective buckling unit is **not** the whole filament but the **unsupported segment between adjacent cross-linking points**, `L_uns`. Euler critical load `F_cr ∝ κ_b / L_uns²` (Eq. 10). More cross-linkers → shorter `L_uns` → higher `F_cr` → fewer buckling events. This is an *emergent, magic-number-free* buckling threshold that falls straight out of cross-linker geometry.
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF has explicit bending κ and explicit cross-linker bonds, so `F_cr ∝ κ_b/L_uns²` should already emerge — but FF has never *measured* or *validated* it. FF's open problems list "buckling/condensation symmetry-breaking" and "network contraction & stress relaxation" as unsolved. This paper hands FF the exact predictive relation to instrument the native actomyosin cortex: under myosin compression, the cortical actin buckling rate should track the α-actinin/filamin cross-link spacing as `κ_b/L_uns²`. Adopt as a **mechanism assertion to verify in the native cortex**, not a new force law (the force law — bending + EV — FF already has).

### M2 — Biphasic network response in cross-linker density (crosslinker module; HIGH value)
Bundle extension is biphasic in cross-linking linear density `R_X`, maximal at `R_X = 0.25 µm⁻¹`: too few links → filaments buckle (low `F_cr`); too many links → filament mobility is friction-throttled (motors feel higher load, walk slower). Two opposing factors — *buckling resistance* (↑ with R_X) vs *filament mobility/friction* (↓ with R_X) — give an interior optimum.
- **Why it matters to FF:** the same competition governs **actomyosin contractility** (contraction is non-monotone in α-actinin density — percolation/rigidity-percolation window). FF's contraction & stress-relaxation open problem needs exactly this: a myosin-driven cortex should show an emergent optimal cross-link density for contraction/tension generation. Adopt as a **cross-linker-density sweep protocol** (§4) whose non-monotone shape is the acceptance signature — no fitting, the optimum is emergent.

### M3 — Multi-motor cooperativity exceeds single-motor stall to buckle a filament (myosin module; MEDIUM value)
Fig. S3: a single motor cannot buckle a cross-linked filament — the compressive load to buckle exceeds one motor's stall force, so **multiple motors must act cooperatively** on the same filament. Buckling is thus a collective, load-sharing event.
- **Why it matters to FF:** FF's Stam–Hocky bipolar minifilament already has multiple heads, and the DCM-side memory notes cadherin/junction load-sharing. This confirms FF must NOT treat buckling as a single-motor threshold — the native cortex buckles only where enough myosin heads co-load a segment. Use as a **sanity check** on FF's emergent buckling: buckled segments should correlate with local multi-head myosin occupancy, not single stalls.

### M4 — (conditional) Dynein-MT active sliding for the native microtubule compartment (motor + MT modules; MEDIUM, conditional)
The native FF cell runs `--microtubules` (part of the mandated full-compartment baseline). Currently MTs are (as far as this lens can tell) passive bending beams. This paper supplies the **complete mechanistic recipe for making them active**: a dynein motor with cargo end anchored to one MT's plus end and a walking end that binds a neighbor and walks toward its minus end with force-dependent walk/unbind rates. That is the physically correct way (mechanistic, not lumped) to add MT-MT sliding/transport if PI ever wants it.
- **Why it beats a lumped alternative:** it keeps FF's hard rule (explicit motor head, force-dependent kinetics) rather than a bulk MT-flow term. **Conditional** — only if PI scopes active MTs; flag, don't build unprompted.

### M5 — Per-node discrete curvature as a buckling observable (diagnostic, not a force; MEDIUM value)
Eq. 9 defines a per-node discrete curvature; nodes above **0.2 rad/µm** (Memet 2018) are counted "curved/buckled." This gives a clean, threshold-based, per-filament buckling detector.
- **Why FF wants it:** FF currently has no standard scalar to say "this filament buckled." Add this as a shared observable in `common/filament_math` so the cortex, filopodium, and MT compartments all report a curved-fraction time series — directly supporting the visualize-at-closeout rule and the buckling open problem.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### L1 — Cylindrical-segment drag coefficient (Eq. 3) — DIRECT PORT candidate
```
ξ_i = (3 π μ r_{c,i} / 5) · (3 + 2 r_{0,i} / r_{c,i})
```
- `μ` = medium (cytoplasm) viscosity, Pa·s → FF: **65 Pa·s MCF7 cytoplasm** (physiological baseline, not water).
- `r_{c,i}` = segment diameter → FF actin bead-rod: ~7 nm (actin) / per-segment excluded-volume radius.
- `r_{0,i}` = segment equilibrium (rest) length → FF filament segment length.
- **Maps to FF state:** replaces / audits FF's current per-segment drag `γ_i`. This is the anisotropic-aware cylinder drag (Clift–Grace–Weber, ref 44), more physical than a per-bead Stokes sphere `6πμa`.
- **Why load-bearing for FF:** FF's crawl-speed open problem is **grid-drag `Σγ ∝ Nc`** (the 500× slowdown, `--bulk-drag` destabilizes the solver). The drag law FF uses directly sets that sum. Auditing FF's segment drag against Eq. 3 (aspect-ratio-correct) is a concrete lever on the drag budget — a shorter/thinner segment gives a different, defensible ξ than a naive sphere, which changes the total without magic numbers. **Verify dimensional consistency in FF's µm/pN/s units before adopting.**

### L2 — Bell slip-bond cross-linker unbinding (Eq. 8) — CROSS-CHECK (already in FF)
```
k_{u,X} = k⁰_{u,X} · exp( F_s · x_{u,X} / (k_B T) )
```
- `F_s` = spring force on the cross-linker element (pN); `x_{u,X}` = force sensitivity / bond compliance (nm); `k⁰_{u,X}` = zero-force off-rate (s⁻¹).
- **Maps to FF:** this **is** FF's Bell-Evans crosslinker off-rate (`k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b)`, `F_b = k_BT/x_u`). Fig. 1E calibration: `k_u,X ≈ 1 s⁻¹` at F=0 rising to `~4.5 s⁻¹` at 60 pN. This is a **slip bond** (monotone), whereas FF's α-actinin datum (Ferrer 2008) and cadherin (Rakshit KU-4.2) include **catch** character. So: use only as a *shape cross-check* for FF's Tau-like / passive cross-linkers, NOT to overwrite the catch-bond parameters. Do **not** KB-register the numeric `k⁰_{u,X}`/`x_{u,X}` — the paper leaves both **unspecified** in text (only the Fig. 1E curve is given).

### L3 — Dynein force-velocity + force-dependent detachment (Monzon 2018, Fig. 1F) — PORT only if M4 is scoped
- Walking speed: sigmoidal in load `F_M` (F_M positive = pulled toward plus end); ≈ 0 for large hindering load, plateau **~1.1 µm/s** near `F_M ≈ 0–3 pN`; step size **~8 nm**, walk rate `k_{w,M} = v/8nm`.
- Detachment: `k_{u,M} ≈ 0` until `F_M ≳ 0`, rising to **~1 s⁻¹** under load (load promotes walking-end release).
- **Maps to FF:** structurally identical to FF's Hill motor (velocity vs load) + Bell detachment, but with dynein numbers. Only relevant to an MT-dynein module (M4). For actin/myosin, keep Hill (1938) + FF's existing NMII datum — do NOT substitute these dynein curves.

### L4 — Euler critical load & persistence-length conversions (Eqs. 10, 11) — DERIVED CHECK
```
F_cr ∝ κ_{b,MT} / L_uns²        (Eq. 10)
l_p  = κ_{b} / (k_B T)           (Eq. 11)
```
- FF actin: `l_p ≈ 17 µm` → `κ_b = l_p·k_BT ≈ 17e-6 m · 4.1e-21 J ≈ 7.0e-26 N·m²`. Use Eq. 11 to confirm FF's actin bending κ is consistent with the 17 µm persistence length (a magic-number-block derivation check, not a new value). Eq. 10 is the emergent buckling prediction of M1.

## 3. PARAMETERS / RATE CONSTANTS to set

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenarios)

### P1 — Cross-linker-density sweep for buckling/contraction (the reusable protocol)
Fixed motor density, sweep cross-linker linear density over ~3 decades (paper: `R_X = 0.01 → 10 µm⁻¹`), hold everything else at physiological baseline, measure the network output (extension there; **cortical tension / contraction rate** in FF). Acceptance = **emergent non-monotone (biphasic) curve with an interior optimum** — the shape, not a fitted value, is the signature. Maps FF's crosslinker module (α-actinin/filamin) directly. This is the single most portable protocol and directly attacks FF's contraction/γ open problems.

### P2 — Persistence-length sweep (buckling sensitivity)
Sweep filament bending stiffness (paper: `l_p = 1/3/10 mm` for MTs) at fixed cross-link density; measure curved-fraction and compressive/bending energy ratio. Prediction to reproduce: stiffer filaments buckle less and **abolish the low-density arm of the biphasic curve**. For FF actin this is a *bounded* sweep around `l_p = 17 µm` (don't use MT values) testing whether cortical buckling is bending-limited.

### P3 — Confined-bundle geometry with side support (`w_dom`)
Cylinder radius 2 µm, length 20 µm, effective bundle thickness `w_dom` (0.4–0.8 µm); a *narrower* domain gives lateral support that suppresses buckling (ε_end ↑ as w_dom ↓). FF analogue: **membrane containment / confinement suppresses cortical buckling** — a testable coupling between FF's membrane-containment module and the cortex buckling rate. Reuse as a confinement-vs-buckling scenario.

### P4 — Energy bookkeeping split (tensile vs compressive vs bending)
Track extensional energy **split by sign of (r − r₀)** into tensile vs compressive, plus bending energy, per species, as a stacked time series. Buckling shows as compressive-energy → bending-energy transfer within ~20 s. Adopt this decomposition as an FF diagnostic to *see* where the cortex stores load — cheap, and it made the buckling mechanism legible in the paper.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for FF observables

These are cross-check numbers for an FF **motor + crosslinker + buckling** test at our fine-grained scale (treat as qualitative/shape targets — preprint, MT system):

- **Biphasic extension vs cross-link density:** interior optimum at `R_X ≈ 0.25 µm⁻¹`; `ε_max ≈ 0.08–0.1` at peak; both arms decline. (Fig. 4A) — *shape* target for FF's P1 contraction sweep.
- **Buckling threshold scaling:** `F_cr ∝ κ_b / L_uns²` (Eq. 10) — FF should reproduce the inverse-square dependence of buckling rate on cross-link spacing.
- **Curved-fraction dynamics:** plateaus at **~0.2** after ~20 s; spatially peaks near the compression front (up to ~0.6 at z/L ≈ 0.95). (Figs. 2D, S1) — target for FF's M5 curved-fraction observable.
- **Compressive→bending energy transfer** within the **first ~20 s** of loading. (Fig. 2C)
- **Motor kinetics under network load:** walking speed drops from **~0.2 → ~0.05 µm/s** and motor force rises **~5 → ~7 pN** as cross-link density increases (friction loading). (Figs. 4F, G) — the motor-slows-under-network-friction signature FF's Hill motor should also show.
- **Dynein force-velocity (if M4):** v_max ~1.1 µm/s, ~0 under large hindering load; detachment k_u ~1 s⁻¹ under load. (Fig. 1F)
- **Slip-bond cross-linker curve:** `k_u` 1 → 4.5 s⁻¹ over 0–60 pN. (Fig. 1E)
- **Stiffness effect:** curved fraction ~0.25 (l_p=1mm) → ~0 (l_p=10mm); compressive/bending ratio ~0.2 → ~1.3. (Fig. 5B, C).

## 6. RISKS / caveats

1. **Wrong polymer.** MTs (l_p ≈ 1 mm, 25 nm, 280 nm segments) are ~60× stiffer than actin (l_p ≈ 17 µm). **Do not port any MT stiffness/length/density into actin configs.** Only the *mechanism shapes* (buckling↔spacing, biphasic density, drag form, curvature observable) transfer; the numbers do not.
2. **Wrong motor.** Dynein (MT minus-end walker, Monzon 2018) ≠ myosin II (actin barbed-end walker, Hill 1938). Keep FF's Hill + NMII datum for the cortex; use L3/M4 only inside a scoped MT-dynein module.
3. **Preprint + unspecified constants.** Not peer-reviewed; `κ_s`, `κ_b` magnitudes, `k⁰_{u,X}`, `x_{u,X}`, `F_R`, `μ` are not in the text. **No KB registration of a sourced numeric constant from this paper.** Register at most the *mechanism assertions* and the curvature threshold (Memet 2018, a cited primary source), flagged preprint/qualitative.
4. **In-vitro-reconstituted-style bundle vs living cortex.** Uniform MT polarity, no actomyosin cortex antagonist, no turnover/disassembly (formation frozen at 1 s), no membrane osmotic turgor. FF must run these mechanism checks **from the full physiological baseline** (`--from-resting`, native 38000-filament cortex, µ = 65 Pa·s, turgor ON, membrane + nucleus + MTs present) — never in the paper's stripped bundle geometry. The paper's confined-cylinder scenario (P3) is a *dev-only smoke test*; any conclusion must be reconfirmed on the native full cell.
5. **Volume exclusion is coarser than FF's.** Paper uses a harmonic overlap penalty (Eq. 7); FF uses repulsive LJ (finer, mechanistic). **Do not** adopt the harmonic EV — FF's LJ is the higher-fidelity choice per the hard rule.
6. **2D-ish / bundle geometry vs 3D shell.** Extension is a 1-D strain readout; FF's cortex is a closed 3-D shell. The biphasic-density and buckling-spacing *mechanisms* generalize, but the "extension ε" observable does not — translate to FF's native observables (cortical tension γ, contraction rate, curved fraction).
7. **Grid-drag caution (L1).** Eq. 3 is a legitimate drag form, but changing FF's segment drag touches the exact `Σγ ∝ Nc` term behind the unresolved crawl-speed slowdown *and* solver stability (`--bulk-drag` destabilizes). Audit and dimensionally verify before adopting; treat as a lever to *surface to PI*, not a silent swap.
