# DCM lens — p63

**Paper:** Taeyoon Kim (2015), "Determinants of contractile forces generated in disorganized actomyosin bundles," *Biomech Model Mechanobiol* 14:345–355, DOI 10.1007/s10237-014-0608-2.

**One-line honest framing for DCM:** This is a *fine-grained filament-level* (FF-class, Cytosim-lineage) Brownian-dynamics model of a single clamped actomyosin bundle — NOT a surface-mesh continuum. It does not hand DCM a drop-in surface force. Its value to DCM is (a) as an *upstream generator model* that tells us HOW MUCH sustained tension a cortical actomyosin sheet can produce and under what conditions — i.e. a physical anchor for the cortex γ that DCM lumps, addressing the standing **γ-generation** open problem — and (b) as a **rigidity-sensing / durotaxis** law and a **loose→compact network-compaction** protocol that map onto DCM's substrate-response and aggregate-compaction scenarios. Everything below is filtered to those DCM-usable pieces; the bulk of the paper (ACP molar-ratio sweeps, catch/slip bond kinetics, buckling symmetry-breaking) belongs to the FF lens, not DCM.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cortex γ as an *emergent, saturating* function of motor + crosslinker density (not a free constant)
**DCM module:** Actomyosin cortex surface tension γ (currently interphase ≈0.15 mN/m, mitotic ≈1.5 mN/m, pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2).
**Change:** Adopt the paper's central quantitative result — sustained bundle tension is **only ~15% of the summed motor stall force (χ≈0.15)**, and *requires* a sufficient crosslinker density; motors alone give χ≈0.005 (0.5%) and collapse in <5 s. Use this as the **physical ceiling and floor for any first-principles γ estimate** DCM writes down. Concretely: if DCM ever computes γ bottom-up from cortical NMII areal density × per-motor stall × efficiency, it must apply an efficiency factor χ in the **0.005–0.15** band, NOT 1.0.
**Why it beats DCM-now:** DCM's γ is presently a lumped literature constant with no internal check on whether the actomyosin it represents *could* physically produce it. This is exactly the standing γ-floor / γ-generation open problem (MEMORY: `project-gamma-floor-likely-deficit` — "deficit = force-magnitude limit," floored ~530×). Kim gives the missing efficiency oracle: the reason bottom-up γ estimates undershoot is that only ~15% of stall-force sum is ever realized as net tension, and that only with adequate crosslinking. This reframes the "γ floor" not as a missing datum but as a χ≈0.15 efficiency ceiling — a *mechanistic* explanation consistent with the fine-grained-over-lumped rule.

### M2 — Crosslinker-set connectivity is the gate for *sustained* (not just peak) cortical tension
**DCM module:** Cortex γ persistence / cortex–membrane coupling.
**Change:** Encode that peak tension and *sustained* tension are governed by different quantities — sustainability S=⟨F⟩/F_max is set by the ratio (motor load)/(crosslinker density) and by crosslinker off-rate, not by motor density. In DCM terms: a cortex that generates transient γ but loses connectivity relaxes to zero within seconds. If DCM ever makes γ dynamic (turnover-coupled), the *relaxation* term must be crosslinker-connectivity-limited, not motor-limited.
**Why it beats DCM-now:** DCM treats γ as static and always-on. Any future dynamic-γ (e.g. mitotic ramp, bleb-site local drop) needs the correct *decay* physics; Kim shows decay = loss of percolation via crosslinker unbinding, giving the mechanistic form.

### M3 — Rigidity-sensing / durotaxis: biphasic tension-vs-substrate-stiffness law
**DCM module:** Substrate/contact response (durotaxis is an explicitly listed DCM open problem).
**Change:** Reproduce the biphasic law — cortex-generated contractile tension **rises with substrate modulus E up to a transition and plateaus above it**. Kim finds transition ≈300 Pa for the bundle (his earlier 3D-network work, Borau 2012, put it ≈1000 Pa: stress ∝ E below, flat above). Mechanism: a stiff substrate lets motors reach stall after short displacement; a soft substrate lets filaments glide off barbed ends before building force. In DCM, this becomes a rule for how the traction/contact term modulates effective cortical prestress vs. substrate E — the physical basis for durotaxis without a lumped "durotaxis force."
**Why it beats DCM-now:** DCM has no principled substrate-stiffness → tension coupling. This gives a derivable, saturating law (not a tuned gain) directly usable in a durotaxis scenario.

### M4 — Compaction from a *sparse* network as the correct loose→compact protocol
**DCM module:** Aggregate compaction (loose→compact, currently drag-limited; real 24–48 h needs escalation beyond aggregate-σ).
**Change:** Note Kim's stated future direction (p354): form the bundle "by compaction of a sparse network rather than using preassembled bundle structures." His *reference* setup pre-assembles a clamped dense bundle — which is exactly the analogue of DCM's current failure mode (aggregate-σ compaction is only valid as a mechanical END-STATE, per MEMORY `project-dcm-compaction-turgor-blocked`). The mechanistic lesson: genuine compaction is a *connectivity-driven flow* of a loose network toward attractors, rate-limited by crosslinker (→ cadherin, at DCM scale) binding/unbinding kinetics and force-dependent off-rate — NOT by a global surface tension pulled at drag-limited speed. This corroborates the DCM conclusion that real compaction rate needs **cadherin T1-rearrangement escalation**, not aggregate-σ.
**Why it beats DCM-now:** It independently validates (from the filament side) that DCM's aggregate-σ is an end-state shortcut, and points the escalation at the same physics — kinetics of the load-bearing crosslinker/junction, with force-dependent unbinding (Eq. 6 form) — that DCM's cadherin catch-bond junction already implements. This is a *direct pointer* for the open compaction-RATE problem.

### M5 — Symmetry-breaking / net-contractile sign via buckling (informational, low priority for DCM)
**DCM module:** Cortex tension sign.
**Change:** Kim confirms net cortical contraction (tension, not compression) emerges because F-actin *buckles* under compression between crosslink points (Euler beam, Eq. 7), rectifying the sign. DCM's surface γ is contractile by construction, so this is not needed operationally — but it justifies, from first principles, that a fine-grained cortex under NMII loading yields *net tension*, so DCM's always-contractile γ sign is physically sound. Keep as a note, not a code change.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

Most equations here are per-filament and do NOT lift to the DCM surface. The two DCM-usable transcriptions:

### E1 — Efficiency-bounded cortical tension (from χ, η definitions, p349)
Paper: `χ = F_max / Σ f_M^stall` (fraction of summed stall force realized as net tension), `η = F_max / Σ f_M^max`.
**Port to DCM cortex:** for a cortex patch of area A_patch carrying an NMII areal density ρ_M (motors/µm²) each with stall f_stall,
```
γ_cortex  =  χ · ρ_M · f_stall            [N/m]
```
with **χ ∈ [0.005 (crosslink-poor), 0.15 (crosslink-adequate)]** as an efficiency oracle, and per-motor stall f_stall ≈ 4.5 pN per head-ensemble (Kim's PCM output). Symbol map: γ_cortex ↔ DCM surface tension field on faces; ρ_M ↔ cortical NMII density (physiological config field); χ ↔ new efficiency constant (band, derivable from crosslink adequacy, NOT tuned). This is the bottom-up sanity check for the lumped γ.

### E2 — Biphasic tension vs substrate modulus (rigidity sensing, p354 / Fig S13c)
Qualitative law (paper gives no closed form, only the transition):
```
γ_active(E_sub)  ∝  E_sub          for  E_sub < E*      (rising)
γ_active(E_sub)  =  γ_plateau      for  E_sub ≥ E*      (saturated)
E* ≈ 300 Pa (bundle) ;  ≈ 1000 Pa (3D network, Borau 2012)
```
Symbol map: E_sub ↔ DCM substrate/ECM modulus at contact faces; γ_active ↔ the myosin-borne part of DCM cortical tension (excluding the pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2). Implement as a saturating multiplier on the active-γ term in a durotaxis scenario; the plateau and transition are literature values, not fit parameters.

### E3 (reference only — NOT ported) — Euler buckling threshold, Eq. 7
`F_seg^buckle ≈ π² κ_b,A r0,A / L_seg²`. This is filament-segment mechanics (FF-scale). No DCM surface analogue; recorded only to justify the contractile sign of γ (M5). Do not implement in DCM.

The over-damped Langevin update (Eqs. 1–2), harmonic potentials (Eq. 3), binding-spot lever rule (Eqs. 4–5), and Bell/slip ACP off-rate (Eq. 6) are **FF-engine equations** — they belong to the FF lens and must not be lifted to DCM faces (that would be a fine-grained→lumped inversion in the wrong direction).

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce as DCM scenarios

### G1 — Rigidity-sensing sweep (durotaxis primitive)
Clamp/adhere a single DCM cell to substrates of increasing modulus E_sub spanning ~10 Pa → ~10 kPa; measure the active (myosin-borne) cortical tension and spread. **Expected DCM result to match:** tension rises with E_sub then plateaus above ~300–1000 Pa (E2). This is a clean, physiological-baseline-compatible DCM scenario (cell starts at resting turgor + resting γ; only substrate stiffness is swept) and directly seeds a durotaxis study.

### G2 — Loose→compact aggregate compaction *from a sparse start*
Mirror Kim's future-direction protocol at DCM aggregate scale (N≈400): initialize a **sparse, loosely-touching** cell aggregate (NOT pre-confluent), turn on cadherin catch-bond junctions with force-dependent off-rate, and let compaction *emerge* from junction binding/turnover — measuring compaction RATE, not just end-state porosity. **Contrast with aggregate-σ:** run both and confirm (per MEMORY `project-dcm-ipc-large-dt`) that aggregate-σ reaches the same end-state faster only because it is drag-limited, whereas the junction-kinetics route gives the physiological rate. This is the concrete experiment for the open compaction-RATE problem.

### G3 — Clamped-cortex tension readout (validation harness, not a physiological run)
Kim's `F(t)=E·A·ε` readout of a substrate-clamped contractile sheet is a template for a DCM *diagnostic*: hold two opposite regions of a flattened DCM cortex patch fixed to elastic supports and read the transmitted tension over time to check γ magnitude and its persistence. Label non-authoritative (it is a stripped diagnostic, not a full-compartment physiological cell — per the NATIVE+FULL rule, any finding must be reconfirmed on the native cell).

*Not usable:* the bundle assembly (50 aligned 9-µm filaments, sever-and-clamp, 5×5×20 µm PBC box) is filament-scale FF geometry — no DCM surface analogue.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Regime mismatch — in-vitro purified bundle vs living MCF7 cortex.** Kim's system is a reconstituted minimal bundle (actin + one ACP + NMII) clamped to a substrate, no membrane, no turgor, no nucleus, no cell-cell junction. DCM's cortex sits on a pressurized, membrane-bound shell. Import only the *dimensionless* results (χ, η, S, biphasic knee) and the *efficiency framing*, never the absolute geometry. Absolute tension (2 nN over 25 µm²) is a ballpark, not a target to fit.
- **1D bundle vs 2D cortical sheet.** Kim's bundle is quasi-1D (aligned filaments, clamped ends); DCM's cortex is a 2D sheet on a closed surface. The χ≈0.15 efficiency and buckling-based sign-rectification are plausibly transferable in spirit, but the *quantitative* efficiency of a 2D isotropic cortex is not measured here — treat χ as a band spanning geometry uncertainty, do not pin to 0.15.
- **Crosslinker ≠ cadherin.** The ACP off-rate (Ferrer 2008, k0*=0.115 s⁻¹, slip bond) is an *intracellular* actin crosslinker, NOT the cell-cell cadherin catch-bond DCM uses for junctions. The *structural analogy* (load-bearing reversible crosslink with force-dependent off-rate gates connectivity → compaction/tension persistence) is the transferable idea; the *numbers* are not — DCM's cadherin uses Rakshit KU-4.2, and the α-actinin datum was already re-anchored to 0.066 s⁻¹ in the KB. **Do not overwrite DCM/KB kinetic constants with Kim's ACP values.**
- **Physiological-baseline compliance.** Kim starts from a pre-assembled clamped bundle and releases at t=0 — an artificial, non-physiological baseline. Do NOT copy that init style into DCM. Any DCM scenario derived from this paper (G1, G2) must start from the full physiological operating point (resting turgor Π₀≈40 Pa, resting γ, native full-compartment cell, `--from-resting`), sweeping only the one variable of interest (substrate E for G1; aggregate sparseness for G2).
- **No actin turnover in the model.** Kim explicitly holds actin dynamics off (fixed filaments; treadmilling only discussed). His long-filament (9 µm vs in-vivo 0.5–3 µm) choice inflates tension/sustainability. So his S≈1 persistence is an upper bound; a turnover-ON cortex will sustain less. Weight the persistence target as optimistic.
- **Native + full-compartment mandate.** G3 (clamped-patch diagnostic) is a stripped harness — explicitly non-authoritative under the HARD rule; every finding from it must be reconfirmed on the native `--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules` full cell before it is reported. (This is properly an FF-engine test anyway.)

**Bottom line for DCM:** low-to-moderate direct impact. This paper's real home is the FF lens. For DCM it delivers three usable levers — (1) a χ≈0.005–0.15 *efficiency oracle* that reframes the γ-generation/floor problem as an efficiency ceiling rather than a missing datum; (2) a biphasic rigidity-sensing law (knee ≈300–1000 Pa) for a durotaxis scenario; (3) independent corroboration, from the filament side, that genuine loose→compact compaction is *crosslinker/junction-kinetics-rate-limited*, not surface-tension-drag-limited — pointing the open compaction-RATE escalation at DCM's cadherin catch-bond junction.
