# FF lens — p10

**Paper:** Rabbi, Koenderink, Mulla, Kim (2025) "Fine-tuning of material properties by catch bonds," *Acta Biomaterialia* 201:372–384. DOI 10.1016/j.actbio.2025.06.004. Code: github.com/ktyman2/CatchSlip.

**One-line relevance:** This is FF's *exact native regime* — fine-grained actin filaments + explicit cross-linkers with a force-dependent (Bell/catch-slip) off-rate + a Stam–Hocky-style bipolar myosin minifilament, run as passive shear rheology and single-motor contraction. It is a sibling of Cytosim (FF's physics reference), by the Taeyoon Kim lab. It hands us (a) a directly portable catch-slip cross-linker off-rate law, (b) a **cross-linker turnover** mechanism FF does not currently have and that AFINES/MEDYAN/ALENS/Cytosim also lack, and (c) quantitative σy/γy/contractile-stress validation targets. Caveat: the primitive force-law constants (λ, k0, on-rate, drag, stiffnesses, timestep) live in the external SI Table S1, NOT in the main PDF — those must be fetched from the SI before a full port.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cross-linker **catch-slip** off-rate (supersedes FF's slip-only Bell-Evans) — HIGH priority
FF's current cross-linker (α-actinin/filamin) uses a pure **slip** Bell-Evans off-rate `k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b)` — dissociation monotonically increases with force. This paper (and its experimental predecessor Mulla 2022, Nat. Mater., the wild-type α-actinin-4 datum) shows α-actinin-4 WT and filamin A/B are **catch-slip**: off-rate *decreases* with force at low load (catch, from a cryptic strong actin-binding site exposed under tension) then increases at high load (slip). The FF change: add a catch term (a second decaying exponential) to the α-actinin off-rate — Eq. (1) below. Why it beats the current mechanism: (i) it is the *more* fine-grained/mechanistic option (the real molecule is catch-slip, the slip-only form is the lumped proxy — this is exactly the CLAUDE.md "fine-grained over proxy" rule; note the memory already flags Rakshit catch-bonds for cadherin, this is the actin-crosslinker analog); (ii) it is what actually lets a network raise **both** yield stress and yield strain, breaking the stiffness↔extensibility trade-off — a slip-only FF cortex cannot reproduce this.

### M2 — Cross-linker **turnover / spatial redistribution** (NEW capability, not in FF today) — HIGHEST leverage
The paper's *distinguishing* feature vs AFINES/MEDYAN/ALENS/Cytosim: after a cross-linker unbinds it does not just re-bind locally — it undergoes a **"turnover" event**, disappearing and reappearing at a new location within a hop radius (default **5 µm**, short variant 2 µm). This makes the *bound population mobile*: catch-slip CLs "search" while at low force (short dwell), then "settle" and accumulate where force is intermediate (near τmax), reinforcing load-bearing pathways. Control result: **without turnover, catch-slip networks are WEAKER than slip** (Fig. S3) — turnover is *necessary* for the advantage. FF today re-binds crosslinkers from a local pool; adding a hop-distributed re-insertion after unbinding is a small kernel change with large mechanistic payoff. Why it matters for FF's open problems: this is a candidate mechanism for **cortical-tension γ generation** (force-adaptive reinforcement raises the stress a cortex can bear) and for **motor-driven contraction** (see M3) — both listed FF open problems.

### M3 — Catch-slip redistribution **amplifies motor contraction** — port into the myosin/active-cortex scenario
Single 512-head motor: catch-slip CLs produce **~3–4× larger contractile stress (~90 Pa vs ~25 Pa slip)** and propagate force over a larger radius. Mechanism: catch-slip CLs *arrive at the motor before* filament aggregation (redistribution precedes contraction; Fig. 6H), improving connectivity so the motor's force transmits. FF's Stam–Hocky myosin minifilament + catch-slip crosslinker is exactly this system. Why it beats current FF: FF contraction currently rides on a static crosslinker field; force-adaptive CL accumulation is a mechanistic route to higher network tension and to the grid-drag-limited contraction/crawl-speed problem (better connectivity = force transmitted further per motor).

### M4 — Cross-linker **size/stiffness → catch↔slip transition rate** (mechanistic knob for α-actinin vs filamin)
Discussion (p.12): α-actinin ~35 nm (short, stiff) vs filamin A ~160–250 nm (long, flexible). Shorter/stiffer CL → force rises faster on the bond → faster catch→slip transition; longer/flexible → gradual force rise → slower transition. FF already carries per-crosslinker length + link stiffness (Ferrer values in memory: link_k 4.6e5 / 8.2e5). This says: the catch-slip *dynamics* are not a free parameter — they are set by the CL's mechanical length/stiffness that FF already models. Adopt as: derive the effective transition force-rate from FF's existing per-CL geometry, not as a magic number.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### Eq. (1) — catch-slip cross-linker unbinding rate (THE port)
```
             ⎧ k_s0 · exp( +λ_s · |F_s,CL| / kB T )                        (slip / Bell term)
k_u,CL(F) =  ⎨   + k_c0 · exp( −λ_c · |F_s,CL| / kB T )   if r ≥ r0,CL      (catch term)
             ⎩ k_s0 + k_c0                                 if r < r0,CL     (compressed/slack: force-free)
```
Symbol → FF state map:
- `|F_s,CL|` = tensile force magnitude on the crosslinker bond = FF's crosslinker spring tension (link_k · (r − r0,CL)), already computed each step.
- `r` = current CL extension; `r0,CL` = CL rest length. **Below rest length the bond feels no tensile load** → rate collapses to the force-free sum `k_s0 + k_c0`. (FF must gate on `r ≥ r0,CL`, i.e. only tensile, not compressive, load drives the catch/slip terms.)
- `k_s0`, `k_c0` = zero-force off-rate constants of slip and catch parts (units s⁻¹). **Slip-only limit: set k_c0 = 0** — this recovers FF's current Bell-Evans exactly, so the change is strictly additive/back-compatible (catch-slip is default-ON at physiological α-actinin values per the physiological-baseline rule; k_c0=0 remains available as the K255E-mutant / slip control).
- `λ_s`, `λ_c` = force sensitivities (compliance lengths, units nm) for slip and catch. `kB T` = thermal energy (4.114 pN·nm at 310 K).
- **Bond lifetime** τ(F) = 1/k_u,CL(F). Rises from τ0 at F=0 to τmax at F=Fmax, then falls (slip-dominated).

### Reparameterization the paper actually uses (transcribe both — the 4 constants are the physics, the 3 are the phenomenology)
The paper drives everything by three **phenomenological descriptors** {τ0, τmax, Fmax} which are a 1:1 reparameterization of the four constants {k_s0, k_c0, λ_s, λ_c}:
- τ0 = 1/(k_s0 + k_c0) (zero-force lifetime)
- τmax = lifetime at its peak; Fmax = force where dτ/dF = 0.
Implementation note: it is cleaner in FF to **store {k_s0, k_c0, λ_s, λ_c}** (that is what the kernel evaluates) and *derive* {τ0, τmax, Fmax} for reporting/validation. The four primitive constants are in SI Table S1 — **must fetch**; the main text only gives the three descriptors' values (§3, below).

### Turnover kinetic scheme (M2, new)
On an unbinding event at position **x**:
1. remove the CL bond;
2. re-insert at **x' = x + hop**, where hop is a random displacement of magnitude ≤ R_hop (default **R_hop = 5 µm**; short variant 2 µm), re-binding to a filament pair found within reach at x'.
3. (no-turnover control: leave the CL attached to the *other* filament at the old location — reproduces AFINES/Cytosim behavior, and reproduces the "catch-slip loses to slip" negative control.)
Reported turnover-rate observable ≈ 0.15–0.17 s⁻¹ (catch-slip, passive; §5 validation).

### Shear-rate ↔ lifetime scaling (protocol invariant, use when adapting to living cortex rates)
Paper fixes γ̇ = 0.001 s⁻¹. Explicit prediction (p.9): "τ0 and τmax should be inversely proportional to γ̇" to hold σy/γy fixed; in active networks the analog of γ̇ is the **motor walking rate** (crossbridge cycle) — so τ0, τmax ∝ 1/v_walk. Use this to rescale the in-vitro lifetimes to FF's physiological motor velocity rather than copying τ0=9.5 s blindly.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenarios)

### Scenario A — passive bulk shear rheology (validate σy/γy trade-off break)
- Domain 30×30×1 µm, quasi-2D slab, filaments random but ⊥ z. PBC all directions during assembly.
- After assembly: **deactivate PBC in y**; filaments crossing the two y-normal faces are **severed and clamped** to the boundaries → shear walls.
- −y wall fixed; +y wall displaced in +x at γ̇ = 0.001 s⁻¹ (strain 0 → ~2.0). Measure shear stress on +y wall vs strain.
- Run matched pair: catch-slip CLs (k_c0>0) vs slip CLs (k_c0=0, area-matched). n=4 seeds.
- Sweeps: CF ∈ {2.5…10} µM × RCL ∈ {0.002…0.015}; then Fmax, τmax, τ0 one-at-a-time from the reference.
- NOTE for FF: this is a *purified reconstituted* geometry (dev-scale scenario), NOT the native cell. Use it as a **kernel-parity / unit-validation** scenario for the new catch-slip + turnover code, then reconfirm any conclusion on the native 38000-filament cortex per the CLAUDE.md native-scale rule.

### Scenario B — single-motor active contraction (validate contractile-stress amplification)
- Same network, no external strain. One **immobile 512-head motor** (64 arms × 8 heads) at center generates internal contraction. Measure contractile stress vs time, CL radial density vs distance, and filament-vs-CL accumulation order near the motor.
- FF map: this is FF's Stam–Hocky minifilament dropped into a catch-slip-crosslinked patch — directly runnable.

### Analysis constructs to implement (reusable FF diagnostics)
- **"critical crosslinkers"** = top-10% by tensile force just before yield; track backward in time vs global average (force, turnover rate, displacement).
- **"critical filaments"** = top-10% force-bearing filaments; **overlap %** between adjacent times = how much the load-bearing set changes (the Fmax-vs-τmax-vs-τ0 signature).
- **# CLs per high-force filament** vs strain (accumulation curve).

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Absolute numbers are purified-in-vitro, not living cortex.** σy~100–200 Pa, ~90 Pa motor stress, τ0=9.5 s come from a 30×30×1 µm reconstituted 2D slab at γ̇=0.001 s⁻¹. FF's native MCF7 cortex is 3D, R=7.5 µm, turgor-pressurized, at 65 Pa·s cytoplasm. **Port the mechanism and the ratios/scaling laws, not the raw Pa values.** Rescale τ0,τmax by FF motor walking rate (paper's own γ̇-scaling prediction) rather than copying.
- **SI dependency (blocking for a full port).** The Eq.(1) primitives (k_s0, k_c0, λ_s, λ_c), motor stall force, drag ζ, filament/CL stretch+bend stiffness, segment length, and timestep are all in the external SI Table S1 — **not in the 13-page main PDF.** Fetch the SI (or read github.com/ktyman2/CatchSlip) before implementing; do not invent the constants (magic-number rule). The single-molecule *source* for α-actinin catch-slip is Mulla 2022 Nat. Mater. [18] — that, not this simulation's fitted τ's, is the KU citation anchor.
- **No actin turnover in the paper (their stated limitation, p.12).** FF's cortex DOES have barbed-end growth + (planned) fragmentation/turnover. The paper's cross-linker redistribution interacts with actin turnover in real cortex (they flag it explicitly). This is an *advantage* for FF — we can study the coupling they could not — but it means their σy/γy targets assume frozen filaments; when validating in native FF, either (a) freeze actin turnover for the parity run, or (b) treat divergence as physics, not a bug.
- **Connectivity gate is real, not incidental.** Catch-slip *loses* to slip at low RCL/CF. If a native FF run is in a loose regime the mechanism will look useless or harmful — must validate at the dense cortex/stress-fiber connectivity (CF=10 µM-equivalent), consistent with the native-scale + physiological-baseline rules. Do not conclude "catch-slip doesn't help" from a coarse/loose config.
- **Turnover hop distance (5 µm) is a model choice, not measured.** It is ~2/3 the whole cell radius — physically a *diffusive re-binding* proxy, not a literal 5 µm jump. In FF, implement it mechanistically as unbinding → cytoplasmic diffusion (at 65 Pa·s) → re-binding, and let the effective hop emerge, rather than hard-coding 5 µm (which would be a magic number). Test sensitivity (2 µm vs 5 µm vs none) as they did.
- **Isoform/species scope.** α-actinin-4 WT (human, non-muscle) is the catch-slip; K255E mutant is the slip control (a disease mutant, used *only* as the slip comparison — not a physiological state to run). Filamin A/B are also catch-slip but at very different length/stiffness (M4) — do not use α-actinin τ's for a filamin crosslinker.
- **Single immobile motor ≠ real actomyosin.** The active scenario uses one fixed 512-head motor to inject controlled internal force, explicitly "not to mimic a real actomyosin network." FF's multi-minifilament cortex is the real target; use their single-motor result as a *unit test* of the catch-slip↔contraction coupling, then scale to FF's motor population.
