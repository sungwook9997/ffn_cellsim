# FF lens — p30

**Paper:** Sakamoto, Miyazaki & Maeda, *State transitions of a confined actomyosin system controlled through contractility and polymerization rate*, Phys. Rev. Research **5**, 013208 (2023). DOI 10.1103/PhysRevResearch.5.013208.

**One-line for FF:** An in-vitro reconstitution (Xenopus M-phase egg extract in a cell-sized 200–300 µm quasi-2D water-in-oil droplet) plus a coarse-grained active-fluid + phase-field model showing that a *single* confined actomyosin system self-organizes into **steady inward flow**, **periodic ring waves**, or (with heterogeneous onset) a **rotational wave**, and that the two controlling knobs are **myosin contractility σ_act** and **F-actin polymerization rate k_p**. The paper's *theory* is a lumped continuum PDE — the exact abstraction FF exists to replace — so it is **not a parameter source** for FF. Its value to FF is as a **confinement scenario + emergent-pattern validation target**: FF's fine-grained cortex/cytoplasm, with explicit myosin minifilaments and explicit Arp2/3/formin polymerization, should reproduce the flow→wave→rotation phase behavior *from filaments up*, without ever writing down an active-stress field.

Honest framing: **FF relevance is PARTIAL and confirmatory, not extractive.** There are no Bell-Evans / Hill / crosslinker rate constants here. Everything below is either (a) a scenario/protocol to build, (b) an emergent observable to hit, or (c) a *dimensionless-group sanity map* (Péclet) that tells FF which fine-grained knob (myosin duty/density vs. barbed-end k_on) moves the system across the boundary. Treat continuum σ_act, D, γ as **derived diagnostics computed from FF micro-state**, never as inputs.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

Nothing here supersedes an FF force law. What it adds is a **new scenario class and a two-knob organizing principle** that FF already has the machinery to express mechanistically:

1. **Confinement-driven self-organization scenario (NEW FF test class).**
   Encapsulate the FF cortex+cytoplasm in a **cell-sized circular confinement with a no-flux, non-deforming wall** and let pattern emerge. FF modules touched: `filament` (cortex actin), `myosin` (Stam–Hocky minifilaments), `Arp2/3` (branch nucleation at the wall), `turnover` (depolymerization/severing), `membrane containment` (rigid circular wall as boundary). Why it beats current FF practice: FF has been validated mostly on *tension/rheology at a point* and *crawl*; it has **not** been asked to produce the emergent **spatiotemporal pattern** (inward flow vs. periodic contraction wave). This is the cleanest published in-vitro target where the pattern itself is the observable and the two drivers are cleanly separated.

2. **Surface-enriched nucleation as an explicit Arp2/3-at-boundary rule (MECHANISTIC upgrade of the paper's lumped `k_surf_p`).**
   The paper *lumps* "surface polymerization 10× faster than bulk" into a phenomenological ratio α_p = k_bulk_p/k_surf_p = 0.1 imposed over a 0.1R shell (0.9R<r<R). Its physical origin (stated in the paper) is **WASP/VCA adsorption at the water–oil interface → localized Arp2/3-mediated nucleation** (Boukellal 2004 "soft Listeria"; Pinot 2012). FF should implement this **mechanistically, not as a rate ratio**: raise the Arp2/3 **nucleation-site density (NPF surface concentration)** in the cortical shell adjacent to the membrane, so branched nucleation rate emerges from local NPF + mother-filament + Arp2/3 availability. This is strictly the fine-grained option the CLAUDE.md hard rule demands — the α_p<0.5 wave criterion then becomes a *prediction*, not a tuned input.

3. **Two-knob mechanistic phase control: σ_act ← myosin, k_p ← barbed-end kinetics.**
   The paper's Fig. 2 perturbation map maps molecular levers to pattern:
   - CalA (↑ myosin activity) → wave ; Y27632 (↓ myosin) → flow. In FF this is **myosin duty ratio / bound-head fraction / minifilament density**, not a stress scalar.
   - VVCA (↑ Arp2/3 nucleation → ↑k_p) → flow ; Cytochalasin D / CK666 (↓ barbed-end growth / ↓Arp2/3) → wave. In FF this is **barbed-end on-rate, formin/Arp2/3 nucleation rate, capping**.
   Adopt this as the **acceptance grid**: sweep FF's myosin activity and FF's polymerization kinetics and check the emergent flow↔wave boundary reproduces the sign of every arrow in Fig. 2(c). Why it matters: it validates that FF's *coupling* between motor contractility and filament turnover is correct — a coupling that lumped models put in by hand.

4. **Depolymerization / turnover as a first-class rate (reinforces existing FF turnover channel).**
   The wave period is set **not by contractility but by polymerization/turnover dynamics** (T≈1.5 min plateau, independent of CalA level). This is a direct statement that **turnover, not motor strength, sets the oscillation clock** — motivating FF to keep an explicit, physiological severing/depolymerization channel (cofilin-like) rather than treating filaments as permanent. FF already has barbed-end growth + distributed lengths; this says the *sink* (k_d ρ) must be equally explicit for the wave clock to emerge.

5. **Bulk-vs-surface competition as the symmetry-breaking mechanism (interpretation to reproduce, not to hardcode).**
   Paper's interpretation: bulk network → steady inward flow; surface (wall-nucleated) network → periodic waves; the two compete, and rotational waves live exactly on their balance line. FF should let this competition emerge from spatially-resolved nucleation + advection and confirm it, rather than imposing a bulk/surface split.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES (what to port — with the caveat that FF replaces them from below)

These are the paper's continuum equations. **In FF they are NOT integrated as PDEs.** They are (i) the *coarse-grained observables* to compute from the filament state for cross-check, and (ii) the definitions that fix the Péclet sanity map. Symbol → FF-state map given.

- **(Eq. 3) Density-dependent saturating active stress:**
  σ_act = (ζμ)₀ · f(ρ) · I, with f(ρ) = ρ/(ρ₀+ρ)  (Michaelis–Menten in actomyosin density; isotropic).
  FF map: (ζμ)₀·f(ρ) is a *diagnostic* — compute the local **virial/Kirkwood contractile stress** from the explicit myosin-head forces on bound actin (FF already computes σ_vm virially in DCM audit). The saturation f(ρ)=ρ/(ρ₀+ρ) should **emerge** because bound-motor fraction saturates as actin density rises (finite motor number). Do not impose it.

- **(Eq. 4) F-actin reaction–advection–diffusion:**
  ∂ρ/∂t + ∇·(ρv) = D∇²ρ + k_p − k_d ρ.
  FF map: ρ = local filament-mass density (bin the explicit beads); ∇·(ρv) = advection by the emergent network flow (already present — no term needed, particles carry mass); D = effective filament diffusivity (emergent from BAOAB thermal + steric); **k_p = barbed-end elongation + Arp2/3/formin nucleation** (explicit); **k_d = depolymerization/severing** (explicit turnover channel). This equation is the *bookkeeping* FF satisfies automatically at native scale; use it only to *measure* effective D, k_p, k_d for the Péclet cross-check.

- **(Eq. 1–2, 6) Momentum balance (over-damped active fluid):**
  ∇·(σ_vis + σ_act) = γ v ; σ_vis = η[∇v+(∇v)ᵀ] + η_b(∇·v)I ; nondim: ∇²v + λ∇(∇·v) + ∇f = v, λ = 1+η_b/η = 1/3.
  FF map: FF already runs over-damped (BAOAB, LJ, drag). γ = per-node drag from cytoplasm viscosity (physiological η=65.9 Pa·s, MCF7 — set from t=0 per hard rule). η = network viscosity, **emergent** from crosslink turnover (α-actinin/filamin Bell-Evans off-rates). **Do not port λ=1/3**; it is a continuum closure assumption, not physics.

- **Péclet number (THE portable dimensionless sanity group):**
  Pe = C·l/D = (ζμ)₀/(Dγ),  characteristic velocity C = (ζμ)₀/(ηγ)^{1/2}, length unit l = (η/γ)^{1/2}.
  **This is the one genuinely useful export.** After an FF confinement run, compute effective (ζμ)₀ (from motor stress), D (from filament MSD), γ (from drag) and form Pe. The paper's map — **Pe≈38 → steady flow, Pe≈115 → periodic wave**, onset in the Pe~40–80 window depending on nucleation — becomes a **regime predictor**: it tells you whether the FF micro-parameters you chose sit in flow or wave territory *before* you run the expensive native job. Use it to place FF runs, not to tune them.

- **No-flux boundary (Eq. 7 energy term ε_ρ∇²(δE/δρ)) & phase field (Eq. 5,8):**
  Continuum tricks to keep gel off a soft wall. **FF does not need these** — FF's membrane containment + LJ excluded volume already enforce no-flux at an explicit wall. Skip entirely. (Relevant to DCM, not FF.)

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenario spec)

**Scenario "FF-CONFINED-PATTERN": confined cortex+cytoplasm pattern-selection assay.**

- **Geometry:** rigid, non-deforming circular (or spherical-cap / thin-disk) confinement. Native scale demands full compartments, but the paper's droplet is 200–300 µm — far larger than a single MCF7 cell. **Adapt to FF's native cell:** run the *same physics* in FF's MCF7 R=7.5 µm confined cortex (the native `--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules`, Nc≈266k), treating the membrane as the no-flux wall. The paper's value is the *phenomenon and its knobs*, not its 200-µm length.
- **Compartments (all ON at physiological setpoint from t=0):** membrane containment, cortex actin (explicit filaments, exponential lengths 1–10 µm), cytoplasm at η=65.9 Pa·s, turgor/ΔP=40 Pa (`--from-resting`), microtubules, gravity. No stripping.
- **Active elements:** explicit myosin-II Stam–Hocky minifilaments (contractility knob) + Arp2/3 branched nucleation with **elevated NPF density in the outer cortical shell** (0.9R<r<R) to represent membrane-localized WASP → the surface-polymerization mechanism. Explicit barbed-end growth + depolymerization/severing turnover.
- **Initial condition:** physiological resting cortex (from checkpoint), zero net flow — analogous to paper's ρ=1, v=0 uniform start.
- **Two-knob sweep (the experiment):**
  - Myosin axis (↔ CalA/Y27632): vary bound-head fraction / minifilament density / duty ratio.
  - Polymerization axis (↔ VVCA/CytoD/CK666): vary Arp2/3 nucleation rate + barbed-end on-rate + capping.
- **Rotational-wave protocol (optional, Fig. 4):** break symmetry with a **system-wide, quadrant-staggered onset of myosin activation** (τd ≈ 1% of run) rather than local noise (paper explicitly found local noise insufficient; symmetry breaking is system-wide). Expect a rotating wave *only near the flow↔wave boundary* — a stringent structural test.
- **Measurement protocol:** angle-averaged filament-density kymograph over an annular ROI 0.45R<r<0.55R; Fourier transform → peak frequency 1/T (1/T→0 = steady flow, finite = wave). PIV on the FF density field for inward-flow speed. This measurement pipeline is directly portable.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for FF observables

Emergent, order-of-magnitude and qualitative targets (in-vitro extract regime — treat as *pattern/sign* checks, not tight numeric gates):

1. **Two coexisting patterns in one system:** the same FF cortex must produce **steady inward flow** at low myosin/high polymerization and **periodic ring waves** at high myosin/low polymerization — no parameter class swap.
2. **Flow→wave transition sign:** ↑myosin (CalA-analog) → wave; ↓myosin (Y27632-analog) → flow; ↑polymerization (VVCA-analog) → flow; ↓polymerization (CytoD/CK666-analog) → wave. All four arrows of Fig. 2(c) reproduced.
3. **Wave period ≈ 1.5 min**, and **period set by turnover/polymerization, not by myosin level** (period roughly invariant as myosin knob increases past threshold). This is the sharpest mechanistic prediction.
4. **Steady-flow inward speed ~0.7 µm/s** (PIV scale) — order-of-magnitude on the emergent flow.
5. **Péclet regime map:** FF-derived Pe in flow runs ≲ ~40–80; in wave runs ≳ ~100. Reproduce the monotone "higher Pe → waves" trend (Fig. 3f).
6. **Surface-nucleation criterion:** waves require surface nucleation contrast (α_p<0.5 analog); with uniform bulk nucleation (α_p→1), waves suppressed → flow. Reproduce loss of waves when the cortical-shell NPF enrichment is removed.
7. **Reentrant flow at very high polymerization:** at very high polymerization rate, flow re-stabilizes (Fig. 3g ii→iii) — new filaments swept to center before they can build the periphery-heavy density that drives waves. A non-trivial second-transition check.
8. **Rotational wave only at the flow/wave boundary** (Fig. 4d), and **period independent of the τd perturbation magnitude** — i.e., the stabilized state is set by physical parameters, not initial condition. A robustness/structural test for FF's confined dynamics.

## 6. RISKS / CAVEATS

- **Continuum, not filament-resolved.** The paper's *model* is exactly the lumped active-stress abstraction FF forbids. Import **only** the phenomenon, the two-knob logic, and the Péclet regime map. Do **not** port σ_act=(ζμ)₀f(ρ), the phase field, λ=1/3, or any nondimensional numeric (Dφ, ε_ρ, α₀). Those are magic-number-flavored closures — using them as FF inputs would violate the no-lumped-mechanism and no-magic-number hard rules.
- **Length-scale mismatch (large).** Droplets are 200–300 µm; a single MCF7 cell is R=7.5 µm (~13–40× smaller). The paper's absolute Pe values, T≈1.5 min, and 0.7 µm/s are for the big droplet with extract turnover — **not guaranteed to transfer numerically** to a single native cell. Adapt by matching *dimensionless* structure (Pe ordering, α_p<0.5 criterion, period-set-by-turnover) rather than the raw numbers. Keep the run at FF native scale + full compartments (hard rule); do **not** blow the cell up to 200 µm just to match the assay.
- **Xenopus M-phase egg extract ≠ MCF7 interphase cortex.** Different isoforms, Arp2/3 hyper-active in M-phase, 20 °C not 37 °C, no membrane-attached focal adhesions, high cytoplasmic turnover. So the *values* are regime-specific; the *mechanistic coupling* (motor contractility × turnover → pattern) is expected to be universal (the paper itself argues rotational waves are cell-type-universal). Use as sign/structure validation, not a parameter transplant.
- **2D quasi-planar vs FF 3D shell.** The assay is quasi-2D (h=60 µm midplane); FF cortex is a 3D spherical shell. Waves that are rings in 2D become surface bands on the sphere (cf. refs 35,36 Mietke/Bonati cortex-on-sphere). Adapt the ROI/kymograph to a surface-angular coordinate.
- **Surface nucleation origin is uncertain even in the paper** (WASP adsorption "exact mechanism remains uncertain"). FF's mechanistic version (elevated NPF in the outer shell) is a *hypothesis to test*, not an established datum — flag to PI, don't KB-register the α_p ratio as a physiological constant.
- **Isotropy assumption dropped orientation.** The paper neglects filament orientation; FF resolves it. FF may therefore predict *additional* orientation-driven effects (polarity sorting, chirality — refs 8, 41–43) the continuum model can't. That's a feature (fine-grained > lumped), but means one-to-one agreement with this paper is neither expected nor required where orientation matters.
- **Not a rheology/kinetics source.** Zero Bell-Evans / Hill / link-stiffness / off-rate constants here. Do not mine it for micro-parameters — it can only *benchmark emergent output*.
