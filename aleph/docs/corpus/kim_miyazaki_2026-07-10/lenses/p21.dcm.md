# DCM lens — p21

**Paper:** Yadav, Banerjee, Tabatabai, Kovar, Kim, Banerjee, Murrell (2019), *Filament Nucleation Tunes Mechanical Memory in Active Polymer Networks*, Adv. Funct. Mater. 29, 1905243. DOI 10.1002/adfm.201905243.

**Up-front honesty (read before using this lens).** This is a fine-grained, single-filament / actin-network paper (purified G-actin + formin + model membrane, quasi-2D, **no myosin driving**, in-vitro reconstitution). Its native home is the **FF engine**, not DCM. DCM represents the cortex as a *lumped surface tension γ on a closed triangulated mesh* — it does not resolve filaments, persistence length, nucleator number, Frank director fields, or per-filament bending energy. Therefore **most of this paper does not port to DCM.** What *does* transfer is one cell-scale continuum idea and its timescale: **cortical stress relaxation is set by F-actin turnover, and at a critical regime the two timescales DECOUPLE so stress is stored rather than dissipated (mechanical memory).** That single mechanism maps to DCM's cortex as a *finite surface-tension relaxation time* (Maxwell/Kelvin surface rheology), which DCM currently lacks. Everything below is scoped to that; I flag the rest as FF-only so it is not mis-imported.

Overall DCM relevance: **LOW-MODERATE (~1.5/5).** One real lever (turnover-set cortical relaxation clock), one order-of-magnitude sanity anchor (cortex turnover ≈ minutes), and a conceptual caution for the compaction-RATE open problem.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cortex surface tension gains a *turnover-set relaxation time* (Maxwell surface rheology). **[the one real lever]**
- **DCM module/force:** cortex surface tension γ (currently pressure-borne, `γ = ΔP·R/2`, treated as an instantaneous elastic/quasi-static surface stress).
- **Change:** give the cortical surface stress a finite relaxation time τ_c so that an *applied* areal/tension perturbation δγ decays as `δγ(t) = δγ₀·exp(−t/τ_c)`, with τ_c set by **F-actin turnover** (the paper's central mechanistic claim, refs [15–17]: "turnover is linked to cortical tension through its ability to relax applied mechanical stress"). Physically: the cortex is a viscoelastic surface whose Maxwell time is the actin renewal time, not infinite (pure elastic) nor zero (pure viscous).
- **Why it beats what DCM does now:** DCM's cortex has **no intrinsic stress-relaxation clock** — γ is a static setpoint. Any stored cortical stress in DCM either persists forever (elastic) or is dissipated only through the node drag (η-limited). The paper shows the *correct* physics is that cortical stress relaxes on the turnover timescale, and — critically — that this relaxation can be **partially defeated** (stress stored) in specific regimes. Adding τ_c makes DCM's cortex mechanistically viscoelastic with a physiological clock instead of an ad-hoc numerical one.

### M2 — "Mechanical memory": stress can be *stored* when relaxation decouples from turnover — a caution/handle for the compaction-RATE open problem.
- **DCM open problem it touches:** loose→compact aggregate compaction RATE (real 24–48 h; DCM's aggregate-σ compaction is drag-limited at ~100 s and is only a mechanical end-state, per project memory). The paper's τ₂/τ₁ decoupling (relaxation time ≫ turnover time at the critical condition, Fig 4E) is a concrete demonstration that a cytoskeletal material can **hold internal stress far longer than its chemical turnover time** — i.e. a slow, non-dissipative structural relaxation is real and mechanism-derivable, not a fudge.
- **Why relevant:** DCM currently has no slow structural-relaxation channel other than cadherin T1 rearrangement (open). This paper is upstream evidence that *cortical* stored stress + templating gives a second slow clock. Use it as a **conceptual anchor / literature support** for building a finite, mechanism-derived slow relaxation, NOT as a number to import. Do not fit compaction rate to this paper.

### M3 — (FF-only; DO NOT put in DCM) polymerization growth-pressure buckling, formin two-mode elongation, nucleator-pool length control, Frank director defects, templating.
- Listed here explicitly so a future session does not mistakenly graft filament-scale mechanics onto the surface mesh. These belong to `aleph/laws/` (cortex nucleation/turnover, barbed-end growth kernel, length distribution). DCM has no filaments to buckle.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

Only two equations have a continuum image; the rest are filament-scale.

### E1 — Turnover time from FRAP recovery → cortex remodeling rate. (Eq. S6, single-exponential.)
```
I(t) = I_∞ · (1 − exp(−t/τ₁))          # FRAP fluorescence recovery
k_turn ≡ 1/τ₁                          # cortex chemical turnover rate
```
- **DCM map:** τ₁ is the actin turnover time. In DCM there are no monomers to recover, so use k_turn = 1/τ₁ as the **rate at which cortical stored surface stress is reset** (drives M1's τ_c) and, optionally, as the physical clock behind the cortex **remesh** cadence (currently a numerical convenience). Symbol map: τ₁ → `cortex.tau_turnover`; k_turn → cortex surface-stress relaxation rate.

### E2 — Viscoelastic (Maxwell) surface-stress relaxation → gives DCM a cortical relaxation law. (Constructed from the paper's τ₂/τ₁ framing, Eqs S7 + Fig 4E; not written explicitly in-paper.)
```
dσ_c/dt = −σ_c/τ_c + K_A·(dε_A/dt)      # Maxwell surface element on the cortex
σ_c(t) = σ_c(0)·exp(−t/τ_c)   (relaxation, no forcing)
τ_c = τ₁     in the DISSIPATIVE regime (τ₂/τ₁ ≈ 1)
τ_c ≫ τ₁     in the STORED/decoupled regime (τ₂/τ₁ ≫ 1)   # "mechanical memory"
```
- **Symbol map to DCM state:** σ_c → deviatoric part of cortical surface tension γ carried per face; ε_A → areal strain of the triangulated surface (already computed for K_A area-elasticity); K_A → existing DCM membrane area-elastic modulus (the fast elastic branch); τ_c → new relaxation-time field. The stored-vs-dissipative branch is selected by physiological cortex state (turnover-rich MCF7 interphase → dissipative; not by formin concentration, which is a reconstitution knob we do NOT have).
- **Note:** implement as a per-face Maxwell relaxation added to the existing surface-tension force; it is additive/default-off for back-compat but MUST be ON at physiological τ_c in production (physiological-baseline rule).

### E3 — (validation-only, NOT a DCM force) filament bending energy `E_bend = (κ/2)·(L/R²)`, κ = k_B T·l_p (Eq. S2).
- Filament-scale. The only DCM contact point: it sets the scale of the membrane/cortex **bending modulus** sanity — DCM's κ_m for a curved surface patch of radius R stores `~(κ_m/2)·(A/R²)`, same functional form. Use E3 to sanity-check that DCM's κ_m is on the right *order* for cortex-mediated curvature, not to import κ (which is the filament persistence-length modulus, κ = k_BT·l_p ≈ 15 µm·k_BT ≈ 6×10⁻²⁶ J·m — a filament property, not a shell property).

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

Little here is a DCM scenario — the paper's geometry is a flat quasi-2D supported bilayer, explicitly **not** cell-sized confinement (dossier confirms). Two usable items:

- **P1 — FRAP-as-relaxation protocol (adapted):** perturb a patch of the DCM cortex tension (local δγ bump on a set of faces), release, and measure the decay time of the surface-stress field. Target: decay time ≈ τ_c (minutes-scale). This is a clean unit test that M1/E2 were implemented correctly, and it mirrors the paper's FRAP-derived τ₁ directly.
- **P2 — stress-storage vs dissipation regime test:** drive the cortex with a transient areal load (e.g. a brief turgor/ΔP pulse) and measure whether cortical stress relaxes on τ_c (dissipative, τ₂/τ₁≈1) — DCM's default and correct physiological interphase behavior. The stored/memory branch (τ₂/τ₁≫1) is NOT a physiological MCF7 target and should not be reproduced as a production state.
- **Not usable:** the microfluidic-channel xz side-view, the formin-concentration sweep, and the vortex/defect director imaging are filament-network protocols → FF.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

1. **Regime mismatch — purified, passive, quasi-2D, no myosin, not a cell.** The system is G-actin + formin + bilayer crowded flat by methylcellulose; there is **no myosin motor activity** driving the stress (bending energy is *polymerization*-driven). MCF7 cortex is 3D-closed, myosin-active, and living. So the paper's mechanism (formin-nucleation-tuned templating) is **not** the mechanism that sets MCF7 cortical tension. Adopt only the *turnover→relaxation-time* abstraction, not the biochemistry.
2. **Do NOT import the numeric FRAP τ₁ (1.7/21.8/12.6 min) as a physiological DCM value.** Those numbers are tuned by formin concentration (10 nM–1 µM), a reconstitution knob with no MCF7 counterpart. Using them would violate the physiological-baseline + no-magic-number rules. Anchor τ_c to the *cell* cortex turnover ("seconds to minutes", the paper's own Intro refs [5–7]); the in-vitro minutes only serve as an order-of-magnitude cross-check.
3. **Mechanical-memory / stored-stress branch is NOT a physiological MCF7 baseline.** The τ₂/τ₁ ≫ 1 decoupling is a special engineered condition (intermediate formin, no myosin). Reproducing it as a DCM production state would be starting from an unphysical baseline. Use it only as *literature support* that slow non-dissipative relaxation is real — the actual DCM compaction-RATE fix must remain cadherin T1 rearrangement (fine-grained, mechanism-derived), per project scope memory. **Do not fit DCM compaction rate to this paper.**
4. **Filament-scale quantities (l_p, K₃₃, growth-pressure buckling, nucleator-pool length law) must stay in FF.** Grafting them onto the surface mesh would be a fidelity-reducing lump-in-reverse (adding filament proxies to a continuum). Keep DCM at the surface-tension/κ_m level; send the rich content to the FF lens.
5. **Implementation hygiene.** The Maxwell surface term (E2) is additive; keep it default-off for back-compat but ON at physiological τ_c in production. τ_c must be derivable from a cited cortex turnover time (grid-invariant), never tuned to make a compaction or relaxation gate pass.
