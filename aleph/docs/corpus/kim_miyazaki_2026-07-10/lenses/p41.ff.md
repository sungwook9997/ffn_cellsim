# FF lens — p41

**Paper**: Ding, Chou, Deguchi, Kim. "Impacts of structural properties of myosin II filaments on force generation." eLife 2025;14:RP105236. DOI 10.7554/eLife.105236.
**Why this is a near-direct FF sibling**: This is a Kim-lab agent-based Brownian-dynamics actomyosin model — the SAME lineage as Cytosim/AFINES and the FF engine's design targets. Explicit F-actin bending/stretch beams, harmonic excluded volume, overdamped Langevin + Euler, cylinder drag, and — critically — a **bipolar myosin minifilament with an explicit backbone + N_a arms (each arm = 8 heads), catch-bond load-dependent walking/unbinding via the Parallel Cluster Model (PCM)**. FF's myosin is described as "Stam–Hocky multi-head bipolar minifilament, Hill (1938) force-velocity." This paper is a strictly more detailed sibling of that module and supplies (a) the exact geometric DOF of the minifilament, (b) the PCM catch-bond kinetic scheme (which is *more mechanistic than a Hill closed-form*), and (c) a clean emergent contractile-unit mechanism that FF can reproduce and validate. **Highest value: it directly attacks two of FF's named open problems — cortical-tension γ generation/floor, and network contraction/stress — by giving the force-vs-motor-number scaling law and its efficiency ceiling.**

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Myosin minifilament: adopt the explicit bipolar-backbone + arm geometry as first-class DOF (FF module: myosin)
Current FF myosin = "Stam–Hocky multi-head bipolar minifilament." Ding et al. make the *geometry itself* a controlling variable and show it is load-bearing on total force. Adopt three explicit structural DOF on the FF minifilament, all currently implicit/fixed:
- **backbone as a serial chain of stiff segments** (L_MB = 42 nm reference), bending stiffness κ_b,M > κ_b,A so the backbone stays straight; stretch κ_s,M1 = κ_s,A.
- **central bare zone L_bz** (no arms) — length ≈160 nm for real NMII; FF should carry it explicitly, not lump the two head clusters into one point.
- **arm count N_a and arm spacing L_sp** as parameters; each arm = N_h = 8 heads.
**Why it beats current FF**: FF currently treats the minifilament head cluster as a small set of heads without an explicit bare-zone / arm-spacing geometry. This paper proves the *bare-zone length* and *arm spacing* change generated tension by ≈2–3× at fixed head count (Fig 6), through the critical-overlap length L_c = 2L_sp(N_a/4 − 1). A lumped head cluster cannot express this — it is exactly the "replace a mechanistic process with a lumped one" the FF hard rule forbids.

### M2 — Cooperative-overlap / ACP-partitioned contractile units (FF modules: myosin + crosslinker, EMERGENT)
The central result: two motors with **no ACP between them add forces** (F_tot → F_M^max, i.e. 2× one motor); an **ACP between them buckles the intervening F-actin and counterbalances one motor** (F_tot → F_M^max/2). ACPs thereby self-partition a disordered bundle into serially-connected contractile units — *emergently*, not by assumption. FF already has explicit α-actinin/filamin bonds + explicit minifilaments + F-actin buckling (bending beams) + excluded volume, so **this mechanism should already emerge in FF for free** — this paper is the acceptance test that it does. If it does NOT emerge, FF has a bug (e.g. filaments not buckling under compression, or crosslinker not transmitting counterbalancing load).
**Why it matters for γ-floor**: cortical tension γ is set by how myosin force is transmitted through the crosslinked network. This mechanism says the *ACP density* directly gates whether motor forces add or cancel — a concrete, non-tuned lever on cortical tension that FF can test at native scale instead of scaling a lumped γ.

### M3 — PCM catch-bond walking + unbinding for NMII (FF module: myosin kinetics) — supersede/augment the Hill closed form
Walking rate k_w,M and unbinding rate k_u,M come from the **Parallel Cluster Model (Erdmann & Schwarz 2012; Erdmann et al. 2013)**, benchmarked in Jung et al. 2015. **Both rates DECREASE under higher load (catch-bond).** This is strictly more mechanistic than a Hill (1938) force-velocity closed form because it derives v(F) AND the detachment kinetics from the same head-cluster mechanochemical cycle, and it makes the low-duty-ratio ensemble behavior (few heads bound at any instant, but minifilament stays proximal) emergent.
**Why it beats current FF**: FF's Hill law gives force–velocity but does NOT give a load-dependent *unbinding* rate coupled to the same cycle. Under the FF hard rule (mechanistic > lumped: "Motor force-velocity: Hill" is listed as the *right* choice vs a linear stall-convenience, but a Hill law is still a phenomenological closed form). PCM is the finer-grained option: it replaces the Hill v(F) + a separately-parameterised Bell/Kramers off-rate with one head-cluster kinetic scheme. Recommend: keep Hill as the acceptance oracle for unloaded v and stall, but move the *runtime* motor kinetics to PCM (matches the "runtime = fine-grained, closed-forms = oracles" architecture principle exactly).

### M4 — F-actin turnover competes with motor stalling (FF module: turnover) — noted lever, not yet implemented here
Discussion (p12, citing Yu 2018; Jung 2019): actin turnover reduces forces by (i) competing with motor activity, (ii) changing connectivity over time, (iii) preventing motors from being stalled indefinitely. This is a direct pointer for FF's "filament turnover/fragmentation" open problem: turnover is not just morphological, it is a *tension regulator* that keeps motors off the stalled branch of the force–velocity curve. FF should add severing/turnover as a channel that modulates network tension, not only length distribution.

### M5 — ACP slip-vs-catch changes the contractile-unit partition (FF module: crosslinker)
Discussion (p12, citing Mulla 2022): if ACPs are **slip** bonds they unbind under the high tension at unit boundaries → motors' forces get a chance to add up (higher F_tot); if **catch** bonds, more ACPs recruited between motors → forces cancel (lower F_tot). FF already uses Bell-Evans slip for α-actinin (k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b)) — this paper says that choice directly sets the emergent contractile-unit length and thus network tension. A concrete, testable coupling: sweep FF's crosslinker slip/catch character and measure F_tot; the sign of the response is a validation of the crosslinker force-response implementation.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

**Overdamped Langevin + Euler (already FF's regime; cross-check the drag law)**
- Eq 9: `F_i − ζ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_i^T = 0` (inertia neglected).
- Eq 10: `⟨F_i^T(t)F_j^T(t)⟩ = (2 k_B T ζ_i δ_ij / Δt) δ` — fluctuation–dissipation.
- Eq 11 (cylinder drag, **port as FF's per-segment drag if not already this form**): `ζ_i = 3π μ r_{c,i} (3 + 2 r_{0,i}/r_{c,i}) / 5`, with μ = medium viscosity, r_{0,i} segment length, r_{c,i} segment diameter. → FF state: per-bead/segment drag coefficient. NOTE: FF physiological-baseline rule requires μ = cytoplasm viscosity (~65 Pa·s MCF7), NOT the in-vitro water-like μ this paper uses. Use the *form* of Eq 11, our μ.
- Eq 12: `r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (1/ζ_i)(F_i + F_i^T)Δt`. FF uses constrained L-M BAOAB (better) — Eq 12 is Euler; keep BAOAB, this is only a cross-check of the deterministic forces.

**Deterministic potentials (map 1:1 to FF beam model)**
- Eq 13 bending: `U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ_0)²`, θ_0 = 0 for actin, ACP, motor backbone. κ_b,A ↔ ℓ_p = 9 µm (their value; FF uses ℓ_p ≈ 17 µm — see §6 caveat).
- Eq 14 stretch: `U_s = ½ κ_s (r − r_0)²`. κ_s,M1 = κ_s,A.
- Eq 15 excluded volume (one-sided harmonic): `U_r = ½ κ_{r,A}(r_12 − r_{c,A})²` for r_12 < r_{c,A}, else 0. → FF LJ-repulsive is finer; Eq 15 is the cross-check.
- **Motor arm two-spring crossbridge**: transverse spring κ_s,M2 with equilibrium r_0,M2 = 13.5 nm (backbone endpoint ↔ bound actin segment); longitudinal spring κ_s,M3 with r_0,M3 = 0 nm (enforces right angle). → FF minifilament arm attachment: replace any single-spring tether with this transverse+longitudinal pair so the arm holds a right angle to the filament.

**Motor kinetics (PCM — the port)**
- Arm binding rate: `k_bind = 40·N_h = 320 s⁻¹`, ONLY when arm aligned to F-actin polarity; the two arms on one backbone point cannot bind the same F-actin; after binding, walk toward barbed end.
- Walking k_w,M and unbinding k_u,M: **PCM (Erdmann–Schwarz)**, both load-dependent and DECREASING with load (catch-bond). Exact PCM constants (F_0, d, k_m) are in Supp file 2 — "slightly different from literature." → FF state: per-arm bound/unbound + walk-step advancing the arm's actin attachment point toward the barbed end. Anchor unloaded v ≈ 140 nm/s and stall = F_st·N_h.

**Force bookkeeping (Eqs 1–8) — these become FF validation oracles, §5**
- Eq 2: `F_M^max = ½ Σ_i F_{M,z}^i`. Per-motor z-force at steady state ≈ F_st N_h N_a / 2 (two antiparallel filaments) or ≈ F_st N_h N_a (bundle, ≥1 filament per polarity).
- Eq 3: efficiency `η = F_tot / F_M^max`.
- Eq 4: `Ξ = max_{i,j∈{L,R}} [1 + Σ_{k≠i} ζ_{ik}^j]` (max motors in strongest contractile unit).
- Eq 5: `ζ_{ik}^j = 1 if L_c ≤ L_ov ≤ L_M ; = L_ov/L_c if 0<L_ov<L_c ; = 0 if L_ov=0`.
- Eq 6: `L_c = 2 L_sp (N_a/4 − 1)` — critical overlap length for full cooperativity.
- Eq 7: `F_est = F_st N_h N_a Ξ / 2` — analytic bundle-force estimate.
- Eq 8 (network): `F_M^max = ¼(Σ_i F_{M,x}^i + Σ_i F_{M,y}^i)`.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenarios)

Three clean, self-contained FF scenarios, in order of value:

**S-A Two-filament clamped force meter (minimal contractile-unit test).** Box 5×5×20 µm, PBC x,y, repulsive z. Two antiparallel F-actin (19 µm), barbed ends CLAMPED to two rigid z-walls (isometric). 2 minifilaments, 16 ACPs, N_a=24. Assembly: hold actin fixed while ACPs + motor arms bind, then release; motors walk/unbind. **Measure F_tot vs whether an ACP sits between the two motors.** This is the cheapest possible test that FF's buckling + crosslinker + minifilament together produce the 0.5–1.0 F_M^max bound. 20 random-seed runs.

**S-B Disorganized bundle (γ-generation surrogate).** 20-µm box, PBC all directions. F-actin on a 27-nm x,y grid, 2·N_F² filaments (N_F=2→7 gives 8→98 filaments), 9 µm each, random z + random polarity. R_ACP=0.04, R_M=0.005 (→ N_M 4–52). Force = sum of z-components on all segments crossing z-cross-sections every 200 nm, averaged over sections at steady state. Sweep N_F (thickness), N_M (up to ~1045), localization fraction f (0.06–1.0), and motor architecture (N_a, L_bz, L_sp). **This is the FF acceptance harness for myosin+crosslinker force generation.**

**S-C 2D network (cortex-slab surrogate).** Thin slab 20×20×0.1 µm, PBC x,y, repulsive z. F-actin ⟨L⟩~10 µm, random position/orientation, formed by stochastic nucleation k_n,A + barbed-end growth k_p,A. N_M up to ~2500. Force from 20 x + 20 y cross-sections. **Closest geometry to the FF cortex-slab** — the F_tot ∝ N_M^0.65 scaling here is the direct target for how cortical tension should scale with myosin density.

**Contraction variant (for FF crawl / contraction open problems)**: deactivate PBC and detach one bundle/network end from the boundary (as in Li 2017) → the model measures contractile *speed* and *extent* instead of isometric force. Trade-off stated: more contractile units → higher speed but lower peak force; contractile speed ∝ motor density. Directly usable to probe FF's grid-drag crawl-speed issue against a contraction-speed law.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for FF observables

Acceptance oracles (agent-based sim, not wet-lab — so cross-check class, per FF oracle policy). Dimensionally clean:

1. **Two-filament bound**: 0.5 F_M^max ≤ F_tot ≤ F_M^max. Numeric: upper ≈0.55 nN (no ACP between), lower ≈0.28 nN (ACP between), N_a=24, 2 motors, 16 ACP. Steady state by ~50–100 s.
2. **Bundle F_tot vs thickness** (R_ACP=0.04, R_M=0.005, N_a=24): N_F=2/4/6/7 → F_tot ≈ 0.6 / 1.3 / 2.9 / 3.9 nN.
3. **Efficiency**: η ≈ 0.25 at N_F=2 (≈1/N_M, N_M=4); η ≈ 0.12 for N_F=4,6,7 (n.s. among them).
4. **η vs N_M law (bundle, N_F=7)**: η ∝ 1/N_M at low N_M, **plateau η ≈ 0.08** at high N_M; η halves as N_M 10→1000. F_tot slope → 1 (log-log) at high N_M; F_tot up to ~15 nN at N_M~1045.
5. **Localization**: F_tot and η maximal at f=0.06 (centered motors), ~15 nN, Ξ≈4; drop to ~4 nN, Ξ≈1 at f=1.
6. **Architecture (bundle)**: F_tot vs L_M rises 1.5→4.5 nN via N_a (4→48); L_bz curve ≈ N_a curve; L_sp curve noticeably lower (~2–2.7 nN). → long bare zone beats large arm spacing at equal L_M.
7. **Network scaling**: **F_tot ∝ N_M^0.65 ≈ √N_M** (vs ∝N_M for bundles), η slope ≈ −0.4 log-log, F_tot 1–~50 nN over N_M 10–2006. L_bz highest, N_a intermediate, L_sp flat in the L_M sweep.
8. **Estimator identities** (unit/dimension checks): L_c = 2 L_sp (N_a/4 − 1); F_est = F_st N_h N_a Ξ/2; Ξ ∈ [1, N_M].
9. **Kinetic anchors**: unloaded v ≈ 140 nm/s; stall = F_st·N_h with F_st ≈ 5.7 pN.

The **bundle-vs-network exponent (1 vs 0.65)** is the single most valuable target: it tells FF how emergent cortical tension should scale with myosin count as a function of network order (aligned bundle vs isotropic slab) — directly informs the γ-floor open problem without any lumped-γ tuning.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **In-vitro purified vs living cortex.** Purely F-actin + one ACP + NMII, no membrane, no turgor, no ATP gradient, water-like μ. FF must NOT inherit their μ or their zero-turgor baseline: run the FF scenarios at physiological cytoplasm viscosity (~65 Pa·s) and, for the cortex-slab, on the pressurised shell. Use the *scaling laws and mechanism*, not the absolute nN values, as the primary carry-over — the absolute forces are geometry/μ-specific.
- **ℓ_p mismatch**: they use actin persistence length 9 µm; FF uses ℓ_p ≈ 17 µm (Isambert range spans both). Keep FF's 17 µm. Their buckling threshold and unit lengths will differ quantitatively — reproduce the *sign and scaling*, not the exact L_c in nm.
- **Permanent ACPs**: they set ACP k_off = 0 (never unbind). FF's α-actinin is Bell-Evans slip (finer, correct). This means FF's contractile-unit partition will be *dynamic*, not frozen — expect FF F_tot to sit between their slip and catch predictions (Discussion p12). Do not "fix" FF to match their permanent-ACP numbers; instead validate that reducing FF k_off0 → their permanent limit recovers their F_tot.
- **PCM constants are in Supp file 2 and are modified** ("slightly different F_0, d, k_m from literature"). If porting PCM into FF runtime, pull Supp file 2 exact values; do NOT guess — a guessed PCM constant would be a magic number (halt/surface per hard rule). Alternatively keep FF Hill runtime and use PCM only as an oracle.
- **Coarse actin segment (140 nm) and coarse box**: their model is at a coarser filament discretization and MUCH smaller system (≤98 bundle filaments, ≤2500 network motors) than FF native (Nc≈266k, 38000 cortex filaments). Their numbers are for SMALL systems — reproduce the *mechanism and exponents* at FF native scale; do not treat their 15 nN as a native-cortex prediction. Any FF conclusion must still be confirmed on the native full-compartment cell (HARD rule).
- **2D network ≠ 3D curved cortex.** Their slab is flat 0.1-µm thin with z-repulsion; the FF cortex is a curved shell under turgor. The √N_M law is a slab result — treat as a hypothesis to test on the shell, expecting geometry (curvature, prestress) to modify the exponent, not as a guaranteed cortical law.
- **No isoform/species split beyond NMII vs skeletal/smooth.** FF targets MCF7 non-muscle myosin II — use the NMII branch (low duty ratio, bare zone ~160 nm), not the skeletal parameters.
