# DCM lens — p45

**Paper:** Li et al. 2025, *Characterization, Enrichment, and Computational Modeling of Cross-Linked Actin Networks in Transformed Trabecular Meshwork Cells*, IOVS 66(2):65. DOI 10.1167/iovs.66.2.65.
**Modeling engine:** Taeyoon Kim's agent-based F-actin/ACP Brownian–Langevin framework (refs 33–36). Two sim geometries: (A) bulk network rheology; (B) **triangulated-mesh nucleus ± perinuclear cross-linked actin network (CLAN), under compression, with LINC-mimicking cytoskeleton→nucleus links.**

**Bottom line for DCM:** This paper is *mostly* an FF/agent-based paper, BUT geometry B is written in exactly the DCM idiom — a **triangulated sphere nucleus with volume + surface-area conservation potentials, node contact under compression, and explicit cytoskeleton→nucleus tethers (LINC).** That gives DCM (1) a validated form for the nucleus compartment's constraint potentials, (2) a genuinely missing mechanism — the **LINC-complex cortex/cytoskeleton→nucleus mechanical coupling**, which DCM currently does not have — and (3) a clean, quantitative **nucleus-compression validation ladder** plus an **AFM whole-cell modulus** target. The bulk-network anisotropy (tension ≫ shear ≫ compression) is an FF target, not a DCM one, and is flagged as such.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — LINC-complex tether: cortex/cytoskeleton → nucleus mechanical coupling (NEW compartment coupling; highest-value item)
- **DCM module:** nucleus compartment ↔ cortex/membrane coupling. Currently DCM's nucleus is a "stiffer inclusion" that interacts with the shell only through **contact/excluded-volume (node–face penalty)**. It has **no tensile attachment** to the surrounding cytoskeleton.
- **Change:** add an **attractive harmonic tether** connecting a fraction of cortex (or perinuclear cytoskeleton) nodes to the nearest point on the nucleus surface mesh — the paper's LINC mechanism (ref 38, Bouzid/Kim). In the paper, **20% of F-actins** are tethered to the nucleus; the *effect* is dramatic: with the tether, nucleus resistance to compression roughly **doubles** vs. the contact-only case (see §5).
- **Why it beats what DCM does now:** DCM can only *push* on the nucleus (contact). A living cell's cortex/actin also *pulls/holds* the nucleus via LINC, so the nucleus resists deformation even under **tension and lateral** load, not just direct compression. Without this coupling DCM will under-predict nuclear-deformation resistance and cannot represent perinuclear-network stiffening, confinement-driven nuclear shape change, or force transmission from cortex to nucleus — all on the open-problem list (confinement response, membrane–cortex coupling). This is the fine-grained mechanistic option (explicit tether springs) over the lumped alternative (a single global nucleus modulus).

### M2 — Nucleus compartment constraint potentials in the SimuCell3D form (confirm/align DCM's nucleus energy)
- **DCM module:** nucleus mesh elastic energy.
- **Change:** adopt/confirm the exact pair of **enclosed-volume** and **surface-area** conservation potentials the paper uses for its meshed nucleus (Eqs. below). This is the same physics family DCM already uses for the cell shell; the paper validates that a **triangulated sphere with (V-conservation + A-conservation + bending/EV)** reproduces measured nuclear compression stress. Use it to anchor DCM's nucleus stiffness (E_nuc ~1–10 kPa) against a concrete stress-vs-strain curve rather than a bare modulus number.
- **Why:** DCM already lists a nucleus with E_nuc but has no validated stress–strain target for it. This paper supplies one (§5), letting the nucleus κ_V / κ_A / bending be *derived to match a measured compression response* instead of guessed.

### M3 — AFM whole-cell indentation as a DCM scenario (map cytoskeletal state → measured modulus)
- **DCM module:** contact (node–face penalty + IPC), plus a spherical-indenter boundary.
- **Change:** implement the paper's AFM protocol as a DCM scenario: **10 µm spherical indenter, indentation ≤400 nm, tip velocity 800 nm/s, Hertz fit ν=0.5**, and read off effective Young's modulus. Run it with/without the M1 LINC tether + a stiffened perinuclear region to reproduce the CLAN+ vs CLAN− modulus split.
- **Why:** gives DCM a *whole-cell* mechanical observable (effective E) tied to an experiment, closing the loop between the internal compartment parameters and an AFM number. Complements the aspiration/AFM levers already in the DCM open list.

### M4 — Osmotic deswelling / macromolecular crowding as a physiological perturbation of the enclosed-volume constraint
- **DCM module:** osmoregulation / turgor (enclosed-volume Π₀, γ = ΔP·R/2).
- **Change:** the paper drives cell shrinkage with **PEG300 osmotic deswelling** (2–3% w/v), producing measurable cell-area decrease (Suppl. Fig S3) and stiffening. This maps directly to a **step in the DCM osmotic-pressure / target-volume V₀** (lower external water potential → lower V₀ → cortex compaction). Use it as a *perturbation from the physiological baseline* (not a new baseline): start at resting Π₀≈40 Pa / V₀, then impose a deswelling step and measure the shape/stiffness response.
- **Why:** it is a clean, purely-mechanical osmotic lever that the DCM enclosed-volume machinery already supports; gives a perturbation protocol with a directional experimental result (deswell → smaller area → stiffer).

> **NOT adopted (FF-scope, flagged):** the bulk-network *rheology* (geometry A) — triangular-lattice CLAN, shear/tensile/compressive at fixed strain rate, virial boundary stress. That is explicit-filament territory (FF), not surface-mesh DCM. The **network stiffness anisotropy** (tension ≫ shear ≫ compression) is a strong FF validation target but is *not* something DCM's continuum shell should try to reproduce filament-by-filament.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (to the surface-mesh / continuum level)

The paper prints these only descriptively (constants live in Suppl. Materials + refs 33–36). Canonical Kim-lab forms, mapped to DCM state:

**E1 — Overdamped Langevin update (already DCM's integrator family).**
`ζ_i · dr_i/dt = F_i^det + F_i^thermal` → forward-Euler `r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (Δt/ζ_i)(F_i^det + F_i^thermal)`
- Map: `r_i` = DCM mesh node position; `ζ_i` = per-node drag from cytoplasm viscosity (DCM uses η≈65 Pa·s → per-node drag); `F_i^thermal` = Gaussian white noise, `⟨F^th(t)·F^th(t')⟩ = 2 k_B T ζ δ(t−t')`. DCM's Warp integrator is the same overdamped/Langevin family — this only confirms the drag/noise balance, no change needed.

**E2 — Nucleus enclosed-volume conservation potential.**
`U_V = ½ κ_V (V − V₀)² / V₀`
- Map: `V` = volume enclosed by the nucleus surface mesh; `V₀` = resting nuclear volume (sphere Ø5 µm → V₀ = (4/3)π(2.5 µm)³ ≈ 65.4 µm³); `κ_V` = nucleus volumetric stiffness → DCM `nucleus.K_vol`. Same functional form DCM uses for the cell shell's enclosed volume; port to the nucleus sub-mesh.

**E3 — Nucleus surface-area conservation potential.**
`U_A = ½ κ_A (A − A₀)² / A₀`
- Map: `A` = total nucleus mesh area; `A₀` = resting area (sphere Ø5 µm → A₀ = 4π(2.5)² ≈ 78.5 µm²); `κ_A` → DCM `nucleus.K_A`. (Per-face variant `Σ ½ k_a (a_f − a_f0)²/a_f0` is the fine-grained option DCM already prefers for the shell.)

**E4 — Bending + extensional + excluded-volume (deterministic mesh forces).**
Extensional edge spring `F_s = −κ_s (ℓ − ℓ₀)`; bending `U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ₀)²`, `F = −∂U_b/∂r`; EV repulsion `F_r = −κ_r δ` for overlap δ>0.
- Map: `κ_s` = nucleus-mesh edge stiffness; `κ_b` = nucleus bending → DCM `nucleus.kappa_bend`; `θ` = dihedral/segment angle; `κ_r` / `δ` = node–face penalty overlap (Ericson) already in DCM contact. No new form; these are DCM's existing membrane/cortex potentials applied to the nucleus sub-mesh.

**E5 — LINC tether (the NEW one — attractive cortex→nucleus harmonic spring).**
`F_link = −κ_L (d − d₀)  \hat{n}`, applied to the tethered cortex node toward its anchor point on the nucleus surface; `d` = current node-to-anchor distance, `d₀` = rest length (≈ shell gap; set to physiological perinuclear spacing), `κ_L` = LINC link stiffness.
- Map: subset of cortex nodes (paper: **20%**) each carry a spring to the nearest nucleus mesh vertex. In DCM: add a `linc_links` list `(cortex_node_id, nucleus_vertex_id, κ_L, d₀)`; force is Warp-differentiable. This is the mechanistic realization of "physical links between nucleus and F-actin / LINC complex."

**E6 — Boundary/contact stress observable (virial-style).**
Geometry A: `σ = (Σ F_component on clamped +y-boundary ends) / A_boundary`.
Geometry B (the DCM-relevant one): `σ = F_resist,total / A_contact`, `F_resist,total` = total reaction (nucleus + attached cytoskeleton) on the compressing boundary, `A_contact` = nucleus–boundary contact area.
- Map: for a DCM AFM/plate-compression scenario, read the reaction force on the indenter/plate and divide by contact area → effective stress; matches the paper's measurement protocol so the validation numbers in §5 are directly comparable.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce as DCM scenarios

- **S1 — Nucleus compression ± perinuclear coupling (the flagship DCM scenario).** DCM cell with nucleus sphere Ø5 µm; build a stiffened perinuclear shell (r_N→1.1 r_N) and add M1 LINC tethers on 20% of perinuclear nodes. Compress along one axis to ε=−0.2 (snapshots to −0.5) at −0.01 s⁻¹; measure reaction-force stress (E6). Run three arms: (a) nucleus only, (b) nucleus + perinuclear network no-link, (c) + LINC link. Reproduce the stress ladder in §5.
- **S2 — Whole-cell AFM indentation.** 10 µm sphere, ≤400 nm indent, 800 nm/s, Hertz ν=0.5 → effective E. Toggle perinuclear stiffening/LINC to get CLAN+ vs CLAN− modulus split.
- **S3 — Osmotic deswelling perturbation.** From resting baseline (Π₀≈40 Pa), impose a PEG300-equivalent target-volume/osmotic step; measure top-down cell-area decrease and stiffness change.
- **S4 — Substrate-stiffness / durotaxis context.** Glass (stiff) vs 2.36 kPa hydrogel substrate; the paper shows stiffer substrate → stiffer cell → more perinuclear network. Usable as a DCM substrate-stiffness scenario input, though the CLAN-formation step itself is biology DCM won't model.
- **S5 — Microfluidic constriction (confinement).** 7 µm constrictions at 30° sort cells by stiffness. Usable as a DCM **cell-sized confinement** scenario (squeeze a Ø15 µm cell through a 7 µm channel; measure transit / restoring force) — directly on the "confinement response" open-problem list.

The **nucleus stress ladder 2300 → 3200 → 6200 Pa** is the primary DCM validation: it isolates the *incremental* effect of (network) and (network+LINC) on nuclear compression resistance, which is exactly what S1's three arms produce. It lets DCM's `κ_L` + perinuclear-stiffness be **derived to match the 6200/3200 ≈ 1.9× ratio** rather than tuned to a single number.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Cell type mismatch:** GTM3L = transformed *glaucomatous trabecular-meshwork* cells, not MCF7. The nucleus mechanics + LINC coupling are cell-type-generic (LINC/nesprin–SUN is universal), so M1/M2 transfer; but absolute stiffness numbers (~4 kPa cell, nucleus stress ladder) are TM-specific and should be treated as **order-of-magnitude / ratio targets**, not MCF7 setpoints. Adapt by matching the **ratio** (LINC → ~2× nuclear stiffening) rather than the absolute Pa, and keep MCF7 baselines (R=7.5 µm, γ=0.15/1.5 mN/m, η=65 Pa·s) unchanged — do not overwrite physiological setpoints with TM values.
- **CLAN is a special structure, not general cortex:** the huge tensile-resistance anisotropy comes from a *triangular-lattice cross-linked* actin geometry specific to glaucomatous cells. DCM's continuum cortex should NOT be re-parameterized to reproduce that anisotropy; only the *nucleus-coupling* consequence (M1) is portable. Reproducing the anisotropy itself is FF's job.
- **Missing force constants:** κ_V, κ_A, κ_s, κ_b, κ_r, κ_L, Δt, drag are **not in this article** — they are in Suppl. Materials + refs 33–36. Do NOT invent values to hit the stress ladder (that would be fitting-to-outcome, a hard-rule violation). Derive κ_L from a LINC single-link stiffness datum (Bouzid/Kim ref 38) or surface to PI; derive nucleus κ_V/κ_A from E_nuc (1–10 kPa) via standard shell relations, then *check* against the stress ladder — don't fit to it.
- **Spherical-nucleus idealization:** the paper uses a Ø5 µm sphere for an oval nucleus (authors defend this as conservative). Fine for DCM's nucleus compartment; note the real shape is ellipsoidal if quantitative nuclear-shape observables are later needed.
- **Quasi-static, strain-controlled protocol:** strain rates 0.001–0.01 s⁻¹ are slow (near-quasi-static). DCM must run S1/S2 at matching quasi-static rates from the **physiological baseline** (resting, turgor-on, `--from-resting`), never from a floppy zero-pressure shell — otherwise the compression stress is meaningless. The 20% LINC fraction and perinuclear shell must be initialized as part of the resting state, ON, not added as a perturbation-only afterthought.
- **In-vitro purified-ish network vs living cell:** geometry A is essentially a reconstituted network; irrelevant to DCM. Geometry B embeds it around a nucleus but still omits membrane/cytoplasm/turgor — DCM *adds* those, so DCM's S1 is a strict superset and should reproduce the *ratio* even though absolute stress differs once the full compartment stack is ON.
