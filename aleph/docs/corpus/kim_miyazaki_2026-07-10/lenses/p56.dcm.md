# DCM lens — p56

**Paper:** Chiba, Miyazaki & Ishiwata (2014) *Quantitative Analysis of the Lamellarity of Giant Liposomes Prepared by the Inverted Emulsion Method*, Biophys. J. 107(2):346–354. DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2014.05.039.

**Bottom line for DCM:** This is a **wet-lab reconstitution + optical-quantification method paper**. It contains **one analytic geometric-optics fit (Eq. 1)** for fluorescence-vs-radius, lamellarity statistics, and protocol/optics constants. It provides **no mechanical force law, no tension/modulus/viscosity, no bond/motor kinetics**. Its value to the DCM engine is **peripheral and non-mechanistic**: (a) it is the canonical justification for DCM's **single-bilayer (unilamellar) membrane assumption**; (b) it fixes the **bilayer thickness (~5.2 nm)** DCM uses as its membrane sheet; (c) it is the methodological anchor for the **cell-sized-confinement / actin-cortex-in-vesicle** reconstitution lineage (Pontani/Sykes, Carvalho/Sykes) that DCM's cortex compartment idealizes; (d) it prescribes an **osmotic-balance protocol** that is a useful sanity template for DCM's physiological-baseline osmotic-pressure setup. **Do NOT register any of its numbers as DCM validation gates** — σ=0.913 µm and δ=0.143 µm are microscope-optics parameters, not cell physics.

Relevance to DCM: **LOW (1/5).** Adopt nothing into the runtime; use as a membrane-baseline / reconstitution-reference citation only.

## 1. Mechanisms to adopt

Honest answer: **none of the paper's mechanisms are DCM runtime mechanisms.** The paper models fluorescence optics, not cell mechanics. The following are *modeling-justification* and *protocol* levers, not new forces:

- **Membrane compartment — keep the single-bilayer (unilamellar) idealization; this paper is its evidence.**
  DCM's membrane is one closed triangulated sheet (K_A, κ_m, surface viscosity). This paper quantitatively proves that inverted-emulsion GUVs — the standard experimental cell model DCM is compared against — are **~97% unilamellar (98.0% default, >90% under every condition)**. WHY it matters: it licenses DCM to model the plasma membrane as **exactly one bilayer** with a single κ_m/K_A rather than a stochastic stack, and confirms the experimental substrate that any future DCM↔GUV comparison would use is genuinely single-bilayer. This does not change DCM code; it grounds an existing assumption.

- **Cortex/membrane-coupling scenario — the actin-cortex-in-GUV reconstitution is the clean testbed DCM's cortex idealizes.**
  The paper encapsulates 50 µM F-actin and cites the reconstituted-cortex line (Pontani 2009 ref 16; Murrell 2011 ref 17; Carvalho/Sykes 2013 ref 12, "actomyosin cortical tension drives shape change"; Murrell 2014 ref 18, "liposome adhesion generates traction stress"). WHY it matters for DCM: these follow-on papers (not this one) carry the **cortical-tension γ and adhesion-traction** numbers DCM's open γ-generation problem needs. This paper is the *gateway citation* — mine refs 12/16/17/18 next, not this method paper, for γ.

- **Osmotic-baseline protocol — a template for DCM's physiological-baseline enclosed-volume setup.**
  The paper balances osmolality across the membrane deliberately (inner 150 mM sucrose + 350 mM glucose; outer 500 mM glucose) so there is **no net osmotic gradient** and the vesicle is a relaxed, non-lysing sphere, with a *slight* inner-density excess only for sedimentation. WHY it matters: it is a concrete reminder that the physiological baseline for a bare membrane vesicle is **osmotically matched (near-zero net ΔΠ across the bilayer)** — the turgor DCM carries (Π₀≈40 Pa) is the *living-cell* excess above this matched state, generated by active osmoregulation, not a bilayer property. Useful as a sanity check that DCM's ΔP is attributed to the cell's regulation, not mis-assigned to the passive membrane.

## 2. Equations to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

**None are mechanical.** The paper's single equation is an imaging model and does **not** map to DCM state (γ, Π, η, K_A, κ_m, nodes, faces). Transcribed for completeness and to document *why it does not port*:

**Eq. 1 (apparent equatorial membrane fluorescence of a unilamellar vesicle):**

```
I(r) = I0 · ∫_{−arccos((r−δ)/r)}^{+arccos((r−δ)/r)}  exp( − r² sin²θ / (2σ²) ) · r dθ
```

- Symbols: `r` = vesicle radius; `I0` = per-unit-area membrane fluorescence intensity (a.u.); `δ` = camera pixel size (0.143 µm, fixed); `σ` = out-of-focus Gaussian leakage depth ≈ focal depth (0.913 µm, fitted); `θ` = radial-ray angle. Integration window ±arccos((r−δ)/r) = the arc of the equatorial ring lying within one pixel of the focal plane; the Gaussian `exp(−z²/2σ²)` with `z ≈ r sinθ` weights out-of-focus leakage.
- **Mapping to DCM: N/A.** No symbol corresponds to a DCM mechanical field. `I0` is a fluorophore density readout, `σ`/`δ` are microscope properties. There is nothing here to advect, integrate over faces, or add to a force balance.
- **Sole legitimate DCM use:** IF a DCM run is ever rendered/compared to fluorescence microscopy of a labeled membrane, Eq. 1 is the **forward optical model** to synthesize the expected equatorial intensity from a DCM mesh surface (per-face area × I0, blurred by the σ/δ point-spread) so simulation and micrograph are compared in the same observable. This is a *visualization/validation-instrument* utility, not a physics port. Low priority.

**Lamellarity classification rule (decision law, not physics):** assign lamellarity n when F.I. ∈ [(n−0.5)·I(r), (n+0.5)·I(r)); thresholds at 1.5/2.5/3.5/4.5/5.5 × I(r). Irrelevant to DCM (DCM membrane is unilamellar by construction).

## 4. Geometry / protocol to reproduce

- **Cell-sized spherical confinement, unilamellar shell, R ≈ few µm, physiological buffer, osmotically matched.** This is exactly the DCM single-cell geometry (closed triangulated sphere, R≈7.5 µm MCF7-like). The paper validates that this is a *realizable, single-bilayer* object — so a DCM "reconstituted-vesicle" scenario (bare membrane + encapsulated actin cortex, no nucleus/adhesion) is a physically grounded reduced-complexity test case. **Adaptation:** run it as a *dev-only reduced scenario* to isolate membrane–cortex coupling, but per the native/full-compartment HARD rule, **any conclusion must be reconfirmed on the full MCF7 stack** — a bare GUV is not the physiological baseline for a living cell.
- **Encapsulated-cortex protocol → DCM cortex-inside-membrane scenario.** 50 µM F-actin (and the actomyosin/HMM refs 19–20) inside a GUV is the template for DCM's cortex compartment mechanically coupled to (but distinct from) the membrane sheet. Useful as the geometry for testing **membrane–cortex adhesion/detachment (bleb nucleation)**: a cortex that locally delaminates from the bilayer is precisely the GUV-cortex spreading/peeling geometry of Murrell 2011 (ref 17).
- **Osmotic-balance setup as a DCM enclosed-volume sanity protocol.** Initialize the DCM vesicle with matched inner/outer osmolality → confirm it holds a relaxed sphere at ΔP=0 (pure κ_m/K_A shape), THEN add the active osmoregulatory excess to reach Π₀≈40 Pa and re-measure shape/tension. This two-step init cleanly separates passive-membrane from active-turgor contributions and matches the paper's deliberate osmotic matching.
- **α-hemolysin permeabilization → (optional) DCM osmotic-shock scenario.** Pore opening triggers inner/outer solute exchange → osmotic re-equilibration on a ~30–60 min timescale. If DCM ever models regulated-volume decrease / osmotic shock, this is the experimental analog (permeabilize → ΔΠ collapses → volume relaxes). Low priority; DCM has no membrane-transport module today.

## 5. Validation targets for DCM observables

**This paper provides NO mechanical DCM validation targets** (no tension, shape index, division force, ring-closure rate, compaction, contact angle, or flow speed). Its quantitative outputs are:

- Unilamellar fraction 98.0% (inverted emulsion) vs 49.8% (hydration); mean 97.2% across all conditions; 92.1% at 10 mM lipid.
- Optics fit σ=0.913 µm, δ=0.143 µm.
- Actin-polymerization onset ~30 min, plateau ~60 min after permeabilization (a diffusion/reaction timescale of the buffer-exchange assay, **not** a DCM-mechanism rate).

**None of these should be entered as a ValidationGate for DCM.** The single defensible "target" is qualitative: a DCM membrane compartment should be modeled as **exactly one bilayer** (the paper's ~97% unilamellar result is the license, not a numeric gate). Register in the KB as a **method/reference source** (unilamellarity of inverted-emulsion GUVs; bilayer thickness ~5.2 nm; α-hemolysin pore 2.8 nm / stem 5.2 nm), explicitly flagged **not a force/rate validation datum** — consistent with the dossier's TAG-validation = No verdict.

## 6. Risks / caveats

- **Regime mismatch — purified in-vitro GUV vs living MCF7.** This is a **protein-free (or single-cargo) lipid vesicle**, not a cell. It has **no cortex tension, no myosin, no adhesion, no nucleus, no cytoplasm viscosity** as measured quantities. Pulling any "cell mechanics" number from it would be a category error. Adaptation: use only for the membrane-sheet assumption and reconstitution-geometry justification; get γ/traction from the *cited* Sykes/Murrell papers (refs 12,16,17,18), not this one.
- **Optics constants are instrument-specific, not physical.** σ=0.913 µm and δ=0.143 µm depend on the microscope; they are meaningless outside this setup and must never leak into a DCM config. The paper itself states they are "dependent on the microscopy system, but independent of the sample."
- **Osmotic matching ≠ zero turgor for a living cell (physiological-baseline rule).** The GUV baseline is osmotically *matched* (ΔΠ≈0 across the bilayer). A living MCF7 is **not** at ΔΠ=0 — it actively maintains Π₀≈40 Pa turgor via osmoregulation. Do NOT use this paper to justify zeroing DCM's turgor; that would repeat the 2026-06-04 force-free-shell failure. The correct read: the *passive bilayer* contributes ~0 baseline tension, and DCM's cortical/turgor tension is *actively generated on top of* that — which is exactly the pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2 picture DCM already uses.
- **Possible oil contamination inside the bilayer (Discussion).** The paper flags that inverted-emulsion GUVs may retain trace mineral oil in the bilayer, altering membrane dynamics vs electroformed vesicles — though it notes their viscoelastic response is *closer to real cell membrane* than electroformed. Implication for DCM: if a future DCM κ_m/surface-viscosity is calibrated against inverted-emulsion GUV data, note this systematic; prefer cell-membrane-derived moduli for the physiological baseline.
- **2D equatorial imaging, static vesicles.** All measurements are on settled, static, spherical vesicles at the equatorial plane — no dynamics, no deformation, no 3D shape statistics. Nothing here constrains DCM's dynamic observables (division, confinement response, compaction rate). Do not over-read.

**Net recommendation:** Do not modify the DCM runtime for this paper. Cite it as (1) the unilamellar-membrane justification, (2) bilayer-thickness ~5.2 nm reference, (3) the reconstitution-geometry / osmotic-balance protocol template, and (4) a pointer to the Sykes/Murrell cortex-in-GUV papers that actually carry cortical-tension mechanics. KB verdict: method/reference source, **not** a validation gate.
