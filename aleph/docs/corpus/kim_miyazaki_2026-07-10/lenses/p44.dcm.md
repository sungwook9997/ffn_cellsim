# DCM lens — p44

**Paper:** Bidone, Jung, Maruri, Borau, Kamm, Kim (2017) "Morphological Transformation and Force Generation of Active Cytoskeletal Networks." PLoS Comput Biol 13(1):e1005277. DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005277.

**One-line verdict for DCM:** This is a *sub-grid* (fine-grained filament) paper — its actin/ACP/motor mechanics live inside the FF engine, not the DCM surface mesh. But it delivers **one first-class DCM lever**: a rigorous, quantitative demonstration of the **load-sharing → per-bond-force → force-dependent off-rate → cluster/bundle lifetime** law that DCM's cadherin catch-bond junction cluster already implements — plus a governing principle that **structural compaction RATE is gated by cross-linker/bond turnover, not by the driving force alone**, which directly supports DCM's open conclusion that loose→compact aggregate compaction needs T1-rearrangement (bond remodeling) escalation, not more aggregate-σ pull. Everything else (buckling, filament bending, minifilament arms) is FF-only. **DCM relevance: low-moderate (2/5)** — principle-level, not equation-port-level.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Junction cluster stability law: load-sharing sets lifetime (STRONG, validates DCM's cadherin cluster architecture)
**DCM module:** cell–cell cadherin catch-bond junction, load-sharing clusters (`dcm/` junction interface; the S1 redesign — load-sharing cluster that dissolves only as m→0, lifetime emergent).
**Paper's central result (Fig 2C–2F, Discussion p13):** bundle **sustainability ∝ R_ACP (cross-linker density) and ∝ 1/R_M (per-bond load)**. Mechanism: more cross-linkers share the motor load → **smaller force per cross-linker** → (via slip-bond Bell off-rate, Eq 8) → **fewer unbinding events** → the load-bearing structure survives; conversely few cross-linkers → each overloaded → runaway unbinding → the bundle disintegrates into aggregates and tension relaxes to ~0.
**Why it beats what DCM does now:** DCM already builds cadherin clusters, but this paper is the quantitative, mechanistic *proof of the governing law* — that a cluster's lifetime is set by (total load)/(N bonds) fed through a force-dependent off-rate, and that below a critical bond count the cluster fails catastrophically (not gracefully). It tells DCM to keep the junction **explicitly N-bond load-sharing** (not a single lumped bond with an effective off-rate), because the failure is *non-linear in N* (a lumped bond cannot reproduce the "disintegrate into aggregates" collapse). This is exactly the fine-grained-over-lumped hard rule, applied to the junction. Use it as an acceptance oracle: a DCM junction cluster's mean lifetime should *rise* with cluster occupancy and *fall* super-linearly with per-junction interfacial load.

### M2 — Compaction RATE is gated by bond turnover, not driving force (STRONG, directly supports the T1-escalation conclusion)
**DCM module:** aggregate compaction / loose→compact transition; remesh + T1 rearrangement (open problem).
**Paper (Fig 2G, Fig 6, Discussion p14):** compaction time is set by cross-linker density and turnover — networks with *more* cross-linkers compact *slower* but *tighter/more stably*; and imposing **actin turnover with intermediate depolymerization-inhibition (ξ_d,A≈0.6)** is what lets a low-cross-linker bundle keep reorganizing and *sustain* tension instead of jamming into dead aggregates. The reorganization (filament reorientation, 180° flips, antiparallel-pair formation — Fig 4F, S5) that produces the compact state **requires bonds to release and rebind**; a network that cannot turn over jams.
**Why it matters for DCM:** DCM's memory-of-record states aggregate-σ compaction reproduces only the *mechanical end-state* and its rate is drag-limited (~100 s), whereas real 24–48 h compaction "needs cadherin T1 rearrangement escalation, not aggregate-σ." This paper is the fine-grained analogue of that exact conclusion: **compaction of a connected active network is rate-limited by bond release/rebind (turnover) that permits neighbor exchange, not by the contractile driving force.** It argues DCM's T1 module should be driven by *cadherin bond turnover kinetics* (a junction that must unbind to let neighbors swap), giving the compaction a real, kinetics-set timescale — not a drag-limited σ-pull ceiling.

### M3 — Cortex γ generation has a mechanistic, density-limited ceiling (WEAK–MODERATE, informs the γ-floor open problem)
**DCM module:** actomyosin cortex surface tension γ generation (γ-floor open problem).
**Paper:** an emergent, purely-mechanistic actomyosin network condenses and builds **~4 nN** max tension (Fig 2C) from myosin (f_stall 5.7 pN/head, N_h=8) + cross-linkers, and the magnitude scales with *both* motor and cross-linker density (Fig 2E). Suppressing the sub-grid ingredient that DCM cannot represent (filament buckling) collapses the tension to near-zero (Fig 3D).
**Why it matters:** DCM's γ-floor work concluded the deficit is a **force-magnitude/density limit** (buckling/turnover/crosslink ruled out as levers). This paper is an independent, fine-grained confirmation that (a) network tension is genuinely density-limited (∝ R_M·R_ACP), and (b) a large fraction of the achievable tension *comes from filament buckling* — a mechanism DCM's smooth surface mesh structurally cannot carry. **Implication for DCM:** the cortex γ a surface mesh can generate from a distributed active-tension term is a *lower bound*; the buckling-derived contribution is inaccessible to DCM and must be supplied as an emergent parameter from FF (or as a boundary condition), NOT tuned in. This is a "surface" for the DCM↔FF handoff, not a DCM equation.

### M4 — Turnover with intermediate protection stabilizes the active layer (WEAK)
**DCM module:** cortex remesh / turnover representation.
**Paper (Eq 9, Fig 6G):** k_d,A = k⁰_d,A(1−ξ_d,A); sustainability peaks at *intermediate* ξ_d,A — too little turnover jams, too much dissolves the load-bearing bonds. **DCM takeaway:** if/when DCM adds a cortex-turnover or junction-maturation "protection" term (bound crosslinker/junction shields its filament from depolymerization/dissolution), expect a non-monotonic optimum, not "more turnover = better." Do not port the filament kinetics; carry only the *shape of the law*.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

Almost nothing ports as-is — the governing equations (Eqs 1–7) are per-segment Langevin/harmonic-spring filament mechanics that live in FF. Two force-dependent kinetic laws port *in form* to DCM's junction:

### Eq 8 — Bell slip-bond off-rate (already present in DCM as the catch-bond variant; use as cross-check for the slip limit)
```
k_u,ACP = k⁰_u,ACP · exp( λ_u,ACP · |F_s,ACP| / (k_B T) )      if r ≥ r₀,ACP
        = k⁰_u,ACP                                             if r < r₀,ACP     (Eq 8)
```
**Symbol → DCM state:** `F_s,ACP` → tensile load on a single cadherin bond in a junction cluster = (interfacial line-tension load on the contact patch) / (N bound cadherins). `k⁰_u,ACP` → zero-force cadherin off-rate. `λ_u,ACP` → reactive compliance (Bell length). **DCM uses Rakshit KU-4.2 catch-bond for cadherin, not this slip form** — so Eq 8 is a *slip-limit oracle*, not a replacement: it says that once a catch-bond is past its catch peak (high load), it must revert to Bell-slip acceleration, and the load fed in is the *per-bond* share (M1). Map the load-sharing explicitly: `|F_bond| = F_patch / N_cadherin`, then feed that into the DCM cadherin off-rate.

### Eq 9 — Turnover/depolymerization inhibition (port the *form* only if DCM adds junction/cortex protection)
```
k_d = k⁰_d · (1 − ξ_d)                                          (Eq 9)
```
**Symbol → DCM state:** for a cortex-turnover or junction-maturation term, `ξ` = fraction of dissolution suppressed by a bound/mature junction; expect a stability optimum at intermediate `ξ` (M4). No numeric value to set — the paper sweeps ξ∈[0,1] with optimum ≈0.6 at low cross-linker density.

### Compaction-time observable (protocol, not a force law) — directly reusable for DCM
```
σ_x(t) = std-dev of x-positions of the active elements (compaction metric)
compaction_time := first t where |dσ_x/dt| > 0.01 × ⟨dσ_x/dt⟩_(first 5 s)
```
**DCM mapping:** for aggregate compaction, `σ` → std-dev of cell-centroid (or surface-node) positions, or porosity; use the same **rate-of-change-drops-below-1%-of-initial** definition to time DCM's loose→compact transition consistently. This is a clean, grid-invariant, non-tuned protocol — adopt it verbatim for DCM's compaction observable so the 24–48 h timescale is measured the same way the paper measures its ~10–20 s one.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

The paper's scenario — self-assemble a homogeneous actomyosin network in a **4×8×0.5 µm thin slab (PBC in y, repulsive x/z walls)**, start motors at t=0, watch condensation into a y-spanning bundle — is an **FF scenario**, not DCM. For DCM the reusable pieces are:

- **Compaction-timing protocol (§2):** the σ-based, rate-of-change-thresholded compaction time. Adopt directly for DCM aggregate compaction and for any cortex-condensation timing, so DCM's compaction rate is reported on the same definition.
- **Load-sharing acceptance test (from M1):** a DCM validation scenario in which a junction cluster is held at fixed total interfacial load while the bond count N is varied; measure cluster mean-lifetime vs N. Expected law (from Fig 2F): lifetime rises with N, and there is a critical N below which the cluster fails catastrophically. This is a DCM *unit test* the paper motivates, run at native single-cell / small-aggregate scale.
- **Density sweep as a DCM aggregate acceptance oracle:** the qualitative 2-D law "compaction tighter & more stable with more junctions, faster but looser with more contraction" (Fig 2E–2H) is the pattern a native N≈400 DCM aggregate should reproduce when sweeping cadherin junction density vs aggregate-σ magnitude.

No aspiration/AFM/division/durotaxis geometry is present in this paper.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

1. **Wrong scale — sub-grid to DCM.** The entire mechanism (individual actin filaments as bending beams, minifilament arms, buckling, polarity sorting, ACP arms) lives *below* the DCM surface mesh. DCM cannot represent filament buckling, and Fig 3D shows buckling supplies most of the tension — so DCM's smooth cortex *structurally cannot* generate the buckling-derived fraction of γ. Do not attempt to reproduce the filament mechanics in DCM; take only the emergent laws (M1, M2) and hand the filament physics to FF. This keeps the fine-grained-over-lumped rule intact by putting the fine-grained part where it belongs (FF).

2. **Timescale mismatch is the trap.** The paper's compaction is **10–20 s** (fast, contraction-driven, thin purified slab). DCM's aggregate compaction is **24–48 h** (cadherin T1 rearrangement). Adopt the compaction-time *definition* (§2) but **never import the ~20 s number** as a DCM target — that would repeat the aggregate-σ rate error flagged in DCM's memory (drag-limited ~100 s ≠ real 24–48 h). The transferable claim is the *mechanism* (rate gated by bond turnover), which is precisely why DCM needs T1 escalation.

3. **In-vitro-like, not living MCF7.** Purified/computational actomyosin, lamellipodial transverse-arc context, thin 2-D slab with PBC — not a 3-D turgor-pressurized MCF7 cortex. The load-sharing and turnover laws are cell-type-agnostic *principles*, so they transfer; but any *number* (4 nN, 5.7 pN, 0.115 s⁻¹) is context-specific and must NOT be set as a DCM MCF7 baseline. Respect the physiological-baseline rule: DCM's cadherin/cortex parameters stay at their MCF7-anchored values; this paper contributes *structure of the law*, not baseline values.

4. **Model-relative densities.** R_M, R_ACP are dimensionless model-internal densities, not concentrations. Never register them as absolute parameters; only their *ratios/trends* are meaningful.

5. **Slip vs catch bond.** The paper's cross-linker is a **slip** bond (Bell, filamin); DCM's cadherin is a **catch** bond (Rakshit KU-4.2). The load-sharing→lifetime *architecture* is identical, but the force-response is opposite at low-to-moderate load (catch bonds *strengthen* first). Use Eq 8 only as the high-load slip limit; keep DCM's catch-bond law as primary. Do not "simplify" DCM's cadherin to this slip form.
