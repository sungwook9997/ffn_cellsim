# DCM lens — p25

**Paper:** Slater, Jung & Kim (2024), "Emergence of diverse patterns driven by molecular motors in the motility assay," *Cytoskeleton* 81(12):902–912, DOI 10.1002/cm.21808.

**Verdict up front (honest):** This is a molecular-scale, agent-based Brownian-dynamics motility-assay study — explicit surface-anchored myosin motors gliding semiflexible F-actin in a quasi-2D periodic box, producing four collective patterns (network / flock / band / ring). It is an **FF-engine** paper (Kim-lab lineage, the same agent-based framework FF emulates). For the **DCM** engine — a closed triangulated surface mesh with lumped compartment continua (γ, Π, K_A, κ_m, cadherin junction) — there is **no direct mechanism to port**: DCM has no explicit filaments, no motor binding sites, and no gliding-assay geometry. What DCM *can* take from it is (a) one sharp **conceptual validation of the mechanistic-over-lumped hard rule** that bears directly on the aggregate-σ compaction open problem, (b) two ready-made **surface-flow observables** for the day DCM adds a cortical flow field (membrane–cortex coupling), and (c) a **contact-CFL discipline** note. Everything load-bearing numerically (κ_b*, κ_r*, stall force, rates, drags, r0) is deferred to SI Table S1 and is unavailable in this file. **DCM relevance = low (1/5).**

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

Nothing here is a new *force* for DCM. Two items are genuinely useful, framed at the level DCM actually operates.

- **[Conceptual, high-value] Implicit-motor caution → aggregate-σ compaction (contact/junction module).**
  The paper's central result (§2.6, §3, Fig S6): replacing explicit motors with a *constant propulsion force tuned to match average gliding speed* reproduces the mean speed but **fails to form rings** and **misrepresents the propulsion magnitude and direction** — "the magnitude and direction of propulsion forces exerted by myosin motors can be misrepresented in models with implicit motors" (p2). This is the *exact* category error DCM already flagged for loose→compact aggregate compaction: an aggregate surface tension σ reproduces the mechanical **end-state** (porosity, contact) but not the **rate/mechanism** — the real 24–48 h compaction is cadherin T1 rearrangement, not a σ-pull (see `project-dcm-compaction-turgor-blocked`, `project-dcm-ipc-large-dt`). p25 is an independent, published demonstration that a lumped propulsion term "matches the scalar summary statistic but loses the emergent structure/rate." **Why it beats what DCM does now:** it is external evidence supporting the CLAUDE.md mechanistic-over-lumped rule and specifically warns against citing aggregate-σ rates — DCM must keep the cadherin catch-bond junction (Rakshit KU-4.2) + T1 rearrangement as the compaction driver, not fall back to σ. Actionable as a **validation-gate rationale / DevLog citation**, not a code change.

- **[Latent, low-value] Explicit motor kinetics (parallel cluster model) as the eventual γ-generation substrate.**
  Motors here walk with a force–velocity law and unbind force-dependently via the Erdmann–Schwarz **parallel cluster model (PCM)**, one arm = 8 heads. DCM's cortical tension γ is currently a lumped scalar (pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2), which is the open "γ generation" problem. p25 shows PCM is the mechanistic motor-kinetics standard, but **DCM has no filament substrate to attach motors to** — this belongs in FF (which builds the cortex from explicit filaments). For DCM the only takeaway is: *if* γ is ever made emergent, it must come from a filament+motor cortex (FF handoff), not a richer scalar law. No DCM change now.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

None of the four equations is a physics law DCM integrates; three are **analysis metrics** and one is a persistence-length identity. Two of the metrics are portable as DCM surface observables *if/when* a cortical flow field exists on the mesh.

- **Eq 1 — velocity-correlation (collective-motion metric).** Original:
  ⟨|cos α(r,t)|⟩ = ⟨ (v_i·v_j)/(|v_i||v_j|) ⟩, over endpoint pairs within 0.5 µm.
  **DCM map:** replace filament endpoints i,j with **mesh surface nodes**; v_i = nodal velocity of the cortex/membrane surface; pair cutoff → geodesic (or Euclidean) neighbor radius on the mesh. Gives a scalar "how aligned is the cortical surface flow" for a DCM cell that has a tangential actin flow field. **Only meaningful once DCM carries surface flow** — today the cortex is a scalar γ with no advection, so v_i is just bulk deformation and the metric is trivial. Park for the membrane–cortex-coupling milestone.

- **Eq 4 — morphology homogeneity Q.** Q = (1/2N_G)[Σ_i std_j(ρ_{i,j}) + Σ_j std_i(ρ_{i,j})], ρ = endpoint count per grid cell, N_G=30, at steady state (morphology-correlation ≥0.8). **DCM map:** ρ_{i,j} → surface-density field of a cortical marker (e.g. myosin or F-actin surface concentration) binned on a lat-long grid over the cell surface; Q measures cortical-density heterogeneity (blebs, myosin puncta, polarized cortex). Directly usable as a **DCM cortical-uniformity observable** — DCM already checks "uniformity not V/V0" (`project-dcm-fullcompartment-reconstruction`); Q is a cleaner, published homogeneity scalar. Adopt the *form* only; N_G is grid-choice, keep it grid-invariant (report Q vs N_G, no magic number).

- **Eq 2 — persistence length.** l_p = κ_{b,A} · r0_A / (k_B T), κ_b in N·m. **DCM map:** the only bending stiffness DCM owns is **membrane bending κ_m** (and nucleus). This is the standard worm-like-chain identity; it does not add anything DCM's κ_m parametrization lacks, but it is the correct sanity relation if DCM ever reports an effective persistence/correlation length of membrane undulations. Low value.

- **Eq 3 — tangent-vector correlation** ⟨cos θ(Δs)⟩ = ⟨u(s)·u(s+Δs)⟩, fit to exp(−Δs/l_p). Curve-analysis of 1D filament contours; **no surface-mesh analog** worth porting.

- **Curl of the velocity field** (§2.3, not numbered): grid-average nodal velocities, take |curl|, time-average — the paper's **rotational/ring metric**. **DCM map:** on a DCM cell with cortical surface flow, the surface curl (vorticity of the tangential velocity field) would detect **cortical swirling / rotational flow** (e.g. during polarization or division). Same "needs a flow field first" caveat as Eq 1. Note both Eq 1 and curl require the flow to be sampled on the mesh, not the bulk.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

The paper's geometry is a **quasi-2D periodic gliding assay** (10×10×0.1 µm³, z frozen, PBC, motors on a plane at z=0). This is **not a DCM scenario** — DCM cells are closed 3D surfaces; there is no aspiration, AFM, confinement, division, or aggregate protocol here to lift. Two protocol *disciplines* are transferable:

- **Steady-state onset criterion:** compute a correlation between the current density field and the final density field; declare steady state where correlation ≥ 0.8, and analyze only after. DCM can reuse this to define when a relaxed/compacting run has reached its measurement window (cleaner than a fixed wall-clock cutoff).
- **Noise-suppressed velocity sampling:** sample nodal velocity every ~2 s (not every step) to suppress thermal noise before computing flow observables. Applies if DCM computes surface-flow metrics.

No aspiration/AFM/division/durotaxis/confinement geometry is present to reproduce.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

**No DCM-observable target** (tension, division force, ring-closure, compaction porosity, contact angle) is provided — the paper reports assay pattern-class phase maps and dimensionless metric ranges. For the record, its numbers are FF/TAG-level pattern-formation targets, not DCM cell-mechanics numbers:

- Four emergent classes; transition order **network→flock→band→ring** as κ_r↑ or κ_b↓ (Fig 1).
- Velocity correlation (Eq 1) range ~**0.5–0.95**, peak at tight bands.
- Curl (rotational metric) ~**0.018–0.033** (1/s), peak when many rings.
- Critical concentration: thick bands vanish for **C < 60 µM**.
- Curvature ordering ring > band > flock > network; ring *size* set by rigidity+repulsion, not C or ⟨L⟩; #rings ∝ ⟨L⟩.
- Explicit vs implicit motors: implicit forms **no rings** and skews the low-speed fraction.

The **one DCM-usable target is negative/conceptual, not numeric:** a lumped propulsion (read: aggregate-σ) that matches the summary statistic (speed / end-state porosity) **must not be expected to reproduce the emergent structure or its rate** — a DCM compaction run should be *gated* to report the mechanism (cadherin T1) not to claim a σ-derived rate. This anchors an existing DCM open-item, it is not a new band.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Regime mismatch is total for the physics.** In-vitro purified actin+myosin, quasi-2D, z-frozen, periodic, no membrane / no turgor / no cortex-as-surface / no nucleus / no cell-cell junction. **Nothing about the cell-scale physiological baseline (η≈65 Pa·s, Π₀≈40 Pa, γ, K_A, cadherin) is in this paper.** Do not import any parameter into a DCM physiological config — it would violate the physiological-baseline rule by injecting an assay constant where a cell setpoint belongs.
- **z-frozen 2D over-exaggerates volume exclusion** (paper says so, p3): its collision statistics are stronger than 3D. Any curl/correlation magnitudes are 2D-assay-specific and must not be treated as DCM surface-flow bands.
- **Metrics need a flow field DCM doesn't yet have.** Eq 1, Eq 4, curl are only non-trivial once DCM carries a tangential cortical flow / surface-concentration field (membrane–cortex-coupling milestone). Adopting them now would be premature — implement the physics first, then the observable.
- **SI-deferred constants.** All numeric force/rate/drag parameters are in SI Table S1 (not in the provided text). Do not guess or back-fill them; that would be a magic number. If FF (not DCM) later needs them, fetch SI.
- **Adaptation without violating rules:** the only safe adoptions are (i) the *form* of Q (Eq 4) and the steady-state≥0.8 criterion as grid-invariant DCM analysis utilities, and (ii) the implicit-motor caution as a DevLog/gate rationale reinforcing the mechanistic rule. Neither touches a physiological setpoint or introduces a tuned constant.

**Bottom line:** p25 advances **FF**, not DCM. For DCM its value is a published, on-point warning that a lumped surface tension (σ) — like an implicit motor — reproduces the scalar end-state but loses the emergent mechanism and rate, directly reinforcing DCM's decision to keep cadherin-T1 as the compaction driver; plus two flow-metric forms (Eq 1, Eq 4/curl) to shelve for the membrane–cortex-coupling milestone. No equation, parameter, geometry, or numeric validation target ports into the DCM engine today.
