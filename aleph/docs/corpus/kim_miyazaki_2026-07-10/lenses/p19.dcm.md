# DCM lens — p19

**Paper:** Rabbi, Yim, Boyd, Nam, Chaudhuri, Kim (2026) — *Mechanical Checkpoint for Cell Division in Three-Dimensional Microenvironments*, bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.06.16.732593 (NOT peer-reviewed; flag provenance).

**One-line:** A closed volume-conserving membrane cell (2D loop / 3D triangulated mesh) is *grown* (ramp V₀ ×2) then *divided* (imposed cytokinetic-ring + spindle-elongation force fields) inside a discrete collagen fiber network — giving DCM a ready-made, mechanistic **growth→mitotic-elongation→cytokinesis protocol** and a small set of shape validation targets. The paper's science is mostly on the ECM (FF territory); its gift to DCM is the *cell-division boundary-condition machinery* and confirmation that DCM's own volume-penalty + membrane-elasticity formulation is the right substrate.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Division module: cytokinetic-ring (CRC) + spindle-elongation (ISE) as an explicit two-field surface load  ⭐ (highest DCM value)
DCM's "cell division / cytokinetic ring" is listed as an OPEN problem. This paper hands us a fully-specified, fine-grained-compatible mitotic-elongation driver that acts directly on membrane nodes — no lumped "split the cell in two" shortcut:
- **CRC (cleavage furrow):** a *constant inward* nodal force `F_CRC` applied uniformly to every membrane node within **0.5 µm of the equatorial plane** (the plane ⟂ the mitotic/spindle axis through the cell centroid). A node stops being driven once it is within **1 µm of the mitotic axis** (furrow-closure cutoff → prevents nodes overshooting through the axis).
- **ISE (interpolar spindle elongation):** a *constant outward* nodal force `F_ISE` applied uniformly to membrane nodes within **5 µm of the mitotic axis**, pushing the two poles apart.
- **Stop condition:** both fields switch off once the cell has **elongated 58.74%** (pole-to-pole length / initial diameter − 1 = 0.5874), which keeps the two nascent daughters from over-elongating past a spherical shape.

**Why it beats DCM-now:** DCM has no division mechanism at all; the alternatives on the table are lumped (prescribe two spheres, or a scripted mesh cut). This is a *force-field on nodes* that respects DCM's existing membrane + volume-conservation dynamics, so the resulting shape, cortical-tension redistribution, and contact response with neighbours all emerge. It composes with DCM's γ (cortex tension), Π (turgor), and node-face contact rather than bypassing them. The furrow is a **line-tension band** (inward force concentrated in a narrow equatorial ring) — exactly the mechanistic ansatz for a contractile actomyosin ring, and the ISE band is the microtubule-spindle push.

### M2 — Volumetric growth driver: ramp the volume-conservation target V₀ (G1 two-fold growth)  ⭐
Growth is implemented purely by **linearly ramping the equilibrium enclosed volume `V₀` ×2 over 4000 s**; the existing volume penalty (Eq. 9) then pushes the membrane outward uniformly, membrane radius grows ~1.26× (= 2^{1/3}). DCM already has an enclosed-volume constraint (turgor / IPC large-dt); this says the *correct, minimal* growth mechanism is a time-varying `V₀` setpoint, not an added body force. This is the physiological-baseline-clean way to grow a DCM cell before dividing it.

**Why it beats DCM-now:** DCM currently has no growth phase; a naive approach would inject an outward pressure. Ramping V₀ keeps growth *mechanistic and isotropic-by-construction* and reuses the module DCM already trusts.

### M3 — Soft-core node–segment excluded-volume repulsion (Eq. 8) as an alternative/adjunct contact kernel
The paper's membrane-vs-fiber and membrane-vs-membrane repulsion is a smooth soft-core: `U_r = κ_r (r₁₂−r₀,r)²[½ + ⅓(1−r₁₂/r₀,r)⁴]` for `r₁₂ < r₀,r`, zero beyond. This is a C¹ short-range repulsion with a *finite* stiffness (no singularity), well-suited to a differentiable Warp engine. DCM uses Ericson node-face penalty + IPC; this form is a candidate for the *membrane–membrane* self-contact and for soft cell–cell overlap where IPC's barrier is overkill, and it is differentiable end-to-end (matches DCM's autodiff requirement).

**Why it beats DCM-now:** IPC gives an infinite log-barrier (great for hard non-penetration, awkward for gradients across the barrier). Eq. 8 is a bounded, differentiable soft repulsion — a useful complement for aggregate self-contact where slight interpenetration is physical (cell–cell junctions), not a violation.

### M4 — Force-dependent (Bell) unbinding as the cadherin-junction / cortex-crosslink off-rate law (Eq. 7)
Not new physics for DCM's cadherin junction (already Rakshit KU-4.2 catch-bond), but the paper's clean statement — tensile-only acceleration `k₋ = k₋⁰·exp(λ|F|/k_BT)` for `r≥r₀`, plain `k₋⁰` under compression — is the exact functional form for the **aggregate compaction / T1-rearrangement** open problem: junction/crosslink turnover must be *force-gated* so that load-bearing bonds break first. The paper's Fig. 7 result (fast turnover `k₋⁰ ×100` → shell resistance → 0, "matrix fluidization") is the mechanistic template for why T1 rearrangement must accelerate under tension to let a loose aggregate compact — DCM's compaction is currently drag-limited precisely because it lacks this stress-triggered bond-turnover escalation.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh level

All symbols mapped to DCM state: nodes `i` (positions `r_i`), faces (triangles), γ (cortex tension), Π (turgor), η (cytoplasm viscosity), K_A (area elasticity), κ_m (bending), V (enclosed volume).

**Eq. 9 — enclosed-volume conservation (→ DCM turgor/volume penalty, direct):**
```
U_v = ½ κ_v (1 − V/V₀)²        →  Π-equivalent restoring force
```
Map: `V` = DCM mesh enclosed volume (already computed via signed-tetrahedra), `V₀` = target (ramp ×2 for growth, M2). The conjugate pressure is `Π = −∂U_v/∂V = (κ_v/V₀)(1−V/V₀)`. This is the *same* mechanism as DCM's osmotic/turgor constraint; the paper confirms the harmonic form and gives the growth protocol (ramp V₀). **For 2D** the volume is estimated by axisymmetric rotation of the cross-section about the long axis — irrelevant for our 3D mesh but worth noting the 2D runs used it.

**Eq. 4 / Eq. 5 — membrane stretch + bend (→ DCM K_A and κ_m):**
```
U_s = ½ κ_s (r − r₀)²          (edge stretch; r₀,m = 400 nm edge length)
U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ₀)²          (θ₀,m = 0; 2D angle / 3D dihedral)
```
Map: `κ_s` ↔ DCM edge-spring backing out K_A; `κ_b` ↔ DCM bending κ_m on dihedrals. Confirms DCM's membrane discretization is the standard one. Edge rest length 400 nm sets a mesh-resolution reference (at R=10 µm, ~157 edges around the equator in 2D; 10,242-node icosphere in 3D).

**Eq. 8 — soft-core repulsion (→ DCM contact, M3):**
```
U_r = κ_r (r₁₂ − r₀,r)² [ ½ + ⅓ (1 − r₁₂/r₀,r)⁴ ],  r₁₂ < r₀,r ; else 0
```
Map: `r₁₂` = min distance node-i to a face/segment; `r₀,r` = sum/avg of contacting element half-thicknesses (a *derived* geometric cutoff — NOT a tuned number → satisfies magic-number rule). Differentiable, bounded.

**Eqs. 1–3 — over-damped Langevin + FDT + forward-Euler (→ DCM integrator sanity cross-check):**
```
F_i − ζ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_i^T = 0
⟨F_i^T F_j^T⟩ = (2 k_B T ζ_i / Δt) δ_ij
r_i(t+Δt) = r_i + (1/ζ_i)(F_i + F_i^T) Δt,   Δt = 3.97e-4 s
```
DCM is over-damped Warp; this is the same regime. The per-node drag they use (Eq. 6, `ζ_i = 3πμ r_c (3 + 2 r₀/r_c)/5`, cylinder formula) is a *segment-drag* model — for DCM's surface nodes our per-node drag is derived from η (65 Pa·s) differently, but Eq. 6 is the reference if we ever give membrane edges an explicit hydrodynamic drag. **Do NOT adopt their Δt or μ blindly** — their μ is a collagen-medium viscosity, not our 65 Pa·s cytoplasm.

**Eq. 7 — Bell force-dependent off-rate (→ DCM cadherin junction / crosslink turnover, M4):**
```
k₋ = k₋⁰ · exp( λ |F_s| / (k_B T) )   if r ≥ r₀ (tensile)
k₋ = k₋⁰                               if r <  r₀ (compressive)
```
Map: `F_s` = junction/crosslink spring force; `λ` = force-sensitivity length; tensile-only gating. Feeds the aggregate-σ compaction / T1 open problem (M4).

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce as DCM scenarios

**S1 — Division scenario (the deliverable):** single DCM cell, physiological baseline (γ, Π, η, nucleus, membrane all ON), R rescaled to 7.5 µm.
1. Equilibrate at resting operating point (`--from-resting`).
2. **Growth phase:** ramp `V₀ ×2` linearly over a growth interval (their 4000 s; ours set by physiological G1 duration) → cell radius ~1.26×.
3. **Mitotic elongation:** pick a mitotic axis; apply CRC (inward, ±0.5·(R/10) µm equatorial band, stop 1·(R/10) µm from axis) + ISE (outward, 5·(R/10) µm axial band); run until 58.74% elongation, then release.
4. Analyze at steady state. Optionally continue to furrow scission → two daughters.

**S2 — 3D vs 2D fidelity note:** they ran mostly 2D (thin cylinder, 157-edge membrane loop) and verified once in 3D (spherical domain R=30 µm, 10,242-node icosphere membrane). DCM is inherently 3D → go straight to the 3D form; the 3D reference gives higher elongation (~60%, AR ~2) than 2D (AR 1.41) because it is less confined. Use the **3D numbers** as our targets.

**S3 — Confinement / ECM-embedded division (composite FF⊗DCM):** the *full* paper scenario is a DCM cell dividing *inside* an FF collagen network — the pericellular densified shell that forms during growth then resists elongation. This is the natural FF⊗DCM coupling test: DCM cell grows → compacts surrounding FF fibers into a shell → shell hoop-stress + equatorial anchoring resists DCM mitotic elongation. Reproduces the "mechanical checkpoint." (Cross-listed to the FF lens; DCM owns the cell, FF owns the matrix.)

**S4 — No-growth control (their Fig. S2):** run mitotic elongation *without* the prior growth phase → shell tension markedly reduced → division easier. A clean discriminating test that growth-history prestress is what resists division (history-dependent checkpoint).

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Cell type / baseline:** experiments + FE modulus are **MDA-MB-231** (invasive, mesenchymal), not our MCF7 (epithelial). AR/extension targets are protocol-driven (fixed 58.74% stop) so they transfer as *shape geometry*, but do not import MDA-MB-231 stiffness as an MCF7 physiological setpoint — keep DCM's MCF7 γ (0.15/1.5 mN/m), η (65 Pa·s), Π₀ (40 Pa). E_cell=324 Pa is an *oracle to cross-check against*, not a config value.
- **Division forces are IMPOSED, not emergent:** the paper *prescribes* F_CRC and F_ISE (magnitudes not even numerically stated — set to reach 58.74%). This violates DCM's "fine-grained over lumped" preference if we stop there. Adopt the *protocol/geometry* (band placement, stop condition) as scaffolding, but the physiological-baseline-clean version routes the furrow force through a **mechanistic contractile ring** (equatorial cortex-tension band, actomyosin line-tension) and the polar push through **microtubule/spindle forces** — DCM already has a cortex (γ) and microtubules. Treat CRC/ISE as the *interface* to those mechanisms, not as permanent lumped body forces. Flag F_CRC/F_ISE magnitude derivation to PI (currently tuned-to-outcome in the paper — do NOT copy that; drive to a physiological ring tension instead).
- **2D dominance:** most results are the 2D thin-slice (axisymmetric-rotation volume estimate). Our engine is 3D; use only the single 3D run's numbers (AR~2, +20% shell) as targets and ignore 2D-specific artifacts (thin-domain fiber-orientation constraint).
- **The paper's core science is the ECM (FF), not the cell (DCM):** the three resistance modes, buckling (~30 pN), Bell crosslinker turnover, and shell hoop-stress all live in the fiber network → FF lens. For DCM standalone, the durable, high-value extractions are M1 (division protocol) and M2 (growth-via-V₀). The full pericellular-checkpoint story only materializes in the FF⊗DCM composite (S3).
- **Not peer-reviewed (bioRxiv):** register KB claims with preprint provenance; the FE constitutive anchors (Eldridge 2019 E=324 Pa; Lane 2018 E_mat=832 Pa/ν=0.313) trace to peer-reviewed sources and are the citable numbers.
- **μ and Δt are matrix-medium values:** do not adopt their Δt=3.97e-4 s or medium μ; DCM's timestep is set by our own CFL with turgor+cytoplasm-η, and their cylinder drag (Eq. 6) is a fiber-segment law, not our surface-node drag from η=65 Pa·s.
