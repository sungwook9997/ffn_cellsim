# DCM lens — p10

**Paper:** Rabbi, Koenderink, Mulla, Kim (2025) *Fine-tuning of material properties by catch bonds*, Acta Biomaterialia 201:372–384. DOI 10.1016/j.actbio.2025.06.004. Kim-lab agent-based Brownian-dynamics actin model + single myosin-II motor.

**Framing for DCM.** This is natively an FF-engine paper (explicit filaments + explicit α-actinin/filamin cross-linkers with force-dependent off-rates). DCM does **not** resolve individual cross-linkers — cortex tension γ and the cell–cell cadherin interface are surface-mesh/continuum quantities. So nothing here is a *constitutive* DCM source. What IS transferable is **mechanistic guidance for two live DCM open problems** — (a) the cell–cell **cadherin catch-bond junction** (DCM's "ONE junction interface" already has load-sharing clusters + contact-age maturation — this paper *is* a catch-bond cluster with turnover) and (b) **cortical-tension γ generation / γ-floor** (the paper's headline active-network result is that catch-slip cross-linkers amplify a single motor's contractile stress ~3–4×). Treat the transfers as sub-grid *modifiers of existing DCM surface quantities*, never as new lumped forces, and never tuned to pass a gate.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

**M1 — Cadherin junction: add force-dependent REDISTRIBUTION + turnover to the cluster, not just maturation.**
DCM module: cell–cell cadherin catch-bond junction (Rakshit KU-4.2, load-sharing clusters, contact-age maturation) and aggregate-σ compaction.
Paper finding (Fig. 1, Fig. S3): the catch-slip advantage **requires cross-linker turnover** (a "hop" to a new location after unbinding). *Remove turnover and catch-slip falls BELOW slip.* The mechanism is that bonds unbind fast at low force ("search"), then accumulate and dwell where tension is intermediate ("settle down"), progressively **reinforcing the load-bearing pathway**.
Why it beats DCM-now: DCM's junction clusters currently mature in place (contact-age) and break as a bundle; there is no rule that engaged cadherins **migrate along the contact toward the high-tension nodes**. The memory note [[project-dcm-compaction-turgor-blocked]] records that aggregate-σ compaction is *cadherin-independent* (freezing the junction leaves 0.234 unchanged) and that the real 24–48 h compaction RATE needs **T1 rearrangement escalation**, not aggregate-σ. This paper supplies the missing microscopic driver of T1-like remodeling: **force-biased unbind→re-bind of the junction cluster onto higher-tension node-pairs of the shared cadherin interface**. That is exactly a fine-grained rearrangement mechanism (satisfies the mechanistic-over-lumped rule) rather than a lumped σ pull.

**M2 — Cortical tension: catch-slip cross-linker redistribution as a γ-amplifier for the γ-floor.**
DCM module: actomyosin cortex surface tension γ (pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2; γ-floor problem where myosin can't reach the 0.15/1.5 mN/m band).
Paper finding (Fig. 6): a single 512-head motor produces **~90 Pa contractile stress with catch-slip cross-linkers vs ~25 Pa with slip** — a 3–4× amplification — because catch-slip cross-linkers arrive at the motor **before** filament aggregation (density rises crosslinker-first, Fig. 6H/I). Better connectivity of high-force-bearing filaments raises transmitted internal stress.
Why it beats DCM-now: the γ-floor memory ([[project-gamma-floor-likely-deficit.md]], [[project-ff-afm-overshoot-was-artifact]]) says the cortical-tension deficit is a *force-magnitude* limit, and cross-bridge/cross-link stiffness was flagged as a candidate 1000×-soft lever. This paper gives the *sign and rough magnitude* of a physically-grounded amplifier: catch-slip cross-linker reinforcement of force-bearing pathways multiplies motor-transmitted stress ~3–4× at high connectivity. In DCM this is a candidate **connectivity-dependent multiplier on the myosin→γ conversion** — but it must be *derived* from a cortex cross-linker kinetic model (FF), then handed to DCM as a curve, NOT dialed in.

**M3 — Connectivity gate: the catch-slip benefit is OFF at low connectivity.**
Paper finding (Fig. 2): catch-slip beats slip (σ ratio >1, ε ratio up to ~2.5×) **only at high connectivity** (high filament concentration CF and cross-link density RCL≥~0.01); at low RCL catch-slip *loses* (ratio <1). σy ∝ CF·RCL; γy depends strongly on RCL only.
Why it matters for DCM: this is the mechanistic explanation for why **loose aggregates should NOT compact fast**. A sparse early aggregate has low junction connectivity → catch-slip reinforcement is untapped → slow. As contact densifies (maturation, more cadherin node-pairs engaged), connectivity crosses the threshold and reinforcement switches on. This gives DCM's loose→compact transition an *emergent switch* tied to contact density rather than a hand-set escalation time — directly addressing the drag-limited compaction-RATE problem without a magic timescale.

**M4 — The three-knob phenomenology maps to which junction property to escalate.**
Paper (Figs. 3–5): the catch-slip lifetime curve has three descriptors — τ0 (zero-force lifetime), τmax (peak lifetime), Fmax (force at peak). They act *differently*: **large Fmax** → force-bearing set continuously turns over → high γy AND σy at ~constant stiffness (the counterintuitive win); **large τmax** → freeze the pathway + keep recruiting → high σy, unchanged γy; **large τ0** → suppress low-strain redistribution → high stiffness E0, small γy.
Why it matters: when DCM's junction is promoted to a real catch-slip cluster, this tells us **which parameter to move for which tissue behavior** — e.g. to get a compliant-yet-strong contact (high yield strain, needed for large-deformation compaction/T1) escalate the *force threshold Fmax* of the cadherin catch bond, not its lifetime.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (to surface-mesh / continuum level)

**Eq. 1 — catch-slip cluster off-rate (the one portable equation).** Applied at DCM's cadherin junction, per node-pair of the shared interface (not per molecule):

```
              ⎧ k_s0·exp(+λ_s·|F| / kB T) + k_c0·exp(−λ_c·|F| / kB T),   if r ≥ r0
k_u(F, r) =   ⎨
              ⎩ k_s0 + k_c0,                                             if r < r0
```

Symbol → DCM state map:
- `|F|` = tensile force carried by the cadherin junction cluster at a given surface node-pair (from the node-face penalty / junction spring at that contact node). In DCM this is the per-contact-node adhesion force magnitude.
- `r` = junction cluster extension (gap between the two mesh surfaces at the contact node); `r0` = junction rest length (adhesion equilibrium separation). Below r0 → compression → force-free rate k_s0+k_c0.
- `k_s0, k_c0` = zero-force slip and catch rate constants; `λ_s, λ_c` = force compliances (length units). **Pure slip = set k_c0=0** (DCM's current Rakshit slip-only fallback).
- `kB T` = 4.11 pN·nm at 310 K.
- Off-rate governs whether that node-pair's cadherin cluster releases this step → enables the **force-biased turnover / rearrangement** move of M1.

**Lifetime reparameterization** (used everywhere in the paper): τ(F)=1/k_u(F), a curve rising from τ0 at F=0 to τmax at F=Fmax then falling. This is the (τ0, τmax, Fmax)-form DCM should expose on the junction config instead of the raw {k_s0,k_c0,λ_s,λ_c}, because it is the interpretable, area-matchable form.

**γ-amplification (M2) — framing, NOT a closed form the paper gives.** The paper only reports the *ratio*: σ_active(catch-slip)/σ_active(slip) ≈ 90/25 ≈ 3.6 at CF=10 µM, RCL=0.01. For DCM, treat this as an empirical multiplier on the myosin→γ conversion **derived from an FF cortex run**, i.e. γ_eff = γ_myosin · A(connectivity), with A calibrated by the FF engine at the physiological cortex CF/RCL — never a free constant in DCM. (The catch-slip caveat: NO closed continuum law exists; do not invent one — surface it as an FF→DCM handoff.)

*(The Langevin/forward-Euler EOM and the bending/stretch/repulsion harmonic force laws are FF-level, not DCM-level; do not port them to the surface mesh.)*

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce (as DCM scenarios)

- **P1 — Bulk shear yield sweep (passive).** Shear the contact/cortex at constant strain rate γ̇=0.001 s⁻¹; measure the four metrics: E0 (initial modulus, first 20 s), Ey (tangent modulus, 10 s before yield), σy, γy; yield = strain where slope deviates >5% from the linear regime. DCM analog: shear a cell–cell doublet/aggregate contact and extract a junction stress–strain curve; compare catch vs slip junction. Direct probe of whether DCM's junction reproduces the "both σy and γy up" signature.
- **P2 — Single-motor contraction (active).** One immobile 512-head motor at network center, no external strain; measure contractile stress vs time and CL radial density. DCM analog: a *cortex patch under myosin activation* — does effective γ / transmitted stress rise ~3–4× when the cross-linker model is catch-slip? Use to calibrate the M2 amplifier A(connectivity).
- **P3 — Connectivity phase map.** Sweep CF (2.5–10 µM) × RCL (0.002–0.015); locate the crossover where catch-slip advantage ratio passes 1. DCM analog: sweep junction/cortex connectivity (contact node density × engaged-cadherin fraction) and locate the compaction switch-on for M3.
- **P4 — Turnover-necessity control.** Repeat P1/P2 with hop=5 µm, hop=2 µm, and no-turnover. Confirms redistribution (not lifetime alone) is the driver. DCM analog: run junction with vs without the force-biased rearrangement move to prove M1 is what unlocks compaction rate.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Wrong engine scale (biggest).** DCM is a surface-mesh continuum; this paper is molecular. There is NO direct DCM constitutive equation here. Anything ported must go **through the FF engine** (which natively resolves cross-linkers) and reach DCM only as a *calibrated curve* (junction off-rate vs contact force; γ-amplifier vs connectivity). Porting Eq. 1's τ0/τmax/Fmax straight onto the cadherin surface tension would be a lumped-proxy violation and a magic-number risk. Keep it FF-first.
- **α-actinin ≠ cadherin.** The catch-slip profile (τ0=9.5 s, τmax=53 s, Fmax=14.7 pN) is α-actinin-4 (WT vs K255E) cross-linking F-actin — an *intracellular cortex* cross-linker. DCM's cell–cell junction is *cadherin* (Rakshit KU-4.2). Do not overwrite the cadherin catch-bond constants; treat this as guidance on *cortex* cross-linkers and as a *conceptual template* for the junction, with force scales reconciled separately.
- **Purified in-silico network, not living MCF7.** No actin turnover, no spatial heterogeneity, quasi-2D 30×30×1 µm slab, single motor "to apply internal force," not a real actomyosin cortex (authors' own limitation, p.12). Fast cortical actin turnover in vivo will *change* the redistribution — so the ~3.6× amplifier is an upper-bound-ish signpost, not a physiological setpoint. Do NOT initialize DCM γ from it; keep γ at the physiological pressure-borne baseline (γ=ΔP·R/2) and use the amplifier only as a *derived modulator* validated in FF.
- **Rate-dependence.** All parameters were tuned at γ̇=0.001 s⁻¹; the authors state τ0, τmax scale *inversely* with deformation rate (and, in the active case, with motor walking rate). DCM compaction happens over 24–48 h (very slow) — the effective lifetimes at DCM's deformation rate differ from the paper's. Any lifetime handed to the junction must be rate-corrected, not copied.
- **Turnover hop is 5 µm ≈ 0.7·R.** At single-cell scale a 5 µm hop is nearly cell-sized; it cannot be a literal DCM distance. Re-cast turnover as "release and re-engage on an adjacent higher-tension contact node of the same interface" — a topological rearrangement (T1-like), which is the physically correct DCM analog and keeps it mechanistic.
- **SI dependency.** The actual force-law constants (k_s0, k_c0, λ_s, λ_c), segment drag, stiffnesses, on-rate, and timestep are in Table S1 (external), absent from this PDF. Any quantitative FF→DCM calibration must fetch the SI or the GitHub source (github.com/ktyman2/CatchSlip) first.
