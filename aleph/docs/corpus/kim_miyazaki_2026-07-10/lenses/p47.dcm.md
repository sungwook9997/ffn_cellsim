# DCM lens — p47

**Paper:** Yu, Kim, Rajagopal (2022) "Role of actin filaments and cis binding in cadherin clustering and patterning," PLOS Comput Biol 18(7):e1010257, DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010257.
**One-line:** A 2D agent-based Brownian-dynamics model of the adherens-junction interface that resolves cadherin trans/cis binding kinetics, actin corralling/tethering, and cluster-size/lifetime laws — a mechanism-and-parameter reference for the DCM cadherin-junction module, not a stress/compaction oracle.

**Scope caveat up front.** This is a *sub-µm, intra-interface* model (1×1 µm² AJ patch, 2.5 nm cadherin disks, ns–s kinetics). DCM's cadherin junction is ONE lumped interface between two ~7.5 µm cells. So p47 is **below** DCM's node/face resolution: nothing here ports as a new mesh force. Its value is to (a) supply the **effective junction-state law** (cluster size, lifetime, fraction bound) that DCM's single junction should emergently reproduce or be parameterized by, and (b) settle the mechanistic question the project has open — *is cadherin maturation the compaction rate-limiter?* — with a concrete cluster-lifetime scaling. Treat every adoption below as parameterizing/validating DCM's existing junction latch, never as a new fine-grained particle layer (that belongs to FF, if anywhere).

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

**M1 — Cadherin junction as a load-sharing CLUSTER with emergent lifetime, not a per-bond latch (DCM module: `dcm/` cell-cell cadherin catch-bond junction, the Rakshit KU-4.2 interface + S1 load-sharing cluster already in the redesign).**
The paper's central result is that a junction's mechanical persistence is set by **cluster size and cluster lifetime**, and that cluster lifetime scales `τ_cluster ∝ [cad]²` (Fig 2F), rising from ~1 s (singletons) to ~100 s (clusters of ~100). DCM already models the junction as a load-sharing cadherin cluster (memory: S1 cluster survives only while `n_b>0`, lifetime emergent). p47 gives the **independent target law** for that emergent lifetime: it should scale ~quadratically with local cadherin surface density and saturate around 100 s for physiological cluster sizes. WHY it beats current: DCM's cluster lifetime is currently emergent-but-uncalibrated; p47 supplies the density→lifetime curve and the "seed-then-slow-growth" mechanism (trapped central cadherins lose mobility → cluster stabilizes) that explains WHY lifetime grows with size.

**M2 — Contact-age maturation is consistent with, but NOT the compaction rate-limiter — p47 corroborates the project's S4 conclusion.**
The paper shows cluster lifetimes are **seconds-to-~100 s**, even in the "actin-facilitated, long-turnover" best case (Fig 5F: 2→6 s with cad/actin binding; ~0.3 s without). These are the AJ *molecular* timescales. Real loose→compact aggregate compaction is 24–48 h. So p47 independently confirms: **cadherin cluster/junction kinetics operate ~10³–10⁵× faster than compaction** — the junction cannot itself be the 24–48 h rate-limiter. WHY it matters: this is direct external support for the project's S4 finding ("aggregate-σ compaction is NOT maturation-rate-limited → (b) T1 rearrangement escalation needed") and for the scope note in `project-dcm-compaction-turgor-blocked` that the real rate is T1/rearrangement-driven, not cadherin-maturation-driven. Adopt as: **do not put the 24–48 h clock inside the cadherin junction law; it belongs in the T1-rearrangement/remesh layer.**

**M3 — "Weak cis → LARGER, more persistent clusters at high density" — counterintuitive junction-strength law.**
Higher cis *off*-rate (weaker lateral affinity, `k_ass/k_dis=100`) produces LARGER clusters (up to ~600–700 monomers) at high cadherin density than strong cis (`ratio=1000`, ~150–200, flat) (Fig 2C). Mechanism: strong cis locks small rigid crystallites early; weak cis lets transient seeds anneal and grow as central cadherins get kinetically trapped. WHY for DCM: DCM's junction "maturation" should NOT be modeled as monotonically-strengthening bonds → bigger junction. The mature, mechanically-strong junction can emerge from **weak, high-turnover** cis binding at high cadherin density. This changes how the maturation escalation should be written: escalate by *raising local cadherin density / recruitment*, not by *lowering off-rate*. (Also guards against a magic-number: don't tune junction strength by cranking a bond affinity to pass a compaction gate — p47 shows affinity is non-monotonic.)

**M4 — Actin corralling → sub-diffusive cadherin confinement sets a density ceiling (DCM: membrane–cortex coupling / cortex density feeding junction recruitment).**
F-actin mesh corralling drives cadherin motion sub-diffusive (MSD slope <1) and, by trapping cadherins, both seeds and caps cluster size (Fig 3). Denser/longer projected actin → finer mesh → smaller clusters. WHY for DCM: the local cortical actin density at a contact **regulates the cadherin recruitment/retention** that feeds M1/M3. DCM already carries a cortex; this says the cortex density at the contact face should modulate the junction's effective cadherin pool (higher cortex density → more corralling → more nucleation sites but smaller individual clusters). A physiologically-grounded coupling, not a free knob.

**M5 — Cad/actin tethering is non-monotonic: cooperation vs. competition (DCM: junction ↔ cortex anchoring / α-catenin-vinculin linker).**
Tethering cadherins to actin *facilitates* clustering only in a narrow regime (high linker affinity + SMALL binding footprint, ~20%); large footprints (≥50%) string cadherins into linear cis chains and **deplete trans dimers** (the actual cell-cell bond) (Fig 4). WHY for DCM: if DCM ever couples the cadherin junction to the cortex via an α-catenin/vinculin linker, over-coupling *reduces* trans (cross-cell) adhesion. So membrane–cortex→junction coupling has an optimum; do not assume "more anchoring = stronger junction."

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (to junction-state level, not mesh forces)

DCM is a force-based continuum-surface engine; p47 is rule-based/kinetic with **no interaction potentials**. So none of these become node/face force terms. They port as the **kinetic law governing the single junction interface's state variable** (e.g. `n_b` = number of engaged cadherin bonds, or junction "strength" s∈[0,1]).

**Eq 3 — intrinsic bond firing probability (port to junction bond turnover):**
`p_ass^j = 1 − exp(−k_ass^j · Δt)`, and identically `p_dis^j = 1 − exp(−k_dis^j · Δt)`, j∈{trans, cis, cad/actin}.
Map: Δt → DCM junction-update substep; `k_ass/k_dis` → the per-bond on/off rates already in DCM's Rakshit catch-bond cluster. This is the standard `p=1−e^{−kΔt}` DCM already uses; p47 confirms the form and supplies the rate magnitudes below. Note DCM's off-rate is **force-dependent (Bell-Evans/Rakshit catch-bond)** — a fidelity UPGRADE over p47's constant `k_dis`; keep DCM's force-dependence, use p47 only for the zero-force baseline rates.

**Eq 4 — projected F-actin length (port to cortex-density → corralling coupling, M4):**
`L_F = h_actin · tan φ`, h_actin = effective cortex depth, φ = filament tilt from membrane normal.
Map: in DCM there is no explicit projected line, but the *ratio* controls corralling mesh size. Use to set the contact-face cadherin recruitment scale: vertical cortex (φ→0, short L_F) → punctate/uniform recruitment; parallel cortex (φ→90°, long L_F) → coarser/linear. Feeds a cortex-orientation → junction-nucleation-density modifier, if DCM adds one. h_actin ≈ 50–100 nm.

**Empirical scaling (port as VALIDATION law for junction lifetime, M1):**
`τ_cluster ∝ [cad]²` (Fig 2F, quadratic). Map: DCM junction lifetime vs local cadherin surface density. Not a force; a target the emergent junction should trace.

**Diffusion step (context only, not ported):** step `= √(4 D Δt)`, random 2D direction. DCM does not diffuse cadherins individually; the junction is lumped. Retain only to interpret the sub-diffusion result (M4).

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

**Not a whole-cell scenario source** — p47 has no confinement/aspiration/AFM/division geometry usable at DCM's cell scale. What it offers:

**P1 — Contact-interface parameter sweep as a DCM junction-calibration harness.**
Reproduce the density sweep [cad] ∈ {400,800,1200,1600,2000} µm⁻² at a single DCM cell-cell contact and check that the emergent junction cluster size steps up above ~800–1200 µm⁻² and that lifetime traces `∝[cad]²`. Pure junction-module unit test (no full aggregate needed), but per the NATIVE-full rule it must ultimately be confirmed on the native full-compartment two-cell contact, not a bare interface.

**P2 — Cortex-orientation → junction-pattern check.**
Set contact-face cortex tilt φ = 15° vs 75° and verify DCM's junction nucleation is more punctate/uniform at vertical (75°) — nearest-neighbor cluster spacing ~31 nm — vs coarser/linear at parallel (15°), spacing ~45 nm (Fig 5H). Sub-grid observable; realizable only if DCM tracks junction sub-patches.

**P3 — Linker cooperation/competition regime map.**
Two-cell contact with junction↔cortex linker at (k_ass^{cad/actin}, %footprint) ∈ {10¹,10⁵}×{20%,60%}; confirm the trans(=cross-cell adhesion) fraction is MAXIMIZED at high-affinity/small-footprint and DEPLETED at large-footprint (Fig 4). Guards the M5 optimum.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Regime gap 1 — dimensionality & scale.** p47 is 2D and sub-µm (1 µm² patch, 2.5 nm agents). DCM is 3D and cell-scale (7.5 µm). Do NOT import any absolute length/area cluster number as a DCM mesh quantity; they live *inside* one DCM junction face. Adapt by treating p47 outputs as the **constitutive law for the lumped junction state variable**, calibrated once and used as an emergent target.
- **Regime gap 2 — rule-based, force-free.** No potentials, no mechanics, no maturation-escalation, no T1. p47 cannot tell DCM anything about junction *forces* or compaction *stress*. DCM's force-dependent catch-bond (Rakshit) is strictly higher-fidelity; keep it. Use p47 only for zero-force baseline rates and topology/lifetime laws. This respects the HARD "fine-grained mechanistic over lumped" rule: DCM should not downgrade its force-dependent junction to p47's constant-rate rules.
- **Regime gap 3 — rigid immobile actin.** p47's cortex is a static projected line network with only stochastic replacement. DCM's cortex is a live deforming surface. The corralling/tethering (M4/M5) is therefore a *qualitative* coupling direction (cortex density/orientation → junction recruitment optimum), not a quantitative import.
- **Regime gap 4 — cell type / context.** The model is generic epithelial E-cadherin AJ; DCM targets MCF7 (weak-adhesion carcinoma, junction σ≈0.15–0.75 mN/m per KB-PIV-9, NOT embryonic ~5). So the *productive weak-cis, high-turnover* regime (M3) is actually well-aligned with MCF7's weak junctions — a fit, not a mismatch. But cluster sizes/densities should be taken as MCF7-appropriate (lower end of sweeps), and the physiological-baseline rule means the junction must be initialized already-engaged at MCF7 density, not from zero.
- **Physiological-baseline compliance.** When adopting M1/M3, the junction cadherin pool must be set at its resting physiological surface density from the START (not a convenient zero that "grows in"), consistent with the HARD rule; p47's [cad] span gives the plausible range but the MCF7 setpoint must be sourced separately (surface to PI if unknown, do not default).
- **KB integrity flag.** `k_ass^{cad/actin}` is explicitly arbitrary (S1 Table) — do not register as sourced. `k_dis^{cad/actin}` (0.1 / 10 s⁻¹) and the linker footprint ARE sourced [4,8,24] and registrable. Confirm `verdict=OK` before citing any p47-derived source row in a deliverable.
