# DCM lens — p29

**Paper:** Weady, Palmer, Lamson, Kim, Farhadifar, Shelley — *Mechanics and Morphology of Proliferating Cell Collectives with Self-Inhibiting Growth*, PRL 133, 158402 (2024). DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.133.158402.

**One-line verdict for DCM:** Not a surface-mesh / cortex paper (cells are rigid growing spherocylinders), so it offers **no new membrane/cortex/nucleus force**. Its real value to DCM is threefold and concrete: (1) a **constraint-based, potential-free contact solver** (Lagrange-multiplier complementarity, minimizes frictional dissipation) that is the same family as the IPC large-dt path DCM already chose — a proven recipe for O(10⁵) dense cells at large dt; (2) a **clean, cited stress→growth feedback law** g = (1/τ)e^{−λp} that DCM should adopt verbatim the moment it adds cell growth/division to aggregates; (3) a **Darcy tissue-flow continuum + closed-form radial solution** that is a ready-made analytic oracle for validating any growing-aggregate DCM run. Everything is at bacteria/yeast scale (dimensionless, ℓ₀≈1 µm, τ≈54 min) — provenance must be flagged; do NOT KB-register the numbers as MCF7 cytoskeletal data.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Contact resolution: constraint-based complementarity solver (validates DCM's IPC/large-dt direction)
- **DCM module:** contact (node-face penalty + IPC large-dt; `dcm/` contact kernel).
- **Change:** the paper resolves dense cell overlap NOT with a stiff repulsive potential but as a **nonlinear convex optimization** with non-negative Lagrange multipliers, under three constraints: (i) no overlap, (ii) non-contacting pairs generate zero force (complementarity), (iii) collisions minimize the rate of frictional dissipation. This is the granular/computer-graphics complementarity family (Stewart-Trinkle, Anitescu, Macklin; adapted to inertialess active matter by Yan-Shelley aLENS, refs [29–36]).
- **Why it beats what DCM does now:** DCM's penalty contact needs small dt for stability (the driver of the whole IPC large-dt effort, `project-dcm-ipc-large-dt`). This paper is independent, peer-reviewed evidence that a complementarity/constraint solve reaches O(10⁵) dense growing cells with large dt — exactly DCM's N≈400→N=2000 compaction bottleneck (loose→confluent blows up penalty contact at dt=0.1). **Actionable:** treat aLENS's constraint solver as a reference implementation / cross-check for DCM's contact solver; the "minimize frictional dissipation subject to non-penetration" variational statement (Eq. 2 continuum analog) is a principled objective DCM's contact step can be benchmarked against. Note: it is a proxy for our node-face IPC, so keep it as a **method cross-check**, not a replacement of the fine-grained node-face barrier (mechanistic-fidelity rule).

### M2 — Growth/division: stress-inhibited exponential growth law (for aggregate proliferation)
- **DCM module:** cell growth / division (currently: division stable inset only; no stress→growth-rate coupling). This is the direct lever for the **loose→compact aggregate compaction RATE** open problem.
- **Change:** each cell's volumetric growth rate is damped by the local mechanical pressure it feels: g = (1/τ) e^{−λp} (Eq. 2b/7). At the operating point, a DCM cell's enclosed-volume target V₀(t) should grow as dV₀/dt = (V₀/τ) exp(−λ·p_local), where p_local is the cell's contact/turgor pressure read from its node-face contact multipliers (or the enclosed-volume Lagrange pressure).
- **Why it beats what DCM does now:** DCM currently has NO mechanistic pressure→growth feedback; if it grows/divides aggregates it does so at fixed rate. Real MCF7 spheroids self-limit growth under compressive stress (refs [22,23] Delarue/Alric, *live* cells). This law is the fine-grained, cited mechanism — it converts colony expansion from exponential to **linear** at long times (Eq. 8), which is the physical reason a compacting aggregate stops swelling. It gives compaction dynamics an emergent rate control that is NOT a magic number (λ is derivable from measured dR/dt, ℓ₀, τ — see M-param).
- **Caveat for the compaction-rate problem:** this mechanism sets *growth* rate under stress; it does NOT by itself supply the cadherin-T1-rearrangement escalation that the real 24–48 h compaction needs (`project-dcm-compaction-turgor-blocked`). Use it as the *proliferation* half of aggregate dynamics, complementary to (not a substitute for) the junction-rearrangement work.

### M3 — Macroscopic tissue-flow as an emergent-check (Darcy law)
- **DCM module:** aggregate-scale observable / validation harness (not a runtime force).
- **Change:** the coarse-grained tissue obeys Darcy flow ξu + ∇p = 0 with ∇·u = g (Eq. 2). For a DCM aggregate, coarse-grain node velocities into a field u(r) and node-face pressures into p(r) and check that they satisfy this relation with the SAME friction ξ used per-node. This is an emergent-physics audit: if DCM's per-node drag (η-derived, `project-dcm-physio-nodeface-contact`) and contact pressures are consistent, the aggregate must reproduce Darcy flow with radial-outward u and a central pressure peak.
- **Why:** gives a first-principles closure test on DCM's aggregate mechanics with zero fitting — a way to catch drag/pressure inconsistencies that per-cell gates miss.

### M4 — "Inheritance" bookkeeping for division (Lagrangian accumulated-growth)
- **DCM module:** division scheduler.
- **Change:** cell length (→ our cell volume phase) along a material trajectory is 2^{frac[∫g dt / ln2]} (Eq. 4). This tells DCM exactly *when* each cell divides given its stress history: division at accumulated integral crossing multiples of ln2. Cheaper and more principled than a fixed division clock — the division time emerges from the pressure the cell actually experienced.
- **Why it beats now:** removes any fixed/arbitrary division period; division timing becomes a stress-history integral (mechanistic, grid-invariant).

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (mapped to DCM state)

Notation map: paper ℓₙ (cell length) → DCM cell enclosed-volume target V₀ⁿ (or an effective size); paper σᶜₙ / p → DCM local pressure p_local (from node-face contact multipliers + enclosed-volume Lagrange pressure Π); paper ξ → DCM per-node friction/drag coefficient (η-derived); paper τ → cell-cycle timescale; paper λ → stress-sensitivity of growth; u → coarse-grained node velocity field.

**(E1) Stress-inhibited growth — Eq. 7 (discrete) / Eq. 2b (continuum).** PORT THIS.
```
dV₀ⁿ/dt = (V₀ⁿ / τ_cyc) · exp( −λ · p_localⁿ )
```
- V₀ⁿ = enclosed-volume setpoint of cell n (the physiological turgor target). p_localⁿ = mean compressive pressure on cell n's surface (average of node-face penalty pressures + osmotic Π). τ_cyc = cell-cycle time. λ = growth stress sensitivity.
- λ = 0 recovers unconstrained exponential growth. Sign: compression (p>0) slows growth. Dimensionally λ has units 1/pressure.

**(E2) Division boundary condition — Eq. 1c.** PORT (as a division rule).
```
(V̇₀ Ψ)|_{V₀,min} = 2 (V̇₀ Ψ)|_{V₀,max}
```
- On division, one mother at 2V₀ → two daughters at V₀ (factor 2). In DCM: when a cell's setpoint reaches 2V₀,min, split into two cells each at V₀,min, conserving volume; remesh each daughter surface.

**(E3) Darcy tissue mechanics — Eq. 2 + Eq. 3 (validation closure).**
```
ξ u + ∇p = 0 ,   ∇·u = g = (1/τ_cyc) e^{−λ p}     inside Ω(t)
p = 0 on ∂Ω ,   ∂_t X = u(X)                        (free boundary)
```
- Map: coarse-grain DCM node velocities → u(x); node-face contact pressure → p(x); free colony edge = zero-pressure BC (matches DCM's un-anchored aggregate boundary). Use as an emergent-audit residual, not a solved PDE.

**(E4) Average size / inheritance along trajectory — Eq. 4.**
```
V̄₀(x,t) = 2^{ frac[ (1/ln2) ∫₀ᵗ g(Φ⁻¹(x,t′),t′) dt′ ] }
```
- Φ = Lagrangian flow map (track each DCM cell's material path). frac[·] = fractional part. Uninhibited (λ=0): V̄₀ = 2^{frac[t/ln2]} → synchronous division at t = n·ln2. Gives division phase per cell from its integrated stress history.

**(E5) Closed-form radial solution — Eq. 5 (the analytic ORACLE).**
```
λ = 0:   p(r) = (R² − r²)/4 ,   u(r) = r/2
λ ≠ 0:   p(r) = (2/λ) ln( 1/(8c) − c λ r² )
         u(r) = 32 c² r / (1 − 8 c² λ r²)
         c(R,λ) = [ (1 + λR²/2)^{1/2} − 1 ] / (2 λ R²)
```
- Radially-symmetric growing colony. Pressure peaks at center, zero at edge. These are exact (no fitting) → direct DCM validation targets (§5).

**(E6) Colony radius scaling — Eq. 8.**
```
λ = 0:            R(t) = R₀ e^{t/2}         (exponential)
λ ≠ 0, √λ·R ≫ 1:  R(t) ~ (2/λ)^{1/2} t      (linear asymptote)
```
- Slope of the linear regime = (2/λ)^{1/2}. A growing DCM aggregate must cross over exp→linear if E1 is wired in.

**Overdamped mobilities (Eq. 6a/6b)** are spherocylinder-specific (translational 1/(ξℓ), rotational 12/(ξℓ³)) — **do NOT port**; DCM nodes are not rods and already have η-derived per-node drag.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce as DCM scenarios

- **P1 — Growing free aggregate (unconfined colony).** Seed one DCM cell, enable E1 growth + E2 division, no outer boundary (p=0 at aggregate surface). Run until N grows ~10–100×. Reproduces the paper's core setup at our scale (N≈400 ceiling). This is the natural testbed for aggregate compaction-under-proliferation.
- **P2 — Central-pressure profile.** In a grown radial aggregate, bin cells in equal-area annuli and measure mean node-face pressure vs r; compare to Eq. 5. Confinement-response cousin: the paper predicts a colony can *reduce* internal pressure by raising stress sensitivity λ — a testable DCM knob.
- **P3 — Radius-vs-time crossover.** Track aggregate bounding radius R(t); confirm exp→linear crossover (Eq. 8) as λ increases, and that the linear slope → (2/λ)^{1/2}. Onset of divergence between λ curves at t≈5 τ.
- **P4 — Concentric size-ring emergence.** Color DCM cells by their current size-phase V₀/V₀,min; check for concentric rings whose wavelength decreases with λ and with radius r. This is an emergent morphology test (no ring is coded in).
- Confinement (§ known-open "confinement response"): the paper's free-boundary p=0 edge and its λ-pressure-relief prediction are directly usable to set up a *confined* variant (fixed outer wall instead of free edge) as a DCM confinement scenario.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS (DCM observables)

All dimensionless / bacteria-scale — use as **model-consistency oracles** for a growing-aggregate DCM run, NOT as MCF7 physiological bands.

1. **Ring wavelength scaling:** final wavelength ∝ λ^{−1/2} (Fig 4c). At λ=10⁻², wavelength ≈ 5 ℓ₀.
2. **Wavelength radial dependence:** decreases with distance r from center (Fig 4a).
3. **Wavelength oscillation period:** ≈ ln2 ≈ 0.7 τ (the growth-cycle period).
4. **Central pressure (λ=0):** p(r) = (R²−r²)/4, peak at center, zero at edge — closed form (Eq. 5).
5. **Radial velocity (λ=0):** u(r) = r/2.
6. **Max stress location:** always at colony center; drops ~10× from λ=0 to λ=10⁻² (Fig 3a,c).
7. **Radius growth law:** R(t)=R₀e^{t/2} early; R(t)~(2/λ)^{1/2} t late (Eq. 8); λ-curves diverge at t≈5.
8. **Micro↔macro parameter identity:** the SAME λ′=(τ/ξℓ₀²)λ governs discrete and continuum — a DCM aggregate and its coarse-grained Darcy field must share λ with no refit.
9. **E. coli anchor:** τ≈54 min, ℓ₀≈1 µm, dR/dt≈1.3×10⁻² µm/s ⇒ λ≈1.1×10⁻³ (Eq. 8 inversion). Reproduces the parameter-estimation procedure, not an MCF7 number.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Regime mismatch — rods, not surface-mesh cells.** Cells here are rigid growing spherocylinders with NO body elasticity, membrane, cortex, or nucleus (the paper explicitly defers material elasticity, ref [57]). DCM's whole point is the deformable surface. So port the *growth/division/contact* machinery, never a "cell = rod" shortcut. Keep DCM's fine-grained node-face contact and enclosed-volume/turgor as the physical cell; layer E1/E2 growth on top.
- **2D substrate vs 3D aggregate.** The paper is 2D on a frictional substrate (Darcy through substrate friction). DCM aggregates are 3D. The Darcy closure (M3/E3) and the radial solution (E5) are 2D-specific; use them for a 2D-plated DCM monolayer scenario, or re-derive the 3D analog before quantitative comparison. The growth law E1 is dimension-agnostic and transfers cleanly.
- **Bacteria/yeast vs living MCF7.** τ≈54 min, exponential single-cell growth, and λ≈1.1×10⁻³ are microbial. **Physiological-baseline rule:** an MCF7 DCM run must set τ_cyc to a mammalian cell-cycle time and re-derive λ from mammalian compressive-stall data (Delarue 2016 / Alric 2022, refs [22,23], which ARE live-cell) — never reuse the E. coli numbers as the operating point. If no MCF7 λ datum exists, surface to PI rather than defaulting.
- **Growth-rate feedback ≠ compaction driver.** E1 governs proliferation slowdown under stress; it does not supply the cadherin-T1 junction rearrangement that the real 24–48 h loose→compact spheroid compaction requires. Adopt E1 as the *proliferation* half only; keep it orthogonal to the junction-rearrangement escalation (`project-dcm-compaction-turgor-blocked`). Do NOT cite this paper's ~linear-growth timescale as a compaction rate.
- **Constraint solver is a method proxy.** M1 validates DCM's large-dt contact *direction*, but adopting the complementarity solver wholesale would replace the fine-grained node-face IPC barrier with a lumped granular constraint — treat it as a cross-check/benchmark, not a mechanism swap, to stay within the fidelity rule.
- **Orientational isotropy assumption.** The continuum theory assumes isotropic orientation (σᶜ→scalar p); the discrete sims show real orientational anisotropy the continuum drops. For DCM, cell shape anisotropy under compaction is exactly what we care about — the continuum p is a lower bound on true directional stress (paper notes continuum p < discrete ⟨σ⟩). Use E5 as a floor, not an exact target.
