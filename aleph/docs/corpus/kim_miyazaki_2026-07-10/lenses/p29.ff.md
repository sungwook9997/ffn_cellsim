# FF lens — p29

**Paper:** Weady, Palmer, Lamson, Kim, Farhadifar, Shelley, *Mechanics and Morphology of Proliferating Cell Collectives with Self-Inhibiting Growth*, PRL 133, 158402 (2024). DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.133.158402.

**Bottom line for FF:** This is a proliferating-active-matter paper about **rigid growing spherocylinders** (bacteria/yeast colonies) with a stress-inhibited growth law and a constraint-based contact solver. There is **no actin, no myosin, no crosslinker, no formin/Arp2/3, no adhesion clutch, no fine-grained cytoskeleton** anywhere in it. Cells are rigid rods that only elongate; body elasticity is explicitly *not* modeled (ref [57]). The paper's true home is the **DCM** engine (aggregate/proliferation direction) and the TAG proliferation-oracle layer — the dossier flags "FF? — No" and that assessment is correct. What follows is the *narrow* set of items that could touch FF at all, each with an honest "beats current FF?" verdict (mostly **no**). FF relevance score: **1/5**.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

Nothing here is a cytoskeletal mechanism FF is missing. Three items are *methodological/kinetic analogies* worth logging, none of which replaces an existing FF mechanism:

- **(A) Constraint-based (complementarity) excluded volume vs FF's LJ repulsion — numerical method, NOT a mechanism.**
  The paper enforces no-overlap as a **non-negative-Lagrange-multiplier convex minimization** (constraints: particles cannot overlap; non-contacting particles generate zero force; collisions minimize the rate of frictional dissipation) *instead of* a stiff repulsive potential — deliberately, "to avoid numerical stiffness associated with repulsive potentials, allowing for larger time steps at the cost of an iterative solution" (p.2). FF currently uses **LJ-repulsive excluded volume** (CLAUDE.md: "Excluded volume LJ repulsive on from Phase 1"), which *is* a stiff penalty and contributes to the CFL/timestep ceiling. This is the exact same trade IPC gives DCM (`project-dcm-ipc-large-dt`). **Verdict for FF: do NOT adopt.** FF's mechanistic mandate wants the *physical* soft-repulsion/steric interaction, not a hard complementarity constraint, and FF's stiffness bottleneck is dominated by bond/bending/turgor stiffness (`k_turgor` in the CFL, per `project-ff-gpu-native`), not by excluded volume. Log as a cross-engine note; the payoff lives in DCM.

- **(B) Load-inhibited elongation `v = v₀·exp(−λσ)` as a phenomenological check on FF's barbed-end ratchet.**
  Eq. 7: `dℓ/dt = (ℓ/τ)·exp(−λσᶜ)` — exponential elongation damped by axial compressive stress. This is *functionally identical in form* to the Brownian-ratchet / Peskin–Mogilner force-velocity of filament polymerization `v = v₀·exp(−F·δ/k_BT)` that already underlies FF's barbed-end growth (`barbed_end_growth_kernel`, KB-3.6 ratchet; `project-ff-filament-length-growth`, `project-ff-active-movement-pieces` piece1 v₀=0.623 µm/s). **Verdict: FF already has the mechanistic version and it beats this.** The paper's law is a *cell-length* growth law (whole-cell biomass), not a monomer-addition ratchet; using it in FF would be a lumping step backward. Value = an *external consistency check* that FF's ratchet reproduces exponential-under-zero-load, sub-exponential-under-load behavior.

- **(C) "Inheritance of accumulated resistance" — conceptual, not portable to FF.**
  The concentric size rings emerge from *anisotropically accumulated growth resistance over many cell-cycle generations* (Eq. 4, `ℓ̄ = 2^frac[∫g/ln2 dt]`). FF is a single-cell engine with no division/generations, so there is no state variable this maps to. Not applicable.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

Only one item is a clean equation FF could *cross-check* against; none supersede an FF force law.

- **Rodlike (anisotropic) overdamped drag — mobility formulas (Eq. 6a/6b).**
  `dxₙ/dt = (1/(ξℓₙ))·Fᶜₙ` (translational mobility ∝ 1/(ξℓ))
  `dθₙ/dt = (12/(ξℓₙ³))·Tᶜₙ` (rotational mobility ∝ 1/(ξℓ³))
  Map to FF: FF filaments are bead-rod/bead-spring chains with **per-segment** drag; the paper treats a whole rod of length ℓ with length-scaled translational and cubic-length rotational mobility (slender-body-like scaling on a 2D substrate with constant friction ξ). **Relevance:** FF's open **grid-drag / crawl-speed** problem (`project-ff-crawl-mechanism`: native crawl ~500× slow because Σγ ∝ Nc) is precisely a drag-model question. The `ℓ` and `ℓ³` scalings are a reminder that a *filament-length-resolved* drag (not per-bead uniform γ) is the physically correct form, and that rotational drag scales steeply with length. **Verdict: cross-check only.** FF already resolves drag per particle; adopting a per-whole-filament substrate-friction law would be a coarse-graining unless done as an emergent sum over beads. Worth noting when auditing whether `--bulk-drag` should be length/orientation-aware rather than isotropic-per-node.

- **Stress-inhibited growth flux (Eq. 1b / 2b):** `ℓ̇ = (ℓ/τ)e^{−λp}`, `g = (1/τ)e^{−λp}`. Same law as §1(B); it is a *scalar pressure*→growth kinetic, not a filament force law. No FF port.

- **What is NOT here (so FF gains nothing):** no Bell/Bell-Evans off-rate, no catch-bond spectrum, no Hill force-velocity, no myosin duty cycle, no branching-angle distribution, no crosslinker unbinding force. The Parameter/ForceLaw sections of the dossier confirm: "No Bell/catch bonds, no motor force-velocity, no bending/stretching elasticity." FF's existing mechanistic laws (Bell-Evans k_off, Hill 1938, Stam–Hocky minifilament, Rakshit catch bond) are all strictly finer-grained than anything in this paper.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Regime mismatch is total for FF.** Purified/agent-based rigid-rod *bacterial colony* ↔ FF *living single-cell actomyosin cortex*. There is no isoform, no cytoskeletal element, no in-vitro reconstitution to reconcile. Any attempt to "use" this paper in FF would violate the mechanistic-over-lumped rule (its growth law is a whole-cell scalar lump) and the physiological-baseline rule (its parameters are dimensionless/bacterial, not MCF7).
- **Do not import λ, τ, ℓ₀, dR/dt into any FF config** — they are bacterial-colony quantities and would be magic numbers in FF (no-magic-number hard rule).
- **The one defensible cross-engine action:** file the *constraint-based contact solver* idea (§1A) and the *length-resolved drag scaling* (§2) as notes against FF's grid-drag/timestep open items, but pursue neither as an FF change without a mechanistic justification — the real solver payoff is DCM's (IPC large-dt, `project-dcm-ipc-large-dt`). Route this paper to the **DCM lens**, where growth-induced stress + complementarity contact are genuinely load-bearing.
- **Coincidental-number trap:** ℓ₀≈1 µm (bacterial) vs FF actin filament 1–10 µm is a units/entity collision, not a shared datum — do not let it leak into a filament-length prior.

**Net:** p29 advances DCM/proliferation, not FF. FF impact is limited to two logged cross-engine reminders (complementarity contact; length-resolved rod drag), neither of which supersedes an existing FF mechanism.
