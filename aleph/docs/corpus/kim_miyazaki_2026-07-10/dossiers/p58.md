## Summary
The paper asks the *reverse* of the usual cell-shape question: not how actomyosin shapes a boundary, but how a confining boundary shape directs actomyosin self-organization. Using Xenopus laevis egg cytoplasmic extract (F-actin + Arp2/3 + myosin II) confined in semiclosed NOA81 microwells shaped as circular segments (parametrized by flat-side length W over diameter D, W/D from 0=circle to 1=semicircle), plus square, letter, and polygon wells, they image (confocal, TMR-LifeAct) the gelation–contraction process. The extract contracts into a dense "actin cluster"; PIV of tracer beads reveals periodic inward actin flow (period T=101.0±7.9 s, speed v=1.20±0.41 µm/s) in the XY plane and an upward flow along Z that accumulates actin beneath the top glass. The key finding: the microwell's asymmetry is *transferred* to the cluster shape (circle→crescent as W/D 0→1), quantified by roundness R=m/M (minor/major axis of fitted ellipse); R drops with W/D but is independent of well diameter, while cluster *area* scales with well *volume* not shape. A 2D phase-field active-fluid model (viscous + myosin active stress, advection–diffusion mass balance with polymerization source k_p and depolymerization sink k_d, phase-field boundary) reproduces the shape transfer using two governing dimensionless parameters: Péclet number Pe (contractility/advection) and surface polymerization rate k_p^surf. Phase diagrams show roundness is highly sensitive to k_p^surf (rounder at low k_p^surf) but weakly to Pe; cluster area grows with k_p^surf and shrinks with Pe. Chemical perturbations confirm predictions: CytoD (polymerization inhibitor, lowers k_p^surf) → rounder; VCA (nucleation promoter, raises k_p^surf) → larger; CalA (myosin phosphatase inhibitor, raises contractility/Pe) → smaller cluster. Square wells give square clusters that turn star-like under CalA (quartic parameter, 4-fold Fourier mode, 0.069→0.088); letters/polygons (T, A, X, arrow) transcribe into cluster morphology. Conclusion: motor-driven contraction sets cluster *size*, actin polymerization primarily sets cluster *shape*, and 3D actin advection transfers boundary geometry into the gel.

## Model & Methods
**Experimental (in-vitro reconstitution):**
- **Material:** Xenopus laevis egg cytoplasmic extract — F-actin + G-actin monomer, Arp2/3 complex (nucleator), myosin II motor. Bulk undergoes gelation–contraction. F-actin visualized with TMR-labeled LifeAct (binds F-actin not monomer).
- **Confinement:** NOA81 (UV-curable adhesive) microwells, passivated with PEG–PLL (poly(ethylene glycol)–poly(L-lactic acid)). Shape = circular segment defined by flat-side W and diameter D; W/D ∈ {0.0, 0.5, 0.7, 0.85, 1.0}. Also square (side 270 µm), letters (T, A, X), arrow, quarter-circle, irregular polygons.
- **Semiclosed geometry:** small gap Hg between chamber and top coverslip; Hg/H ≈ 0.067 (effective confinement). Well depth H ≈ 150 µm; D = 350 µm (also 200–350 µm swept). Cluster region D_c ≈ 100–140 µm, H_c ≈ 10 µm, gap height Hg ≈ 10 µm (Fig 1a annotations).
- **Imaging:** Confocal microscope Olympus IX73 + Yokogawa CSU-X1 spinning disk + Andor iXon-Ultra EM-CCD + Evident UPlanXApo ×20, 561 nm channel; time-lapse every 5 s.
- **Analysis:** Fiji + MATLAB. Cluster segmented by Shanbhag thresholding (manual fallback). Roundness/area/boundary via Fiji built-ins. PIV via PIVlab (MATLAB) on 1.0 µm fluorescent tracer beads. Quartic parameter = ratio of 4th to 0th Fourier mode of cluster boundary.
- **Perturbations:** Cytochalasin D (CytoD, 1.5 µM, polymerization inhibitor); VCA (10 nM, N-WASP nucleation-promoting domain, promoter); Calyculin A (CalA, 0.6 µM, myosin phosphatase inhibitor → ↑ myosin contractility).
- **Statistics:** Mann–Whitney U test; ***p<0.001, **p<0.01, n.s. p≥0.05. n=11/4/4 (Fig 1j/k/l); n=8 (Fig 4c/d), n=3 (Fig 4e); n=10 control / 13 CalA (Fig 5d).

**Simulation (theory):**
- **Model type:** Continuum 2D active-fluid model with a phase-field boundary (adapted from Kumar–Bois–Jülicher–Grill pulsatory active fluids, ref 31, and Sakamoto–Miyazaki–Maeda PRR 2023, ref 17). Actomyosin = active fluid with passive viscous stress σ_vis + myosin-generated active stress σ_act. Myosin density ∝ F-actin density → single-component mass conservation.
- **Dynamics:** force balance (viscous+active stress vs. friction) + mass conservation (advection–diffusion with polymerization source / depolymerization sink) + phase-field equation for the confinement boundary. Nondimensionalized. 2D. No conservation of total monomer number imposed (extract has excess free G-actin).
- **Control parameters:** Pe (Péclet), k_p^surf (surface polymerization rate). Fixed set for figures: Pe = 50, k_p^surf = 1.5 (Figs 3, 5); k_p^bulk/k_p^surf = 0.5; λ = 1/3. Polymerization higher near surface than bulk (contraction leaves boundary-nucleated growth). Δ = 0.1 R_μ (interface width scale, Fig 3b). Sweeps: Pe ≈ 30–120, k_p^surf ≈ 0.5–3.

## Governing Equations
Nondimensionalized equations of motion (transcribed from p.4/p.5, Fig 3 region):

**(Eq 1) — velocity of the active fluid (force balance):**
  **v = ∇²v + λ ∇(∇·v) + ∇f**
  Meaning: fluid velocity from balance of active+viscous stress against confinement friction; f encodes the density dependence of the active stress. λ = 1 + η_b/η (bulk-to-shear viscosity ratio), set λ = 1/3.

**(Eq 2) — actomyosin mass conservation (advection–diffusion with turnover):**
  **∂ρ/∂t + Pe ∇·(ρ v) = ∇²ρ + k_p − k_d ρ + ε_ρ ∇² (δE/δρ)**
  Meaning: density ρ transported by advection (Pe·advective flux) + diffusion (∇²ρ), with source = polymerization k_p, sink = depolymerization k_d ρ, plus an effective-energy term (E, ε_ρ) enforcing no-flux boundaries.

**(Eq 3) — phase-field boundary:**
  **∂ϕ/∂t = D_ϕ ∇²ϕ − Γ_ϕ U′(ϕ)**
  Meaning: phase field ϕ defines the confinement boundary geometry (double-well potential U(ϕ), mobility Γ_ϕ, diffusion D_ϕ).

**Péclet number definition:**
  **Pe = (ζΔμ)₀ / (D γ)**
  where (ζΔμ)₀ = contractile stress per F-actin due to myosin, D = diffusion constant, γ = friction coefficient. Pe = ratio of advective to diffusive time scales.

**Viscosity ratio:** λ = 1 + η_b/η (η_b = bulk viscosity, η = shear/dynamic viscosity); set to 1/3.

**Roundness:** R = m/M (m = minor axis, M = major axis of ellipse fit to cluster). R=1 ⇒ circular.

**Quartic parameter:** ratio of the 4th to the 0th Fourier mode of the cluster boundary (larger ⇒ stronger 4-fold symmetry).

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Active stress** σ_act ∝ myosin density ∝ ρ (F-actin density); enters Eq 1 through ∇f, where f gives the density dependence of the active (contractile) stress. Contractile stress per filament = (ζΔμ)₀.
- **Passive viscous stress** σ_vis: Newtonian, controlled by shear viscosity η and bulk viscosity η_b via λ = 1 + η_b/η = 1/3.
- **Friction/drag:** confinement friction coefficient γ balances the divergence of the stress tensor (basis of Eq 1). Enters Pe denominator.
- **Turnover kinetics (first-order):** source k_p (polymerization; surface k_p^surf vs bulk k_p^bulk, ratio 0.5), sink k_d ρ (depolymerization) — linear reaction terms, not Bell/catch bond (this is a continuum model, no explicit crosslinker/motor bond kinetics).
- **Phase-field double-well potential** U(ϕ) with derivative U′(ϕ): stabilizes the sharp confinement interface; no-flux enforced through effective energy E (ε_ρ ∇²(δE/δρ) term).

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Weak-to-moderate — no. This is a continuum active-fluid/phase-field model, the opposite of DCM's surface-mesh cell; the cluster is a bulk contractile gel, not a cortex/membrane cell. But the *phenomenology* (boundary geometry transferred to contractile gel shape; area set by confinement volume; corner stress concentration) is a useful qualitative cross-check for confined-aggregate behavior. Not a direct parameter source for DCM.
- **FF?** Yes (moderate) — this is the FF physical regime (actin + myosin + Arp2/3 contractile network) but at continuum scale. The measurable validation targets — inward actin flow speed v=1.20±0.41 µm/s, wave period T≈101 s, contraction-driven cluster formation, and the mapping of polymerization rate → shape vs contractility → size — are meaningful emergent-behavior benchmarks for a native FF actomyosin cortex/network under confinement. The Xenopus-extract actin flow speed (~1 µm/s) is a good sanity number for FF myosin-driven advection.
- **TAG-validation?** Yes — quantitative in-vitro numbers worth registering: actin wave period 101.0±7.9 s, inward flow 1.20±0.41 µm/s, roundness R vs W/D law, cluster-area∝well-volume, quartic 0.069→0.088. All measured (not oracle), DOI-verified (10.1021/acs.nanolett.5c02558). Candidate SourceEvidence rows for the confined-actomyosin/active-fluid claim cluster. Note the model parameters (Pe, k_p^surf) are nondimensional and model-internal, not physiological constants — flag as model, not to be registered as physical setpoints.
