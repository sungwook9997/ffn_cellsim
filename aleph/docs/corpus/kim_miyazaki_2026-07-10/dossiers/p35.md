## Summary
Using their previously developed agent-based Brownian dynamics model of a cortex-like thin actomyosin network (F-actin + actin cross-linking proteins (ACPs) + myosin thick filaments, all represented as interconnected cylindrical segments), the authors ask how buckling-induced F-actin severing modulates myosin-driven network contraction — a mechanism prior theory/simulation had neglected. They first map the contraction phase space in the absence of severing over motor density R_M and ACP density R_ACP (molar ratios to actin), reproducing the known result that large contraction (>50%) requires R_M above a critical minimum with an intermediate window of R_ACP (buckling is required, but too many ACPs suppress buckling). They then add a curvature-dependent severing rate k_s,A = k⁰_s,A·exp(θ_s,A/λ_s,A) and sweep the zero-angle severing rate constant k⁰_s,A against F-actin bending stiffness κ_b,A (up to 64× the reference). The central finding: when filaments can sever, the *largest* contraction occurs at *intermediate* bending rigidity, not the lowest — because very flexible filaments buckle and sever so frequently that the network fragments into multiple small foci and loses connectivity, while too-stiff filaments do not buckle and barely contract. This optimal intermediate κ_b,A becomes more conspicuous in loosely cross-linked networks (low R_ACP) and with more motors (high R_M), i.e. when the ratio R_ACP/R_M is low. They summarize the outcome as a 3D phase diagram over (R_M, R_ACP, k⁰_s,A, κ_b,A) with three end states: single-focus large contraction, single-focus negligible contraction (buckling inhibited), and multiple-foci small contraction (over-severing).

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** Agent-based / coarse-grained Brownian dynamics of a reconstituted-like actomyosin network. Three explicit agents, all built from interconnected cylindrical segments:
  - **F-actin:** serially connected cylindrical segments with polarity (barbed + pointed ends).
  - **ACP (actin cross-linking protein):** two cylindrical segments (a cross-linker connecting two F-actins).
  - **Motor (myosin thick filament):** a backbone connected to 8 arms; each motor arm represents 8 myosin heads (→ effectively a bipolar minifilament-like construct with 8 arms × 8 heads = 64 heads).
- **Equation of motion:** Langevin equation integrated with the **Euler (Euler–Maruyama) scheme** (explicit; overdamped/inertialess Brownian dynamics). Deterministic forces = bending forces (maintain equilibrium angles) + extensional/stretching forces (maintain equilibrium segment lengths) + repulsive volume-exclusion forces between F-actins. Stochastic (thermal) forces drive fluctuation.
- **ACP kinetics:** ACPs bind to F-actin at a constant rate; unbind in a **force-dependent** (slip-bond / Bell-like) manner.
- **Motor kinetics:** a motor arm binds to F-actin and walks toward the **barbed end** (force–velocity relation used in the underlying Kim model; specifics in ESI).
- **Severing:** F-actin is severed at a curvature-dependent rate (Eq. 1). Severing = breaking the chain between two adjacent points on the F-actin = disappearance of one actin segment. Model taken from Jung, Murrell & Kim, ACS Macro Lett. 2016 (ref 15), validated there vs. in vitro.
- **Domain / geometry:** very thin quasi-2D computational domain **20 × 20 × 0.1 μm**, **no periodic boundary condition**. Network self-assembles within a **circular region of radius 8 μm** (Fig. 1B). "Cortex-like thin" network.
- **Protocol:**
  1. **Assembly stage:** actin monomers nucleated + polymerized into F-actin; ACPs bind to form functional cross-links; motors assembled into thick filaments with arms bound but **not walking**.
  2. **Contraction stage:** motors begin walking → network contracts to foci over ~100–500 s.
- **Contraction quantification:** endpoints of actin segments projected on the x–y plane → spatial density map at final time → resolution reduced to a 3D histogram → foci auto-detected (density above threshold; adjacent centers merged into one focus). Endpoints back-traced to t=0; for each focus, center = mean of endpoint x,y positions; mean radius R_i(t) = mean distance of member endpoints to that center. Extent of contraction ξ from Eq. 2.
- **Binding sites:** each actin segment has a limited number of binding sites (40) — cited as the reason volume-exclusion between motors/ACPs is only indirectly captured.

## Governing Equations
1. **(Eq. 1) Curvature-dependent F-actin severing rate:**
   k_s,A = k⁰_s,A · exp( θ_s,A / λ_s,A )
   — Severing rate of an F-actin increases exponentially with the local bending. θ_s,A = sum of the two adjacent bending angles at a point on the F-actin; k⁰_s,A = zero-angle severing rate constant; λ_s,A = sensitivity (characteristic angle) governing how steeply rate rises with θ_s,A. (This is not Bell force — it is an *angle*-activated rate; buckling raises θ_s,A → raises k_s,A.)

2. **(Eq. 2) Extent of network contraction:**
   ξ(t) = 1 − [ Σ_i^N π R_i(t)² ] / [ Σ_i^N π R_i(0)² ]
   — Fractional reduction of the total foci "area" (sum over N foci of the disk area π R_i²). ξ=0 → no contraction; ξ=1 → maximal contraction. R_i(t) = mean radius of focus i.

3. **(Definition) Instantaneous contraction rate:**
   ξ̇(t) = dξ/dt.
   Steady state defined as the time when ξ̇(t) < 0.1·ξ̇(0). t_ss = time to reach steady state; ξ_ss = ξ at steady state; **average contraction rate = ξ_ss / t_ss**.

4. **(Implicit, from model description) Langevin / Brownian dynamics EOM** (form not written explicitly in the main text; deterministic bending + extensional + repulsive forces plus a stochastic thermal force, integrated by Euler). Full functional forms (harmonic bending/stretch potentials, motor force–velocity, ACP unbinding) live in the ESI and in refs 14 (Jung, Murrell & Kim, Comput. Part. Mech. 2015) and 16 (Mak, Zaman, Kamm & Kim, Nat. Commun. 2016).

## Force Laws & Potentials
Stated qualitatively in the main text (explicit functional forms deferred to ESI / prior Kim-model papers):
- **Bending force:** restores each triplet of segments to an equilibrium angle (harmonic in angle in the canonical Kim model); its stiffness is the tunable κ_b,A (bending rigidity of F-actin), swept up to 64× the reference κ*_b,A.
- **Extensional (stretching) force:** restores each segment to its equilibrium length (harmonic in length).
- **Repulsive volume-exclusion force:** between F-actins only (no volume exclusion between motors, and only indirect between motors/ACPs due to the 40-binding-site cap → motors can overlap and form very compact aggregates).
- **Thermal (stochastic) force:** Gaussian white noise satisfying fluctuation–dissipation (drives Brownian motion).
- **ACP unbinding:** force-dependent (slip / Bell-Evans-type) off-rate.
- **Motor:** walks toward the barbed end with a force–velocity relation (Kim-model canonical; Hill-like / linear-stall variants used in the underlying references).
- **Severing "law":** angle-activated exponential rate, Eq. 1 (not a mechanical force but a stochastic bond-breaking rate).

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Low-direct. This is a fine-grained filament network, not a surface-mesh continuum cell. It does NOT model a membrane/cortex-tension/turgor/nucleus cell. Only tangential relevance: the emergent contraction-to-foci and the ξ area-reduction metric echo aggregate/cortex compaction qualitatively.
- **FF?** **Yes — high.** This is essentially the FF engine's target physics: explicit F-actin as bending-segment beams, ACP cross-linkers with force-dependent (Bell-like) unbinding, myosin thick filaments (8 arms × 8 heads, barbed-end walking), volume exclusion, Langevin/Euler Brownian dynamics — the same Taeyoon-Kim agent-based lineage FF draws on. The **curvature-dependent severing law (Eq. 1)** is a concrete, importable mechanism FF currently lacks (buckling-induced fragmentation → connectivity loss → foci count). The contraction-vs-(R_M, R_ACP, κ_b,A, k⁰_s,A) phase behavior and ξ(t) traces are direct FF-parity/validation targets. Note the ×40-mesoscale caveat: this model uses cortex-density parameters that FF should reconcile against its native cortex.
- **TAG-validation?** **Yes.** Multiple quantitative targets suitable for KB/gate anchoring: >50% contraction requires R_M ≥ R_M^min and intermediate R_ACP; buckling-suppression critical R_ACP ~0.32; non-monotonic ξ_ss(κ_b,A) with severing; t_ss ∝ κ_b,A; critical κ_b,A ∝ R_ACP/R_M; 64× κ_b,A ↔ L_p 0.58 μm. Caveat: the *dimensional* parameters (spring constants, motor stepping rate, k⁰_s,A absolute values, λ_s,A) live in the ESI which is not in this file — full parameter provenance requires ingesting the ESI† and the underlying refs 14/15/16.
