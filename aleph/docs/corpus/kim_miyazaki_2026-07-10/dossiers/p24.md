## Summary
The paper asks how myosin motors move (or stall) in *disorganized* cross-linked actin networks — the actin-side complement to the well-studied kinesin/dynein-on-microtubule active transport — and what rescues them from confinement. Using Taeyoon Kim's well-established agent-based Brownian-dynamics actomyosin model (F-actin, ACP cross-linkers, and myosin motors coarse-grained as cylindrical segments obeying the Langevin equation, with force-dependent motor walking/unbinding from the Parallel Cluster Model and Bell's-law ACP slip-bond unbinding), motors were tracked on pre-assembled 5×5×0.2 µm cortex-like networks under swept network connectivity (low/med/high via ⟨L_f⟩ and R_ACP), motor number N_M, ACP unbinding rate k⁰_u,ACP, and F-actin turnover rate k_t,A. In the ABSENCE of F-actin turnover, motor motion is confined/stalled by three distinct mechanisms — (1) **local force-induced stalling** in highly connected networks (independent of motor density), (2) **global force-induced stalling** by network-scale force transmission through a web-like taut-filament structure in low-connectivity networks with many motors and very slow ACP unbinding, and (3) **confinement by network aggregation** into aster-like polarity-sorted structures at low connectivity + high motor density + fast ACP unbinding (motors keep walking near the aster center but cannot escape). Two of these (local force stalling, aggregation confinement) were qualitatively verified with reconstituted 2-D phalloidin-stabilized F-actin + α-actinin + skeletal-muscle-myosin networks. Crucially, WITH physiological F-actin treadmilling (k_t,A > 0), all three confinement regimes vanish: turnover continuously relaxes forces and disassembles aggregates, so motors move superdiffusively and ergodically for long times. Conclusion: fast cortical F-actin turnover is what keeps intracellular myosin-driven transport effective, and cells likely tune transport by tuning turnover.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type**: Agent-based coarse-grained **Brownian dynamics** (over-damped Langevin), the Kim-lab actomyosin framework (Jung, Murrell & Kim 2015; Kim 2015; Kim, Hwang, Lee & Kamm 2009; Mak, Zaman, Kamm & Kim 2016). F-actin, ACP, and motors are all built from **cylindrical segments** connected by elastic hinges.
- **Governing dynamics**: Each cylindrical segment's motion obeys the **Langevin equation** (over-damped/Brownian; explicit form is in SI). Deterministic forces = extensional (spring) + bending (hinge) + volume-exclusion (repulsive) + motor/cross-link forces; plus thermal (stochastic) force and viscous drag.
- **F-actin dynamics**: nucleation (rate k_n,A) of one segment → fast barbed-end polymerization (k_+,A) → optional pointed-end depolymerization (k_-,A). No-turnover case: k_-,A = 0. Turnover case: k_-,A = k_+,A → **treadmilling** at rate k_t,A (≡ k_-,A = k_+,A), constant mean filament length at steady state.
- **ACP dynamics**: bind to actin binding sites with no contact-angle preference. Two flavors: **permanent** (never unbind; mimics scruin) and **transient** (unbind via Bell's-law slip bond, Eq 1; reference values from filamin A, Ferrer 2008).
- **Motor dynamics**: **one-arm** (binds 1 F-actin, cannot generate large force — "pulling a rope on a slippery surface") and **two-arm** (binds a pair of F-actins; can develop large force if bound to relatively antiparallel filaments; represents a multi-myosin cargo or a myosin thick filament). After binding, each arm either unbinds (k_u,M) or walks toward the barbed end (k_w,M); both rates from the **Parallel Cluster Model (PCM)** (Erdmann & Schwarz 2012; Erdmann, Albert & Schwarz 2013), which captures cooperative mechanochemistry of several myosin heads. Both rates decrease with increasing arm load; walking stops beyond stall (**3.8 pN per myosin head**). On reaching the barbed end the arm slides off; after slide-off/unbinding an arm may rebind a different F-actin (**hopping**); if all arms detach the motor **diffuses freely** until rebinding.
- **Geometry / BCs**: 3-D thin rectangular slab **5 × 5 × 0.2 µm** (cortex-like), **periodic boundary in x and y** only. Nucleation random, perpendicular to z, equal probability → randomly oriented cortex-like network.
- **Network assembly protocol**: networks assembled WITHOUT motors, run 1,000 s to reach steady-state morphology (avoids ongoing bundling confounding motor tracking), THEN motors imported onto the frozen-morphology preassembled network (mirrors Linsmeier 2016 in-vitro protocol).
- **Measurement / analysis**: TE-MSD (Eq 2), E-MSD (Eq 3), τ-MSD with τ=10 s (Eq 4); local power-law slope α classifies motion (α<1 subdiffusive, =1 diffusive, 1<α<2 superdiffusive, =2 ballistic). TE-MSD of actin segments used as a proxy for network elasticity/stiffness (higher connectivity → lower actin TE-MSD → stiffer). Aggregation/heterogeneity quantified with radial distribution function g(r) (Eq 5), Δr = 20 nm, averaged over 0.1<r<1 µm every 100 s → ḡ(t); metric g_max/g_init. 10–100 motor trajectories per condition (multiple runs when ≤10 motors/sim).
- **Integrator/timestep**: not stated in this main text (Brownian-dynamics scheme detailed in SI / prior Kim-lab papers). Simulations run to t = 1,000 s.
- **In-vitro experiment**: 2-D biomimetic cortex on a supported lipid bilayer (Murrell & Gardel 2014 protocol). Prepolymerized phalloidin-stabilized F-actin crowded onto bilayer with methylcellulose; α-actinin as transient cross-linker; **skeletal-muscle myosin (SKMM)** as the (highly non-processive, ~4% duty) motor, fluorescently tracked. Low motor density: individual-motor TE-MSD; high motor density: inter-frame image correlation as a proxy for motion.

## Governing Equations
1. **Bell's-law ACP unbinding rate (Eq 1)** — force-dependent slip-bond off-rate of transient cross-linkers:
   k_u,ACP = k⁰_u,ACP · exp( λ_u,ACP · |F⃗_s,ACP| / (k_B T) ),  if r ≥ r_0,ACP;
   k_u,ACP = k⁰_u,ACP,  if r < r_0,ACP.
   Meaning: cross-link off-rate rises exponentially with the spring force |F⃗_s,ACP| only when the ACP arm length r exceeds its equilibrium r_0,ACP; positive λ_u,ACP ⇒ slip bond. k⁰_u,ACP = zero-force off-rate; k_BT = thermal energy.
2. **TE-MSD (Eq 2)** — time- and ensemble-averaged mean-squared displacement:
   TE-MSD(τ) = (1/N) Σ_{i=1}^{N} [ 1/(T−τ) ∫_0^{T−τ} ‖r_i(t+τ) − r_i(t)‖² dt ].
   Meaning: average distance² a motor moves over lag τ, averaged over time and motors.
3. **E-MSD (Eq 3)** — ensemble-averaged MSD from the initial position:
   E-MSD(τ) = (1/N) Σ_{i=1}^{N} ⟨ ‖r_i(τ) − r_i(0)‖² ⟩.
   Meaning: displacement² from t=0; if motion is time-invariant (ergodic), E-MSD ≈ TE-MSD.
4. **τ-MSD (Eq 4)** — fixed-lag MSD as a function of absolute time t:
   τ-MSD(t) = (1/N) Σ_{i=1}^{N} ⟨ ‖r_i(t+τ) − r_i(t)‖² ⟩,  with τ = 10 s.
   Meaning: resolves WHEN (at which absolute time) motors start slowing down / stalling.
5. **Radial distribution function (Eq 5)** — network aggregation/heterogeneity metric:
   g(r) = ⟨ P(r) / (2π r Δr ρ) ⟩.
   Meaning: normalized local particle (actin-segment) density at radial distance r from a particle; g>1 aggregation, g<1 depletion, g≈1 uniform. Aggregation measure = g_max/g_init of ḡ(t) averaged over 0.1<r<1 µm.
- **(Implicit) Langevin equation of motion** for each cylindrical segment (form not transcribed in main text; over-damped Brownian dynamics with extensional + bending + repulsive + thermal forces). Extensional (κ_s) and bending (κ_b) potentials maintain equilibrium segment lengths and angles respectively (harmonic springs / hinges — see Force Laws).

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Extensional (stretching) force**: harmonic spring on each cylindrical segment enforcing equilibrium length; stiffness **κ_s** (per-species: κ_s,A actin, κ_s,ACP cross-linker, κ_s,M motor arm — labeled in Figure 1a).
- **Bending force**: harmonic hinge between adjacent segments enforcing an equilibrium angle; stiffness **κ_b** (κ_b,A actin, κ_b,ACP cross-linker — Figure 1a).
- **Volume-exclusion (repulsive) force**: repulsive interaction preventing overlap of F-actin cylinders.
- **Motor force generation**: two-arm motor develops tensile force by walking toward barbed ends on a pair of (ideally antiparallel) filaments; force magnitude capped by stall (3.8 pN/head); walking & unbinding rates from PCM as functions of arm load (monotonically decreasing with force — Figure S1).
- **ACP cross-link force**: harmonic arm springs; unbinding governed by Bell's law Eq 1 (slip bond).
- (Numerical spring constants κ_s,·, κ_b,·, drag coefficients, and thermal-force amplitude are tabulated in Supporting-Information Tables S1–S2, NOT in the 15-page PDF.)

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?**: **Weak/indirect.** DCM is a surface-mesh continuum cell model; this paper is a filament-resolved network model with no membrane/turgor/nucleus. Only tangential relevance: the cortical actomyosin turnover → stress-relaxation insight informs how cortical tension might relax in DCM, but no directly transferable equations/parameters.
- **FF?**: **YES — strongly.** This is essentially the Cytosim-class fine-grained target that FF re-implements: explicit actin filaments as segmented beams (κ_s, κ_b), Bell's-law slip-bond cross-linker unbinding (Eq 1, filamin-A reference k⁰_u,ACP=0.115 s⁻¹, λ from Ferrer 2008 — already the FF cross-linker anchor), force-dependent myosin walking/unbinding with 3.8 pN/head stall (matches FF's Hill/stall convention and the CLAUDE.md F*=7pN debt note), NMII unloaded walking <140 nm/s, F-actin treadmilling k_t,A. Directly usable as a mechanism cross-check for FF motor/cross-linker kinetics, aggregation, and turnover-driven force relaxation.
- **TAG-validation?**: **YES.** Provides clean, citable validation targets: stall force 3.8 pN/head; k⁰_u,ACP = 0.115 s⁻¹ (filamin A, Ferrer 2008); NMII v_walk < 140 nm/s (Cuda 1997); SKMM <5 µm/s & 4% duty (Harris & Warshaw 1993); MSD anomalous-exponent regimes; turnover rate thresholds (k_t,A=20 s⁻¹ erases local stalling). Worth SourceEvidence rows — several already overlap existing KB anchors (Ferrer 2008 for α-actinin/filamin k_off; PCM Erdmann/Schwarz for myosin).
