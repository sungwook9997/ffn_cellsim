## Summary
The paper asks how the neuronal actin-side-binding protein **drebrin E** regulates actomyosin force generation, and why literature affinity (Kd=120 nM) is inconsistent with strong inhibition at ~10 nM. Using an **optically controlled microscopic heat pulse** (a focused 1455 nm IR laser creating a concentric temperature gradient, switchable at 0.1 s) applied to an **in-vitro actin–HMM motility assay**, the authors measure single-filament sliding velocity from room temperature up to physiological 37 °C without thermally denaturing myosin. They find that drebrin-E inhibition of actin sliding is **rapidly and reversibly relieved by heating**, in a **drebrin-concentration-dependent** way. Quantum-dot-labeled drebrin E imaging shows drebrin E **stays bound** to the filament during heat-induced sliding (Friedman ANOVA p=0.344, no intensity change), ruling out simple thermal dissociation as the mechanism. A re-measured Kd (luminescence densitometry) is **6 nM at 25 °C, rising to 32 nM at 37 °C** — far below the old 120 nM. Fitting the velocity-vs-[drebrin] curves with a **Hill equation** yields an **IC50 rising from a few nM to >30 nM** and a **Hill coefficient jumping from ~1 (below 34 °C) to 4.0 at 36.5–37.5 °C**, i.e. strong cooperativity emerges specifically at body temperature. The authors conclude drebrin E **allosterically modifies actin filament structure** (helical pitch change, analogous to tropomyosin) to cooperatively regulate the actomyosin duty at physiological temperature — bound drebrin regulates without dissociating.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** Experimental **in-vitro sliding (motility) assay** — purified heavy meromyosin (HMM) immobilized on a glass surface, rhodamine–phalloidin-labeled actin filaments slid over it in presence of ATP; single-filament fluorescence tracking. NOT a computational model — no integrator/timestep. Only an empirical Hill fit and geometric velocity/temperature estimators.
- **Heating apparatus:** Focused **1455 nm IR laser** creates a concentric temperature gradient in the aqueous medium (Peltier-free optical microheater); on/off shutter with **0.1 s** temporal resolution. Focal point >40 °C (room 25 °C + IR rise); field-of-view border ~30 °C. Usable physiological window: within ~10 µm of the heat source.
- **Temperature calibration:** From rhodamine–phalloidin fluorescence-intensity thermosensitivity (Figure S1); calibration done in the **absence of ATP** with long, high-density actin filaments on HMM (so filaments don't slide during mapping).
- **Sliding-velocity measurement geometry:** distance traversed by a filament over **0.5 s = 5 frames** (from point (x1,y1) to (x2,y2)); local temperature = average of the map-derived temperatures at the two endpoints (from distance to heat source at (x0,y0)). Immobile filaments assigned velocity 0.
- **Filaments:** relatively short **~1–5 µm** actin filaments for velocity assay; long filaments for calibration. Actin monomer diameter ~7 nm.
- **Drebrin E conditions:** 0, 10, 20, 50, 250 nM (unlabeled) + 31 nM Qdot-labeled drebrin E.
- **Direct drebrin tracking:** drebrin E labeled with quantum dots (Qdots) via anti-drebrin-E antibody; dual-excitation imaging (actin rhodamine λex=549 nm green; Qdot λex=485 nm blue). Kymographs over before(2.5 s)/heating(5 s)/after(2.5 s).
- **Affinity assays:** cosedimentation assay + **luminescence densitometry** to re-measure Kd (vs old Coomassie/SDS-PAGE densitometry). Preincubation control: drebrin E held at 45 °C for 1 min → inhibition statistically unchanged (thermal-denaturation control, Figure S5).
- **Statistics:** Friedman analysis of variance for Qdot F.I. (p=0.344, NS); means ± s.e.m. (velocity) and ± s.d. (mobile fraction) over 3 experiments.

## Governing Equations
All equations are empirical/geometric (no dynamical model in this paper).

**(1) Temperature-gradient calibration (radial profile):**
`T(x) = −3.1 · ln(x / x0) + 42.9`,  with `x0 = 1 µm` (set to make the log argument unitless), T in °C, x = distance from heat source in µm.
Meaning: fitted logarithmic decay of temperature away from the IR focal spot (Fig 1E).

**(2) Sliding velocity (per event):**
`v = sqrt( (x2 − x1)^2 + (y2 − y1)^2 ) / 0.5`
Meaning: displacement of a filament over the 0.5 s (5-frame) window → sliding speed in µm s⁻¹ (Fig 4A).

**(3) Local temperature assignment (per event):**
`T = [ T1(x1,y1 ; x0,y0) + T2(x2,y2 ; x0,y0) ] / 2`
Meaning: event temperature = average of the map temperatures at the two filament endpoints, each obtained from its distance to the heat source (Fig 4A).

**(4) Hill equation (velocity vs drebrin concentration fit):**
`v = 1 − [ C^n (1 − b) ] / ( k^n + C^n )`
where v = normalized sliding velocity, C = drebrin E concentration, k = IC50, n = Hill coefficient, b = baseline (residual normalized velocity at saturating drebrin). Meaning: inhibitory dose–response of actomyosin sliding by drebrin, curve shape shifts decay-type → sigmoidal as temperature rises (Fig 4C; fit params in Table S1).

**(5) Drebrin occupancy of actin binding sites (equilibrium fractional saturation):**
`p_drebrin = [drebrin] / ( Kd + [drebrin] )`
Meaning: fraction of drebrin binding sites on the actin filament occupied at free concentration [drebrin] with dissociation constant Kd (used to argue occupancy stays high even where inhibition is relieved).

## Force Laws & Potentials
None. This is an experimental single-molecule/ensemble assay — there are no interaction potentials, bending/stretching laws, motor force-velocity laws, or bond-kinetics rate laws written down. The only "force" statements are qualitative: actomyosin generates sliding force via ATP-driven HMM cross-bridges; drebrin E inhibits sliding velocity; temperature raises actin–myosin affinity and number of force-generating heads (cited from muscle-physiology refs 19–21). The mechanistic gold here is the **cooperativity parameters (Hill n, IC50, Kd(T))** and the **velocity(T, [drebrin]) dataset**, not a force law.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** No (direct). DCM is a surface-mesh continuum (membrane/cortex/nucleus); this paper has no continuum mechanics, no cell-scale geometry. Indirect only: it constrains the temperature/regulator dependence of cortical actomyosin activity, not usable as a DCM parameter.
- **FF?** Yes (weak-moderate). FF's myosin minifilaments (Hill force-velocity) slide actin; this paper supplies an **actomyosin sliding-velocity dataset** (~7–15 µm s⁻¹ HMM/actin over 28–37 °C) and a mechanistic **actin-side-binding-protein regulator** (drebrin E) that inhibits sliding cooperatively (Hill n up to 4.0, IC50 few–30 nM, Kd 6–32 nM). If FF ever adds an allosteric actin-structure modulator or a temperature-dependent motor, these are candidate anchors. The HMM sliding speed and its T-dependence are useful sanity references for FF motor speed. Not a core cortex/crosslinker/clutch parameter today.
- **TAG-validation?** Yes (as a literature/parameter source). Clean quantitative anchors worth KB registration: drebrin E–actin **Kd = 6 nM (25 °C) / 32 nM (37 °C)**, drebrin:actin **1:5 stoichiometry**, actin **helical half-pitch 36→40 nm**, **Hill coefficient 1→4** cooperativity of an actin side-binding regulator, HMM actin sliding velocity ~7–15 µm/s.
