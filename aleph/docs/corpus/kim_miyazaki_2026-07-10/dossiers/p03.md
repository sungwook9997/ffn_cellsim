## Summary
The paper asks how a cross-linked actin cortex can flow (undergo large, irreversible, fluidlike creep) while maintaining long-range connectivity, and specifically how stress relaxation depends on the interplay of **network architecture** (filament length, density, cross-link density) and **turnover** (treadmilling / ACP unbinding). Using an agent-based **Brownian dynamics** simulation of dynamically treadmilling semiflexible actin filaments cross-linked by elastic ACPs (α-actinin-like), the authors run **uniaxial creep tests**: a thin 5×5×1 µm cortical slab is pinned at −x, clamped filament ends, and a constant tensile stress σ is applied at +x while normal strain ε(t) is monitored for 100 s. They find a **nonaffine** regime where, at any instant, ~5% of filaments (percolating, network-spanning pathways) carry ~99% of the load, and macroscopic creep proceeds by a continuous cycle of pathway formation → force buildup → turnover → force release. Key results: (1) a roughly **constant creep rate** (ε̇) → constant **effective viscosity** η_eff = σ/ε̇; (2) η_eff is **inversely proportional to the turnover scale factor n** (ε̇ ∝ n, i.e. ∝ filament turnover rate); (3) a **biphasic stress dependence** — below a critical stress σ_th (~1 Pa) creep rate rises linearly with σ (constant η_eff), above σ_th creep rate is **stress-independent** (η_eff ∝ σ) over ~2 decades. All (n, σ) data collapse onto a master curve η_eff·n = η_eff⁰·max(σ,σ_th)/σ_th with η_eff⁰ ≈ 35 Pa·s, σ_th ≈ 1 Pa. A simple analytic **toy model** of parallel network-spanning springs that form at rate k⁺, elongate, and turn over at rate k⁻ reproduces both regimes: with linear force-extension ε̇ ~ k⁻σ/E₀; with a nonlinear (stiffening) force-extension law the model predicts the linear→plateau transition at σ_th ~ E₀·Δε_avg, above which ε̇ = k⁻·Δε_avg (independent of σ). The transition is traced to the **nonlinear force-extension of individual pathway subsegments** (serially connected filament+ACP chains) whose relative extension L_r = L_ee/L_c stiffens sharply as L_r → 1. Biphasic behavior is robust to actin concentration C_A, cross-link ratio R, filament length ⟨L_f⟩, turnover mode (treadmilling/severing/bursting), y-direction boundary conditions, and force-dependent (Bell) vs force-independent ACP unbinding.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** Agent-based **Brownian dynamics** (Langevin, inertia neglected → overdamped) of a coarse-grained semiflexible actin filament network with explicit elastic ACP cross-linkers. Direct extension of Kim/Hwang/Kamm 2009 (ref 19) and 2011 (ref 17). This is the ancestral "Taeyoon Kim agent model" underlying CytoSim-style fine-grained simulators.
- **Filament representation:** actin filaments = series of cylindrical segments joined by elastic hinges (semiflexible rods) with fixed polarity (barbed/pointed ends). Segment length r₀,A = 140 nm, diameter r_c,A = 7 nm (coarse-graining justified because 140 nm ≪ actin persistence length 9 µm).
- **ACP representation:** pairs of rigid rods (two cylindrical arms) connected to each other and to actin filaments by elastic hinges; arm length r₀,ACP = 35 nm, diameter r_c,ACP = 10 nm (mimics α-actinin geometry). ACPs cross-link filament pairs with **no angular preference**.
- **Integrator:** explicit **Euler** scheme (Eq. 2), timestep **Δt = 2.3×10⁻⁶ s**.
- **Domain / BCs:** thin 3D rectangular slab; reference domain 5×5×1 µm (larger 10×10×1 µm used for Fig 3 C,D to avoid finite-size artifacts). **Periodic BCs in x and y** during preassembly; for the creep test, filaments crossing the ±x boundaries are severed and clamped, periodic BC in x is deactivated, −x is pinned, +x carries the applied stress. (A control varies y-direction PBC on/off, Fig S4.)
- **Preassembly:** actin monomers + ACPs self-assemble to a dynamic steady state until free-monomer concentration falls to ~1 µM under treadmilling.
- **Creep protocol:** hold −x fixed; impose constant σ (default 10 Pa; 0.2 Pa for the "linear-regime" comparisons) on +x; monitor ε(t) over 100 s. Constant stress maintained by **proportional+derivative (PD) feedback** adjusting the +x boundary position. Axial stress = Σ(forces on filaments clamped at +x)/area.
- **Turnover model:** idealized steady-state **treadmilling** — net monomer addition at barbed end, removal at pointed end, constant mean length. Single scale factor **n** tunes overall turnover rate independent of length/density. Simple nucleation added to keep filament number constant. Alternative turnover modes (severing ref 25, bursting disassembly ref 26) give similar creep if per-monomer turnover rate + mean length + density are matched.
- **Measurements:** η_eff = σ/ε̇ (ε̇ = slope over the quasi-constant window); viscous stress σ_v = Σ(viscous drag forces on the +x-connected network)/area; elastic stress σ_e = σ − σ_v; frequency-dependent moduli E′(f), E″(f) for f = 0.001–10 Hz (Fig S2).
- **Hydrodynamics:** neglected filament-filament hydrodynamic interactions (actin volume fraction <1%, high aspect ratio); per-segment drag constant (length-independent).

## Governing Equations
1. **Langevin (overdamped) equation of motion** — Eq. (1):
   `F_i − ζ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_i^B = 0`
   where r_i = position of i-th segment endpoint (actin or ACP), ζ_i = effective drag coefficient, F_i^B = stochastic force satisfying fluctuation-dissipation, F_i = net deterministic force. Meaning: inertialess force balance — deterministic + thermal + drag = 0.

2. **Euler position update** — Eq. (2):
   `r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (dr_i/dt)Δt = r_i(t) + (1/ζ_i)(F_i^B + F_i)Δt`, with Δt = 2.3×10⁻⁶ s. Meaning: explicit time integration of overdamped dynamics.

3. **Harmonic extension & bending potentials** — Eq. (3):
   `U_s = ½ k_s (r − r₀)²`  and  `U_b = ½ k_b (θ − θ₀)²`
   r = endpoint distance of a segment, θ = bending angle, subscript 0 = equilibrium. Meaning: filament and ACP stretch/bend elasticity as harmonic springs/hinges.

4. **Effective drag coefficient** — Eq. (4):
   `ζ_i = 3π η_m r_c,i · (3 + 2 r₀,i / r_c,i) / 5`
   η_m = medium viscosity. Meaning: Stokes-like translational drag of a cylindrical segment (from Clift, Grace & Weber, ref 23).

5. **Treadmilling monomer (de)polymerization rates** — Eq. (5):
   `k⁺_B = 6n [µM⁻¹s⁻¹]`, `k⁺_P = 0.6n [µM⁻¹s⁻¹]`, `k⁻_B = 0.6n [s⁻¹]`, `k⁻_P = 6n [s⁻¹]`
   (B = barbed end, P = pointed end; +/− = addition/removal). Meaning: force-independent stochastic polymerization kinetics; scale factor n tunes global turnover. Yields treadmilling rate **5.4n s⁻¹** at steady state.

6. **Bell's equation for ACP unbinding** (used only in force-dependence controls) — unnumbered (p. 3):
   `k_ub = k⁰_ub · exp(λ_ub F_s / k_B T)`
   k⁰_ub = zero-force off-rate, λ_ub = mechanical compliance (bond loading length), F_s = force, k_BT = thermal energy. Reference values k⁰_ub = 0.115 s⁻¹, λ_ub = 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m (from single-molecule expt, Ferrer 2008, ref 28). Meaning: force-accelerated (slip-bond) cross-link detachment.

7. **Master-curve collapse** — Eq. (6):
   `η_eff · n = η_eff⁰ · max(σ, σ_th) / σ_th`
   with η_eff⁰ ≈ 35 Pa·s, σ_th ≈ 1 Pa. Meaning: single scaling law unifying η_eff over n∈[≤0.3] and σ∈[0.1–100 Pa]; encodes ε̇ ∝ n and the biphasic σ dependence.

8. **Autocorrelation of per-filament force** — Eq. (7):
   `R(τ) = ⟨(F(t) − µ)(F(t+τ) − µ)⟩ / std(F)`
   µ, std(F) = mean & standard deviation of F(t). Meaning: measures the lifetime/turnover timescale of tension-bearing segments; decay slope ∝ n.

9. **Toy-model nonlinear force-extension law** — Eq. (8):
   `F(ε) = K ε if ε < ε_c ; F(ε) = ∞ if ε ≥ ε_c`
   Meaning: idealized stiffening element — linear spring up to critical strain ε_c, then rigid (infinite resistance). The linear counterpart is `F(ε) = Kε` (Fig 5A–C).

**Toy-model analytic creep predictions** (from Eq. 8 analysis; SI has full derivation):
- Linear elements: `ε̇ ~ k⁻ σ / E₀`, with `E₀ = n_L K` the linear elastic modulus of the spring array (n_L = k⁺/k⁻ = number of elements, held constant).
- Nonlinear elements: transition at critical stress `σ_th ~ E₀ Δε_avg` (Δε_avg = average strain increment to bring the next element to threshold). For σ < σ_th: `ε̇ ~ k⁻σ/E₀` (linear). For σ > σ_th: `ε̇ = k⁻ Δε_avg` (constant, independent of σ). In both regimes ε̇ ∝ k⁻ (turnover rate).

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Stretching:** harmonic, `U_s = ½ k_s (r − r₀)²` → force `F_s = −k_s(r − r₀)`. Actin extensional stiffness k_s,A and bending stiffness k_b,A assigned from experiment (refs 20, 22 — Isambert 1995, Kojima 1994) to reproduce persistence length 9 µm under thermal noise. Full numeric values are in Table S1 (SI, not in this file).
- **Bending:** harmonic hinge, `U_b = ½ k_b (θ − θ₀)²`. ACP parameters (θ₀,ACP1 = equilibrium angle between the two ACP arms; θ₀,ACP2 = angle between an ACP arm and the bound actin axis; k_s,ACP, k_b,ACP1, k_b,ACP2) set to "reasonable values" as in Kim 2009 (ref 19), owing to lack of data.
- **Excluded volume / repulsion:** harmonic repulsive force based on the minimum distance between pairs of actin segments (as in ref 19).
- **Drag (medium):** Eq. (4), Stokes cylinder, per-segment, length-independent.
- **Thermal force:** F_i^B satisfies fluctuation-dissipation (Gaussian white noise consistent with ζ_i and k_BT).
- **Cross-link (un)binding:** default = ACPs unbind only when the containing actin segment depolymerizes (turnover-driven detachment, force-independent). Optional = Bell slip-bond (Eq. 6 above) parameterized to Filamin A (ref 28) for the force-dependence control.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Indirect/moderate.
