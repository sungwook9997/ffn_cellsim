## Summary
The paper asks **when and how tension-induced fragmentation (severing) of individual actin filaments contributes to the macroscopic contraction of actomyosin networks** — a mechanism previously neglected in favor of buckling-induced fragmentation and cross-linker (ACP) unbinding. The authors combine (i) an **in-vitro reconstituted network** of purified rabbit skeletal-muscle actin + non-muscle myosin II thick filaments + the cross-linker **anillin** (with gelsolin as an external severer), imaged by fluorescence microscopy, and (ii) a **well-established agent-based Brownian-dynamics model** (Taeyoon Kim's actomyosin framework) in which F-actin, myosin thick filaments, and ACPs are coarse-grained cylindrical segments, ACPs unbind via **Bell's law**, myosin arms follow a **catch-bond force-velocity** law via the **parallel cluster model** (ATP-dependent head unbinding rate k₂₀), and a filament segment is **deterministically removed (fragmented) when its extensional tensile force exceeds 500 pN** (Tsuda 1996 single-molecule datum). Experiments show that cross-linker density, motor density and ATP tune contraction (more ACP → slower/larger clusters and eventually no contraction; more myosin → faster/larger; ATP → biphasic optimum); that gelsolin severing triggers contraction of otherwise-stable heavily-cross-linked networks; and that during contraction individual filaments fragment and their fragments **retract with a ≥2× speed jump in 8/10 events**, diagnostic of tension (not buckling) release. Simulations show tension-induced fragmentation matters most for **highly connected networks (high RACP, long ⟨Lf⟩, high RM, high duty ratio = low k₂₀)**: with long filaments (⟨Lf⟩=2.6 µm) fragmentation-only contraction is **catastrophic and faster** than ACP-unbinding-only, whereas at low connectivity ACP unbinding dominates. Conclusion: tension-induced F-actin fragmentation is a genuine, distinct force-relaxation / connectivity-reduction mechanism enabling contraction of networks that neither buckling nor ACP unbinding could contract.

## Model & Methods

### Experimental (in-vitro reconstitution)
- **Proteins:** actin + myosin II purified from rabbit skeletal muscle (Pardee & Spudich 1982); recombinant human **anillin** (the ACP; localizes to cytokinetic contractile ring) and human **gelsolin** (Ca-dependent severer). Concentrations by UV absorbance (myosin A₂₈₀=0.53 /mg/mL/cm, MW 470 kDa; HMM A₂₈₀=0.6, MW 350 kDa; G-actin A₂₉₀=0.63, MW 42 kDa) and SDS-PAGE.
- **Network contraction assay:** mix G-actin (3 µM) + myosin II + anillin in assay buffer (50 mM PIPES-Na pH 7.4, 50 mM KCl, 1 mM CaCl₂, 1 mM MgCl₂, 1 mM EGTA) + 2 mg/mL BSA + 1.0 µM alexa488/rhodamine-phalloidin + 0.5 mM DTT + variable ATP; oxygen scavenger (0.2% catalase, 1.5 mg/mL glucose, 50 U/mL glucose oxidase); ATP-regenerating system (20 mM creatine phosphate + 0.3 mg/mL creatine kinase). Flow chamber from 18×18 and 24×36 mm coverslips, height 0.11±0.03 mm, BSA-blocked; observation begins 30 s after mixing.
- **Imaging:** confocal (Olympus IX83 + CSU-X1, 488 nm 20 mW, 60× NA1.2, ~80 µm field) every 10 s / 0.5 s exposure; or epifluorescence (IX70, 20× NA0.5, ~300 µm field). Fragmentation assay: 100× NA1.40, imaged every 2 s.
- **Gelsolin trigger:** stable network assembled (RMYO=0.33, RANLN=0.24, 50 µM ATP), then 0.02–0.2 µM gelsolin loaded → final 0.002–0.02 µM.
- **Fragmentation-observation assay:** 3 µM G-actin, 1 µM dark phalloidin, 0.1% rhodamine-labeled F-actin, 0.5 µM anillin, 1 µM myosin II thick filament, 1 mM ATP; fragment position by 2D-Gaussian fit; retraction speed by Mark2 tracking (Furuta & Toyoshima 2008); speed from displacement over 10 s (5 frames).
- **Quantification:** ImageJ background subtraction + binarization; total actin area vs time (increase = formation, decrease = contraction); **contraction speed = linear-fit slope of area decrease**; **moving speed = PIV mean**; cluster defined intensity >150 a.u., long axis <200 µm; max cluster size = largest in final image.

### Computational (agent-based Brownian dynamics — Taeyoon Kim framework)
- **Model type:** agent-based Brownian/Langevin dynamics of coarse-grained interconnected cylindrical segments (Bidone 2017; Jung 2015; Kim 2015; Li 2017 lineage). Details in Supporting Text; parameters in Table S1 (not in this PDF).
- **Representation:** F-actin = serially connected polar segments (barbed/pointed ends). ACP = two arm segments joined at a center point; binds pairs of F-actins at constant rate, unbinds force-dependently (Bell's law). Motor = symmetric backbone of serially connected segments + **8 arms** equally spaced from center to left/right (bipolar myosin II thick-filament geometry); **each arm represents the ensemble of 8 myosin heads** with catch-bond binding kinetics + force-velocity (walk slower & unbind less under higher tension; Figure S4).
- **Forces:** extensional + bending harmonic potentials (equilibrium lengths & angles) for all segments (F-actin, motor backbone/arms, ACP) + repulsive volume-exclusion between overlapping actin segments (Equations S1–S3). Displacements from the Langevin equation (deterministic forces + thermal noise).
- **Domain / BCs:** thin slab **20 × 20 × 0.1 µm**; periodic in x,y; repulsive wall in z (force ∝ overlap with z boundary).
- **Protocol:** network self-assembly phase — actin slow nucleation + fast polymerization (stochastic, fixed rate constants) to target ⟨Lf⟩; ACPs transiently cross-link; motor arms self-assemble into thick filaments and bind/unbind without walking. After assembly, no further nucleation/polymerization (no depolymerization, monomers depleted → **Gaussian filament-length distribution**). Then motors begin walking toward barbed ends → stress generation.
- **ATP proxy:** ATP concentration is mimicked by varying the **ATP-dependent myosin-head unbinding rate k₂₀** in the **parallel cluster model** (Erdmann, Albert & Schwarz 2013; Erdmann & Schwarz 2012); higher k₂₀ ↔ higher CATP (lower duty ratio, higher walking speed).
- **Fragmentation rule:** deterministic — remove an actin segment whenever its **extensional (tensile) force > 500 pN** at any time (Tsuda 1996). ACP unbinding and/or fragmentation can each be independently switched off to isolate mechanisms.
- **Quantification:** each frame binned into **70×70 grid**; network area = fraction of grids occupied by ≥1 actin segment; **contraction speed = mean area-decrease rate over first 8 s**; moving speed = ensemble/time-averaged segment speed over first 8 s. Clusters detected by filtering grids with actin-segment count > 3× grid-mean, then K-means clustering (Hartigan & Wong 1979) with adjacency/boundary merging.

## Governing Equations
The explicit functional forms (Equations S1–S3) are in the Supporting Text, which is not in this PDF. The equations referenced/implied in the main text:

1. **Langevin (overdamped Brownian) equation of motion** — position update of every cylindrical segment: deterministic forces (extensional + bending + repulsive, from harmonic potentials) balanced by drag plus a Gaussian thermal-fluctuation term. `(governs all segment displacements per timestep)`
2. **Extensional harmonic potential (Eq. S1, form not shown):** restores equilibrium segment length; U_ext = ½ k_s (r − r₀)². `(F-actin stretch, motor backbone/arm stretch, ACP arm stretch)`
3. **Bending harmonic potential (Eq. S2, form not shown):** restores equilibrium angle between consecutive segments; U_bend = ½ k_b (θ − θ₀)². `(F-actin semiflexibility, motor/ACP geometry)`
4. **Repulsive volume-exclusion potential (Eq. S3, form not shown):** force ∝ overlap between actin segments (and z-boundary). `(excluded volume)`
5. **Bell's law for ACP unbinding:** k_off = k_off,0 · exp(F·λ / k_BT), the force-dependent off-rate of cross-linkers (Bell 1978). `(ACP catch/slip unbinding; explicit constants in Table S1, not shown here)`
6. **Myosin catch-bond force-velocity (parallel cluster model):** motor arms walk more slowly and unbind less frequently as tensile load increases; head unbinding governed by the ATP-dependent rate **k₂₀** (Erdmann–Schwarz PCM). `(motor mechanochemistry; curve = Figure S4)`
7. **Tension-induced fragmentation criterion:** F_ext(segment) > **F_frag = 500 pN** ⇒ segment removed. `(deterministic severing rule)`
8. **Force-accumulation estimate (worked example in text, p.12/13):** for ⟨Lf⟩=2.6 µm (≈370 actin monomers) with RM=0.32, ≈60 myosin heads act on a filament; assuming half bound and 5 pN stall/head ⇒ **average force ≈ 300 pN** on the filament; the Gaussian length distribution + stochastic binding push a subset above 500 pN. `(rationale for why fragmentation only fires at high connectivity/length/motor)`

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Extensional:** harmonic spring per segment (stretch stiffness k_s; Eq. S1). Constants in Table S1 (not in PDF).
- **Bending:** harmonic angular spring per pair of segments (bending stiffness k_b; Eq. S2). Sets F-actin persistence length.
- **Excluded volume:** repulsive force ∝ overlap between overlapping actin cylinders and against the z-wall (Eq. S3).
- **ACP unbinding:** Bell's law slip-bond, exponential force-dependence (Bell 1978).
- **Myosin II arm:** catch-bond binding kinetics + force-velocity representing an ensemble of 8 heads per arm, 8 arms per bipolar thick filament; per-head **stall force = 5 pN**; ATP-dependence via k₂₀ (parallel cluster model).
- **Fragmentation:** hard threshold, F > 500 pN → irreversible removal (no annealing/turnover in the model).

## Relevance flags
- **DCM? — LOW/INDIRECT.** DCM is a surface-mesh continuum cell; this is a filament-level in-vitro network with no membrane/cortex-tension continuum. Only indirect relevance: informs the emergent cortical-tension → network-contraction phenomenology and the anillin/contractile-ring context.
- **FF? — YES, HIGH.** This is exactly the FF regime: agent-based/Brownian-dynamics fine-grained actin with explicit ACP Bell's-law unbinding, bipolar myosin minifilament (8 arms × 8 heads) catch-bond force-velocity, excluded volume, harmonic stretch/bend, and a **tension-induced fragmentation rule (F>500 pN)** — a mechanism FF does not yet implement. Directly maps onto FF's cross-linker/myosin/severing machinery; the 500 pN threshold, 5 pN/head stall, k₂₀ parallel-cluster-model, and connectivity-dependent catastrophic-rupture dynamics are candidate FF force laws / validation phenomenology.
- **TAG-validation? — YES.** Rich quantitative validation targets: 500 pN fragmentation threshold (Tsuda 1996), 5 pN/head stall, biphasic contraction vs ATP/k₂₀, ≥2× fragment retraction speed jump (8/10), cluster-size vs RANLN/RMYO, contraction/moving-speed sweeps, severing-frequency vs RM/⟨Lf⟩/RACP/k₂₀. Several are dimensionless ratios (RM, RACP, RANLN) — record with the concentration provenance.
