## Summary

The paper asks **how a fibrillar ECM (type-I collagen) mechanically resists cell division** in 3D, a process previously reduced to bulk stiffness. Using a discrete, fiber-level agent-based Brownian-dynamics model (Langevin, over-damped) of a growing/dividing cell embedded in a self-assembled network of semiflexible fibers connected by dynamic, force-sensitive (Bell's-law) cross-linkers, plus a membrane with volume conservation and CRC/ISE division forces, the authors simulate the full sequence: 100 s matrix assembly → 4,000 s two-fold volumetric growth (G1) → mitotic elongation (to ~59%, ~20,000 s). They validate against MDA-MB-231 breast-cancer cells dividing in 1 mg/mL collagen (Chaudhuri-lab imaging, ref. 11). Central finding: pre-mitotic volumetric growth compacts and prestresses the proximal matrix into a **densified pericellular shell**, which then resists mitotic elongation through **three distinct mechanical modes**: (i) polar compressive resistance (largely dissipated by fiber buckling, capped near the ~30 pN critical buckling load), (ii) resistance to areal expansion of the pericellular shell (a pre-tensioned hoop-stress cage), and (iii) equatorial tensile anchoring linking the shell to the far-field matrix through a few straightened, high-tension fibers. Shell hoop-stress/anchorage (Tmat ≈ 37% of Tpole in the reference case) dominates as the primary barrier. Parametric sweeps show fiber density and length, cross-link density, force-dependent cross-linker unbinding, and fiber bending/extensional stiffness tune whether the checkpoint is "engaged" (dormancy) or "released" (invasive division). A continuum finite-element (Neo-Hookean) model reproduces global displacement but fails to reproduce the three coexisting discrete resistance modes — arguing that fiber-resolution is essential. They propose the pericellular shell as a natural mechanical checkpoint on division whose bypass (via short fibers / fast cross-link turnover / matrix fluidization) is a step in cancer progression.

## Model & Methods

- **Model type**: Discrete, agent-based **Brownian dynamics** with the Langevin equation, **inertia neglected (over-damped)**. Elements defined by nodes. Three physical constituents modeled explicitly:
  1. **ECM fibers**: each fiber = serially-connected cylindrical (rod) elements with extensional + bending harmonic potentials (semiflexible worm-like-chain-like). Self-assembled from a monomer pool (nucleation rate kn,f, elongation rate k+,f, bidirectional). Non-disassembling once formed.
  2. **Cross-linkers**: two cylindrical arms joined at a center point; bind fibers at sites spaced every 100 nm; extensional + two bending stiffnesses; **force-dependent unbinding via Bell's law** (tension-accelerated) → gives viscoelasticity/plasticity.
  3. **Cell membrane**: 2D (interconnected rectangular elements forming a circular wall, cross-section of a cytokinetic cell) or 3D (triangulated mesh); extensional + bending stiffness; **enclosed-volume conservation** (harmonic penalty); excluded-volume repulsion vs fibers/cross-linkers and vs neighboring membrane elements.
- **Integrator**: forward (explicit) Euler, over-damped update rᵢ(t+Δt) = rᵢ(t) + (1/ζᵢ)(Fᵢ + Fᵢᵀ)Δt.
- **Timestep**: Δt = 3.97 × 10⁻⁴ s.
- **Thermostat / noise**: stochastic force Fᵀ satisfying fluctuation–dissipation theorem (variance 2 k_BT ζᵢ / Δt).
- **Drag**: per-element ζᵢ from an approximate cylinder formula (Clift/Grace/Weber; ref. 27), function of medium viscosity µ, element diameter r_c,i and length r_0,i.
- **Geometry / BCs**:
  - **2D setup**: thin cylindrical domain, radius 50 µm, height 1 µm (z). Circular membrane radius 10 µm (157 elements) at center. Fibers initially oriented random-perpendicular to z (to avoid thin-domain hindrance). 2D membrane nodes move only in x,y (span domain in z); fibers/cross-linkers/3D-membrane nodes move in x,y,z. Total elements = 180,474.
  - **3D setup**: spherical domain radius 30 µm; spherical triangulated-mesh membrane radius 10 µm (10,242 nodes). Total elements = 1,093,992 (~6× the 2D cost). A single 3D run used to verify 2D.
- **Protocol / phases** (per simulation):
  1. Matrix assembly: 100 s (fiber + cross-linker self-assembly between membrane and domain boundary).
  2. Cell growth (G1): equilibrium membrane volume increased linearly two-fold over 4,000 s; volume-conservation potential pushes membrane out uniformly; membrane radius grows up to ~1.26×.
  3. Mitotic elongation: two applied force fields (as in Nam 2021, ref. 11):
     - **CRC (cytokinetic ring)**: constant **inward** force F_CRC uniformly on membrane nodes within 0.5 µm of the equator; nodes move inward until >1 µm from mitotic axis.
     - **ISE (interpolar spindle elongation)**: **outward** force F_ISE uniformly on membrane nodes within 5 µm of the mitotic axis.
     - Forces stop when the cell has elongated by **58.74%** (prevents daughters over-elongating past a spherical shape). Mitotic-elongation snapshots analyzed at 20,000 s.
- **Continuum comparison — Finite Element (FE)**: ANSYS Academic Research Mechanical r22.2. Quarter-symmetry (x,y symmetric BCs). Cell = central disk r=10 µm; ECM = annulus r=50 µm; optional shell = thin annulus r=10.5 µm (0.5 µm thick) between them. All regions 1 µm thick. Cell–adjacent interface frictionless; shell–matrix interface bonded. Both cell and matrix **Neo-Hookean hyperelastic**. Cell = incompressible, E = 324 Pa (ref. 28). ECM = compressible, E_mat = 832 Pa, Poisson ν = 0.313 (bulk modulus 741 Pa) (ref. 29). E_shell = E_mat or 10× E_mat. Growth = constant surface tractions on cell boundary; CRC = inward equatorial forces; ISE = outward polar forces.
- **Experimental validation platform** (from ref. 11, Nam et al. 2021): MDA-MB-231 human breast cancer cells expressing GFP-α-tubulin + RFP-histone, in 1 mg/mL type-I collagen gels (rat tail, pH 8.5 NaOH, 0.1–0.5 M cells/mL, polymerized 37 °C 45–60 min). Leica SP8 confocal, 25×/0.95 NA water objective, ex 488/555/639 nm (collagen reflectance). ImageJ; matrix deformation via PIV (PIVlab, MATLAB).
- **Statistics**: 4 independent simulations per condition (unless noted); mean ± s.d.; one-way ANOVA + Tukey; unpaired two-tailed t-tests. ns p≥0.05; * <0.05; ** <0.01; *** <0.001; **** <0.0001.

## Governing Equations

**(Eq. 1) Over-damped Langevin (force balance, inertia neglected):**
Fᵢ − ζᵢ (drᵢ/dt) + Fᵢᵀ = 0
— deterministic force + drag + thermal force balance to zero for node i.

**(Eq. 2) Fluctuation–dissipation (thermal-force correlation):**
⟨Fᵢᵀ(t) Fⱼᵀ(t)⟩ = (2 k_B T ζᵢ δᵢⱼ / Δt) δ
— white-noise stochastic force variance set by drag and temperature (δᵢⱼ Kronecker, δ unit 2nd-order tensor).

**(Eq. 3) Forward-Euler over-damped position update:**
rᵢ(t+Δt) = rᵢ(t) + (drᵢ/dt)Δt = rᵢ(t) + (1/ζᵢ)(Fᵢ + Fᵢᵀ)Δt
— explicit time integration of node positions.

**(Eq. 4) Extensional (stretching) harmonic potential:**
U_s = ½ κ_s (r − r₀)²
— linear spring for element length r about equilibrium r₀.

**(Eq. 5) Bending harmonic potential:**
U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ₀)²
— angular spring for angle θ about equilibrium θ₀ (fibers, cross-linker arms, membrane).

**(Eq. 6) Cylinder drag coefficient:**
ζᵢ = 3π µ r_c,i · (3 + 2 r₀,ᵢ / r_c,i) / 5
— per-element translational drag from an approximate formula for cylinders (ref. 27); µ = medium viscosity, r_c,i = element diameter, r₀,ᵢ = element length.

**(Eq. 7) Bell's-law force-dependent cross-linker unbinding:**
k₋,xl = k₋,xl⁰ · exp( λ₋,xl |F⃗_s,xl| / (k_B T) )  if r ≥ r₀,xl (tensile);
k₋,xl = k₋,xl⁰                                             if r < r₀,xl (compressive)
— unbinding rate accelerated only under tensile spring force; k₋,xl⁰ zero-force rate, λ₋,xl force sensitivity (a length scale), F⃗_s,xl cross-linker spring force. (Text labels the reference "[3]" for Bell.)

**(Eq. 8) Excluded-volume / repulsive potential (membrane vs fiber/cross-linker; membrane vs membrane):**
U_r,m = κ_r,m (r₁₂ − r₀,r)² [ ½ + ⅓ (1 − r₁₂/r₀,r)⁴ ]   if r₁₂ < r₀,r;
U_r,m = 0                                                if r₁₂ ≥ r₀,r
— soft repulsion acting below cutoff r₀,r (= average of the two element thicknesses), steepening as r₁₂ → r₀,r; κ_r,m repulsion strength; r₁₂ minimum distance between a membrane element and a cylindrical segment.

**(Eq. 9) Cell-volume-conservation harmonic potential:**
U_v,m = ½ κ_v,m (1 − V_m / V₀,m)²
— penalizes deviation of instantaneous enclosed volume V_m from target V₀,m (V₀,m is ramped ×2 during growth). For 2D, V_m estimated by axisymmetric rotation of the cross-section about the long axis.

## Force Laws & Potentials

- **Fiber extensional force**: −∂U_s/∂r with κ_s,f, r₀,f = 1 µm.
- **Fiber bending force**: −∂U_b/∂θ with κ_b,f, θ₀,f = 0 rad; κ_b,f reference chosen so persistence length ℓ_p ≈ 100 µm. Critical buckling load ∝ κ_b,f (fibers buckle under compression → asymmetric response; buckling ≈ 30 pN in reference).
- **Cross-linker extensional force**: κ_s,xl about r₀,xl = 200 nm.
- **Cross-linker bending**: two bending stiffnesses — κ_b,xl,1 (arm–arm, θ₀,xl,1 = 0) and κ_b,xl,2 (arm–bound-fiber axis, θ₀,xl,2 = π/2). Forces distributed to the two nodes of a fiber element weighted by proximity of the binding point (ref. 26).
- **Cross-linker binding/unbinding**: binding rate k+,xl to sites every 100 nm; force-dependent unbinding Eq. 7. After unbinding, cross-linker stays bound to one fiber and awaits rebinding → transient network (viscoelasticity/plasticity).
- **Membrane extensional/bending**: κ_s,m about r₀,m = 400 nm; κ_b,m about θ₀,m = 0 rad (2D angle / 3D dihedral).
- **Repulsion (excluded volume)**: Eq. 8, strength κ_r,m, cutoff r₀,r.
- **Volume conservation**: Eq. 9, strength κ_v,m.
- **Division loads** (imposed, not potentials): F_CRC inward (equator, within 0.5 µm), F_ISE outward (within 5 µm of axis); FE analogs F_CR (=F_CRC) and F_IS (=F_ISE).
