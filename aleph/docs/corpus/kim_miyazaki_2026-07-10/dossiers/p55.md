## Summary

The authors ask whether single cells can sense a **spatial temperature gradient** (as opposed to the well-studied sensing of absolute/temporal temperature via ion channels). Using a focused 1455-nm infrared laser to locally heat a single spherical HeLa cell (mitotic/metaphase, or trypsin-rounded interphase) under an optical microscope, and calibrating the local temperature field with thermal-quenching fluorophores (TMR-dextran, Alexa Fluor 555-dextran, Eu-TTA), they find that a **sharp** intracellular temperature gradient (as small as **1.3 °C difference across a ~20 µm cell**, R ≈ 0.065 °C µm⁻¹, i.e. ~50–100 °C mm⁻¹ locally) drives a single **directional membrane extension toward the heat source — a "polar bleb"** (88%, 78/89 metaphase cells; 98%, 42/43 trypsinized interphase cells). Gradual/uniform heating instead produces **multiple non-directional blebs** (84%, 59/70), and strong heating above ~50 °C gives a "**Motionless**" state (organelle arrest by thermal protein denaturation). Pharmacology shows **actomyosin is the essential thermosensor/effector**: actin-polymerization inhibitors (latrunculin B, cytochalasin D), myosin-II ATPase inhibition (blebbistatin), and ROCK inhibition (Y-27632) suppress polar blebs, while MLCK inhibition (ML-7) does not; actin stabilization (jasplakinolide) and microtubule depolymerization (nocodazole) lengthen blebs. Fluorescence imaging of Lifeact/actin-RFP, mCherry-MRLC (myosin regulatory light chain) and CellMask membrane reveals a **symmetry break of the actomyosin cortex**: F-actin and myosin density **drop at the warm (near) side and accumulate at the cool (far) side**, moving in-phase with the plasma membrane, followed by cortex–membrane detachment and cortex rupture at the warm side that nucleates the bleb. Ca²⁺ bursts occur but are **not essential** for polar blebs (unaffected by EGTA/BAPTA chelation), though they modulate multiple-bleb probability and bleb length. The proposed mechanism: local heating breaks the symmetry of cortical actomyosin contractile force (increased contractility and/or local cortex rupture at the warm side), unbalancing the cortical-tension/hydrostatic-pressure force balance that normally holds a rounded cell spherical, and driving a directional bleb.

## Model & Methods

- **Model type:** In-vivo single-cell experiment. No computational/mechanistic simulation, no integrator, no timestep. "Model" here = the conceptual **force-balance/bleb-nucleation schematic** (Fig. 4E) and an analytic **steady-state heat-conduction description** used only to fit the measured temperature field.
- **Cells:** HeLa. Cultured in DMEM+10% FBS, 37 °C/5% CO₂. Observation in Leibovitz L-15 + 10% FBS at 25 ± 1 °C, 35 ± 0.5 °C, or 37 ± 0.5 °C (stage incubator INUG2-ONICS). Spherical interphase cells produced by brief trypsin (0.025%, 1 min, 25 °C) rounding.
- **Local heating actuator:** IR laser λ = 1455 nm (Keopsys), focused through 60×/1.45 oil objective; mechanical shutter gates the 20-s (or 5-s) pulse; power at specimen measured with thermal-disk sensor/power meter. Distance from focus to cell and laser power are the two knobs setting ΔT and the intracellular gradient.
- **Temperature measurement (calibration):** thermal quenching of TMR-dextran (25 µg mL⁻¹) or Alexa Fluor 555-dextran (10 µg mL⁻¹) conjugated to 10-kDa dextran, excited 561 nm, confocal; independently Eu-TTA (500 µM in DMSO) in a sealed pulled glass capillary. Fluorescence-vs-T calibrated in a cuvette with thermostatic circulator + digital thermometer. Steady temperature reached within ~2 s of laser onset (Fig. S1C).
- **Imaging:** Spinning-disk confocal (Yokogawa CSU-X1) + EMCCD (Andor iXon-Ultra) and epifluorescence EMCCD (iXon EM+ 897), 60×/1.45 oil; excitation 488/561 nm; also mercury-lamp epifluorescence. Labels: Lifeact-RFP / Lifeact-GFP (F-actin), actin-RFP, mCherry-MRLC (myosin II regulatory light chain 2), CellMask Orange (plasma membrane), Tubulin-GFP (spindle/microtubules), Fluo-4-AM (Ca²⁺), calcein-AM / EthD-1 (live/dead).
- **Geometry / "boundary conditions":** single suspended/rounded cell (~20 µm diameter) in aqueous medium on glass; heat source is an off-cell point (the laser focus). Cell and bleb each modeled geometrically as spheres for size/angle analysis.
- **Perturbations (pharmacology):** see Parameter Table.
- **Analysis / measurement protocol:**
  - Polar-bleb length = segment AB between fitted pre-heat cell circle C₀ and max-extension bleb circle C_bleb along the line L_C through both centers; angle = angle between L_C and the cell-to-heat-source line L_L (Fig. S2A).
  - ΔF = (fluorescence 1.7 s after heating end) − (F₀ at 0.5 s before heating).
  - Cortex dynamics via **polar-coordinate transform** of the cortical ROI (contour subdivided into 360 points; 0° = nearest to heat source; kymographs).
  - Ca²⁺: Fluo-4 normalized to 1-s pre-heat average; F_max/F₀ over first 35 s (polar) or 50 s (multiple/Motionless).
  - Statistics: Wilcoxon/Mann-Whitney (lengths), Fisher's exact (multiple-bleb probability), F-test→Student's/Welch's t-test (fluorescence between treatments), paired t-test (near vs far side). SE for statistically analyzed data, else SD.

## Governing Equations

This is an experimental paper; the only transcribed equations are the thermometry calibrations and the analytic heat-conduction fits. No dynamical/force ODEs or potentials are solved.

1. **TMR-dextran fluorescence–temperature calibration (normalized at 25.1 °C):**
   `y = 1.31×10⁻⁴ x² − 2.47×10⁻² x + 1.53`   (x = T[°C], y = normalized F.I.) — converts fluorescence to local temperature.

2. **Alexa Fluor 555-dextran calibration (normalized at 24.9 °C):**
   `y = 2.36×10⁻⁴ x² − 3.66×10⁻² x + 1.76`.

3. **Eu-TTA calibration (normalized at 25.1 °C):**
   `y = 3.33×10⁻⁴ x² − 5.17×10⁻² x + 2.09`.

4. **Spatial temperature-gradient empirical fit (used to describe T vs distance from heat source):**
   `y = a·ln(x/x₀) + b`   (x = distance from heat source [µm], x₀ = 1 µm reference, y = temperature [°C]; a,b > 0, fit in Excel). Logarithmic → consistent with a cylindrical (line) heat source.

5. **Steady-state temperature around a cylindrical heat source (physical basis for Eq. 4):**
   `T(x₁) = T(x₂) − Q/(2πlκ)·ln(x₁/x₂)`
   where x₁,x₂ = radial distances [m] from heat-source center, Q = dissipated power [W], l = cylinder height [m], κ = medium thermal conductivity [W m⁻¹ K⁻¹]. (The ln-dependence matches the measured field.)

6. **Steady-state temperature around a spherical heat source (tested, REJECTED as a fit):**
   `T(x₁) = T(x₂) + Q/(4πκ)·(1/x₁ − 1/x₂)` — a 1/x form; did NOT fit the measured gradients, so the source behaves as effectively cylindrical.

7. **Conceptual force balance for a rounded cell (stated, not written as an equation):** cell sphericity is set by the balance of **inward cortical actomyosin contractile force (cortical tension) vs outward hydrostatic (turgor) pressure** on the plasma membrane; a **local imbalance immediately triggers blebbing** (refs 29–32). This is the qualitative law the DCM/FF engines encode explicitly (γ·2/R Laplace tension vs ΔP).

## Force Laws & Potentials

None in a simulation sense (no bond/bending/motor force laws, no rate constants fit). The relevant physics invoked from cited literature (not measured here):
- **Cortical tension vs hydrostatic pressure** governs cell shape and bleb nucleation (Laplace-type balance; refs 29,30).
- **Temperature dependence of myosin force** (invoked mechanism): tension on an actin filament by skeletal-muscle myosin *increases* with temperature via an increased number of attached crossbridges (Ishiwata-lab refs 20,33,34) — cited to justify the "increased contractility at the warm side" model. Note a competing literature (refs 35–40) reports cortical tension/stiffness *decreasing* with temperature; the paper explicitly leaves the sign of the cortical-actomyosin temperature-dependence open.
- **Myosin-II as an actin-network-disassembly / severing agent** (refs 41–44) — invoked to explain cortex rupture (density loss) at the warm side.

- **DCM? YES (high).** This is almost a direct experimental target for the DCM engine's core physics: a rounded cell held spherical by **cortical actomyosin tension vs outward hydrostatic/turgor pressure**, where a **local imbalance → cortex–membrane detachment → bleb**. DCM already models membrane + cortex tension γ + turgor ΔP + node-face contact; a spatially asymmetric cortical-tension field (warm side ↑ then rupture, cool side accumulation) is exactly the perturbation that should nucleate a directional bleb. Quantitative bleb-extension lengths (7.5±5.3 µm metaphase; 8.1±3.0 µm rounded interphase; angle 8.4±5.2°) and the min-gradient threshold are usable geometric validation targets. Caveat: the driver here is a temperature gradient (not in DCM's parameter set) — DCM would impose the asymmetry directly as a γ / rupture field, so the paper informs the *phenomenology and magnitudes*, not new force constants.
- **FF? YES (partial).** FF's native actomyosin cortex (filaments + myosin minifilaments + Bell-Evans crosslinkers) is the natural engine to reproduce the observed **cortex-density symmetry break** (F-actin/myosin ↓ at warm side, ↑ at cool side) and **cortex rupture via myosin-II-driven network disassembly** (refs 41–44). The cited temperature dependence of myosin crossbridge number/force (Ishiwata refs 20,33,34) touches the Hill/force-velocity motor layer, though this paper reports no rate constants. Cortex-membrane detachment + local rupture as a bleb-nucleation mechanism is directly in FF's scope.
- **TAG-validation? YES (qualified).** Provides clean, citable in-vivo observables (bleb lengths, incidence %, onset latency ~3 s, angle, min 1.3 °C gradient, cortical near/far ΔF/F₀ asymmetry). Good as KnowledgeClaim-style qualitative mechanism + validation-target measurements for blebbing/cortex-tension contracts. Not a source of simulation parameters/force laws (no moduli, off-rates, or motor constants measured), so it feeds ValidationGate/observable targets more than Parameter rows. All fluorescence-thermometry quadratics and heat-conduction fits are apparatus calibrations, not cell-mechanics constants.
