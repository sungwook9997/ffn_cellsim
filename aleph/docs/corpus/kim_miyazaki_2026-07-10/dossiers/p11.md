## Summary
The paper addresses a single-molecule biophysics inference problem: in optical/probe-particle experiments on molecular motors and conformationally-switching proteins, one only observes the trajectory of a large attached probe particle *y(t)*, whose motion is delayed and thermally noisy relative to the true (hidden) protein reaction coordinate *x(t)*. The authors model the coupled system as two overdamped Brownian particles (hidden protein *x*, visible probe *y*) connected by an elastic linker, governed by coupled Langevin equations, and pose the estimation of the hidden trajectory as finding the Dominant Reaction Path (DRP) / Maximum-a-Posteriori (MAP) estimator that maximizes the Onsager–Machlup path probability (equivalently minimizes an action functional S). Naive gradient descent on this action is Ginzburg–Landau-like and relaxes to the DRP extremely slowly (kink motion, local-minima trapping). Their contribution is the **Go-and-Back method**: exploiting the small friction ratio γ/Γ ≪ 1, they decompose the Euler–Lagrange equation into a forward-time ODE for x(t) (Eq. 11/17) and a reverse-time ODE for an auxiliary "deterministic noise" variable v(t) (Eq. 12/18), together with a perturbation expansion in ε that lets them solve the two alternately (go forward for x, come back for v) at successively higher order. Applied to two canonical toy models — a symmetric double-well (equilibrium two-state switching, Model A) and a tilted periodic potential (nonequilibrium stepping motor à la F₁-ATPase, Model B) — the method reproduces the hidden stepwise trajectory well, is highly robust to boundary and initial conditions (effect diminishes on a timescale τ_dim ~ γ/(a₁+k) ≈ 0.001–0.005; initial-condition perturbation changes the estimate by only ~10⁻⁴), and converges to a lower action (−3.04×10⁴ vs −2.91×10⁴ for gradient descent) with **>10³-fold lower computational cost** and no case-by-case tuning.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** Two coupled one-dimensional overdamped (inertia-free) Langevin equations — a hidden protein reaction coordinate x(t) and a visible probe coordinate y(t) linked by an elastic potential. Straightforwardly extendable to higher dimension; kept 1D because experiments monitor 1D motion.
- **Physical picture:** γ = friction of protein (internal friction + protein–medium viscous friction, assumed position-independent); Γ = viscous friction of the probe particle; U(x,y) = elastic-linker potential; V_eff(x)=V(x)−fx = tilted effective protein landscape with f a "driving force" from catalysis (ATP hydrolysis); F(y) = external force applied to the probe (optical trap / constant load). All potentials assumed time-independent.
- **Inference target:** the most-probable hidden trajectory [x̂] (the DRP / MAP estimator) given the observed [y] and *known* model + parameters. Formulated as maximizing the path probability P([x,y]|Π), decomposed into initial × transition probabilities, transition probability given by the Onsager–Machlup form C·exp(−βS), β⁻¹=k_BT. Maximizing P ⇔ minimizing the action functional S([x,y];Π) w.r.t. [x] under a Dirichlet boundary condition (x₀ fixed → initial distribution avoided).
- **Baseline method (for comparison):** Gradient descent — introduce a "virtual time" s, evolve ∂x/∂s = −δS/δx (Eq. 6), which is formally a time-dependent Ginzburg–Landau equation for nonconserved order parameter → slow global (kink) relaxation, local-minima trapping. Discretized with the Forward-Time Centered-Space (FTCS) scheme (Numerical Recipes, Ch. 20). Needs both initial condition (raw [y] or moving-averaged [y], bin sizes 20/50/100/200 → time lengths 0.2/0.5/1/2) and Dirichlet boundary {x̂₀,x̂_τ}={y₀,y_τ}.
- **Proposed method — Go-and-Back:**
  1. Reduce the 2nd-order Euler–Lagrange Eq. (8) to two 1st-order ODEs by introducing a deterministic variable v(t) that mimics the random force ξ(t) (Eq. 9/11).
  2. Under γ/Γ ≪ 1, drop the two negligible terms containing the unknown true position x* and noise η* (their ensemble contribution ≈ 0), giving the closed pair Eqs. (11)–(12).
  3. Because G_xx>0 near a potential minimum, x is stable forward-in-time and v is stable backward-in-time. So integrate **Eq. (11) for x forward (t=0→τ)** and **Eq. (12) for v backward (t=τ→0)**; iterate.
  4. Make convergence systematic with a perturbation expansion in a dummy parameter ε (Eq. 13, ε set to 1 after derivation): define the i-th order approximants x_(i), v_(i) as *cumulative* sums of orders 0..i (Eqs. 14–15), with the zeroth-order closure v_(0)(t)=0 (Eq. 16). Then alternate Eq. (17) (forward for x_(i)) and Eq. (18) (backward for v_(i+1)).
- **Boundary/initial conditions used:** {x_(i)(0), v_(i)(τ)} = {y(0), 0}. The initial condition v_(0)=0 is baked into the algorithm; only a boundary condition is user-supplied, and results are robust to it.
- **Integration / time-stepping:** Model equations (1)–(2) integrated to generate the synthetic "true" [x,y] with Δt=0.01. Because the relaxation timescale of x(t) is shorter than Δt for the chosen parameters, [y] is linearly interpolated at step size Δt/10 to stably integrate Eqs. (17)–(18).
- **Iteration counts:** Go-and-Back converges at i≈15 (Model A action) / uses i=50 for the displayed trajectory estimates (Figs. 2, 3); gradient descent uses i=10⁵ (Figs. 6, 7) and is ~10⁴× slower to relax.
- **BCs/geometry:** 1D unbounded reaction coordinate; no spatial domain/confinement (this is a time-series problem, not a spatial PDE). Data length τ = 100 (Model A) / τ = 50 (Model B).

## Governing Equations
1. **Hidden protein Langevin eq. (Eq. 1):** `γ ẋ = −∂ₓ[V_eff(x) + U(x,y)] + ξ` — overdamped motion of the hidden coordinate in its effective landscape plus linker coupling, driven by thermal noise ξ.
2. **Visible probe Langevin eq. (Eq. 2):** `Γ ẏ = F(y) − ∂_y U(x,y) + η` — overdamped probe motion under external force F and linker coupling, with noise η.
   - Noise statistics: ⟨ξ⟩=⟨η⟩=0; variances 2γk_BT and 2Γk_BT respectively (fluctuation–dissipation).
   - `V_eff(x) ≡ V(x) − f x` — tilted effective protein potential (f = catalytic driving force).
3. **Path-probability decomposition (Eq. 3):** `P([x,y]|Π) = P_init(x₀,y₀|Π) · P_tr((x₀,y₀)→[x,y]|Π)` — split into initial and transition parts.
4. **Onsager–Machlup transition probability (Eq. 4):** `P_tr = C exp[−β S([x,y];Π)]`, β⁻¹≡k_BT — path weight is Boltzmann-like in the action S.
5. **Action functional (Eq. 5):**
   `S = (1/4γ)∫₀^τ [γẋ + V_eff_x(x) + U_x(x,y)]² dt − (k_BT/2γ)∫₀^τ [V_eff_xx(x)+U_xx(x,y)] dt + (1/4Γ)∫₀^τ [Γẏ − F(y) + U_y(x,y)]² dt − (k_BT/2Γ)∫₀^τ [−F_y(y)+U_yy(x,y)] dt`
   — the Onsager–Machlup action; each squared term penalizes deviation from the deterministic drift, each Laplacian term is the Jacobian/Stratonovich correction. Normalization C=[√(γΓ)/(4πk_BT Δt)]^N, τ≡NΔt.
6. **Gradient-descent virtual-time PDE (Eq. 6):** `∂x(t,s)/∂s = −δS/δx = −∂W(x,y)/∂x|_{x=x(t,s)} + (γ/2)∂²x/∂t²` — steepest-descent relaxation of the action in virtual time s; formally Ginzburg–Landau (s↔time, t↔space).
7. **Potential-like density W (Eq. 7):**
   `W(x,y) ≡ (1/4γ)[V_eff_x + U_x]² − (k_BT/2γ)[V_eff_xx+U_xx] + (1/4Γ)[−F+U_y]² − (k_BT/2Γ)[−F_y+U_yy]` — the effective multiwell "potential" whose gradient drives Eq. 6.
8. **Euler–Lagrange equation (Eq. 8):** `δS/δx = ∂W/∂x − (γ/2)ẍ = 0` — stationarity condition defining the DRP.
9. **Definition of auxiliary variable v (Eq. 9):** `γ ẋ = −[V_eff_x(x)+U_x(x,y)] + v` — v(t) is a deterministic surrogate for the random force ξ.
10. **Exact v-evolution (Eq. 10):** `γ v̇ = [V_eff_xx+U_xx]v − k_BT[V_eff_xxx+U_xxx] + (γ/Γ)U_xy[U_y(x,y)−U_y(x*,y)] + (γ/Γ)U_xy·η*` — full equation for v; last two terms carry the unknown true x* and noise η* (Itô product ·).
11. **Reduced forward-x ODE (Eq. 11):** `γ ẋ = −G_x(x,y) + v`, with `G(x,y) ≡ V_eff(x)+U(x,y)` — integrate forward in time (stable).
12. **Reduced backward-v ODE (Eq. 12):** `γ v̇ = G_xx(x,y)v − k_BT G_xxx(x,y)` — integrate backward in time (stable), the two unknown-x* terms dropped since they average to ~0 and γ/Γ≪1.
13. **Perturbation-tagged v-ODE (Eq. 13):** `γ v̇ = G_xx(x,y)v − ε k_BT G_xxx(x,y)` — ε (→1 at the end) marks the small 2nd term for expansion.
14. **i-th order x (Eq. 14):** `x_(i)(t) ≡ Σ_{j=0}^{i} εʲ x^(j)(t)` — cumulative (orders 0..i) approximant, key to algorithm simplification.
15. **i-th order v (Eq. 15):** `v_(i)(t) ≡ Σ_{j=0}^{i} εʲ v^(j)(t)`.
16. **Zeroth-order closure (Eq. 16):** `v_(0)(t) = 0` — approximation justified because v^(0) is stable→0 only in reverse time (see Appendix A4); supplies the built-in initial condition.
17. **Go-and-Back step A — forward x (Eq. 17):** `γ ẋ_(i) = −G_x(x_(i),y) + v_(i) + O(ε^{i+1})`, integrate t=0→τ.
18. **Go-and-Back step B — backward v (Eq. 18):** `γ v̇_(i+1) = G_xx(x_(i),y)v_(i+1) − ε k_BT G_xxx(x_(i),y) + O(ε^{i+2})`, integrate t=τ→0. Alternate A/B.
- **Appendix (perturbation expansion):** full series x(t)=Σεⁱx^(i), v(t)=Σεⁱv^(i) (A1–A2); zeroth order (A3) `γẋ^(0)=−G_x(x^(0),y)+v^(0)`, (A4) `γv̇^(0)=G_xx(x^(0),y)v^(0)`; first order (A5–A6); second order (A7–A8); recombined general forms (A9–A12) reproducing Eqs. 17–18. Key structural fact: x^(i) never appears in the v^(i)-equation, so the reverse-time v solve is stable/closed.
- **Diminishing-timescale estimate (Ref. 56):** `τ_dim ~ γ / G_xx(x_b) = γ/(a₁+k)`, x_b = potential-minimum position.
- **Thermal-fluctuation SD of x (Ref. 58):** `SD = √(2 k_B T Δt / γ)` (discrete-time); = 0.2 for Model A.

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Elastic linker U(x,y):** harmonic spring of stiffness k connecting protein and probe (spring constant k=160 in model units; drawn as a spring in Fig. 1). Enters both Langevin eqs via U_x, U_y and the action via U_xx, U_yy, U_xy.
- **Model A effective potential (double well, equilibrium):** `V_eff(x) = Σ_{i=1}^{8} aᵢ xⁱ` — 8th-order polynomial fitted to two Gaussians (the experimentally-observed bimodal probe distribution). Coefficients: a₁=2.57, a₂=114.4, a₃=−14.0, a₄=−191.9, a₅=−81.3, a₆=196.4, a₇=29.1, a₈=−51.5. Here f=0, F=0 (Kramers double-well, two-state switching).
- **Model B effective potential (tilted periodic, nonequilibrium motor):** `V(x) = A sin(2πx/l)`, tilted by driving force → `V_eff(x)=V(x)−f x`. A=20, l=1 (period), f=40. Represents an F₁-ATPase-like stepping motor in nonequilibrium steady state.
- **External probe force F(y):** general term for optical-trap or constant-load force; taken zero (F=0) in both worked models.
- **Driving force f:** catalytic "tilt" of the protein landscape (ATP hydrolysis), f=0 (Model A), f=40 (Model B).

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** No. This is a 1D single-molecule inference method; no surface-mesh cell mechanics, no cortex/turgor/nucleus/junction content. No DCM parameter or force law applies.
- **FF?** Marginal / indirect only. Shares the *substrate physics* of the FF/DCM engines — overdamped Langevin/Brownian dynamics with fluctuation–dissipation noise (variance 2γk_BT), which is exactly the BAOAB/Leimkuhler–Matthews regime our integrators target — but the paper contributes an *inference/estimation* algorithm on time series, not a filament/motor/crosslinker force law. The Onsager–Machlup action and DRP machinery are not part of the FF forward simulator. No motor force-velocity (Hill), no bond kinetics (Bell), no bending beam.
- **TAG-validation?** No usable literature validation targets. All quantitative numbers are in dimensionless model units for synthetic toy trajectories (double-well, tilted washboard); the only "real" cited data are others' instrument resolutions (9.1 µs, 0.1 nm) and F₁-ATPase's 120° step — general context, not MCF7/cell-mechanics constants. Not a source for KB parameter registration. Best filed as a *methods/statistical-inference* reference in the Miyazaki corpus, useful only if the project ever does hidden-trajectory/path-integral inference on simulated or experimental single-molecule data.
