## Summary
The paper asks whether *disordered* (non-sarcomeric, non-stress-fibre) actomyosin networks — like the cell cortex — can by themselves reproduce the cooperative, size-scaled contractile behaviour seen in cells, or whether ordered/aligned architecture (stress fibres, apolar bundles) is a prerequisite. Using a 2D biomimetic cortex — stabilized F-actin crowded by methylcellulose onto a lipid bilayer, decorated with non-muscle myosin II held inactive by blebbistatin, then locally uncaged with 405 nm light — the authors spatiotemporally control myosin activity over circular regions from 25 µm² to >1000 µm² and measure the F-actin velocity field by PIV. They find contraction is (i) **highly cooperative**: maximum strain vs myosin thick-filament density follows a Hill curve with an extraordinarily large Hill coefficient nH ≈ 11 and a sharp critical density ρc = 0.56 µm⁻² (percolation-like transition), while strain *rate* is linear in density; (ii) **telescopic**: the boundary contraction velocity grows with the activation-region radius while spatially-averaged strain and strain rate stay ≈constant — exactly the "telescopic" law previously seen only in ordered bundles; and (iii) capable of generating non-uniform stress (radial stress peaks at the center, decays to boundary). A minimal **isotropic active-viscoelastic-gel continuum model** (central active-stress core σₐ in a passive medium of Young's modulus E, viscosity η, substrate friction ζ) analytically reproduces the strain/strain-rate/boundary-velocity trends, with telescopic velocity emerging from the interplay of active-stress buildup and viscoelastic dissipation, tunable by E/σₐ. An **agent-based Brownian-dynamics simulation** (F-actin as serially-connected 280 nm cylindrical segments; myosin as bipolar thick filaments with load-dependent kinetics) reproduces the critical-density cooperativity and telescopic velocity, and shows telescopic behaviour survives even when F-actin persistence length is raised from 20 µm to 2.6 mm (bending suppressed) — proving F-actin bending/buckling is *not* required for telescopic contraction; only total active-motor content matters.

## Model & Methods
Three complementary approaches:

**1. In-vitro biomimetic experiment (Murrell lab).**
- 2D quasi-2D actomyosin network: 1.3 µM Alexa-568 F-actin, stabilized with 2 µM phalloidin, crowded onto a 99.6% egg-PC / 0.4% FITC-DHPE phospholipid bilayer by 0.25% 14 kDa methylcellulose (depletion agent). 20 µM blebbistatin inhibits myosin. 24 nM Alexa-647 non-muscle myosin II (RM = [myosin]/[actin] = 0.085) added in dimeric form, polymerizes into thick filaments on the F-actin. Network disordered in F-actin orientation, alignment, length; **no F-actin crosslinker** (all connectivity via myosin) except in the filamin-A control.
- Spatiotemporal activation: Nikon Ti inverted microscope + spinning disk (Yokogawa) + Mosaic (Photonics Instruments) patterned illumination. 405 nm light photo-inactivates blebbistatin in circular ROIs → local myosin activation. ROIs pulsed 100 ms with 405 nm immediately before each image; images every 10 s; 60× 1.4 NA oil lens; Coolsnap Hq2 CCD.
- Readout: Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV, MATLAB) → velocity field; time-integrate → displacement field; divergence of displacement → strain ε; divergence of velocity → strain rate dε/dt. Boundary velocity averaged in a 2.65 µm window about the ROI circumference. Filament length by tracing contour in ImageJ.
- Field of view 95.4 × 95.4 µm; fields chosen >500 µm apart for mechanical isolation; myosin density varies field-to-field by diffusion, giving a density sweep from one prep.

**2. Continuum active-gel model (Banerjee).** Homogeneous isotropic viscoelastic gel, overdamped, driven out of equilibrium by isotropic active contractile stress. Central active core of radius ξ inside a passive medium; medium couples to substrate through friction ζ. Characterized by Young's modulus E, Poisson ratio ν, viscosity η. Equations of mechanical equilibrium solved analytically for radially symmetric geometry (Supplementary Notes 1–2). A density-dependent variant (E, η functions of actin density) in Supplementary Note 5.

**3. Agent-based model (Kim lab, W.J. + T.K.).** Brownian/Langevin dynamics of thin actomyosin networks, following Jung-Murrell-Kim 2015 (ref 56) and Mak-Zaman-Kamm-Kim 2016 (ref 57). F-actin = serially connected cylindrical segments (each 280 nm long, 7 nm diameter) with fixed polarity (barbed/pointed). Myosin = rigid backbone (3 cylindrical segments of 42 nm, symmetric polarity, connected by elastic hinges) with 2 motor arms at each of the 2 backbone endpoints → bipolar thick-filament geometry (Craig & Megerman side-polar). Motor arms bind actin binding sites spaced every 7 nm, exert force with **load-dependent kinetics**. Mean F-actin contour length 7.1 µm, persistence length 20 µm (varied to 320 µm and 2.6 mm). Within the activation region motors have low off-rate (processive/load-dependent); outside they are non-processive → stresses generated locally, propagate outward. Activation radius e.g. 6 µm in a 40 µm image.

## Governing Equations

**(1) Active-stress buildup (continuum model), Methods:**
σₐ(r, t) = σ₀(r) · (1 − e^(−t/τₐ))
— isotropic active contractile stress; σ₀(r) > 0 is the spatial activity profile (taken spatially constant σ₀ for r ≤ ξ, zero outside), τₐ = timescale for accumulation of active stress. This drives the overdamped viscoelastic gel; mechanical equilibrium solved analytically for radial geometry.

**(2) Strain from displacement field (analysis):**
ε = ∇·**u** — network strain is the divergence of the displacement field **u** (obtained by time-integrating the PIV velocity field).

**(3) Strain rate from velocity field (analysis):**
dε/dt = ∇·**v** — strain rate is the divergence of the F-actin velocity field.

**(4) Hill-equation fit for cooperativity (Methods / SI):**
εmax(ρ) fit to a Hill function of myosin density ρ with Hill coefficient nH = 11 and critical density ρc = 0.56 µm⁻² (functional form is the standard Hill sigmoid εmax = εsat·ρ^nH/(ρc^nH + ρ^nH); exact form in Supplementary Methods).

**(5) Overdamped force balance (implicit, continuum):** the gel is overdamped and exerts frictional forces on the substrate with friction coefficient ζ; predicted radial stress distribution (Fig 2e) derived from the velocity profile assuming radial symmetry and substrate friction, without assuming network material properties (Supplementary Methods).

**(6) Composite dimensionless control parameter:** E/σₐ (ratio of medium Young's modulus to active stress) governs telescopic slope; σrr/E is the reported normalized radial stress (Fig 5c). RM = [myosin]/[actin] (concentration ratio) and RC = [filamin]/[actin] are the composition control ratios.

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Active contractile stress**: isotropic, exponential saturating buildup σ₀(1 − e^(−t/τₐ)) (Eq 1). Not a per-motor force law but the coarse-grained tissue-level active stress.
- **Myosin motor (agent-based)**: load-dependent binding/force kinetics (Bell-type / catch-slip family; detailed in Supplementary Fig 8, Supplementary Note 4 — "strong load dependence" inside the activation region, non-processive outside). Explicit functional form not in main text.
- **F-actin bending**: characterized by persistence length lp (20 µm baseline; raised to 320 µm and 2.6 mm to suppress bending). Bending resistance sets whether filaments buckle under motor load.
- **Viscoelastic passive stress**: linear viscoelastic solid (Young's modulus E, Poisson ratio ν, viscosity η) — chosen (solid, not fluid) to reproduce rapid short-time strain rise + long-time plateau.
- **Substrate coupling**: linear friction, coefficient ζ (overdamped, drag ∝ velocity).
- **Crosslinker (filamin-A)**: bundles F-actin, increasing network stiffness; modeled/added at RC = [filamin]/[actin] = 0.035 (45 nM filamin with the actin used; text also says 300 nM in the strain-comparison bar plot Fig 5h,i — see note).

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** — Partial/indirect. This is a fine-grained, sub-cellular cortex reconstitution + active-gel continuum. The continuum active-gel (σₐ buildup, E/η/ζ, radial-stress-peaks-at-center) is conceptually adjacent to DCM cortex tension but DCM is a whole-cell surface-mesh model; the direct transferable pieces are the active-stress accumulation timescale and the cortical-tension-generation ideas, not the geometry. Low-to-moderate.
- **FF?** — **YES, strongly.** This is a Taeyoon-Kim-lab agent-based fine-grained actomyosin model — exactly FF's physics domain (F-actin as segmented beams, bipolar myosin thick filaments with load-dependent Bell-type kinetics, disordered cortex, no crosslinker → myosin-only connectivity, persistence-length sweep). The geometry constants (280 nm segments, 7 nm diameter, 42 nm myosin backbone segments, 7 nm binding-site spacing, lp=20 µm, mean length 7.1 µm) are directly usable as FF myosin/actin parameterization, and refs 56 (Jung-Murrell-Kim 2015) + 57 (Mak-Zaman-Kamm-Kim 2016) are the FF agent-based lineage.
- **TAG-validation?** — **YES.** Rich quantitative validation targets for the FF cortex-contractility engine: cooperativity (nH=11, ρc=0.56 µm⁻²), telescopic law (boundary velocity ∝ activation size at constant εmax, dε/dt), strain phases (P1/P2/P3), filamin stiffening reduces strain (~2.4→0.3) and rate, and the bending-invariance result (telescopy survives lp 20 µm→2.6 mm). These are cross-check oracles (measured in-vitro + reproduced by the Kim agent-based model), not runtime mechanisms.
