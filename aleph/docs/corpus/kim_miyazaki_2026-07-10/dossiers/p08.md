## Summary
This paper asks what each structural domain (motor **head**, **neck**, **tail**) of a mitotic kinesin actually does *in a living cell*, using the fission yeast *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* as a model. The authors systematically build chimeras and truncations that swap the head, neck and tail between the **plus-end-directed tetrameric kinesin-6 Klp9 (K9)** — which localizes to the anaphase spindle **midzone** to slide antiparallel MTs apart and elongate the spindle — and the **minus-end-directed kinesin-14 Pkl1 (P1)** — which localizes to the **spindle poles** to focus MT minus ends. Every chimera is expressed at its parent gene locus under its native promoter, C-terminally eGFP-tagged, in cells co-expressing mCherry-Atb2 (tubulin). By imaging localization, 3-phase spindle elongation dynamics, photobleaching recovery, MT protrusions, over-expression, and an artificial mini-chromosome loss assay, they find: (1) the **tail** (not the head, not the neck) dictates cellular localization and, remarkably, can reverse the effective directionality of a foreign head (P1-head on K9-neck-tail walks plus-end toward the midzone); (2) the **neck modulates head activity** — the K9 neck speeds the head up, the P1 neck slows it down — but plays *no* role in localization/direction (contradicting prior in-vitro claims that the neck sets directionality); (3) the **head produces the sliding/binding force**, and the P1 head is inherently ~faster than the K9 head. Domain dysfunctions cause two distinct aneuploidy pathways: chimera **P1h:K9nt** slides ~1.5× faster than WT Klp9, over-elongating and buckling the spindle to breakage (~2% aneuploidy via spindle breakage), while the **P1 chimeric/truncated series** fail to accumulate enough motor at poles to focus MTs, producing abnormally long pole MT protrusions that push nuclei to the cell center → "chromosome cut" (~8–11% aneuploidy via nuclear misposition).

## Model & Methods
**Model type:** Purely EXPERIMENTAL — in vivo molecular genetics + quantitative live-cell fluorescence microscopy. **No mathematical/physical model, no simulation, no governing equations, no force laws, no in-vitro reconstitution assay.** Explicit statement (Data availability): "No in-house code was used in this study."

**Organism / system:** *S. pombe*, all isogenic to WT strain 972; "closed" mitosis (nuclear envelope stays intact). Strains built by transformation and genetic crosses, random spore germination, drug/supplement selection.

**Domain delineation:** head/neck/tail boundaries of Klp9 and Pkl1 defined by combining SMART + COILED-COILS + AlphaFold2 predictions on PomBase. Head = ATPase globular domain; tail begins at first coiled-coil region; neck is the short linker between head and tail.
- Klp9 (K9), kinesin-6, chromosome II, N-terminal head: head aa 2–399, neck aa 400–419, tail aa 420–633.
- Pkl1 (P1), kinesin-14, chromosome I, C-terminal head: tail aa 1–465, neck aa 466–476, head aa 477–830.

**Chimera construction:** Full-length DNA (with 5′ promoter + 3′ terminator) sub-cloned into pFA6a parent vectors carrying C-terminal eGFP + Geneticin(G418)-resistance (GenR). Individual domains PCR-amplified and swapped via **Gibson Assembly**; chimeric cassette transformed at the parent gene locus by the **Li-acetate method**; all verified by DNA sequencing (Eurofins). Fluorescent markers introduced by mating.

**Naming convention:** motors named by domain order from N→C using head(h)/neck(n)/tail(t) and parent (K9 or P1). WT Klp9 = K9hnt; WT Pkl1 = P1tnh (tail-neck-head, since head is C-terminal). Example chimera P1h:K9nt = Pkl1 head on Klp9 neck-tail.

**Live-cell microscopy:** Cells grown in YE5S overnight at 25 °C to OD600 ~0.5, mounted on 2% agarose/YE5S pads. Inverted Nikon Eclipse Ti-E + Yokogawa CSU-22 spinning-disk confocal, Plan Apochromat 100×/1.4NA, PIFOC stepper, Mad City Lab piezo stage, Photometrics Evolve EMCCD; 488 nm (100 mW) + 561 nm (100 mW) lasers (Gataca). Imaging lasers GFP 488 nm 10 mW, mCherry 561 nm 5 mW; Bin 1, EM-Gain 300. Thermal box held 25 °C. Z-stacks of 7 planes × 1 µm, 100 ms exposure/channel, 1-min interval, 60–90 min total. Controlled by MetaMorph 7.

**Photobleaching (FRAP-like) assay:** Same rig + iLAS2 plugin. Bleach GFP with max laser (488 nm, 100 mW) 50 ms; bleach region = focused beam ~0.5 µm diameter circle. Capture 100 ms exposure, 5-s interval, 5 min total.

**Over-expression assay:** P1 series sub-cloned (Gibson) into thiamine-repressible **nmt1+** promoter multicopy vector **pREP81** (Leu1+ selection), in P1Δ background. Grown in EMM-AU + 250 mg/L thiamine, then shifted to EMM-AU (no thiamine) to induce; imaged 20–40 h post-induction. Multicopy → cell-to-cell spread of low→high expression.

**Image analysis:** MetaMorph 7 on max-projected Z-stacks. Velocity = spindle length (mCherry-Atb2) vs successive time frames. Motor number proxied by 6-pixel-wide line-scan of motor-eGFP intensity along spindle. Over-expression signals normalized to 1 µm² (≈ area of a spindle pole).

**Mini-chromosome loss assay:** Chimeric strains mated to a strain carrying artificial mini-chromosome **Ch16-ADE6**; progeny on YE4S –adenine; plated on YE4S –adenine, 30 °C, 3 days. Colonies retaining Ch16 = white; colonies that lost Ch16 (segregation error) = red. Red-colony fraction quantifies aneuploidy.

**Statistics:** Student t-test and Chi-squared test (Microsoft Excel); plots in GraphPad Prism 10; data as mean ± SD; ≥3 biological replicates.

## Force Laws & Potentials
**None.** No interaction potentials, force fields, or explicit force laws are defined or used. Forces are discussed only qualitatively/conceptually:
- Motor head produces MT "binding and sliding forces."
- Klp9 midzone motors generate outward antiparallel-MT sliding force to elongate the spindle.
- Pkl1 pole motors generate inward MT-minus-end focusing force.
- In closed mitosis, the nuclear membrane exerts a *tensile resistive force* opposing fast spindle elongation (invoked as speculative mechanism for why buckled/broken spindles arise; cites refs 38–41). No magnitudes given.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM? — Weak/indirect.** DCM is a surface-mesh continuum cell (membrane/cortex/nucleus/junctions). This paper is about the mitotic spindle (microtubule + kinesin motor apparatus) inside a closed-mitosis yeast nucleus — a compartment DCM does not currently model. The only conceptual touchpoint is the speculated **nuclear-membrane tensile resistive force opposing spindle elongation** in closed mitosis, which could inform a future nuclear-envelope mechanics module. Not a source of DCM parameters/equations.
- **FF? — Partial/conceptual.** FF is a Cytosim-style fine-grained filament engine (actin, cross-linkers, myosin Hill motors, adhesion clutches). Cytosim itself was built for MT/kinesin spindle mechanics (Nédélec & Foethke 2007), so the *class* of physics (filaments + directional motors + antiparallel sliding) is exactly FF's wheelhouse. However this paper provides **no motor force-velocity law, no stall force, no run length, no bond kinetics** — only in-vivo sliding velocities (0.35–2.39 µm/min ≈ 6–40 nm/s) as coarse speed observables. Useful as qualitative context (motor speed sets emergent sliding rate; more motors = faster; neck modulates head speed) but not directly plug-in parameters. FF currently models actin, not the mitotic MT spindle.
- **TAG-validation? — Low.** These are yeast-spindle in-vivo velocities and aneuploidy frequencies, not single-cell mechanobiology moduli/tensions/clutch parameters that the ffn_cellsim validation gates target. Could be registered as SourceEvidence for a "microtubule motor / kinesin directionality" KnowledgeClaim if the project ever extends to a spindle/MT module, but it is **not** a validation target for the current actomyosin-cortex + adhesion + ECM DCM/FF gates. Provenance is clean (peer-reviewed Current Biology, verified DOI), so it is citation-safe if used.
