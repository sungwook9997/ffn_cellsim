## Summary
The paper asks how molecular **catch bonds** (which strengthen under tension but are intrinsically weaker/shorter-lived at low force because their strong binding site is cryptic) affect the mechanical strength of cytoskeletal actin networks, using the actin crosslinker **α-actinin-4** (WT = catch bond) versus its disease point mutant **K255E** (constitutively-exposed strong site = slip bond; causes focal segmental glomerulosclerosis type 1). Single-molecule optical-tweezer force spectroscopy directly confirms WT α-actinin-4 is a catch bond (lifetime rises, peaks near ~4 pN, then falls) while K255E is a slip bond (monotonic decay), and co-sedimentation/FRAP show the mutant has ~10× higher zero-force affinity and much longer bond lifetime. Counter-intuitively, bulk shear rheology to rupture shows the **weaker** catch-bond (WT) networks are simultaneously **stronger** (rupture stress 24.5 Pa vs 6.5–8.1 Pa for slip) **and** more deformable (rupture strain 221% vs 63–129%). A minimal 1D Gillespie load-sharing model with idealized Bell–Evans catch/slip kinetics reproduces this and reveals the mechanism: catch bonds **"dissociate on demand"** from low-stress regions (their shorter low-force lifetime frees them), rebind in high-stress regions, homogenize the per-bond force distribution, and thereby suppress the large gaps that seed cracks — postponing crack **initiation** (the rate-limiting step of rupture). A detailed 3D agent-based Brownian-dynamics network simulation confirms catch bonds form up to 80% more crosslinks between pairs of high-tension filaments than slip bonds despite binding less on average. The mechanism is emergent (needs mobile crosslinkers, N≳10 bonds, and fast-binding/slow-unbinding regime) and generalizes to cell–matrix / cell–cell catch-bond adhesions and to tough biomimetic hydrogel design.

## Model & Methods

### (A) Minimal 1D crosslinker model (Koenderink/Mulla)
- **Type:** stochastic agent-based, **Gillespie algorithm** for binding/unbinding kinetics of N reversible linkers sharing an externally applied load σ (1D).
- **Force sharing:** nearest-neighbour load sharing — force on linker i proportional to global stress and to the distance between its nearest neighbours on both sides lᵢ (mimics crack initiation in viscoelastic materials, the rate-limiting rupture step).
- **Kinetics:** Bell–Evans force-dependent off-rate (Eq. 1); binding rate held fixed at kon = 1 (time is normalized by binding rate). Unbound linkers rebind at a **random new location** (mobility by diffusion). Immobile control = rebind at same location.
- **Binding-site excess:** 10× more binding sites than crosslinkers (actin ≫ crosslinker, both in vitro 48 vs 0.48 µM and in cells ~100 vs ~1 µM) to prevent competition.
- **Initialization:** randomly place N·kon/(kon+koff,0) linkers.
- **BC:** periodic (avoid edge effects). Load σ increases linearly in time.
- **Slip vs catch:** slip = set kcatch_off,0 = 0 (no force-activated catching term), all else identical.
- **Rupture/yield:** networks fracture when the bound linker number rapidly → 0 (Extended Data Fig. 5a).
- **Statistics:** N = 100 independent simulations per condition; 3-state variant (doubly/singly/unbound) in Supplementary Note 1 (homodimer).
- **Code:** Python, github.com/YuvalMulla/Minimal_1D_Crosslinker_Model.

### (B) 3D agent-based actin-network simulation (Kim lab; W. Jung, T. Kim)
- **Type:** agent-based computational model based on **Brownian dynamics** (Langevin equation of motion for cylindrical segments), Kim-lab canonical actin-network engine (refs 18–21, 34–37).
- **Representation:** F-actin filaments = serially connected cylindrical segments; crosslinkers = two arm segments joined by an elastic hinge; bending (κb) and extensional (κs) stiffnesses maintain equilibrium angles/lengths; repulsive force = volume exclusion between overlapping F-actins.
- **Domain:** thin rectangular box **30 × 30 × 1 µm**, periodic BC in x only.
- **Assembly:** network spontaneously self-assembles by nucleation + polymerization of F-actin and crosslinker binding to elongating filaments; **average filament length ~12 µm**. During assembly all crosslinkers behave as slip bonds.
- **Unbinding:** force-dependent (slip or catch). After unbinding one F-actin, the crosslinker quickly unbinds the other arm, disappears, and reappears at a new location **within 5 µm** of the unbinding site (mobility).
- **Deformation:** after formation, shear strain applied linearly at **rate 0.001 s⁻¹**, bottom fixed, top displaced in +x (Fig. 4b). Runs done separately with catch OR slip crosslinkers.
- **Measurement:** tensile forces on F-actins measured during shear; crosslinker redistribution analyzed vs filament pair tension. Strain-controlled ⇒ no sudden strain divergence; **yield point = first data point where network stress decreases (dσ/dt < 0)**; raw noisy σ(strain) convolved with a 60-data-point time window before rupture-point detection.
- **Parameters:** Supplementary Table 2 (not in this PDF).

### (C) Experimental methods
- **Single-molecule force spectroscopy:** dual-trap optical tweezers + commercial C-Trap (LUMICKS), 500 Hz, Python 2.7 analysis. α-actinin-4 tethered to polystyrene bead via 2.5-kbp dsDNA handle (ybbR tag → CoA-DNA via SFP synthase); actin-coated bead (biotin/Alexa647 actin on NeutrAvidin beads, 2.1 µm). Trap stiffness κ = 0.39 ± 0.04 pN nm⁻¹ (calibrated by power-spectrum of Brownian motion). Approach–retract; hold at preset force; measure lifetime until force drops to zero. Single-tether discrimination: dsDNA overstretching plateau > 65 pN and WLC contour length 850 ± 30 nm. Lifetimes from tethers dissociating within 1 min (55% of tethers).
- **Rheology:** stress-controlled Kinexus Malvern Pro rheometer, cone–plate (20 mm radius, 1° cone). 48 µM actin + 0.48 µM crosslinker (1:100 molar) in F-buffer. Linear rheology (G′, G″) via SAOS 0.01–10 Hz; rupture by linear stress ramp at **2.0 mPa s⁻¹**; rupture point = stress where differential elastic modulus K′ peaks. Temperature tuned (10 °C = high lifetime, 25 °C = low lifetime) to isolate bond-lifetime effect.
- **Co-sedimentation:** G-actin at increasing conc. + fixed 0.1 µM crosslinker, polymerize 2 h, spin 120,000g, SDS-PAGE quantify bound fraction; Michaelis–Menten fit → affinity ka.
- **FRAP:** Nikon A1 confocal, bleach 2 µm radius, single-exponential recovery → τFRAP (bond lifetime), at 25 °C, 48 µM actin.

## Governing Equations

**(Eq. 1) Bell–Evans force-dependent off-rate (both 1D and 3D models):**
```
koff,i(fi) = ( kcatch_off,0 · exp(−fi / f_catch_(1/e)) ) + ( kslip_off,0 · exp(+fi / f_slip_(1/e)) )
```
First term (negative exponent) = "catching" of the weakly bound state (lifetime rises with force); second term (positive exponent) = "slipping" of the force-activated state (lifetime falls with force). Slip-only bond ⇒ kcatch_off,0 = 0. f_catch_(1/e), f_slip_(1/e) are the characteristic forces at which each exponential changes by factor e.

**(Eq. 2) Per-linker force from nearest-neighbour load sharing (1D):**
```
fi = ( li / Σi li ) · σ · N
```
Force on linker i ∝ global applied stress σ × N × (distance li between its two nearest neighbours normalized by total). Bond load and bond–bond distance are directly proportional ⇒ larger gaps carry larger loads (crack seeds). Normalization: σ·N / f_slip_(1/e) = Σi fi.

**(Eq. 3) Elastic shear modulus (mean-field semiflexible network model, from G′(ω) fit):**
```
G′ ∝ (χ′ + α′(ω)) / [ (χ′ + α′(ω))² + (χ″ + α″(ω))² ]
```

**(Eq. 4) Viscous shear modulus:**
```
G″ ∝ (χ″ + α″(ω)) / [ (χ′ + α′(ω))² + (χ″ + α″(ω))² ]
```
χ = viscous-drag-limited transverse filament fluctuations; α = crosslinker-limited transverse filament fluctuations.

**(Eq. 5) Real part of drag term χ′** (function of ω/ωfluid, prefactor 0.0072/(4√2)):
```
χ′ = [0.0072/(4√2)] · { 2·atan(1+√2·√(ω/ωfluid)) − 2·atan(1−√2·√(ω/ωfluid))
      + log(1 + ωfluid/ω − √2·√(ω/ωfluid)) − log(1 + ωfluid/ω + √2·√(ω/ωfluid)) } / √(ω/ωfluid)
```

**(Eq. 6) Imaginary part of drag term χ″** (same prefactor, sign flip on the log terms):
```
χ″ = [0.0072/(4√2)] · { 2·atan(1+√2·√(ω/ωfluid)) − 2·atan(1−√2·√(ω/ωfluid))
      − log(1 + ωfluid/ω − √2·√(ω/ωfluid)) + log(1 + ωfluid/ω + √2·√(ω/ωfluid)) } / √(ω/ωfluid)
```
ωfluid = fluid-drag timescale, ~100 Hz for actin networks (depends on fluid viscosity, crosslinker & actin conc).

**(Eq. 7) Real part of crosslinker term α′** (sum over normal modes n; N = crosslinkers per filament):
```
α′ = (1/π⁴) · Σ_{n=1}^{N}  n⁴ / ( n⁸ + (ω/ωoff)² )
```

**(Eq. 8) Imaginary part of crosslinker term α″:**
```
α″ = (1/π⁴) · Σ_{n=1}^{N}  (ω/ωoff) / ( n⁸ + (ω/ωoff,0)² )
```
ωoff,0 = zero-force crosslinker off-rate, extracted by fitting G′(ω) to Eqs. 4–7 (Extended Data Fig. 4d).

**(Eq. 9) Stress-dependent differential storage modulus (extract lifetime under stress):**
```
K′ ∝ (1 + (σ + σ0,tr))^{3/2} / ( 1 + √( ωoff(σ) + ω ) )
```
ωoff(σ) = stress-dependent crosslinker-unbinding frequency; σ0,tr = critical stress for stress stiffening in the fast (pre-unbinding) limit. Fit over 0.01<ω<10 Hz, 0.1<σ<8 Pa (Extended Data Fig. 4f–i).

**FRAP recovery fit (Methods):**
```
I(t)/Iref = 1 − (I/I0)·exp(−t/τFRAP)
```

**Co-sedimentation bound fraction:**
```
φbound = (I − I0)/I0   (main Methods)   ;   φbound = c_actin / Ka  (Michaelis–Menten fit, Extended Data Fig. 1c)
```

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Off-rate law:** Bell–Evans double-exponential (Eq. 1) — the single governing force law for bond kinetics.
- **On-rate:** constant (kon = 1 in 1D; concentration-independent binding in 3D given site excess).
- **3D segment mechanics:** harmonic bending potential (stiffness κb, subscripts A = actin, x = crosslinker) + harmonic extensional/stretching potential (stiffness κs, subscripts A = actin, x = crosslinker) maintaining equilibrium angles/lengths; harmonic-type repulsive volume-exclusion force between overlapping F-actin cylinders. Crosslinker = two arm segments + elastic hinge (Fig. 4a shows κb,A, κb,x, κs,A, κs,x). Motion via Langevin equation (Brownian dynamics). Exact constants in Supplementary Table 2 (not in this PDF).

Not a direct DCM parameter source.
- **FF?** — **YES, strongly.** This is the gold for FF: (1) direct single-molecule α-actinin-4 catch-bond lifetime-vs-force data (peak ~4 pN) and K255E slip data = validation targets for FF Bell-Evans crosslinker off-rates; (2) the 3D agent-based Brownian-dynamics actin-network simulation (Kim lab; cylindrical filament segments + two-arm hinged crosslinkers, κb/κs, volume exclusion, 30×30×1 µm, force-dependent unbinding, mobility) is essentially the same fine-grained physics FF implements — the Bell-Evans double-exponential (Eq. 1) is directly usable as FF's crosslinker kinetics; (3) network rheology rupture stress/strain and mesh-size (~200 nm), filament length (~12 µm), crosslinker:actin ratio (1:100 ≈ 1 per 0.5 µm) are concrete FF-cortex parameters/validation observables. The FF engine already uses α-actinin k_off0 re-anchored to Ferrer 2008 per CLAUDE.md; this paper adds the force-dependence (catch) and network-level rupture targets.
- **TAG-validation?** — **YES.** Multiple citable, DOI-verified quantitative anchors (α-actinin-4 affinities 1.95/15.55 µM⁻¹, catch peak ~4 pN, rupture stresses 6.5/8.1/24.5 Pa, rupture strains 63/129/221%, mesh ~200 nm) suitable for SourceEvidence/KnowledgeClaim registration on catch-bond crosslinker mechanics.
