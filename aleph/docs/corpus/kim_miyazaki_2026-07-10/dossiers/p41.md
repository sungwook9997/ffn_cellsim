## Summary
The paper asks how the *structure* of myosin II bipolar thick filaments — number of heads/arms, filament length, central bare-zone length, arm spacing, filament number, and spatial distribution — controls the tensile force generated in *disorganized* (non-sarcomeric) actomyosin bundles and 2D networks. Using an established agent-based Brownian-dynamics model (Langevin equation, forward-Euler) in which F-actin, actin cross-linking proteins (ACPs), and motors are represented as serially-connected cylindrical segments — with each motor an explicit bipolar backbone carrying arms that walk (parallel-cluster-model, catch-bond kinetics) toward barbed ends — the authors dissect force generation across three system types: a minimal two-antiparallel-filament clamped system, disorganized bundles (2N_F² filaments), and 2D networks. The central mechanistic finding is that **cooperative overlap between motors, gated by intervening ACPs, sets the force**: two motors with no ACP between them add their forces (Ftot → F_M^max, twice one motor), whereas an ACP between them buckles the intervening F-actin and counterbalances one motor's force so the bundle only feels the force of a single motor (Ftot → F_M^max/2). ACPs thereby partition the disordered bundle into serially-connected "contractile units" (matching the Thoresen 2013 sarcomere-like hypothesis, but here emergent, not assumed). Consequences: thicker bundles and more motors give larger total force but *lower efficiency* η = Ftot/F_M^max (η ∝ 1/N_M at low N_M, plateau ~0.08 at high N_M); more localized motors (small allocation fraction f) give higher force and efficiency (more cooperative overlaps); longer motors with more arms, or with a *long bare zone* (rather than large arm spacing), generate more force because cooperative overlap requires a smaller critical overlap length L_c. Networks obey the same mechanism but with Ftot ∝ N_M^0.65 ≈ √N_M (vs ∝N_M for bundles) due to random orientation. An analytic estimator (Ξ = max cooperatively-overlapping motors → F_est = F_st N_h N_a Ξ / 2) reproduces the trends. Physiological corollary: ventral stress fibers (many α-actinin ACPs, cascaded units) generate less force per thickness than transverse arcs (few ACPs), consistent with experiment.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type**: Agent-based / coarse-grained mechanistic **Brownian dynamics** (overdamped Langevin, inertia neglected), forward-Euler integration. Three explicit element types built from serially-connected **cylindrical segments**: F-actin (polar, barbed↔pointed), ACP (two arms + central hinge), motor (bipolar myosin thick-filament analog: backbone segments + arms; each arm = 8 real myosin heads).
- **Integrator / timestep**: forward Euler; Δt = 1.15×10⁻⁵ s.
- **Domains / BCs / geometry**:
  - *Two-filament*: 5×5×20 µm box; PBC in x,y; repulsive boundary in z. Two antiparallel F-actins, 19 µm long, barbed ends **clamped** to two rigid boundaries normal to z (force-measuring, isometric). 2 motors, 16 ACPs, N_a=24.
  - *Bundle*: same-shaped box (20 µm long axis, PBC in all directions). F-actins placed on an x,y grid (spacing 27 nm); N_F possible positions per direction ⇒ **2N_F² filaments**, each 9 µm, random z and random polarity. N_F = 2 (8 fil) … 7 (98 fil). Densities R_ACP=0.04, R_M=0.005 (fixed) ⇒ N_M 4–52.
  - *Network*: thin slab 20×20×0.1 µm; PBC in x,y; repulsive boundary in z. F-actins ~10 µm avg, random positions/orientations; formed by stochastic nucleation (k_n,A) + barbed-end polymerization (k_p,A) tuned to ~10 µm mean length. N_M up to ~2500.
- **Assembly protocol**: F-actins held stationary while ACPs bind (permanent cross-links) and motor arms self-assemble/bind; then F-actins released, motors begin force-dependent walking + unbinding.
- **Motor kinetics**: arms bind at rate 40·N_h s⁻¹ only when aligned to F-actin polarity; two arms on the same backbone point cannot bind the same F-actin; after binding, walk toward barbed end. Walking (k_w,M) and unbinding (k_u,M) rates from the **Parallel Cluster Model (PCM)** of Erdmann & Schwarz (2012, 2013) for non-muscle myosin II — both rates *decrease* under higher load (catch-bond behavior). Unloaded walking velocity ~140 nm/s; motor stall force = F_st·N_h.
- **ACP kinetics**: bind to actin binding sites every 7 nm, no angle preference, constant rate; **never unbind** (permanent cross-links).
- **Force measurement**: cross-sections every 200 nm along z (bundle/two-fil); sum z-component of spring forces on all crossing segments; average over sections at steady state = Ftot. Network: 20 cross-sections each in x and y (40 total), sum x/y components, average.
- **Motor structural variations** (3 ways): (i) increase N_a (add arm-bearing backbone segments; L_bz, L_sp = L_MB); (ii) increase L_bz (add bare-zone segments; N_a fixed, L_sp=L_MB); (iii) increase L_sp & L_bz by raising L_MB (N_a fixed).
- **Analysis estimator**: compute Ξ (max cooperatively-overlapping motors) from motor positions → F_est.

## Governing Equations
1. **(Eq 1)** `0.5 F_M^max ≤ Ftot ≤ F_M^max` — bounds of total force in the two-motor two-filament system (lower = ACP between motors → one-motor force; upper = full overlap → two-motor force).
2. **(Eq 2)** `F_M^max = (1/2) Σ_i^{N_M} F_M,z^i` — maximal force all motors can generate; F_M,z^i = z-component of spring force of the i-th motor at steady state. (Factor ½ because only half the arms bind at once.) F_M,z^i ≈ F_st·N_h·N_a/2 (two-filament) or ≈ F_st·N_h·N_a (bundle, ≥1 F-actin per polarity).
3. **(Eq 3)** `η = Ftot / F_M^max` — efficiency of force generation (force per available motor capacity).
4. **(Eq 4)** `Ξ = max_{i=1..N_M, j=L or R} [ 1 + Σ_{k≠i}^{N_M} ζ_ik^j ]` — estimated number of motors in the strongest contractile unit.
5. **(Eq 5)** `ζ_ik^j = 1 if L_c ≤ L_ov ≤ L_M ; = L_ov/L_c if 0<L_ov<L_c ; = 0 if L_ov=0` — cooperative-overlap weighting between motors i,k (L_ov = overlap distance).
6. **(Eq 6)** `L_c = 2 L_sp ( N_a/4 − 1 )` — critical overlap length for full cooperativity (twice the arm-occupied length on one backbone side).
7. **(Eq 7)** `F_est = F_st N_h N_a Ξ / 2` — analytic bundle-force estimate from Ξ.
8. **(Eq 8)** `F_M^max = (1/4)( Σ_i F_M,x^i + Σ_i F_M,y^i )` — network version of F_M^max (average of summed x,y motor force components).
9. **(Eq 9)** `F_i − ζ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_i^T = 0` — overdamped Langevin equation of motion (inertia neglected) for segment i.
10. **(Eq 10)** `⟨F_i^T(t) F_j^T(t)⟩ = (2 k_B T ζ_i δ_ij / Δt) δ` — fluctuation-dissipation for the stochastic force (δ = 2nd-order identity tensor).
11. **(Eq 11)** `ζ_i = 3π μ r_c,i (3 + 2 r_0,i/r_c,i)/5` — drag coefficient, cylinder approximation (μ medium viscosity; r_0,i length, r_c,i diameter of segment).
12. **(Eq 12)** `r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (dr_i/dt)Δt = r_i(t) + (1/ζ_i)(F_i + F_i^T)Δt` — forward-Euler position update.
13. **(Eq 13)** `U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ_0)²` — bending potential (equilibrium angle θ_0).
14. **(Eq 14)** `U_s = ½ κ_s (r − r_0)²` — extensional (stretching) potential (equilibrium length r_0).
15. **(Eq 15)** `U_r = ½ κ_r,A (r_12 − r_c,A)² if r_12 < r_c,A ; = 0 if r_12 ≥ r_c,A` — harmonic repulsive volume-exclusion between neighboring actin segments (r_12 = min inter-segment distance).
- Motor arm extension modeled by a **two-spring model**: transverse spring κ_s,M2 sets equilibrium distance r_0,M2 = 13.5 nm (backbone endpoint ↔ bound actin segment); longitudinal spring κ_s,M3 enforces right angle (r_0,M3 = 0 nm).

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Extensional**: linear (Hookean) spring, F = −κ_s(r−r_0), from Eq 14. κ_s,M1 = κ_s,A (backbone stretch = actin stretch).
- **Bending**: linear-elastic angular, torque ∝ −κ_b(θ−θ_0), from Eq 13. κ_b,A ↔ actin persistence length 9 µm. κ_b,M > κ_b,A (backbone kept stiff/straight). θ_0 = 0 for all elements.
- **Repulsion (excluded volume)**: one-sided harmonic (Eq 15), acts only when actin segments closer than r_c,A.
- **Motor arm (crossbridge)**: two-spring (transverse r_0,M2=13.5 nm, longitudinal r_0,M3=0).
- **Motor force–velocity / kinetics**: NOT a closed-form Hill law here — walking & unbinding rates supplied by the **Parallel Cluster Model** (Erdmann–Schwarz), load-dependent, catch-bond (rates ↓ with load). Unloaded v ≈ 140 nm/s, stall = F_st·N_h per arm, F_st ≈ 5.7 pN per head.
- **Thermal force**: Gaussian white noise per Eq 10.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?**: **Indirect/low.** DCM uses a lumped actomyosin *cortical tension* γ, not explicit filaments. This paper does not give a continuum γ, but its bundle/network Ftot vs motor-number scaling and the ~0.08 efficiency ceiling could inform how a lumped cortical-tension term should scale with myosin density — a sanity anchor, not a direct input.
- **FF?**: **YES — high.** This is a Cytosim-class fine-grained agent-based actomyosin model and is a near-direct architectural sibling of the FF engine: explicit F-actin bending/stretching beams (Eqs 13–14), harmonic excluded volume (Eq 15), overdamped Langevin + Euler (Eqs 9–12), drag from cylinder formula (Eq 11), bipolar myosin minifilament with N_h=8 heads/arm, catch-bond load-dependent walking/unbinding via PCM, permanent ACP cross-links. Directly relevant to FF's myosin minifilament model, ACP kinetics, and the "cooperative overlap / contractile-unit" emergent mechanism. Parameters (F_st=5.7 pN/head, v~140 nm/s, actin segment 140 nm, L_p=9 µm, backbone 42 nm, ACP arm 23.5 nm, bare-zone ~160 nm) are candidate FF inputs / cross-checks. The Kim lab code (ThickFilament) is the same lineage as FF's actomyosin references.
- **TAG-validation?**: **YES.** Quantitative validation targets: F_st≈5.7 pN/head; motor stall F_st·N_h; unloaded v≈140 nm/s; efficiency law η∝1/N_M with plateau ~0.08; bundle Ftot∝N_M (slope 1) vs network Ftot∝N_M^0.65≈√N_M; two-motor bound 0.5F_M^max≤Ftot≤F_M^max; L_c = 2L_sp(N_a/4−1) and F_est=F_st N_h N_a Ξ/2. These are clean, dimensionally-checkable acceptance oracles for FF's myosin+ACP force generation. Provenance: agent-based simulation (Kim lab), values partly cited (F_st, v, L_p) partly model-set; suitable as cross-check oracles, not as measured experimental data.
