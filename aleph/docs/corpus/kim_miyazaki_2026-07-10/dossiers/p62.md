## Summary
The paper asks whether the formin mDia1's processive "capping" motion is *tightly* coupled to actin polymerization — i.e. whether the FH2 dimer translocates by exactly one actin-subunit length at every single monomer association/dissociation, as the accepted stair-stepping (Otomo 2005) and stepping-second (Paul & Pollard 2009) models assume. Using dual-trap optical tweezers in an "actin dumbbell" geometry (a single actin filament stretched between an mDia1-immobilized bead and an avidin bead, held at 4–6 pN), the authors resolved the barbed-end elongation into discrete nanometer steps at low G-actin (40 and 75 nM, near the ~64 nM critical concentration). A step-finding algorithm plus triple-Gaussian fitting showed unitary forward/backward steps of **+2.8 nm / −2.4 nm** (≈ the ~2.7 nm actin subunit half-repeat), consistent with the models — but **double- and triple-sized steps (4.9 & 8.7 nm forward; −5.2 & −8.7 nm backward) were frequently observed** and shown by a rate-constant/temporal-resolution probability model to be genuine, not detection artifacts. Forward-step dwell-time rate constants depended on [G-actin] (0.92 s⁻¹ at 40 nM → 1.4 s⁻¹ at 75 nM) while backward-step rates did not (0.56 & 0.62 s⁻¹), confirming stepping tracks association (concentration-dependent) vs dissociation (concentration-independent) kinetics. The authors conclude mDia1 stepping is **loosely** coupled to polymerization — the formin can stay put through one monomer addition and then translocate two subunits at once — and propose new intermediate FH2 configurations (`<n+1>*`, `<n+1>**`) enabling double-sized steps, with functional implications for cooperation with other barbed-end proteins (e.g. capping protein).

## Model & Methods
- **Study type:** Experimental single-molecule in-vitro reconstitution assay. NOT a simulation — no numerical integrator. The "model" content is (i) the experimental assay design and (ii) an analytical probability model for multi-sized step detection and a kinetic dwell-time model.
- **Core assay — "actin dumbbell" (dual-trap optical tweezers):**
  - Two 1-µm polystyrene beads linked by a single actin filament trapped in two independent IR laser traps.
  - Barbed end coupled to mDia1 (truncated FH1FH2 construct) immobilized on one bead via N-terminal GST-tag ↔ glutathione–BSA on the bead surface.
  - Pointed-end region (<1 µm, 10% biotinylated, phalloidin-labeled) attached to a neutravidin/avidin bead via biotin–avidin, so pointed-end (de)polymerization does NOT contribute to bead-to-bead distance.
  - mDia1-bead trap center is translated to keep tension at 4–6 pN; avidin-bead trap is fixed and reads out the tension. mDia1 tracks the growing barbed end → its bead position reports filament elongation.
  - Because mDia1 is tethered via two FH1 domains, the tracked position is the **centroid of the two FH2 dimer halves**; when one half moves and the other is stationary, only **half** the translocation appears as a step (hence 2.7 nm centroid step for a 5.4 nm half-movement in the double-step model).
- **Imaging:** Inverted microscope (Olympus IX71), dual-trap optical tweezers. Phase-contrast + fluorescence at 30 fps; high-speed phase-contrast recording at **300 fps** (BOBCAT ICL-B0620M camera, Imperx), 1 pixel = 37 nm. Green (532 nm) / red (633 nm) lasers distinguish bead types; IC5-labeled avidin beads.
- **Conditions:** 26 ± 1 °C. Basic buffer: 10 mM imidazole-HCl pH 7.4, 50 mM KCl, 1 mM MgCl₂, 1 mM EGTA, 50 µM CaCl₂, + 10 mM DTT, 1 mM ATP, ATP–G-actin (40 or 75 nM), oxygen-scavenging system (0.22 mg/mL glucose oxidase, 0.036 mg/mL catalase, 4.5 mg/mL glucose).
- **Trap calibration:** stiffness via Stokes drag F = 6πηrv (moving stage, measuring bead displacement). Trap stiffnesses used: 0.081 or 0.073 pN/nm.
- **Data-analysis pipeline:**
  1. Bead centroids tracked with ImageJ "Particle Track and Analysis" plug-in (Y. Arai).
  2. Actin-polymerization-INDEPENDENT but force-dependent bead-to-bead length change (bead rotation) subtracted — calibrated per dumbbell by stretching ≥3× at >1 pN, fit to Dupuis et al. 1997 bead-rotation equations (eqs 6, 19, 20 there) with flexural rigidity EI as the fitting parameter. (Actin filament itself treated as inextensible: stiffness 300 pN/nm per µm length.)
  3. 0.1 s median filtering.
  4. Trajectory split into ~30 s / 10 000-plot segments → Kerssemakers step-finding algorithm (Nature 2006).
  5. Steps in the 4–6 pN window collected; forward and backward steps analyzed separately.
  6. Step-size histograms (40 & 75 nM) globally fit to a triple Gaussian (shared peak centers across concentrations).
  7. Dwell-time histograms fit to eq 1 (sum of 1-, 2-, 3-sequential reactions), with Gaussian-area proportions A,B,C as fixed coefficients.
- **Sample sizes:** 33 dumbbells (40 nM), 55 dumbbells (75 nM). Steps: N=791 (40 nM), N=1288 (75 nM). Dwell counts: 40 nM — 620 forward / 171 backward; 75 nM — 1097 forward / 191 backward.

## Governing Equations
All equations are analytical (data-analysis models), not dynamical integrators.

**(eq 1) Dwell-time histogram fit (sum of single + sequential reactions):**
$$ f(t) = aA\,e^{-kt} + aB\,k t\,e^{-kt} + aC\,k^2 t^2\,e^{-kt} $$
Meaning: superposition of 1-, 2-, and 3-sequential first-order reactions (Gamma-distribution terms of order 1,2,3) sharing rate constant k; A,B,C = areas of the three step-size Gaussians (smallest→largest); a = normalization coefficient; t = dwell time. The k reported is the fitted rate constant.

**(eq 2) Survival probability of a single reaction (no step until time t):**
$$ P(t) = e^{-kt} $$
Meaning: probability the formin has NOT yet stepped by time t, for a single first-order reaction with rate k.

**(eq 3) Probability of detecting a single-sized step at temporal resolution T:**
$$ p_\text{single} = e^{-kT} $$
Meaning: a lone step is resolved when the (single) dwell exceeds the temporal resolution T.

**(eq 4) Probability of detecting a double-sized step:**
$$ p_\text{double} = \int_{S=0}^{S=T} \left(-\frac{dP(t)}{dt}\Big|_{t=S}\right) P(T-S)\,dS = kT\,e^{-kT} $$
Meaning: the first step occurs before T (at time S) and merges with a second step whose remaining time (T−S) still exceeds resolution → the two are counted as one 2× step.

**(eq 5) Probability of detecting a triple-sized step:**
$$ p_\text{triple} = \int_{S=0}^{S=T} \left(-\frac{dP(t)}{dt}\Big|_{t=S}\right)\{1 - P(T-S)\}\,dS = 1 - e^{-kT} - kT\,e^{-kT} $$
Meaning: first+second steps both complete within T (unresolved) → counted as one 3× step. Steps >triple ignored (rare); p_single + p_double + p_triple = 1.

**(assay/calibration) Optical-trap stiffness via Stokes drag:**
$$ F = 6\pi\eta r v $$
Meaning: viscous drag force calibrating trap stiffness; η = viscosity, r = bead radius, v = stage speed.

**(implicit, cited from Dupuis 1997) Force-dependent bead-rotation length correction** — eqs 6, 19, 20 of Dupuis et al. 1997, with flexural rigidity EI of the actin dumbbell as the sole fit parameter (equations not reproduced in this paper).

## Force Laws & Potentials
This is an experimental paper; there is no simulation force field. Mechanically relevant relations:
- **Applied filament tension:** held at **4–6 pN** via optical-tweezers feedback (mDia1-bead trap translation). Within this range mDia1 elongation rate treated as force-independent (per Kubota 2017; note Yu 2017 disputes saturation, attributed to immobilization method differences).
- **Optical-trap restoring force:** linear-spring, stiffness κ = 0.081 or 0.073 pN/nm; force read from bead displacement × κ.
- **Actin filament stiffness (axial):** 300 pN/nm per µm filament length (from Dupuis 1997) — filament treated as effectively inextensible; apparent length changes attributed to bead rotation, not filament stretch.
- **Stokes drag (calibration):** F = 6πηrv.

- **Figure 5 (proposed mechanism):** Stepping model extending stair-stepping (Otomo 2005). Top row = accepted single-sized pathway (each FH2 half moves 2.7 nm centroid per monomer, blocked↔accessible rapid equilibrium, Post/lasso = squares, Knob = triangles). Bottom = new double-sized pathway: FH2 halves stay stationary through the first monomer association/dissociation via new intermediate states `<n+1>*` (magenta subunit, "blocked-state" association) and `<n+1>**` (orange subunit, "accessible-state" dissociation), then both halves migrate together → 5.4 nm double step. These intermediates deviate from crystallography and are posited as energetically unfavorable configurations.
