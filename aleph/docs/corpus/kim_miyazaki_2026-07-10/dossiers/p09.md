## Summary
The paper asks how the **basement membrane (BM)–stroma interface**, rather than the usual epithelium–BM complex, drives tissue-surface morphogenesis — specifically why some surfaces **evaginate** (protrude outward: intestinal villi, dermal–epidermal-junction ridges) while others **invaginate** (hair follicle, tooth germ). The authors build a physiologically faithful three-layer tissue-equivalent model — epithelium (Epi) / **engineered nanometer-thick collagen-I basement membrane (eBM)** / fibroblast-laden collagen-I stroma (Str) — and show that a stiff thin BM is the key element: with an eBM present, stromal-cell condensates exert **polarized actomyosin traction** on the rigid membrane and produce **evaginated folding networks**; without the eBM only invagination/compaction occurs. Mechanistically the folding is a **mechanical (elastic) instability** set by the **stiffness contrast between the soft, fluid stroma and the stiff BM**, driven by proteolytic-path–guided ("stromal-fluidity") migration and **zipper-like polarized traction** (F-actin at pseudopod tips, phospho-myosin-light-chain-2 between tips, paxillin focal adhesions to the eBM; abolished by blebbistatin). A continuum **Ansys finite-element model** (two 70-µm hemispherical contractile condensates under a 2-µm BM on a cuboid stroma) reproduces the invagination↔evagination phase boundary as a function of E_BM and E_Str: at the experimentally measured stiffnesses (E_BM = 756 kPa, E_Str = 354 Pa, E_Cell = 8.6 kPa, contractile stress 200 kPa) the simulation evaginates by **1.91 µm**. Folding then feeds back on migration: short-range (r<90 µm) cells form condensates by direct **mechanical coupling** through aligned collagen fibers; long-range (r>90 µm) cells couple **topographically** by migrating along the fold crest, remodeling interstitial fibers and forming new folds via **joining** (acute ~16°) and **branching** (perpendicular ~100°) — a recursive process that self-assembles a body-wide stromal cellular network.

## Model & Methods
Two complementary methodologies:

**(1) Experimental in-vitro reconstitution (primary).**
- **Engineered basement membrane (eBM):** collagen-I sheet (0.25 mg/ml Col I premix) spin-cast onto a 110-nm cellulose-acetate (CA) sacrificial layer inside donut PDMS rings (8/12 mm ID/OD, 300 µm thick), gelled 2 h at 37 °C, then **vitrified 1 week at 18 °C, 50 % humidity** to self-assemble a nanometer-thick membrane. Areal density 0.25 µg/mm². Dehydrated thickness <0.1 µm; hydrated ~2 µm (10–20× swelling). CA dissolved in acetone to harvest the free-floating eBM; coated with 1/50 Matrigel + 10 mU/ml TR2 transglutaminase ("glue").
- **Layered tissue assembly:** stroma = fibroblasts (3T3 / CCD18Co / NOF, at 2×10⁵/ml, human 1×10⁵/ml) embedded in 2 mg/ml Col-I gel poured (85 µl) into the eBM mold, gelled 2 h. Epithelium = HaCaT / SW480 / SKOV3 seeded 1×10⁵/well on top, 15 min adhesion. Free-floating tissue incubated to self-fold. Sub-models: Epi-eBM-Str, eBM-Str, Epi-Str, Epi-eBM (omit a seeding).
- **Perturbation:** blebbistatin 10 µM (MHC/myosin-II inhibitor) to block contraction; MMP inhibition (GM6001-type) to block proteolytic paths.
- **Readouts:** immunofluorescence (Phalloidin/F-actin, DAPI, Vimentin, E-cadherin, Col-IV, pMLC2, Paxillin, Laminin); live imaging (MitoTracker, CellTracker); EdU proliferation; SHIELD tissue clearing + expansion microscopy for in-vivo dermis/intestine/ovary/lung/esophagus; **FN-FRET** intramolecular strain sensor (double-labeled fibronectin, AF546/AF488); **PIV** matrix-displacement / traction mapping (iterative PIV + StackReg/template-match; PIVlab); mechanical tensile testing (dog-bone, load cell + DIC, strain rate 3.2×10⁻³/s); random-forest wavelength classification.

**(2) Continuum Finite-Element Model (Ansys Academic Research Mechanical, Release 22.2).**
- **Geometry:** two hemispherical stromal condensates, 70 µm diameter, separated (center-to-center) by 140 µm, aligned in y, occupying the top of a cuboid stroma **200 × 400 × 600 µm**; a **2-µm-thick BM layer** covers both condensates and the surrounding stroma.
- **Material model:** linear-elastic isotropic bodies, each defined by a Young's modulus (E_BM, E_Str, E_Cell).
- **Boundary conditions:** bottom face of the stroma fully fixed; **constant contractile stress P_Cell applied to the cell (condensate) faces interfacing both the stroma and the BM** — this is how cell contractility is imposed (coarse-grained, no explicit focal-adhesion dynamics). A probe on top of the BM at the condensate center reads the **vertical deformation** (positive = evagination, negative = invagination).
- **Parametric sweep / phase map:** 31 discrete points each for E_BM (10⁴–10⁷ Pa) and E_Str (10–10⁴ Pa) on uniform log intervals; results interpolated onto a 10,000×10,000 grid; the invagination/evagination boundary is the locus where |deformation| < 0.01 µm; secondary sweeps over P_Cell (contractility), E_Cell (5–50 kPa), condensate volume, and E_Str–E_Cell (Fig S4). All maps rendered in MATLAB.
- **Model class rationale:** authors state FEM is more suitable than vertex or active-shell models for predicting stiffness-difference-driven folding; contractility-as-boundary-stress on mesh elements is "a generally accepted method" for coarse-grained cell contractility (refs [10,36]) though it omits dynamic focal-adhesion behavior. No integrator/timestep reported (static/quasi-static elasticity solve).

## Governing Equations
The paper contains **no dynamical governing PDEs/ODEs written out**; the physics is (a) linear elastostatics solved numerically in Ansys, and (b) a handful of image-analysis definitions. Transcribed faithfully:

**(Eq. 1) Alignment percentage.**
  Alignment% = (1 − D_{A−B}/180) × 100 %
  where D_{A−B} = orientation difference (degrees) between features A and B (e.g., cell vs fold). Meaning: co-orientation metric; 100 % = perfectly parallel.

**(Eq. 2) Focal-adhesion polarity.**
  Polarity = (PAX_whole − PAX_DAPI) / PAX_DAPI
  where PAX_whole = integrated paxillin intensity (RawIntDen) over the whole-cell ROI and PAX_DAPI = paxillin intensity in the nuclear (DAPI-overlapping) center. Meaning: peripheral (leading-edge) focal-adhesion enrichment relative to cell center → higher = more polarized traction.

**(Implicit, elastostatics — governing physics of the FEM).** Linear isotropic elasticity per body: σ = C(E,ν):ε with ∇·σ + f = 0 at quasi-static equilibrium; contractility imposed as a prescribed constant traction/stress P_Cell on condensate faces; Dirichlet fix on the stroma base. (Not written explicitly in the paper; stated here for completeness.)

**(Wavelength index).** wavelength index = (single-line fitted fold length) / (image width); folding line obtained per y-slice by MATLAB `findpeaks` on binarized fluorescence, connected along x. Meaning: larger index → higher-amplitude / longer folds.

## Force Laws & Potentials
No microscopic force law (no Bell/catch bond, no Hill force-velocity, no bending/stretching potential) is used — this is a continuum-elasticity + experimental paper. The only "force law" is the phenomenological imposition of a **constant contractile stress P_Cell (nominal 200 kPa in Fig 2F)** on condensate surfaces as a Neumann boundary condition, plus linear-elastic Hooke's-law restoring stresses in BM/stroma/cell parameterized purely by Young's moduli. FN-FRET provides a **qualitative** strain readout (FRET ratio I_a/I_d decreases with tensile strain / denaturation) rather than a calibrated force law.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** — **Partial / conceptual.** This is continuum FEM at tissue scale (multilayer elastic instability), not single-cell surface-mesh, so it is not a direct DCM parity target. BUT it supplies clean, well-posed **stiffness contrast → buckling/evagination phase behavior** and **cell-contractility-as-boundary-stress** intuition; the measured moduli (E_BM 756 kPa, E_Str 354 Pa, E_Cell 8.6 kPa, contractile stress 200 kPa) and the 1.91 µm deformation are quantitative reference points if DCM is ever extended to cell-in-ECM-sheet mechanics or aggregate-on-substrate folding.
- **FF?** — **Yes, indirect.** Strongly relevant to FF's traction/ECM story: polarized **F-actin-tip / pMLC2-inter-tip / paxillin-FA** zipper-like traction, blebbistatin-abolished contraction, **proteolytic-path migration**, and **collagen-fiber alignment → durotaxis / mechanical coupling** all map onto FF's clutch + fiber-remodeling + protrusion machinery. Useful as a mechanistic/phenomenological validation narrative (traction perpendicular to fold; alignment >90 %) rather than a parameter source.
