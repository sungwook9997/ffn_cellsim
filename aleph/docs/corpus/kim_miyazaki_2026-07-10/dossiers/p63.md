## Summary
This paper asks how macroscopic contractile tension in *disorganized* (non-sarcomeric) actomyosin bundles emerges from the microscopic properties of its three minimal components — semi-flexible actin filaments, passive actin cross-linking proteins (ACPs), and active myosin-II motors — when the bundle is clamped at both ends to an elastic substrate. Kim builds a 3D Brownian-dynamics agent-based model in which every actin filament is a chain of cylindrical segments (harmonic stretch + bend potentials, persistence length 9 µm), ACPs are two-armed catch/slip cross-linkers with Bell-type force-dependent unbinding (Ferrer 2008 single-molecule constants), and myosin motors are geometrically realistic bipolar thick filaments whose arms walk/unbind with force-dependent rates supplied by the Parallel Cluster Model (PCM, Erdmann–Schwarz), giving unloaded velocity ~550 nm/s and stall force ~4.5 pN per head-ensemble. Tension is read out as F(t)=E·A·ε from the substrate strain. Key findings: (1) motors alone *can* build 1–2 nN tension but only at very high motor density (R_M~0.8–1), inefficiently (χ≈0.005, only 0.5% of summed stall forces) and transiently (tension collapses within ~5 s as the network loses percolation by flowing to the clamps). (2) Adding a sufficient density of ACPs lets far fewer motors (R_M=0.025) generate the same ~2 nN, now sustained ~100 s (S≈1) and efficiently (χ≈0.15, ~40× the ACP-free lower limit). (3) ACPs act as a *molecular clutch* with tunable friction: F_max ~ R_M^0.6 with ACPs; sustainability is governed by the ratio R_M/R_ACP (force per ACP) and by ACP unbinding kinetics (k0_u,ACP, λ_u,ACP) — larger k0 or slip-bond compliance destabilizes via a "sorting" process that dissipates elastic energy, whereas ideal (λ=0) or catch (λ=−λ*) bonds sustain tension at much lower R_ACP. (4) Cross-linking *angle* matters: parallel ACPs give higher F_max and efficiency η≈0.61 (they don't compensate motor force) but low sustainability; anti-parallel ACPs extract near-stall motor force but compensate it (η≈0.15, F_max half of parallel), contradicting Oelz's prediction that parallel ACPs are indispensable. (5) Symmetry breaking (net tension not compression) arises from F-actin *buckling* between cross-link points (Euler beam, Eq. 7): a 100× stiffer κ_b,A (persistence length ~0.9 mm) suppresses buckling and builds compressive forces, lowering F_max. Longer filaments and stiffer substrate (biphasic, transition ~300 Pa) both raise tension.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** 3D agent-based Brownian dynamics of a semi-flexible actin bundle with explicit passive cross-linkers (ACPs) and explicit bipolar myosin-II thick-filament motors. Inertia neglected (over-damped Langevin).
- **Integrator:** Euler (forward) integration of the over-damped Langevin equation. **Time step Δt = 2.3×10⁻⁶ s.**
- **Governing dynamics:** F_i − ζ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_i^T = 0 (Eq. 1); position update r_i(t+Δt)=r_i(t)+(1/ζ_i)(F_i+F_i^T)Δt (Eq. 2). F_i^T is a thermal force satisfying the fluctuation–dissipation theorem.
- **Discretization / geometry of agents:**
  - *Actin filament:* series of cylindrical segments, segment length r0,A=140 nm, diameter rc,A=7 nm, connected by elastic hinges; fixed polarity (barbed + pointed ends). Binding spots every 7 nm along segments; each binding spot accepts up to 20 connections.
  - *ACP:* pair of arms, arm length r0,ACP=23.5 nm, joined by an elastic hinge; binds two filaments to form a functional cross-link.
  - *Motor (myosin-II thick filament):* relatively rigid bipolar backbone — a bare zone (r0,M1=42 nm) plus multiple segments (r0,M2=42 nm) connected by hinges, symmetric polarity about the bare zone. Each backbone endpoint carries two arms (each arm kinetically represents N_h myosin heads). Arm extension via a two-spring model: soft spring κ_s,M3 maintains r0,M3=13.5 nm between filament and backbone endpoint; stiff spring κ_s,M4 keeps a right angle (r0,M4=0 nm).
- **Domain / BCs:** Rectangular computational domain **5×5×20 µm**, periodic BC in all directions during assembly. 50 actin filaments of length 9 µm are placed aligned along z on a grid of x,y spacing 27 nm (bundle thickness ~135 nm). PBC then deactivated in z; filaments crossing the z boundary are severed → average filament length ⟨L_f⟩ ≈ 6.5 µm. The severed filament ends are **clamped to the z boundaries, which act as an elastic substrate of Young's modulus E = 5000 Pa** (reference). In the standard bundle, 11 barbed + 11 pointed ends are clamped to each z boundary. z-boundary area A = 25 µm².
- **Assembly protocol:** For t = −100 s to 0 s actin filaments held stationary while (a) motors self-assemble backbone segments and bind arms to actin (no walking), and (b) ACPs bind and form cross-links. At t = 0, filaments released and motor arms begin walking per PCM force–velocity law. Reference molar ratios: R_M = C_M/C_A = 0.025, R_ACP = C_ACP/C_A = 0.032.
- **Motor coarse-graining:** each arm represents N_h myosin heads; a motor with N_a arms behaves as a thick filament with N_a·N_h heads. Binding rate of an arm = 40·N_h s⁻¹ (single head binds at 40 s⁻¹ in PCM). Binding constraints: (i) backbone polarity similar to filament, (ii) backbone-endpoint-to-spot distance in [9.5, 17.5] nm, (iii) the partner arm on that endpoint not already on the same filament — so only ¼ of arms can bind one filament, giving N_head ≡ N_a·N_h/4. Reference: N_h=8, N_a=20 → N_head=40. Walking step ≈ 7 nm toward barbed end; motor slides off filament at the barbed end.
- **Walking/unbinding kinetics:** from the Parallel Cluster Model (Erdmann, Albert & Schwarz 2013; Erdmann & Schwarz 2012); myosin heads modeled as a **catch bond** (lower k_w and k_u at higher force — opposite to ACPs). Mechanochemistry rate k20 = 80 s⁻¹ → unloaded velocity ~550 nm/s and stall force f_M^stall ≈ 4.5 pN. Only forces on the stiff arm spring (F_s,M4=∇U_s,M4) affect k_w,M and k_u,M.
- **Measurements:** F(t)=E·A·ε(t) (substrate strain). F_max = peak tension; S = ⟨F⟩/F_max (sustainability; ⟨F⟩ averaged from F_max time to t=100 s). Microscopic forces: f_M = F_s,M4·û/N_h, f_ACP = F_s,ACP·û (û axial toward barbed end; positive = toward barbed end). f_M^max, f_ACP^max averaged over 1 s before F reaches F_max. χ = F_max/Σf_M^stall (fraction of total possible stall force). η = F_max/Σf_M^max (efficiency). Ensemble averages over 2–4 simulations per condition.
- **Additional computed forces (in Online Resource):** effective drag coefficient ζ_i, thermal forces F_i^T, and repulsive (excluded-volume) forces between pairs of actin segments.

## Governing Equations
1. **Over-damped Langevin (Eq. 1):** `F_i − ζ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_i^T = 0` — force balance on agent i: deterministic + drag + thermal = 0 (inertia neglected).
2. **Euler update (Eq. 2):** `r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (dr_i/dt)Δt = r_i(t) + (1/ζ_i)(F_i + F_i^T)Δt` — explicit position integration, Δt=2.3×10⁻⁶ s.
3. **Harmonic extension / bending potentials (Eq. 3):** `U_s = ½ κ_s (r − r0)²`, `U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ0)²` — linear-spring stretch and angular-spring bend for actin, ACP, and motor elements.
4. **Binding-spot interpolation (Eq. 4):** `r = [y·r_P + (r_A − y)·r_B] / r_A` — position of a binding spot located distance y (from barbed end) along a segment of instantaneous length r_A, from barbed (r_B) and pointed (r_P) endpoints.
5. **Force redistribution to endpoints (Eq. 5):** `F_i^B = F_i (r_A − y)/r_A`, `F_i^P = F_i (y/r_A)` — a force F_i on a binding spot is split (lever rule) onto barbed (B) and pointed (P) endpoints.
6. **ACP force-dependent unbinding, Bell-type slip bond (Eq. 6):**
   `k_u,ACP = k0_u,ACP · exp(λ_u,ACP |F_s,ACP| / k_B T)` if r ≥ r0,ACP (arm stretched);
   `k_u,ACP = k0_u,ACP` if r < r0,ACP (arm compressed) — force-accelerated off-rate; F_s,ACP=∇U_s,ACP. With λ_u,ACP=0 → ideal bond; λ_u,ACP=−λ*_u,ACP → catch bond.
7. **Euler buckling force of a filament segment (Eq. 7):** `F_seg^buckle ≈ π² κ_b,A r0,A / L_seg²` — critical compressive load for buckling a filament segment of length L_seg between cross-link points; origin of tensile/compressive symmetry breaking.

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Actin stretch:** harmonic, κ_s,A (chosen from experiment; sets extensional modulus). Bending: harmonic, κ_b,A chosen so persistence length L_p = 9 µm (in presence of thermal forces).
- **ACP stretch/bend:** harmonic κ_s,ACP, κ_b,ACP set to "reasonable values" from prior work (Kim 2009, 2014) due to lack of data.
- **Motor backbone:** extensional κ_s,M1 = κ_s,M2 = κ_s,A; bending κ_b,M ≫ κ_b,A (rigid backbone).
- **Motor arm (two-spring):** soft spring κ_s,M3 (holds r0,M3=13.5 nm filament–backbone gap); stiff spring κ_s,M4 (holds r0,M4=0 nm right angle). Only κ_s,M4 force feeds the kinetics.
- **ACP off-rate:** Bell/slip law Eq. 6, reference k0*_u,ACP=0.115 s⁻¹, λ*_u,ACP=1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m (Ferrer 2008).
- **Motor kinetics:** PCM force-dependent k_w,M, k_u,M (catch-bond character); arm binding rate 40·N_h s⁻¹; stall ~4.5 pN, unloaded v ~550 nm/s at k20=80 s⁻¹.
- **Excluded volume:** repulsive forces between actin segment pairs (form in Online Resource).
- **Substrate:** linear-elastic, tension F=E·A·ε, E=5000 Pa reference (swept; biphasic transition ~300 Pa).

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Indirect. This is a fine-grained filament-level model (not a surface-mesh continuum), so it does not map onto DCM's cortex-tension term directly, but its bundle-tension outputs (1–2 nN, χ, sustainability) and the E-dependence (rigidity sensing, ~300 Pa transition) are useful references for the cortical actomyosin tension DCM lumps into a surface γ.
- **FF?** YES — strongly. This is essentially an FF-class engine (Cytosim-style: every actin filament a bending beam with harmonic stretch+bend, explicit ACP cross-linkers with Bell force-dependent off-rate Eq. 6, myosin minifilaments with force-velocity/stall from PCM, excluded volume). The exact mechanistic ingredients FF implements.
