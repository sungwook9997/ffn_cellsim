## Summary
The paper asks what physical determinants set the actomyosin-based migration speed of a cell in confinement, stripped of biochemical signaling and transmembrane adhesion. The authors build a minimal in-vitro migratory-cell model: Xenopus laevis egg extract (physiological actin + myosin, Arp2/3-nucleated F-actin) encapsulated in cell-sized water-in-oil droplets bounded by a lipid monolayer, confined between PDMS-coated glass. Adding PIP2 to the lipid recruits/binds F-actin to the membrane interface. Without actin–membrane binding the actomyosin gel contracts to the droplet center (symmetric, no motion); WITH binding the network detaches from the interface, polarizes toward the periphery (asymmetry parameter Φasym rising 0.15→0.50), sustains a rear-directed actin flow, and the droplet spontaneously migrates with dynamic shape deformation. Force-transmission microscopy (tracer silica beads) shows the contractile force of the actin flow is transmitted through the membrane to the substrate only when actin–membrane binding is present; the total transmitted force scales with contact area (F ∝ πR_c²), while the local transmitted stress per area σ_act = α·v_act is geometry-independent and set by actin-flow speed. Magnetic-bead experiments prove polarization alone is insufficient — actin–membrane binding is required for propulsion. Migration is ~4× faster under 2D confinement than unconfined and becomes ballistic (directed) rather than diffusive. Using active-gel theory, migration speed emerges from a force balance between the sliding friction force (reaction of the contractile force, ∝ contact area ∝ h⁻¹) and the confinement-induced fluid drag (∝ h⁻²), yielding a single closed-form V_drop expression with one fitted friction coefficient α ≈ 1.7×10² Pa·s·m⁻¹. This predicts the observed non-monotonic height dependence (optimal chamber height ~60 µm), the droplet-size and viscosity dependence, and a distinct 1D-microchannel regime where elongated shape boosts friction (α ≈ 3.6×10² Pa·s·m⁻¹). The geometric trade-off between contractile-force transmission and viscous drag thus sets actomyosin motility in confinement.

## Model & Methods
**Nature:** Primarily an EXPERIMENTAL in-vitro reconstitution study, paired with a phenomenological/analytical ACTIVE-GEL continuum scaling theory (no particle simulation). No Brownian dynamics, no FEM, no agent model.

**Experimental system:**
- Actomyosin: Xenopus laevis unfertilized-egg extracts (actin-intact, physiological actin+myosin), energy mix (creatine phosphate/ATP/MgCl₂). F-actin nucleated by Arp2/3 (WASP-family activation), visualized with TMR-Lifeact / GFP-Lifeact / Alexa Fluor 546 actin.
- Compartment: water-in-oil droplets, lipid MONOLAYER membrane. Lipid = 1 mM egg-PC in mineral oil (control) or +14% mole-fraction PIP2 for actin–membrane binding. Alternative binders: 7.6% DGS-NTA lipid + 200 nM His-α-actinin, or 10% NTA-lipid + 10 nM His-VVCA.
- Confinement: droplets between PDMS-coated glass slide + coverslip; chamber height h set by spacer (h = 30, 60, 100, 300 µm). Droplet diameter D = 80–650 µm. 1D confinement: annulus PDMS microchannels (widths w = 100, 150 µm; h = 60 µm) fabricated by SU-8 photolithography.
- Microtubules suppressed with 100 µM nocodazole.

**Measurement protocols:**
- Migration: bright-field centroid tracking (MATLAB), V_drop = straight displacement/duration (10 min for 2D, 5 min for 1D). R and D=2R from binarized droplet area S via R=√(S/π). Aspect ratio h/D capped at 1 when h>D.
- Actin-flow speed v_act: kymographs of fluorescence.
- Force-transmission microscopy (after Abercrombie 1970): 3-µm fluorescent silica beads carpet on bottom substrate; bead displacement tracked with Fiji TrackMate.
- Magnetic manipulation: 1-µm streptavidin magnetic beads entangled in the gel, positioned by an external neodymium magnet to impose/remove polarization.
- FRAP: 532-nm laser bleach, ~20 µm Gaussian radius, near membrane, GFP-Lifeact + Alexa-546 G-actin, image every 1 s for 3 min → G-actin turnover / network localization.
- Contact area: confocal (CSU-X1 + EM-CCD, 488 nm).
- Oil viscosity by Ostwald viscometer at 20 °C. Temperature held 20 ± 1 °C.
- Statistics: Mann–Whitney u test.

**Theory (active gel):** Droplet approximated as circular (major/minor axis ratio ≈0.87). Actin flow generates interfacial shear stress σ_act = α·v_act. Force balance across the membrane sets sliding friction force (propulsion) against fluid drag (Stokes dissipation across both radius and height). Closed-form V_drop derived (Eq. 2). No timestep/integrator — analytical.

## Governing Equations

**(Eq. 1) — Force balance (steady migration):**
```
F_fric^contact + F_drag = 0
```
The propulsive sliding friction force (reaction of the transmitted contractile force) balances the viscous drag from the surrounding oil; this determines the steady migration speed.

**(Eq. σ) — Local interfacial shear stress / friction constitutive law:**
```
σ_act = α · v_act
```
The locally transmitted contractile stress per unit contact area equals a geometry-independent friction coefficient α times the actin-flow speed v_act. (Established empirically: bead speed ∝ actin-flow speed, independent of droplet size.)

**(Contact-force area law):**
```
F_act^contact ∝ π R_c²      and      F_fric^contact ∝ π R_c²
```
Total transmitted contractile force (and its reaction sliding-friction force) scale with the droplet–substrate contact area.

**(Total sliding friction force by integration over contact area):**
```
F_fric^contact = -2 ∫ σ_act dS ≈ -2 (α v_act)(π R_c²)
```
Factor 2 from summing over the contact; acts opposite to the actin flow.

**(Contact radius, geometry):**
```
R_c ≡ R - (h/2) f(θ)
f(θ) = -( |tan θ| + 1/cos θ )
θ = 154.7°   (contact angle at droplet–substrate interface)
```
Contact radius shrinks from droplet radius R by a height- and contact-angle-dependent term.

**(Fluid drag, Stokes dissipation across radius and height):**
```
F_drag ≈ η_oil ∇²v_oil (π R² h)
        ~ η_oil [ (D/2)^-2 + (h/2)^-2 ] V_drop [ π (D/2)² h ]
```
Velocity gradients in the surrounding fluid across both the droplet radius and the chamber height contribute to viscous dissipation (after Eri & Okumura 2011).

**(Eq. 2) — Migration speed (2D confinement, master result):**
```
V_drop = (α v_act) / (2 η_oil) · h · [ 1 + (h/D)² ]^{-1} · [ 1 - (h/D) f(θ) ]²
```
Closed form for droplet migration speed under 2D confinement; single free parameter α. Captures increase with droplet size, decrease under strong confinement (small h), and the non-monotonic optimum.

**(Height scalings, competing forces):**
```
F_drag ∝ h^-2        (drag grows fast as chamber thins)
F_fric^contact ∝ π R_c² ∝ h^-1   (friction grows slower, volume πR²h = const.)
```
Because drag rises faster than friction as h→0, migration slows at strong confinement; as h~D both forces fall → also slows; hence an optimum at intermediate h.

**(Geometric-scaling collapse variable, 2D — Fig. 4D x-axis / SI Eq.):**
```
2 η_oil V_drop / (v_act D)  plotted vs  (h/D)[1-(h/D)f(θ)]²[1+(h/D)²]^{-1}   → slope = α
```

**(1D microchannel scaling collapse — Fig. 5F / SI Appendix section 3, SI Eq. 5):**
```
8 η_oil V_drop / (v_act L)  vs  (w/L)[1-(w/L)f(θ)]   → slope = α  (1D)
```
L = droplet major-axis length, w = channel width.

## Force Laws & Potentials
The model is continuum/phenomenological — there are no molecular potentials (no bending/stretching bonds, no Bell-Evans off-rates, no Hill force-velocity). The only "force laws" are:
- **Active interfacial friction (propulsion):** σ_act = α v_act → F_fric^contact = -2(α v_act)(π R_c²), a viscous-friction (linear in flow speed) contact stress transmitted through the actin–membrane bond.
- **Viscous (Stokes) drag:** F_drag ~ η_oil [(D/2)⁻² + (h/2)⁻²] V_drop [π(D/2)² h], Newtonian fluid drag from confinement.
- **Lateral friction neglected:** F_fric^lateral ≈ 0 because η_oil/η_act ≪ 1 (surrounding oil far less viscous than the actomyosin gel).
- Contractility source: myosin-II motors + Arp2/3-polymerized F-actin generate the contractile stress driving the actin flow (mechanism molecular but not parameterized here).
