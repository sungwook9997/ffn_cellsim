## Summary
The authors ask how collective patterns of gliding actin filaments emerge in the myosin **motility assay**, and specifically whether treating motors *explicitly* (as fixed, kinetically resolved binders/walkers) rather than *implicitly* (a constant propulsion force) changes the outcome. They use a well-established **agent-based Brownian-dynamics** model (Langevin equation, Euler integration) in a thin quasi-2D periodic domain (10×10×0.1 µm³, z-motion frozen), with filaments as serially connected cylindrical segments (extensional + bending + repulsive forces) and motors as single surface-anchored cylindrical segments that bind, walk toward the barbed end (force-velocity relationship), and unbind force-dependently. Motor walking/unbinding kinetics are governed by the **parallel cluster model** (Erdmann & Schwarz), with one motor "arm" representing 8 myosin heads (effective myosin 96 µM from CM = 12 µM). Sweeping four control parameters — repulsive strength κr, bending stiffness κb, filament concentration C (15–120 µM), and average filament length ⟨L⟩ (0.5–4 µm) — in pairwise 2-D parameter maps, they observe four emergent structure classes: **homogeneous networks, flocks, bands, and rings**. Transitions run network→flock→band→ring as κr increases or κb decreases; rings always coexist with bands; ring *size* is set mainly by rigidity and repulsion (not concentration/length); longer filaments and higher concentration promote rings/bands. They quantify velocity correlation, curl (rotational/ring metric), filament curvature, structure stability, and morphology homogeneity Q. Explicit motors (unlike implicit ones) reproduce ring formation and a broader speed distribution, showing implicit-motor models can misrepresent propulsion magnitude/direction.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** Agent-based / coarse-grained Brownian-dynamics simulation of the motility assay, built on the Langevin equation. Well-established Kim-lab framework (Bidone et al. 2017; Jung et al. 2015; Kim 2015; Li et al. 2017). Explicit, spatially fixed molecular motors.
- **Filaments:** serially connected cylindrical segments with polarity (pointed end ↔ barbed end). Deterministic forces = extensional + bending + repulsive (between overlapping filaments).
- **Motors:** single cylindrical segment; one end fixed on surface at z = 0; other end binds to discrete binding sites on filaments and walks toward the barbed end. Unbinds (i) when it walks past the barbed end, or (ii) from anywhere force-dependently. Walking & unbinding rates from the **parallel cluster model** (Erdmann et al. 2013; Erdmann & Schwarz 2012). One motor arm ≡ 8 myosin heads (stall force & kinetics).
- **Integrator:** endpoint velocities from Langevin equation each step; positions updated via **Euler integration**.
- **Timestep:** Δt = 1.15 × 10⁻⁵ s (chosen small enough that repulsion engages as soon as filaments overlap).
- **Domain / BCs:** thin 3-D box 10 × 10 × 0.1 µm³; periodic in x and y; motors randomly placed in x–y plane at z = 0; filament z-displacement inhibited (all filaments stay coplanar) → stronger volume-exclusion at collisions than real experiments (where filaments can cross over).
- **Filament assembly:** at t = 0, stochastic nucleation + polymerization assemble filaments; no depolymerization/severing → filament length is normally distributed and fixed during a run. Length tuned via nucleation rate (polymerization rate fixed); higher nucleation → more, shorter filaments. Reference ⟨L⟩ = 2.5 µm.
- **Concentrations:** actin C = 15–120 µM (reference 60 µM implied); motor CM = 12 µM fixed (optimized via Figure S3); effective myosin = 96 µM (= 8 × 12).
- **Run length:** 100 s per simulation. Only ONE run per condition (high cost of explicit motors); authors state cross-run stochastic variation is not significant.
- **Parallelization:** MPI with spatial domain decomposition.
- **Analysis protocols:**
  - Endpoint velocity vi(t) sampled every 2 s (reduces thermal-noise influence). Average speed = ⟨|vi|⟩ over all endpoints/times.
  - Velocity correlation (Eq 1): only endpoint pairs < 0.5 µm apart, over last 20 s; averaged 50–100 s in figures.
  - Ring counting: manual via ImageJ (circle-shaped closed loops; raindrop shapes excluded); ring size = longest chord across circumference (Figure S5).
  - Curl of velocity field: domain gridded NG × NG; NG = 50 for C > 15 µM, NG = 40 for C ≤ 15 µM; grid-averaged velocities; curl computed every 2 s over last 50 s; |curl| averaged over time & grid → rotational/ring metric.
  - Persistence length lp: Eq 2 (from κb) or Eq 3 (tangent-vector correlation fit to exp(−Δs/lp)).
  - Morphology homogeneity Q: Eq 4 with NG = 30, using data after morphology-correlation ≥ 0.8.
- **Explicit vs implicit control:** implicit-motor runs apply a constant propulsion force tuned to match explicit-motor average gliding speed (repulsion off for that calibration, Figure S6).

## Governing Equations
1. **Velocity correlation of neighboring filament endpoints** (Eq 1):
   ⟨|cos α(r,t)|⟩ = ⟨ (vi(t)·vj(t)) / (|vi(t)| |vj(t)|) ⟩
   — mean absolute cosine of the angle between velocity vectors of endpoint pairs within 0.5 µm; measures alignment / collective motion (indices i,j are two endpoints, α the angle between their velocities).
2. **Persistence length from bending stiffness** (Eq 2):
   lp = κb,A · r0,A / (kB T)
   — persistence length = (segment bending stiffness × segment equilibrium length) / thermal energy; note κb has units of N·m in this model.
3. **Tangent-vector (contour) correlation** (Eq 3):
   ⟨cos θ(Δs)⟩ = ⟨ u(s) · u(s+Δs) ⟩
   — autocorrelation of unit tangent vectors u along the filament contour coordinate s; fit to exp(−Δs/lp) gives lp; lower values ⇒ higher curvature.
4. **Network morphology homogeneity** (Eq 4):
   Q = (1 / 2NG) [ Σ_{i=1..NG} std(ρi,j | j=1..NG) + Σ_{j=1..NG} std(ρi,j | i=1..NG) ]
   — mean of row-wise and column-wise standard deviations of the endpoint-count grid ρi,j; higher Q ⇒ more heterogeneous morphology; NG = 30, computed at steady state (morphology-correlation ≥ 0.8).

Note: the underlying Langevin equation of motion, the extensional (stretching) potential, the bending potential, the repulsive/volume-exclusion potential, the motor force–velocity law, and the parallel-cluster-model binding/walking/unbinding rate expressions are described qualitatively in the main text but their explicit functional forms live in the Supporting Information (not in this file).

## Force Laws & Potentials
Deterministic forces in the Langevin equation (functional forms deferred to SI):
- **Extensional (stretching) force** — restoring force keeping filament segment length near equilibrium r0,A. (Harmonic in the Kim-lab framework; constant in SI.)
- **Bending force** — filament semiflexibility; parameterized by bending stiffness κb (units N·m here) tied to persistence length via Eq 2. Swept as ratio κb/κb* (reference κb*).
- **Repulsive / volume-exclusion force** between overlapping filaments — parameterized by repulsive strength κr; swept as ratio κr/κr* (reference κr*). Reference-plus value κr,A/κr,A* = 3.2 used in some length sweeps (Figs 5, 6).
- **Motor force–velocity relationship** — walking velocity of a bound motor decreases with opposing load up to stall; governs gliding speed. Explicit, from parallel cluster model.
- **Motor binding / unbinding kinetics** — stochastic binding when motor and filament binding site are proximal; unbinding both processively (past barbed end) and force-dependently (anywhere); rates from parallel cluster model (Erdmann & Schwarz).
- **Thermal (stochastic) force** — Langevin white noise at temperature T (kB T thermal energy).
- **Motor "arm" scaling:** one motor arm ≡ 8 myosin heads for stall force and kinetics (effective myosin 96 µM).
