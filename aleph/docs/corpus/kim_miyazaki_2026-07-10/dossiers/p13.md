## Summary
The paper asks how breast cancer (BC) cells autonomously produce the TACS-3 signature — radial alignment of type-1 collagen (col1) fibers at the tumor-stroma interface — that guides collective invasion, without invoking cancer-associated fibroblasts. Using alginate–col1 interpenetrating-network hydrogels (acIPNs) engineered to independently tune stiffness (0.6/1.2/3 kPa) and mechanical plasticity (low LP vs high HP, 15% vs 32% permanent deformation), they encapsulate MCF10AT (and 4T1, MDA-MB-231, MCF10A-acini) spheroids. Findings: (i) sufficient matrix mechanical *plasticity* is required for invasion, independent of stiffness; increased stiffness shifts single-cell → collective invasion; (ii) cells radially align col1 by day 3 *before* invading, in an E-cadherin/p120-catenin–dependent manner; (iii) surprisingly, cells at the interface move *tangentially* ("swirling," non-coherent rotation), perpendicular to the alignment direction; (iv) the resolution is **negative normal stress** — a property of fibrous (col1/fibrin) networks whereby applied *shear* generates *contractile* (negative) normal stress of comparable magnitude — so tangential/shear cell forces produce distal *radial contractile* stresses that align col1 radially. This is confirmed by shear rheology (HP acIPNs and IBC tissue show negative normal stress ≈ shear stress; healthy tissue shows positive) and by a **Taeyoon-Kim agent-based Brownian-dynamics fiber-network simulation** (annular geometry, dipole-like shear forces at inner boundary → radial contractile deformations with smaller power-law decay exponent than tangential, → radial fiber alignment). Swirling and alignment require Ecad/p120 (cell-cell adhesion), actomyosin contractility (blebbistatin/ROCK-Y27), and β1-integrin/FAK (cell-ECM adhesion). Finally the basement membrane (laminin-332) acts as a **mechanical insulator**: intact BM (mammary MCF10A acini; human DCIS) shields col1 from swirling forces; once breached (IBC), swirling aligns col1 and collective invasion follows.

## Model & Methods

### Computational fiber model (Taeyoon Kim lab — the mechanistic gold)
- **Model type:** agent-based **Brownian dynamics** (Langevin, inertia neglected / overdamped) of a discretized semiflexible fiber network with force-dependent crosslinkers. Coarse-grained bead–rod (cylindrical-element) representation. This is the same class as Kim-lab actin-network BD codes, here re-purposed for a collagen ECM.
- **Geometry:** 3D matrix confined between **two concentric cylindrical boundaries** (annulus) — outer radius **200 µm**, inner radius **50 µm**, height **1 µm** (quasi-2D slab mimicking the equatorial plane of a spheroid + surrounding ECM). Two z-boundaries confine the slab.
- **Assembly:** matrix self-assembles by nucleation + polymerization of fibers and binding of crosslinkers to fiber pairs. Fiber endpoints within 1 µm of the two cylindrical boundaries bind irreversibly to the boundary.
- **Loading protocol:** after assembly, outer-boundary-bound endpoints stay ~stationary; inner-boundary-bound endpoints **rotate** (clockwise/counterclockwise) to apply **shear**. Three rotation modes: (1) **dipole** — direction set by circumferential angular position of the binding point (clockwise if `(initial angle from +x, CCW) mod 60° < 30°`, else counterclockwise) → this reproduces experiment; (2) **stochastic** — CW/CCW at equal probability; (3) **uniform** — always CCW. Binding point rotated by **0.001° per timestep** until fibers feel tensile force > **100 pN**. Only the dipole mode reproduced the measured tangential+radial deformation profile (stochastic/uniform did not).
- **Integrator:** explicit **Euler** (Eq. 6), timestep **Δt = 4.0×10⁻⁴ s**.
- **Thermal noise:** fluctuation-dissipation (Eq. 4), Kronecker-delta form; k_BT = 4.142×10⁻²¹ J.
- **Also used** as a plain shear-rheology test (parallel-plate shear at 20% strain amplitude) to confirm negative normal stress in the network (Fig. 4g–i).

### Experimental methods (Chaudhuri lab)
- **acIPNs:** alginate (HMW 280 kDa Protanal LF 20/40, or LMW Pronova VLVG) conjugated with GGGGRGDSP (RGD; ~20 peptides/chain, 1500 µM in 2% w/v), ionically crosslinked with CaSO₄; final **4.8 mg/mL alginate + 1.6 mg/mL col1** (rat-tail col1, Corning). Stiffness set by alginate MW; plasticity by Ca²⁺; see Supplementary Table 1.
- **Rheology:** DHR2 / AR2000EX stress-controlled rheometers; storage/loss moduli at 1 rad/s, 1% strain; Young's modulus from Eq. 1 (ν=0.5). Normal stress from logarithmic strain-amplitude sweep (0.1–100%), read at **20% strain amplitude**.
- **Plasticity:** two-cycle spherical indentation (1.5 mm indenter, 8 µm/s, up to 1 mm depth, cycles 1 h apart); plasticity = permanent-deformation depth at 25% peak force / total depth.
- **Imaging/quantification:** Leica SP8 / Nikon Ti2-E confocal (col1 by 488 nm confocal reflectance; H2B-GFP/RFP/mCherry nuclei). Cell velocities by **FIDIC** (fast iterative digital image correlation) between frames 15/30 min apart; radial/tangential decomposition in polar coords about spheroid center; velocities averaged within one cell length (~20 µm) of the boundary. ECM deformations by 0.5 µm fluorescent beads (4% v/v) + FIDIC. Col1 fibers segmented with **CTFire/ctFIRE**; radial alignment index by Eq. 2.
- **Cell lines / perturbations:** MCF10AT (main), MCF10A acini, 4T1 (intravital orthotopic mouse), MDA-MB-231 (Ecad−/Ecad+). shRNA KD of Ecad (CDH1) and p120 (CTNND1). Inhibitors: blebbistatin 25 µM (myosin II), Y-27632 25 µM (ROCK), FAK inhibitor 10 µM, β1-integrin blocking Ab P5D2 1 µg/mL, GM6001 25 µM + marimastat 100 µM (MMP).

## Governing Equations

**(1) Young's modulus from shear moduli:**
E = 2(1 + ν)·√((G′)² + (G″)²)
— converts storage G′ and loss G″ moduli (ν = 0.5) to Young's modulus E.

**(3) Overdamped Langevin (Brownian dynamics), inertia neglected:**
F_i − ζ_i·(dr_i/dt) + F_iᵀ = 0
— force balance on endpoint i: deterministic force F_i, drag −ζ_i·v_i, stochastic (thermal) force F_iᵀ. r_i = position of the i-th cylindrical-element endpoint.

**(4) Fluctuation-dissipation (thermal force statistics):**
⟨F_iᵀ(t)·F_iᵀ(t)⟩ = (2·k_B·T·ζ_i·δ_ij / Δt)·δ
— white-noise variance set by drag ζ_i and temperature; δ_ij Kronecker delta, δ second-order tensor.

**(5) Drag coefficient of a cylindrical element:**
ζ_i = 3·π·μ·r_c,i·(3 + 2·r_0,i / r_c,i) / 5
— approximate drag for a cylinder of length r_0,i and diameter r_c,i in medium of viscosity μ (Clift, Bubbles Drops & Particles 1978).

**(6) Euler integration of positions:**
r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (dr_i/dt)·Δt = r_i(t) + (1/ζ_i)·(F_i + F_iᵀ)·Δt
— explicit position update from Eq. 3.

**(7) Extensional (stretching) harmonic potential:**
U_s = ½·κ_s·(r − r_0)²
— maintains equilibrium length r_0 of fiber segments and crosslinker arms.

**(8) Bending harmonic potential:**
U_b = ½·κ_b·(θ − θ_0)²
— maintains equilibrium angle θ_0 between adjacent fiber elements (θ_0,f = 0 → straight fiber).

**(9) Bell's-law force-dependent crosslinker unbinding:**
k_−,xl = k⁰_−,xl·exp(λ_−,xl·|F⃗_s,xl| / k_B·T)   if r ≥ r_0,xl (arm under **tension**)
k_−,xl = k⁰_−,xl                                   if r < r_0,xl (compression → base rate)
— slip-bond off-rate; unbinding accelerates only under tensile spring force F_s,xl. λ_−,xl = force sensitivity (bond length scale).

**(2) Radial alignment index (image analysis, not a force law):**
radial alignment index = |cos(θ_f − θ_n)|
— θ_f = fiber orientation angle; θ_n = angle of nearest spheroid-boundary normal. 1 = radial (aligned to normal), 0 = tangential.

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Stretching:** linear-spring (Hookean) harmonic, U_s = ½κ_s(r−r_0)² → force = −κ_s(r−r_0). Applied to fiber segments (κ_s,f) and crosslinker arms (κ_s,xl) and boundary-binding springs (κ_s,bnd).
- **Bending:** angular harmonic, U_b = ½κ_b(θ−θ_0)² → torque restoring θ→θ_0. Fibers only (κ_b,f); θ_0,f = 0 (intrinsically straight fiber).
- **Crosslinker binding:** rate k_+,xl per available site (sites every 100 nm along fibers); unbinding by Bell's-law slip bond (Eq. 9). Bound-crosslinker forces distributed onto the two fiber endpoints spanning the attachment.
- **Boundary binding:** fiber endpoints within 1 µm of a cylindrical boundary bind at rate k_+,bnd, then tethered by spring κ_s,bnd.
- **Boundary excluded-volume / repulsion:** cylindrical + z boundaries exert normal repulsive force on elements that cross, magnitude ∝ overlap distance, strength κ_r,bnd (keeps matrix in the 3D annular region).
- **Negative normal stress (emergent, not a coded law):** the physical mechanism — semiflexible fibers stretch under shear and pull inward, producing contractile normal stress of magnitude comparable to shear stress (Janmey 2007, ref 24). Emerges from the network + the above potentials, not imposed.

## Figures
- **Fig. 1** — acIPN design & invasion phenotypes. IBC tissue stiffer (~4.7 vs 1.4 kPa) and more plastic (32 vs 15%). acIPNs tune stiffness (0.6/1.2/3 kPa) × plasticity (LP/HP) independently at fixed 1.5 mg/mL col1. HP required for invasion; low stiffness → single-cell, high stiffness → collective strands. Ecad/p120 KD and MDA-MB Ecad+/− show cell-cell adhesion essential for collective invasion.
- **Fig. 2** — col1 radially aligns by day 3 *before* invasion in HP acIPNs; radial alignment index rises day1→3, plateaus day3→5; abolished in LP and in Ecad/p120 KD. Human histology: IBC shows higher radial alignment (TACS-3) than DCIS. Panel e defines the radial alignment index schematic + Eq. 2.
- **Fig. 3** — swirling. Nuclei tracking + FIDIC velocity fields show dominant *tangential* (not radial) motion at the interface on days 1/3/5. Ecad/p120 KD cells are stuck (no tangential motion). Intravital 4T1: control swirls, Ecad KD does not.
- **Fig. 4** — negative normal stress mechanism. Schematic (a–b): shear from tangential cells → contractile radial stress via negative normal stress → radial col1 alignment. Rheology: HP acIPN (c–d) and IBC tissue (e–f) show negative normal stress ≈ shear stress. Fiber sim (g–i) confirms; ~50% fibers stretch. Experimental deformation fields (j–m): tangential localized (dipole-like alternating sign, exponent 2.2-ish), radial contractile propagates far (smaller exponent). Annular sim (n–r) with dipole shear forces reproduces radial exponent 1.2 vs tangential 2.2 and radial alignment.
- **Fig. 5** — necessity tests. col1 required (alginate-only: radial decays as fast as tangential). Ecad/p120 KD: no deformation. Myosin/ROCK (blebbistatin/Y27): loss of swirling + alignment. β1-integrin block: swirling persists but alignment ↓. FAK inhibition: no swirling, no alignment.
- **Fig. 6** — basement membrane as mechanical insulator. MCF10A acini secrete laminin-332 BM; intact BM (day1) → tangential motion but NO col1 remodeling; breached BM (day5+) → radial deformation, col1 alignment, invasion by day7. Human tumors without intact laminin: lower circularity, higher radial col1 alignment. Schematic v: no-breach shielded → partial breach local alignment → full breach full radial alignment.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM? — Partial.** DCM models cells as surface-mesh continua in aggregates (~N=400) with cell-cell junctions; this paper's *cell-level* biology (Ecad/p120-dependent swirling, collective invasion, spheroid-ECM interface stress) is conceptually adjacent but DCM does not currently model the surrounding fibrous ECM or negative normal stress. The BM-as-mechanical-insulator and swirling/rotational collective motion could motivate a DCM aggregate-on-fiber-ECM extension, but no direct DCM parameter/gate maps here.
- **FF? — YES (strong).** The fiber-network model IS a Cytosim-class fine-grained engine directly parallel to FF: cylindrical (bead-rod) fiber elements with harmonic stretch (κ_s,f=4×10⁻³ N/m) + harmonic bend (κ_b,f=8.27×10⁻²⁰ N·m), Bell's-law slip-bond crosslinkers (k⁰₋,xl=10⁻⁶ s⁻¹, λ=0.4 nm), overdamped Langevin/Brownian dynamics with fluctuation-dissipation, cylinder drag (Eq. 5), Euler integrator Δt=4×10⁻⁴ s. This is a ready-made **cross-check / oracle** for FF's collagen-ECM (Mikado network) side and for FF's Bell-Evans off-rate + bending-beam mechanics.
