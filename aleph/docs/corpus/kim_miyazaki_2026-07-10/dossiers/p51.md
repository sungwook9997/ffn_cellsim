## Summary
The paper asks whether **durotaxis** (cell migration biased toward stiffer substrate) can emerge from **purely mechanical** cell–substrate interaction, **without any cell decision-making** (no stiffness-sensing rule that changes cell properties). The authors build a 2D overdamped biomechanical model: a cell is reduced to two connected "cell-points" (front F and rear R), each with a partial-donut adhesion region; the substrate is an irregular triangulated spring network (equilateral triangular lattice with a fraction *p* of chains randomly removed to mimic ECM polymer connectivity). Each cell-point generates a **constant torque** (τ_F ≫ τ_R) that is balanced by **non-centripetal (tangential) contractile forces** exerted on focal-adhesion (FA) points; force magnitudes are solved, not prescribed, via a kinematic constraint that couples cell-point linear+angular velocity to FA-point velocity. Lamellipodia form dynamically (6 angular sectors of the front region, one active at a time for T = 6 min), giving a persistent random walk (PRW) on a non-deformable substrate. Assembling all force/torque balances plus the kinematic constraints yields a linear system **Mu = f** solved each timestep. On a substrate with a sharp stiffness step (100:1) or a graded stiffness, the cell migrates toward the stiffer/elasticity-dominated region **purely mechanically**. Key result: durotactic efficiency (durotactic index DI) is **biphasic in the soft-region extensional stiffness κ^L_s,M** and **decreases with substrate viscosity ξ**; best durotaxis needs **elasticity-dominated stiff region + viscosity-dominated soft region**. A DI transition to high values occurs when the stiffer region's stiffness κ^H_s,M reaches ~0.15 nN/µm (elasticity overtakes viscosity). Average migration **speed** rises with both κ and ξ (short-timescale resistance) and depends only on local stiffness, not orientation — so speed is explicitly *not* the durotaxis mechanism. Under a gradient, above a critical gradient all cells initially pointed toward soft reorient toward stiff; distance-before-reorientation is inversely proportional to gradient.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** 2D coarse-grained agent-based biomechanical model; **overdamped (inertia-free) quasi-static** force/torque balance solved as a linear system each timestep (not Brownian/Langevin — no explicit thermal noise term; stochasticity enters only via random lamellipodium selection and the random ECM chain removal). No explicit cytoskeleton (actin/MT) — their effects are lumped into polarity, lamellipodia, and contractile forces.
- **Cell representation:** two points (front F, rear R) joined by a spring; each with a partial-donut adhesion region (outer R_out, inner R_in, angular span θ = 180°). Front region larger than rear (R_F,out > R_R,out; R_F,in > R_R,in) → polarity vector R→F. Front region split into 6 angular sectors (potential lamellipodia); one active at a time for lifetime T = 6 min, then a new one randomly activated; rear region always fully active.
- **Substrate representation:** points initially on an equilateral triangular lattice, connected by up to 6 chains each; a random fraction *p* of chains removed (each point ends with 0–6 chains). Chains carry linear (stretch) springs + angular (bending) springs. Connectivity fixed in time. Number density *n* (points per unit area) tunes fiber density.
- **Cell–substrate coupling:** substrate points partitioned to nearest cell-point within its outer radius; those inside the *active* adhesion region instantly become FA points. Cell-point exerts a **tangential (non-centripetal)** contractile force on each FA point, magnitude solved from constant-torque balance + kinematic constraint (mimics actin retrograde flow pulling the FA).
- **Numerical method:** eqs (1)[cell-point force balance], (7)[cell-point torque balance], (8)[substrate-point force balance], (12)[kinematic constraint] together form a linear system **Mu = f** (eq 13). M is built from instantaneous geometry; u = unknown linear+angular velocities and FA contractile-force magnitudes; f = constant torques and spring/bending forces (eqs 3, 9, 10). Solve each step → velocities + contractile-force magnitudes; integrate positions.
- **Domain / BCs:** square substrate **500 × 500 µm**. Periodic BC in y only (infinite substrate in y); x-boundaries fixed (non-periodic) because stiffness κ_s,M and viscosity ξ vary along x. Two setups (Fig 2): (a) sharp stiffness step at center line (left κ^H, right κ^L); (b) stiffness gradient, discretized into stepwise decrease left→right, center-line stiffness κ^C fixed at 1 nN/µm.
- **Timestep / trajectory sampling:** trajectory discretized into displacements of **0.18 min** each (DI calc). Simulated durations: 12 h (Fig 3, 4), 36 h (Fig 5, 6). Fig 3: 30 cells/panel; Fig 4: 10 trajectories/panel.
- **Compute:** XSEDE / Comet supercomputer (SDSC), NIH 1R01GM126256.

## Governing Equations
1. **(eq 1) Cell-point force balance (overdamped):**
   F_d,I + F_s,II' + F_d,II' + Σ_J F_d,IJ + Σ_i (F_C,Ii + F_d,Ii) = 0
   — Sum of drag (medium), F–R spring, F–R internal viscous drag, cell–cell collision drag, and per-FA contractile + FA-viscous forces on cell-point I is zero.
2. **(eq 2) Medium drag on cell-point:** F_d,I = −η v_I — viscous drag ∝ cell-point velocity, coefficient η.
3. **(eq 3) F–R spring potential:** U_s,II' = ½ κ_s,FR (r_II' − r_0,FR)² — harmonic bond keeping front–rear distance at rest length r_0,FR; F_s,II' = −∂U/∂r.
4. **(eq 4) F–R internal viscous drag:** F_d,II' = −α (v_I − v_I') — cell-body viscoelasticity between front & rear, coefficient α.
5. **(eq 5) Cell–cell collision drag:** F_d,IJ = −β (v_I − v_J) — viscous drag between cell-points of *different* cells within critical distance r_crit, coefficient β (relevant for future multi-cell).
6. **(eq 6) FA-point viscous drag:** F_d,Ii = −λ (v_I − v_i) — lumped frictional resistance of transient FAs, coefficient λ.
7. **(eq 7) Cell-point torque balance:** τ_I − γ ω_I + Σ_{i∈R_I} Δ_I,i × F_C,Ii = 0 — constant generated torque τ_I balanced by angular drag (γ ω_I) and torques of tangential contractile forces about cell-point (moment arm Δ_I,i).
8. **(eq 8) Substrate-point force balance:** Σ_{j∈C_i} F_s,ij + Σ F_b,ijk + Σ_{j∈C_i} F_d,ij + F_C,iI = 0 — stretch springs + bending springs + inter-point viscous drag + (reaction) contractile force from cell if point i is a FA.
9. **(eq 9) Chain stretch potential:** U_s,ij = ½ κ_s,M (r_ij − r_0,M)² — harmonic stretch spring between adjacent substrate points; r_0,M identical for all chains.
10. **(eq 10) Chain bending potential:** U_b,ijk = ½ κ_b,M (θ_ijk − θ_ijk,0)² — angular spring on triplet i-j-k; rest angle θ_ijk,0 can vary with number of removed chains.
11. **(eq 11) Substrate inter-point viscous drag:** F_d,ij = −ξ (v_i − v_j) — substrate viscoelasticity, drag coefficient ξ (the tuned "viscosity").
12. **(eq 12) Kinematic constraint (propulsion):** L_I,i · v_I − L_I,i · v_i + (Δ_I,i × L_I,i)·ω_I = 0 — couples cell-point linear velocity v_I, FA-point velocity v_i, and cell-point angular velocity ω_I; encodes cell "reeling in" the FA along an inextensible tangential string (circle of rotation wrapping the FA). This closes the system so contractile force magnitude |F_C,Ii| is solvable.
13. **(eq 13) Assembled linear system:** M u = f — M = geometry-dependent coefficient matrix; u = unknown velocities + |F_C| magnitudes; f = constant torques + spring/bending forces. Solved per timestep.

Reaction relation: **F_C,Ii = −F_C,iI** (Newton's 3rd law between cell-point and FA point); F_C is explicitly **non-centripetal**, directed along the tangent to the circle centered at the cell-point (Fig 1d), consistent with non-centripetal actin retrograde flow.

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Harmonic stretch (cell F–R bond):** U = ½ κ_s,FR (r − r_0,FR)². Linear spring.
- **Harmonic stretch (substrate chain):** U = ½ κ_s,M (r − r_0,M)². Linear spring; κ_s,M is the spatially-varied "stiffness."
- **Harmonic bending (substrate angle):** U = ½ κ_b,M (θ − θ_0)². Reference κ_b,M = 1 nN·µm gives linear-elastic-at-low-strain + strain-stiffening substrate.
- **Viscous drag laws (all linear in relative velocity):** medium η, cell-body α, cell–cell β, FA λ, substrate ξ. These are lumped/phenomenological, fit to reproduce experimental cell velocity & MSD.
- **Active propulsion:** constant torque τ_I per cell-point (dimension of energy; motivated by observation that substrate strain energy is ~constant regardless of stiffness, ref 48) → tangential contractile forces on FAs whose magnitudes emerge from the linear solve. τ_F ≫ τ_R (front dominant).

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Partial/analogous. Not a surface-mesh continuum cell, but the **substrate** is a bending+stretching triangulated spring network — directly analogous to a 2D deformable-mesh substrate/ECM the DCM cell could crawl on. The cell here is a lumped 2-point machine (far coarser than DCM). Useful as a *substrate/ECM mechanics* reference and as a durotaxis test-bed target, not as a cell model.
- **FF?** Yes (conceptually). FF already implements fine-grained crawl on a collagen-Mikado ECM with adhesion clutches; this paper is the **coarse counterpart** and a good validation/behavior target: durotaxis should EMERGE in FF from clutch traction on a stiffness-graded ECM with no decision rule. Their torque-driven non-centripetal traction ≈ FF's emergent traction from actin retrograde flow. Their spring-network substrate maps onto FF's ECM cross-link network (stretch κ_s,M + bending κ_b,M + viscous drag ξ).
- **TAG-validation?** Yes — carries concrete validation targets: DI biphasic in κ^L, DI decreasing with ξ, κ^H≈0.15 nN/µm DI-transition, speed 5–35 µm/hr, distance-before-reorientation ∝ 1/gradient, chain forces up to ~0.5 nN, gradients 0–200 pN/µm². Reference constants (κ_b,M=1 nN·µm, p=0.4, n=4×10⁴ mm⁻², 500×500 µm, T=6 min) are citable. Caveat: most cell lumped params (drag η/α/β/λ/γ, τ, radii) are in **SI Table S1**, not this file — flag as incomplete-provenance until SI ingested.
