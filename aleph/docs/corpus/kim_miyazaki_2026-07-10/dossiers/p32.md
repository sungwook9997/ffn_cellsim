## Summary
This is a Taeyoon Kim–lab review that surveys how single cells behave as **nonlinear elastic and inelastic (not linear-elastic) materials**, and traces those emergent whole-cell behaviors down to the mechanics and dynamics of subcellular constituents. It is organized in four blocks: (1) the load-bearing **cellular structures** — the actin cytoskeleton (F-actin + ACPs + Arp2/3 + myosin II), microtubules, intermediate filaments (IFs: keratin/vimentin/desmin/neurofilament/lamin), and the nucleus (lamina + chromatin); (2) the **rheological measurement techniques** — whole-cell uniaxial microplate stretching, AFM (tipless whole-cell and tip local), micropipette aspiration, microfluidic devices, optical tweezers/optical stretcher, magnetic tweezers/twisting cytometry, rotational magnetic spectroscopy, one/two-point and active microrheology, traction force microscopy (2D/3D TFM), and DNA hairpin tension sensors; (3) the **nonlinear/inelastic properties** themselves — **stiffening** (entropic strain-stiffening of F-actin/IF networks, myosin prestress, actin recruitment, nuclear/lamin-A stiffening), **superelasticity** (actin-cortex fluidization + IF backstop giving a tensional plateau with up to 1000% area increase; lamin-network expansion ~2× area), **viscoelasticity** (stress relaxation, creep, frequency-dependent G′/G″, power-law rheology, ACP unbinding timescales, F-actin turnover, MT dynamics, lamin A viscous / lamin B elastic), **viscoplasticity** (irreversible deformation from force-dependent crosslinker/bond rupture; chromatin realignment; IFs raise resilience), and **poroelasticity** (cytoplasmic/nuclear fluid–solid coupling, exponential-then-power-law relaxation, vimentin's role, poroelastic diffusion coefficient); and (4) **computational models** — continuum/FEM (Maxwell/Kelvin–Voigt/standard-linear-solid lumped models, nonlinear-elastic and poroelastic FEM, immersed-boundary poroelastic crawling) and **discrete models** (energy-minimization triangulated-mesh + filament models, and dynamic Brownian-dynamics [BD] "subcellular element" and dissipative-particle-dynamics [DPD] whole-cell models). The review's thesis, and its forward-looking gap statement, is that a **universal, multi-mechanism, fine-grained model** with explicit F-actin turnover, force-dependent crosslinker unbinding, and myosin activity — plus ECM embedding — is still missing, which is precisely the niche `ffn_cellsim` targets.

## Model & Methods
This is a **review**; it introduces no new model or experiment of its own but catalogs the methods of the field. Relevant method/model families it describes (with the references it attaches):

- **Continuum / FEM models** (Sec. 5.1):
  - Maxwell-viscoelastic membrane+cortex+cytoskeleton FEM of magnetocytometry bead pull [167]; F = 500 pN example, oscillatory force.
  - Nonlinear-elastic FEM of magnetic-bead twisting on adherent epithelia; linear up to ~15° rotation, geometric nonlinearity above [168,169].
  - Viscoelastic FEM of magnetocytometry with explicit focal-adhesion contacts [170].
  - Viscoelastic FEM of nano-indentation (cyclic/stress-relaxation/creep) of fibroblasts [171]; hysteresis loop, moderate stiffening.
  - Nonlinear FEM of micropipette aspiration of chondrocytes — creep attributed to cytoplasmic viscoelasticity not poroelasticity [172].
  - FEM osteoblast + stress-fiber cables on cyclically strained elastomer [173].
  - Poroelastic FEM (1D/2D) with cytoskeletal remodeling + contraction for keratocyte crawling; biphasic motility vs friction; oscillatory motion [161].
  - Immersed-boundary poroelastic crawling cell (larger traction/speed than elastic) [175].
- **Discrete models** (Sec. 5.2):
  - Energy-minimization triangulated membrane + nuclear-envelope + spring F-actins [176].
  - Energy-minimization model with IFs, MTs, adhesions; nano-indentation → stiffening rises with traction & with nucleus [177] (this is the Kim-lab discrete model — Fig. 8(a)-(c), crosslink angles k₁/k₂/π/2/π/φ shown).
  - Neo-Hookean membrane + nuclear envelope + random Hookean-spring F-actins/crosslinkers; nuclear strain vs cytoskeletal connectivity [178].
  - **Brownian-dynamics "subcellular element" model**: cell volume divided into ~1000 subvolumes, each a node with viscous drag + thermal force + Morse-like neighbor interactions; used for whole-cell stretch creep and microbead rheology G′/G″ [179,180].
  - **Dissipative particle dynamics (DPD)** whole-cell: nuclear envelope+membrane as triangular DPD meshes, cytoskeleton+chromatin as serially-linked DPD particles, crosslinks as springs; explicit coarse-grained fluid; micropipette + microfluidic passage speed vs viscosity [181,182].
  - Hybrid FEM(cytoplasm/cortex/nucleus)+discrete(F-actin/MT) indentation model — cortex & MT dominate incompressibility [183].

No integrator/timestep/BCs are specified because the paper reviews others' models rather than running one.

## Governing Equations
This is a review and contains **no transcribable governing equations** in the running text (no numbered force laws, PDEs, or constitutive equations are printed). The physics is conveyed schematically. The equation-like content is qualitative/relational:

1. **Linear elasticity (baseline it argues against):** Hooke's law σ = E·ε with two independent constants (Young's modulus E and Poisson's ratio ν) relating all stress/strain components; rate/time-independent. (Sec. 1, 4.1.)
2. **Entropic spring / strain-stiffening (conceptual):** as a semiflexible filament's end-to-end distance approaches its contour length, configurational entropy drops, giving a diverging (highly nonlinear) force–extension resistance — the "entropic spring effect." (Sec. 4.1.1, ref [93].)
3. **Power-law rheology (conceptual):** creep and G* follow a power law in time/frequency (no single relaxation time / time constant); characteristic of soft-glassy cell rheology. (Sec. 4.2; refs [126,127,130].)
4. **Lumped viscoelastic models (named, not equationed):** Maxwell (spring–dashpot in series, good for stress relaxation), Kelvin–Voigt (spring∥dashpot, good for creep), Standard Linear Solid (2 springs + 1 dashpot). (Fig. 4(e)-(g), Sec. 4.2.)
5. **Young–Laplace relation (named):** used with micropipette aspiration to estimate membrane+cortex line/surface tension. (Sec. 3.)
6. **Poroelastic diffusion coefficient (relational):** D_p is set by (Young's modulus of solid part) × (pore size)² / (fluid viscosity) — governs the timescale of stress relaxation via water efflux. (Sec. 4.4.)
7. **Boltzmann superposition of two power-law creep responses:** used as the null model for reversible viscoelastic recovery; its failure (a residual plastic offset is needed) defines viscoplasticity. (Sec. 4.3, Fig. 6(a)-(c), ref [147].)
8. **Bond kinetics (named, mechanistic anchor for FF):** ACPs unbind faster (slip bonds) or slower (catch bonds) under increasing force; ACP-unbinding timescale sets the frequency of viscoelastic softening (Fig. 5(f), G″ minimum at the fascin unbinding rate). (Sec. 4.2, refs [94–96,141,142].)

## Force Laws & Potentials
The review names but does not write out the functional forms. Mechanistically relevant ones it cites (useful as FF/DCM design anchors):

- **DPD forces**: conservative(repulsive) + random + dissipative pairwise forces, plus coarse-grained fluid particles [181].
- **Harmonic springs** for filament length, crosslinks, and triangulated membrane/nuclear-envelope edges in discrete models [176,178,182].

## Relevance flags
- **DCM? YES (high).** This review is essentially the design brief for a DCM: it enumerates exactly the compartments a surface-mesh continuum cell must carry (membrane, actomyosin cortex tension, cytoplasm/turgor, nucleus = lamina+chromatin) and the emergent whole-cell behaviors DCM must reproduce — **strain-stiffening, superelasticity (cortex fluidization + tensional plateau, area up to 1000%, σ ≈ 1–3 mN/m), stress relaxation/creep, viscoplasticity, poroelasticity**. The superelastic-dome numbers (Fig. 3(e)-(f)), nuclear-stiffening crossover (~3 µm, lamin A), and DPD/FEM whole-cell exemplars (Fig. 7, Fig. 8(d)-(e)) are direct DCM validation targets and modeling precedents.
- **FF? YES (high).** The mechanistic origins it stresses are the FF engine's core: **F-actin as semiflexible bending beam (L_p ~9 µm, d 5–9 nm), entropic strain-stiffening (bending→extension transition), myosin-II prestress, force-dependent ACP catch/slip unbinding setting the viscoelastic frequency (G″ minimum at crosslinker off-rate, Fig. 5(f)), and F-actin turnover-driven stress relaxation/viscoplasticity**. The Kim-lab discrete model [177] (Fig. 8, crosslink-angle detail) is FF's direct ancestor. Persistence-length/diameter constants are usable FF parameter checks.
- **TAG-validation? YES.** As a review it is a citation hub (184 refs) rather than a primary datum, so its own numbers should be TAG-registered as **secondary/quoted** (e.g. F-actin L_p ~9 µm [27], MT L_p 1–5 mm [35], IF d 8–10 nm, superelastic plateau σ ≈ 1–3 mN/m [8], vimentin poroelastic crossover ~0.05 s [101]) with provenance pointing to the primary reference, never to this review as source. Its main value is mapping FF/DCM mechanisms to the primary literature (Latorre 2018 superelasticity, Chaudhuri 2007 reversible softening, Gardel/Koenderink actin prestress, Ferrer/α-actinin catch bonds) for KB cross-linking.
