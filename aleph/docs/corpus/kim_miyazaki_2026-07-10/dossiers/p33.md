## Summary
The paper asks how to build an isopolar, parallel array of actin filaments on a substrate that preserves biological function as a myosin track, for use as a nano-scale unidirectional transport system in micro/nano total-analysis-system (µTAS/nTAS) fluidic devices. The method combines three ideas: (1) a Ca²⁺-insensitive **gelsolin mutant** caps the actin plus (+) end and, in the presence of actin monomers, nucleates polymerization so filaments carry gelsolin at their + end; (2) gelsolin anchors each filament to a biotinylated-BSA-coated glass surface at that one (plus) end; (3) a **shear buffer flow** containing **streptavidin** stretches/orients the filaments downstream and immobilizes them via **biotin–avidin** cross-links (filaments were sparsely biotinylated). Filaments were stabilized with fluorescent phalloidin. The resulting array had immobilized filaments 5.4 ± 3.8 µm long with an angular spread of only σ = 10.8° (Gaussian). Polarity was read out by **myosin V**-coated fluorescent beads (plus-end-directed motor): beads moved unidirectionally with **99.6% orientation accuracy** (n = 264) and at unchanged speed **0.62 ± 0.10 µm/s**, showing streptavidin cross-links do not impede the hand-over-hand motor. Array density is tunable by actin concentration (0.1–5 µM → area density 0.06–3.75 µm⁻²; line density up to 2.9 µm⁻¹ with streptavidin, 14.5 µm⁻¹ with α-actinin). Fabrication takes <10 min. Alternative immobilizers (α-actinin) and surface treatments (collodion + biotin-BSA or biotin-casein; anti-His antibody to the gelsolin His-tag) are demonstrated. Actin-myosin is argued superior to microtubule-kinesin for downsized fluidics (faster motor myosin XI 60–100 µm/s; 100× more flexible filaments for serpentine channels and dense arrays).

## Model & Methods
**Model type:** Purely EXPERIMENTAL in-vitro reconstitution + fluorescence microscopy motility assay. No computational model, no integrator, no governing dynamical equations, no simulation timestep.

**Fabrication / assay protocol (algorithm):**
1. **Gelsolin-actin complex prep:** Use a Ca²⁺-insensitive gelsolin mutant (caps + end without Ca²⁺). Mix gelsolin with actin monomers, add a small fraction of biotinylated actin monomer (→ sparse biotinylation), raise salt to promote polymerization; gelsolin nucleates by recruiting two actin monomers and caps the plus end. Stabilize filaments with fluorescently-labeled phalloidin. Complex stable on ice >1 month.
2. **Flowchamber:** two ~50 µm-thick spacers on a KOH-washed coverslip (32 × 24 mm²), second coverslip (18 × 18 mm²) on top; gap between spacers ~5 mm; inner volume ~15 µL. Solution exchange / flow by wedge-cut paper-filter wicking at the outlet.
3. **Surface coat:** infuse biotinylated-BSA to coat inner surface.
4. **Anchor:** inject sparsely-biotinylated, fluorescent gelsolin-capped filaments; 3 min incubation → filaments bind surface at one (plus) end via gelsolin; wash unbound.
5. **Orient + immobilize:** impose shear buffer flow containing streptavidin (binds 4 biotins, Kd ~10⁻¹⁴ M) → filaments stretch and lie downstream; biotin–avidin cross-links pin them flat. Flow rate ~15 µL/s (inlet volume ÷ exchange time). Wash unbound streptavidin.
6. **Polarity readout:** perfuse myosin V-coated fluorescent beads (bead φ = 0.01 µm), seal chamber with silicon grease (no flow), image bead runs by fluorescence; kymograph gives direction + speed. Count correct/incorrect orientation across 3 chambers.

**Geometry / BCs:** quasi-2D glass surface; filaments laid in the image plane; shear flow sets orientation axis; plus end anchored upstream (gelsolin at +), so myosin V (plus-end-directed) beads travel upstream.

**Controls:** uncapped (no-gelsolin) filaments → negligible nonspecific adsorption (~0.1%); non-biotinylated BSA or casein coating blocks gelsolin/actin binding; anti-His antibody gates gelsolin-His-tag binding.

## Governing Equations
None. This is an experimental method paper — it contains no Langevin/Brownian dynamics equations, no force potentials, no constitutive laws, no motor force-velocity model, and no bond-kinetics rate equations. (Reported quantities are direct measurements, listed below.) A trivial geometric relation used implicitly:
- (G1) line density ∝ area density (assumption used to convert 0.2 µM area density → line density, and to estimate the 1 µM dense-array line density 2.9 µm⁻¹).

## Force Laws & Potentials
None modeled. Physically relevant (measured/cited) interactions, without functional forms:
- Biotin–streptavidin binding: near-irreversible, Kd ~10⁻¹⁴ M (ref 32) — the immobilizing cross-link.
- Gelsolin–actin plus-end capping (Ca²⁺-insensitive mutant, refs 29,30) + two-monomer nucleation (ref 31).
- α-Actinin: actin-binding protein, forms a dimer and cross-links two filaments (alternative immobilizer).
- Myosin V: plus-end-directed, two-headed, hand-over-hand processive motor (refs 35,36,39) — steps over surface obstacles (streptavidin).

## Relevance flags
- **DCM? No.** Surface-mesh continuum cell model has no overlap with an in-vitro gliding/alignment nanofabrication assay; no membrane/cortex/turgor content.
- **FF? Weak/indirect (yes-ish for parameters only).** No filament dynamics model to port, but supplies literature-grade actin single-filament constants the FF cortex/filopodium already uses: actin persistence length l_p = 15–20 µm and diameter 6 nm (cross-check for the bending-beam κ = k_BT·l_p and EV radius), and a myosin-V processive speed 0.62 µm/s + myosin XI 60–100 µm/s as motor-velocity sanity references. It is NOT a myosin-minifilament force-velocity source (no Hill relation, no stall force, no duty ratio here).
- **TAG-validation? Minor.** Reusable as a citation-backed source for actin l_p (15–20 µm) and myosin-V unloaded speed (0.62 µm/s), but the paper's headline results (isopolar-array polarity accuracy, alignment σ, gelsolin capping) are method/nanofab metrics, not cell-mechanics validation targets for DCM/FF gates.
