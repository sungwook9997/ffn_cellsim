## Summary
The paper asks how the **density** of a branched actin network governs the penetration and activity of two size-distinct actin-binding proteins (ABPs): large **myosin II filaments** and small **ADF/cofilin**. To manipulate network density with light, the authors build **OptoVCA**, an iLID–SspB blue-light dimerization system that recruits the WAVE1 **VCA domain** to a membrane, locally activating the **Arp2/3 complex** and triggering branched actin polymerization. They validate it first in live MDCK cells (Lifeact reports cortical F-actin thickening, reversible within minutes, Arp2/3-dependent via CK-666), then reconstitute it in vitro on a **supported lipid bilayer (SLB)** made of POPC + DGS-NTA(Ni) with purified actin, profilin, Arp2/3, and capping protein. By tuning light **power, duration, and spatial pattern**, OptoVCA controls actin network **density, thickness (pillar length up to ~35 µm), and 3D shape** (cups, domes, rings, gradients). Using it, they find: (i) a mere ~2.5-fold increase in actin density (only ~1.5-fold decrease in mesh size) switches myosin-filament penetration **all-or-none** via steric hindrance, with a threshold filament spacing between **110 nm (excluded)** and **160 nm (penetrates)**, comparable to the ~1.3 µm myosin filament length; (ii) in networks with an imposed density **gradient**, penetrated myosin drives **unidirectional actin flow toward the densest region**, requiring only the density gradient + network connectivity (not spatially patterned motor activity); (iii) small **cofilin (~3.5 nm)** penetrates networks regardless of density (cofilin density ∝ actin density, R²=0.89), yet network disassembly is sharply suppressed above ~20–30 µM actin — a non-steric effect attributed to reduced escape of severed fragments and/or impaired filament twisting from branching/entanglement. Disassembly scales as ∝ (actin density)⁻² × bound-cofilin density. The work provides quantitative, tunable in-vitro validation targets for how cortical/branched actin density gates motor and severing-protein function.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** Experimental — no simulation. In-vitro **biochemical reconstitution** on a **supported lipid bilayer (SLB)** + **live-cell optogenetics** (MDCK). This is a *measurement/method* paper, not an agent-based or continuum model. It supplies parameters/observables (validation targets), not a force-law engine.
- **Optogenetic switch:** iLID–SspB blue-light (488 nm) heterodimerization. iLID = LOV2 domain caging an SsrA peptide; blue light exposes SsrA → binds SspB. FMN is the LOV chromophore (added exogenously in vitro). Membrane anchor in vitro: **His-mEGFP-iLID** bound to **DGS-NTA(Ni)** lipid; recruited partner: **SspB-mScarlet-I-VCA** (WAVE1 VCA). In cells: Stargazin-mEGFP-iLID (plasma-membrane anchor) + SspB-mScarlet-I-VCA.
- **NPF/nucleation logic:** VCA (V binds G-actin; CA binds Arp2/3). **Two VCA molecules required to activate one Arp2/3 complex** → light-clustering of VCA on the membrane suppresses basal bulk nucleation and localizes branching to the illuminated region. Nucleation seeded by diffusing bulk-nucleated F-actin fragments binding the membrane Arp2/3·2·VCA complex.
- **SLB:** POPC main lipid + **2–6 mol% DGS-NTA(Ni)** (His-tag anchor) ± 2 mol% Marina Blue DHPE (lipid marker). Vesicle-fusion onto cleaned coverslip in a hand-made flow cell (~10 µL volume, ~300 µm thickness). Bilayer fluidity confirmed by single-molecule TIRF.
- **Illumination hardware:** Digital micromirror device (DMD, Nikon) on ECLIPSE Ti2, SOLA light source; 488 nm; **500 ms pulses at 20 s intervals**; ND filter (NE220B) reduces intensity to ~1% for density control. Intensity measured with a power meter (TQ82210). Field of view 155×216 µm; ROIs of 25×25 or 37×37 µm squares.
- **Imaging:** Spinning-disk confocal (CSU-W1, iXon Life 888 EMCCD, 60× NA 1.42) for 3D; hand-made TIRF (iXon3, 488 nm OBIS, 60× NA 1.45) for single-molecule diffusion; excitation 405/488/561/640 nm.
- **Analysis:** Fiji/ImageJ (rolling-ball background subtraction, binarization, ROI erosion for cortex vs cytoplasm), Python plotting, NIS-Elements 3D reconstruction, MSD tracking for diffusion, non-linear power-law fitting (SciPy) for disassembly.
- **Purified proteins:** actin (rabbit skeletal, Alexa647-C5-maleimide labeled), profilin (mouse), Arp2/3 (Cytoskeleton Inc.), capping protein α/β (mouse), cofilin (mouse, Alexa546-NHS), smooth muscle myosin SMM (chicken gizzard, Alexa546, phosphorylated by ZIP kinase), ZIP kinase (human ZIPK). Energy mix: 1 mM ATP, 10 mM phosphocreatine, 0.1 mg/mL creatine phosphokinase, 10 mM DTT; 2 mM Trolox (antioxidant), 10 µM FMN.
- **Buffer A50:** 50 mM HEPES-KOH pH 7.6, 50 mM KCl, 5 mM MgCl₂, 1 mM EGTA (+1 mM DTT).

## Governing Equations
This is an experimental paper; the only quantitative "laws" are empirical fits and scaling relations (no dynamical force equations).

1. **Cofilin disassembly scaling (main claim, ref. to Manhart/Miyazaki prior work #22):**
   `Disassembly_rate ∝ (local actin density)^(-2) × (actin-bound cofilin density)`
   — network disassembly is inversely proportional to the square of local actin density and proportional to bound-cofilin density.

2. **Disassembly-level power-law fits (Fig. 6c), form `D(x) = a·x^b + 0.8`, x = initial Alexa647-actin fluorescence (∝ actin density):**
   - 0 nM cofilin:   `D = 0.11·x^0.07 + 0.8` (≈ flat baseline ~0.9)
   - 100 nM cofilin: `D = −0.04·x^2.51 + 0.8`
   - 300 nM cofilin: `D = −0.07·x^2.12 + 0.8`
   - 900 nM cofilin: `D = −0.24·x^1.33 + 0.8`
   ("Disassembly level" = actin intensity 10 min after illumination ÷ intensity 10 min after stopping illumination; fit only to x>0.3 to exclude background.)

3. **Cofilin–actin co-recruitment (Fig. 6f):** linear `[cofilin]_local ∝ [actin]_local`, R² = 0.89 (cofilin penetrates freely, density set by actin density).

4. **Mean single-molecule diffusion (SLB, from MSD):** `MSD(Δt) = 4·D·Δt`, giving D(His-mEGFP-iLID) ≈ 0.38 µm²/s.

5. **Mean actin filament spacing estimate (Supplementary Note, geometric):** from estimated actin molar density → average inter-filament spacing ~110 nm in the dense (~50 µM) network (spacing scales roughly as (density)^(−1/2) for a 2D-projected mesh).

## Force Laws & Potentials
None given explicitly (no simulation). The mechanistic *concepts* the paper establishes as force/interaction constraints for a model:
- **Steric exclusion of myosin filaments:** hard-body exclusion when mesh spacing < myosin filament length. Threshold inter-filament spacing **110–160 nm** for a **1.32 µm** myosin filament → myosin filament penetrates only when local mesh ≳ filament length scale; all-or-none.
- **Motor-assisted penetration:** ATPase activity (blebbistatin-sensitive) enhances myosin penetration into sparse networks — active insertion, not pure diffusion.
- **Myosin-driven contraction / directed flow:** contractile stress transported actin (and passenger myosin puncta) toward higher-density region along a density gradient; requires network connectivity to transmit stress.
- **Cofilin severing:** diffusion-limited access (cofilin ~3.5 nm ≪ mesh) then severing; severing efficiency reduced in dense/branched networks (twisting-dependent severing impaired; severed-fragment escape suppressed by entanglement).
