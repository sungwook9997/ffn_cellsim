## Summary
The paper asks how cancer cells divide inside 3D confining collagen matrices that mechanically resist mitotic elongation (the cell lengthening along the division axis required to complete mitosis). Using MDA-MB-231 breast-cancer cells (and normal MCF10A cells) encapsulated as single cells in reconstituted type-1 collagen gels of 1/3/5 mg mL⁻¹ (storage modulus ≈10→400 Pa), with GFP-α-tubulin + RFP-histone live confocal imaging, confocal-reflectance collagen visualization, and PIV matrix-displacement mapping, they show that dividing cells generate **protrusive (pushing) extracellular forces** along the mitotic axis that deform and buckle the surrounding collagen fibers, greatest at anaphase B and telophase/cytokinesis (T/C). This force generation is **independent of cell spreading** (rounded cells and spread/remodeled cells push equally) and **independent of MMP/proteolytic degradation** (protease-inhibitor cocktail does not reduce deformation). Mechanistically, laser ablation of the interpolar spindle (partial relaxation of matrix deformation), low-dose STLC kinesin-5 inhibition, and blebbistatin myosin-II inhibition (reduced lateral contraction + reduced matrix deformation) establish two additive drivers: **interpolar spindle elongation** (couples to matrix via astral microtubules) and **cytokinetic-ring contraction** (drives axial expansion via volume conservation). An **agent-based Brownian-dynamics simulation** of a coarse-grained cell membrane inside a crosslinked collagen fiber network reproduces the physics: a constant inward equatorial force (F_Cyto, cytokinetic ring) plus a constant outward polar force (F_Is, spindle) under a constant-volume constraint elongates the cell (~20% for ring alone, rising toward the experimental ~40% when spindle force is added at ~1 mg mL⁻¹) and pushes fibers outward along the mitotic axis while pulling them inward along the perpendicular axis — matching the experimental inward matrix flow. Conclusion: tumor cells use mitotic pushing forces (spindle elongation + ring contraction), not spreading or matrix degradation, to make space for division in dense collagen.

## Model & Methods
Two experimental + one computational track.

**Experimental (Chaudhuri lab):**
- Cells: MDA-MB-231 (GFP-α-tubulin, RFP-histone); MCF10A (RFP-histone + SiR-tubulin dye).
- Synchronization: thymidine (G1/S) → STLC 20 µM (prometaphase arrest), or Cdk1 inhibitor RO-3306 (9 µM, 18 h). Released into/inside collagen gel.
- Collagen gels: type-I collagen (Corning) at 1/3/5 mg mL⁻¹, pH adjusted to 8.5 with NaOH, gelled 45–60 min at 37 °C; cell density 0.1–0.5 M cells mL⁻¹.
- Imaging: Leica SP8 confocal, 25×/0.95NA water objective, excitation 488 (GFP-tubulin) / 555 (RFP-histone) / 639 nm (collagen reflectance). 3D volume via z-stacks at 2 µm intervals reconstructed in IMARIS.
- Rheology: AR-G2 stress-controlled rheometer, 25 mm plates; storage modulus at strain 0.01, frequency 1 rad s⁻¹; creep (300 s) + recovery (1 h) to ~30% strain for plasticity.
- Laser ablation: Zeiss LSM 780 multiphoton, Mai Tai DeepSee 800 nm, 90–100% of 2.4 W max, on interpolar spindle at anaphase; 1.5 µm fluorescent microbeads embedded in 3 mg mL⁻¹ gel as fiducials; 20×/0.8NA objective, 405/488/555 nm.
- Perturbations: blebbistatin 100 µM (myosin-II / ring); STLC 10 µM low-dose (partial kinesin-5); MMP cocktail — marimastat 20 µM, E-64 20 µM, aprotinin 0.7 µM, leupeptin 2 µM.
- Analysis: matrix deformation by PIV (PIVlab MATLAB) after StackReg drift correction, max deformation within ~15 µm of cell; reflectance intensity in 16 µm ROIs near vs far from cell; ImageJ for lengths/widths; GraphPad Prism 8, Student's t-test / one-way ANOVA + Tukey.

**Computational (Kim lab) — agent-based Brownian dynamics (overdamped Langevin, inertia neglected):**
- Elements: matrix **fibers** = serially connected cylindrical elements; **crosslinkers** = two cylindrical elements (two arms); **cell membrane** = serially connected rectangular solid elements (coarse-grained). Fiber/crosslinker nodes have (x,y,z); membrane nodes have only (x,y) (2D cross-sectional membrane).
- Integrator: **Euler** scheme (Eq 5), overdamped Langevin with thermal noise (Eqs 2–3), timestep Δt (value in Table S1, not in main text).
- Forces: extensional + bending harmonic potentials (Eqs 6,7) for fibers/crosslinkers/membrane; volume-exclusion repulsive harmonic potential (Eq 8) between membrane and matrix elements; crosslinker unbinding via **Bell's law** (Eq 9); volume conservation enforced on the membrane.
- Two sub-models:
  1. **Stress-relaxation test** (rheology calibration): matrix-only, 3D domain 50×50×1 µm, periodic BC in x,y. Fibers grown from 1 µm seeds to avg length 9.3–11.6 µm; crosslinkers bind sites every 100 nm. Shear applied by translating top boundary in +x at strain rate 0.1 s⁻¹ to a goal strain, then held; stress = Σ(forces on clamped top nodes)/area. Reproduces shear modulus, nonlinear elasticity, strain-enhanced stress relaxation (Fig S12).
  2. **Cytokinesis model:** cylindrical domain radius 50 µm, height 1 µm; circular membrane radius 10 µm centered, 158 membrane elements of equilibrium length 400 nm; matrix formed around it. F_Cyto = constant inward (−y) force on membrane nodes within 0.5 µm of equator, 100 pN total per side (turned off when node within 1 µm of mitotic axis). F_Is = constant outward (±x) force on membrane nodes within 5 µm of mitotic axis, 100 pN total per side. Constant-volume constraint: 3D volume computed by rotating the symmetric 2D shape about the long axis each step; deviation from initial volume triggers transverse inward/outward corrective forces on all membrane segments. Runs to T = 2000 s. Fiber concentration mapped to mg mL⁻¹ via Eqs 10–11.

## Governing Equations
1. **Degree of plasticity** (Eq 1): `plasticity = ε_remaining / ε_maximum` — ratio of residual strain after 1 h recovery to peak creep strain; quantifies mechanical plasticity of collagen gel.
2. **Overdamped Langevin / force balance** (Eq 2): `F_i − ζ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_i^T = 0` — deterministic force minus drag plus thermal (stochastic) force = 0 for node i (inertia neglected).
3. **Fluctuation–dissipation** (Eq 3): `⟨F_i^T(t) F_j^T(t)⟩ = (2 k_B T ζ_i δ_ij / Δt) δ` — variance of the stochastic force set by drag and temperature (δ_ij Kronecker delta, δ a 2nd-order/identity tensor, Δt timestep).
4. **Drag coefficient of a cylindrical element** (Eq 4): `ζ_i = 3π μ r_{c,i} (3 + 2 r_{0,i}/r_{c,i}) / 5` — approximate drag for a cylinder of length r_{0,i} and diameter r_{c,i} in medium viscosity μ.
5. **Euler position update** (Eq 5): `r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (dr_i/dt)Δt = r_i(t) + (1/ζ_i)(F_i + F_i^T)Δt` — explicit time integration.
6. **Extensional harmonic potential** (Eq 6): `U_s = ½ κ_s (r − r_0)²` — stretching energy of a cylindrical element (spring stiffness κ_s, rest length r_0).
7. **Bending harmonic potential** (Eq 7): `U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ_0)²` — bending energy from angle θ between adjacent elements (bending stiffness κ_b, rest angle θ_0).
8. **Repulsive (volume-exclusion) potential** (Eq 8): `U_r = ½ k_r (r_12 − r_c)²` if `r_12 < r_c`, else `0` — soft repulsion when the min distance r_12 between a membrane element and a fiber/crosslinker segment drops below critical distance r_c (strength k_r).
9. **Bell's-law crosslinker unbinding** (Eq 9): `k_u = k_{0,u} exp(λ_u |F_{s,X1}| / k_B T)` if `r ≥ r_{0,X1}`, else `k_u = k_{0,u}` — force-dependent unbinding of a crosslinker arm; enhanced only when the spring force F_{s,X1} is tensile (λ_u = force sensitivity, k_{0,u} = zero-force off-rate).
10. **Fiber stretching stiffness ↔ Young's modulus** (Eq 10): `k_{s,f} = E A / r_{0,f} = π E r_{c,f}² / r_{0,f}` — maps fiber element spring constant to collagen-fibril Young's modulus E and fibril cross-section A = π r_{c,f}².
11. **Fiber concentration in mg mL⁻¹** (Eq 11): `C = π r_{c,f}² L_{f,tot} / (V ν)` — converts total fiber length L_{f,tot} in domain volume V to mass concentration using collagen specific volume ν.

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Stretch:** harmonic spring, force `= −κ_s (r − r_0)` (from Eq 6). Fibers use κ_{s,f} (Eq 10 → E≈30 MPa); crosslinker arms use κ_{s,xl}; membrane uses κ_{s,m}.
- **Bend:** harmonic angular, `= −κ_b (θ − θ_0)` (from Eq 7). Fiber κ_{b,f} set to persistence length ℓ_p ≈ 100 µm. Crosslinkers have two bending stiffnesses κ_{b,xl,1} (angle between two arms, rest 0) and κ_{b,xl,2} (angle between arm and bound fiber axis, rest π/2). Membrane κ_{b,m} (rest angle 0).
- **Excluded-volume repulsion:** one-sided harmonic (Eq 8), only within r_c.
- **Crosslinker kinetics:** binds to sites every 100 nm along fibers; unbinds by Bell's law (Eq 9), tensile-force-accelerated.
- **Active mitotic forces (applied, not potential-derived):** F_Cyto = constant inward equatorial force (cytokinetic ring), 100 pN/side; F_Is = constant outward polar force (spindle), 100 pN/side.
- **Volume conservation:** geometric constraint restoring initial 3D (surface-of-revolution) volume via transverse corrective membrane forces.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM? YES (high).** This is a near-exact conceptual twin of the DCM engine: a coarse-grained closed **membrane** (harmonic stretch Eq 6 + bending Eq 7 + **volume conservation**) embedded in a fiber ECM, deformed by applied **cytokinetic-ring inward force** and **spindle outward force**, with node-vs-fiber **excluded-volume repulsion** (Eq 8). Directly maps to DCM membrane bending/tension + turgor/volume constraint + node-face contact, and provides a validated cytokinesis/division force protocol (F_Cyto/F_Is = 100 pN, band geometries) and elongation targets (~20% ring-only, ~40% ring+spindle) for a dividing cell in collagen.
- **FF? YES (high).** The collagen matrix is a fine-grained Cytosim-style network: fibers = serial cylindrical beam elements with extensional κ_s + bending κ_b (ℓ_p≈100µm), explicit two-arm **crosslinkers** with **Bell's-law force-dependent off-rate** (Eq 9), binding sites every 100 nm, overdamped Langevin BD with fluctuation-dissipation (Eqs 2–3) and cylindrical drag (Eq 4). This is exactly FF's crosslinker/off-rate/beam machinery and gives a fiber-concentration↔mg/mL calibration (Eqs 10–11, E≈30MPa, r_{c,f}=6.5nm, ν=0.73) plus a stress-relaxation validation protocol.
- **TAG-validation? YES.** Multiple citable quantitative targets: collagen G' (10/100/400 Pa at 1/3/5 mg mL⁻¹), matrix deformation magnitudes (~0.4–0.8 µm), cell elongation (~40%), force scales (100 pN per side for ring and spindle), fiber persistence length (~100 µm), fiber modulus (30 MPa), fibril radius (6.5 nm). Note many raw simulation constants (κ_s, κ_b, k_{0,u}, λ_u, Δt, μ) live in **Table S1** (Supporting Information, not in this PDF) — flag that SI Table S1 is needed for the complete parameter set before KB-registering the full sim block.
