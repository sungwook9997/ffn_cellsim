## Summary
Cross-linked actin networks (CLANs) are "hub-and-spoke" F-actin structures prevalent in the glaucomatous trabecular meshwork (TM) and associated with ocular hypertension (OHT). Using the GTM3L cell line (transformed glaucomatous TM cells stably expressing LifeAct–GFP that spontaneously form fluorescently labeled CLANs), the authors ask three questions: (1) does LifeAct–GFP lentiviral copy number/expression drive CLAN incidence, (2) can CLAN incidence be enriched, and (3) what is the biomechanical consequence of CLANs. They find each GTM3L cell carries ≈1 viral copy regardless of GFP intensity, so copy number does NOT correlate with CLANs (hypothesis: a "two-hit" model where the lentiviral *insertion locus*, plus a favorable environment, drives CLAN formation). CLAN incidence is boosted from ~0.28% to ~50% by combining three enrichment methods: (i) nine cycles of differential cell–substrate adhesion selection (→4.16%), (ii) PEG300 osmotic deswelling / macromolecular crowding (2% PEG300 5 d → 26.5%; synergy), and (iii) microfluidic cell-stiffness sorting (stiff fraction). CLANs form more on stiff (glass) than soft (2.36 kPa hydrogel) substrates and are mechanosensitive. Live imaging: CLANs take days to *induce* but assemble in minutes; after PEG300 withdrawal they disassemble within ~1 h (persistence 58.9±61.7 min vs 717.1±453.9 min with PEG maintained). AFM confirms CLAN+ cells (~4 kPa) are stiffer than CLAN− cells. Taeyoon Kim's agent-based F-actin/ACP model reproduces the mechanics: a CLAN-like triangular-lattice network resists **tensile** strain far more than shear/compression, and CLANs physically linked (via LINC-like links, 20% of F-actins) to a model nucleus dramatically increase the nucleus's resistance to compression — explaining why CLAN+ cells are stiffer.

## Model & Methods

### Computational model (Taeyoon Kim agent-based framework; refs 33–36)
- **Model type:** Agent-based / coarse-grained Brownian(Langevin) dynamics. F-actin and actin cross-linking proteins (ACPs) are represented as serially connected **cylindrical segments** (nodes/points connected by cylindrical elements). The cell nucleus (when present) is a **triangulated (meshed) sphere**.
- **Governing dynamics:** positions of all segment-defining points and mesh points updated each timestep via the **Langevin equation** integrated with **forward Euler**.
- **Deterministic forces on all elements:** extensional (stretching), bending, and repulsive (excluded-volume) forces. For the nucleus mesh additionally: **volume-conservation** and **surface-area-conservation** forces.
- **Filament kinetics:** F-actins assemble by **nucleation with specified orientation + polymerization**; **no depolymerization**. ACPs bind pairs of F-actins to form functional cross-linking points (self-assembly → CLAN-like network).
- **Integrator / timestep / BCs:** forward Euler; timestep not stated in main text (in Supplementary Materials). BCs are periodic (see geometries below).

### Geometry A — bulk rheology (no nucleus)
- Thin rectangular domain **10 × 8.66 × 0.1 µm**, periodic in x and y.
- Actin concentration **C_A = 100 µM** (physiological, ref 37); ACP molar ratio **R_ACP = C_ACP/C_A = 0.1** (maintains connectivity).
- After assembly: disable PBC in y; sever F-actins crossing the two y-normal boundaries and **clamp** their ends to those boundaries.
- Rheology: displace the **+y boundary** at constant rate in +x (shear), +y (tensile), or −y (compressive); −y boundary fixed.
- **Max strain = 0.05; strain rate = 0.001 s⁻¹.**
- Stress = (sum of the relevant force component on all F-actin ends clamped to +y boundary) / (boundary area). Shear uses x-component of force; tensile & compressive use y-component.
- The rendered network (Fig 6A) is a **triangular lattice** geometry.

### Geometry B — nucleus ± CLAN (compression)
- Larger 3-D rectangular domain **10 × 10 × 5.1 µm** (initial z ≈ nucleus diameter).
- Nucleus = sphere, **diameter 5 µm** (r_N = 2.5 µm), frozen/rigid-shape during actin assembly.
- CLAN-like network assembled in the shell just above the nucleus: radial distance between **r_N and 1.1·r_N**.
- **C_A = 60 µM** (computed from the shell assembly volume); **R_ACP = 0.1**.
- **20% of F-actins** experience attractive forces to the nucleus, mimicking **LINC-complex** links (ref 38).
- After assembly: apply compressive strain to +y boundary, −y fixed. **Max compressive strain −0.2** (20% decrease in z), **strain rate −0.01 s⁻¹**; figure snapshots go to **ε = −0.5**.
- Stress = total resistant force (actin + nucleus) / contact area between nucleus and boundary.

### Experimental methods (biology)
- **Cells:** GTM3L = GTM3 (immortalized glaucomatous pHTM) transduced with pLenti–LifeAct–EGFP–BlastR. Cultured in DMEM/Opti-MEM + 10% FBS + 1% PSG, 37 °C, 5% CO₂.
- **DEX-myocilin control:** 100 nM dexamethasone 7 d → Western blot (myocilin/GAPDH) confirms TM phenotype.
- **FACS + ddPCR:** sorted high/med/low GFP; viral copy number = lentiviral WPRE / host ApoB by droplet digital PCR.
- **Hydrogel:** GelMA 6% w/v + 0.075% LAP photoinitiator, UV-crosslinked 1 J/cm²; soft substrate ~2.36 kPa.
- **Osmotic deswelling:** 2% (or 3% for live imaging) PEG300, 2–5 d.
- **Adhesion sorting:** trypsin two-step (discard fast-releasing, keep strongly adherent); 9 cycles.
- **Stiffness sorting:** microfluidic device, 14 × 7-µm constrictions angled 30°, PDMS on SU-8; flow 3–7 µL/min; 4 stiffness fractions (extra soft/soft/medium/stiff).
- **AFM:** MFD-3D, silicon-nitride cantilever + borosilicate sphere **d=10 µm, nominal k=0.1 N/m**, thermal calibration; **indentation ≤400 nm** (avoid substrate), **tip velocity 800 nm/s** (avoid viscous effects); 5 measurements/cell, ≥5 cells/group; **Hertz model**, Poisson ratio **0.5**.
- **Live imaging:** Nikon Ti2, 40× objective, 30 s / 1 min / 3 min intervals over 6–30 h; microwell glass chip (400 & 800 µm wells).
- **Stats:** GraphPad Prism v10.2.3; Shapiro–Wilk normality; Student t-test or one-way ANOVA + Tukey; α=0.05.

## Governing Equations
The main text does not print the equations explicitly (they live in refs 33–36 + Supplementary Materials). Transcribed as described, with the standard Kim-lab functional forms these refer to:

1. **Langevin equation of motion (overdamped Brownian dynamics), forward-Euler update.** For each point i:
   `ζ_i · dr_i/dt = F_i^det + F_i^thermal`  →  `r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (Δt/ζ_i)·(F_i^det + F_i^thermal)`
   Meaning: viscous drag balances deterministic + stochastic (thermal) forces; positions marched by forward Euler. (Δt in Supplementary Materials.)

2. **Extensional (stretching) force** — harmonic spring on segment length: `F_s = −κ_s (r − r_0)` with meaning: restores each cylindrical segment to rest length r_0 (stretching stiffness κ_s).

3. **Bending force** — harmonic bending on the angle θ between consecutive segments: `U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ_0)²`; force = −∂U_b/∂r. Meaning: penalizes filament (and cross-link arm) curvature; sets persistence length.

4. **Repulsive (excluded-volume) force** — short-range repulsion between segment pairs (harmonic/Hertzian in overlap): `F_r = −κ_r (δ)` for overlap δ>0, else 0. Meaning: prevents interpenetration of filaments.

5. **Nucleus volume-conservation force** — restores enclosed volume V toward V_0: `U_V = ½ κ_V (V − V_0)²/V_0`. Meaning: keeps the meshed nucleus (near-)incompressible.

6. **Nucleus surface-area-conservation force** — restores area A toward A_0: `U_A = ½ κ_A (A − A_0)²/A_0`. Meaning: penalizes membrane/mesh area change.

7. **Nucleus–F-actin attractive (LINC-mimicking) link** — attractive force on the 20% linked F-actin ends toward the nucleus surface (harmonic tether). Meaning: physical coupling of CLAN to nucleus for force transmission.

8. **Stress measurement (macroscopic observable):**
   `σ = (Σ F_component on +y-clamped F-actin ends) / A_boundary` (Geometry A);
   `σ = F_resist,total / A_contact` (Geometry B). Meaning: virial-style boundary stress from clamped-end reaction forces.

> NOTE: Exact κ values, Δt, drag coefficients, and the precise potential forms are in the Supplementary Materials / refs 33 (Li Soft Matter 2017), 34 (Jung Comput Part Mech 2015), 35 (Kim PLoS Comput Biol 2009), 36 (Mak Nat Commun 2016). They are not printed in this article body.

## Force Laws & Potentials
See Governing Equations 2–7. Functional families (Kim-lab canonical): harmonic extensional spring (κ_s), harmonic bending (κ_b, sets L_p), harmonic/Hertzian excluded-volume repulsion (κ_r), harmonic volume & area constraints for the meshed nucleus (κ_V, κ_A), harmonic attractive nucleus–actin tether (LINC). Thermal force is Gaussian white noise satisfying fluctuation–dissipation `⟨F^thermal(t)·F^thermal(t')⟩ = 2 k_B T ζ δ(t−t')`. **Numerical constants for these are not given in the main text** (Supplementary Materials).

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Partial-yes. The nucleus is a **triangulated meshed sphere with volume + surface-area conservation forces** and node-based contact under compression — directly the DCM surface-mesh continuum idiom. The CLAN-vs-nucleus compression test (stress vs ε, LINC links) is a clean validation-style scenario for DCM nucleus stiffness + cytoskeleton-to-nucleus coupling.
- **FF?** **Yes (strong).** This is Taeyoon Kim's agent-based F-actin + ACP cross-linker Brownian/Langevin engine — the same physics family as FF (explicit filaments as bending beams, explicit cross-linkers, excluded volume, self-assembled network, boundary-clamped bulk rheology). The bulk-rheology protocol (clamp ends, shear/tensile/compressive at fixed strain rate, virial boundary stress) is a portable FF validation harness. Network stiffness anisotropy (tension ≫ shear/compression) is a concrete FF target.
- **TAG-validation?** **Yes.** Multiple quantitative targets: network stiffness anisotropy (shear ~1.0e4 / comp ~0.5e4 / tension ~2.4e4 Pa), nucleus compression stress ladder (2300 / 3200 / 6200 Pa), AFM cell modulus ~4 kPa, hydrogel 2.36 kPa, R_ACP=0.1, C_A=100/60 µM. But note the **force constants (κ_s, κ_b, κ_r, κ_V, κ_A), Δt, and drag are NOT in this PDF** — they live in the Supplementary Materials + refs 33–36; a KB parameter row must cite those, not this article body.
