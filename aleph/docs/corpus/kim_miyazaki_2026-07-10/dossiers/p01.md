## Summary
The paper poses a generic single-molecule inference problem: in experiments (e.g. a probe bead attached to the γ subunit of F1-ATPase) only a small subset of degrees of freedom **y** (probe position) is observable while the internal/protein degrees of freedom **x** are hidden, yet one wants the physical parameters **Π*** = {k*, h*, γ*, Γ*} (potential stiffness, linker stiffness, and two friction coefficients). The authors formulate the estimation via **Bayesian inference**: build a mechanical (Langevin/elastic-network) model, write the Onsager–Machlup path probability P([x,y]|Π), integrate out the hidden trajectory x with the **WKB (saddle-point/MAP) method** to get the exact marginal likelihood P([y]|Π) for their linear model, and estimate Π by minimizing a time-normalized "Hamiltonian" Hτ(Π) = −τ⁻¹ ln P([y]|Π). They prove that for infinite observation time τ→∞ the Hamiltonian has its minimum exactly at the true Π*, and that the systematic error decreases ∝ τ⁻¹ (Appendix A). Testing on a **Brownian double-pendulum toy** (two beads + spring, one bead in a harmonic trap; Langevin dynamics), they find the method beats conventional power-spectrum fitting by **1–2 orders of magnitude** in accuracy in the "precise phase." The striking new result is a **loss-of-precision phase transition**: when the probe friction Γ* is large and the linker stiffness h* is small, the sample-averaged variance of the estimate jumps by >4 orders of magnitude across a critical curve — the Hamiltonian landscape flattens and the estimate becomes unreliable even for τ→∞. The critical line between divergent/precise phases is set by Δt vs the probe relaxation time τr ~ Γ*/h*; the precise/non-precise line is set by τ. Measurement noise below a threshold does not degrade the estimate; above it, estimates diverge.

## Model & Methods
- **General framework:** system split into hidden **x**=(x₁…xₙ) and visible **y**=(y₁…yₘ); parameters Π*=(Π₁*…Πₚ*). Bayesian posterior P(Π|[y]) ∝ P([y]|Π)P(Π) with noninformative (flat, log-scale) prior; estimate Π̂ maximizes posterior ⇔ minimizes Hτ(Π)[y] = −τ⁻¹ ln P([y]|Π). Hidden trajectory recovered as MAP estimator [x̂] via δ/δx(t) ln P([x,y]|Π̂) = 0.
- **Concrete model type:** overdamped **Langevin (Brownian) dynamics** of a 2-bead "double pendulum" — a small trapped bead x(t) (proxy for F1-ATPase γ subunit) elastically coupled (spring h) to a large probe bead y(t) (proxy for attached probe particle); ATP-absent case, so x fluctuates about a resting angle. Only rotation about the molecular axis retained; trap center assumed known from the probe-position histogram.
- **Path probability:** decomposed into canonical initial distribution + **Onsager–Machlup transition probability** discretized at interval Δt (N+1 points, N=τ/Δt), **Stratonovich (midpoint) interpretation**.
- **Marginalization:** integrate out x by **WKB method** (Wiegel technique, Refs 26,28,29). Because the model is linear, F([x,y];Π) is quadratic in x̂ so the saddle-point expansion is exact → closed-form P([y]|Π) and the analytic Hamiltonian Eq. (17). MAP estimator [x̂] solves a tridiagonal linear system (exact).
- **Numerical experiment (Appendix B):** (1) integrate Eqs (6)–(7) at true Π* to generate [x,y]; keep only [y]. (2) Estimate Π by **zero-temperature Metropolis** minimization of Hτ in **log-parameter space**: init log Π⁰ = log Π* + U (U~uniform(−1,1)); step log Πˢ⁺¹ = log Πˢ + G (G~zero-mean Gaussian, SD 0.01); accept if Hτ decreases, else restore; **10⁵ iterations**. Multiple random inits = "replicas" (index i); multiple data realizations = "samples" (index j). Estimate Π̂ = replica with minimal Hτ. Results with ‖log Π̂ − log Π*‖ > 3 discarded as divergent.
- **Baseline / comparison:** conventional **least-squares power-spectrum fitting** of [y]: same code, Hτ replaced by spectrum fit function; top decade of high-frequency spectrum dropped to avoid aliasing bias.
- **Units / defaults:** system of units fixed k* = γ* = k_BT = 1. Default Δt = 10⁻⁴, τ = 10 (Fig. 2/5), sweep τ∈[1,100] and Δt∈[10⁻⁵,10⁻²] (Fig. 3). Replicas r and samples s vary per figure (see caption values below).

## Governing Equations
1. **Marginal likelihood** — Eq. (1): P([y]|Π) ≡ ∫ 𝒟x P([x,y]|Π). *Integrate out the hidden trajectory.*
2. **Bayes posterior** — Eq. (2): P(Π|[y]) = P([y]|Π)P(Π)/P([y]). *Posterior over parameters.*
3. **Hamiltonian (objective)** — Eq. (3): Hτ(Π)[y] ≡ −τ⁻¹ ln P([y]|Π). *Negative log-marginal-likelihood per unit time; minimized to estimate Π.*
4. **Hidden-state Bayes** — Eq. (4): P([x]|[y];Π̂) = P([x,y]|Π̂)/P([y]|Π̂). *Posterior over hidden trajectory.*
5. **MAP condition for hidden path** — Eq. (5): δ/δx(t) ln P([x,y]|Π̂) = 0. *Most-probable (classical) hidden trajectory x̂.*
6. **Langevin, hidden bead** — Eq. (6): γ ẋ(t) = −k x(t) − h(x(t)−y(t)) + ξ(t). *Trapped subunit: harmonic trap + coupling spring + thermal noise.*
7. **Langevin, probe** — Eq. (7): Γ ẏ(t) = −h(y(t)−x(t)) + η(t). *Probe: coupling spring + thermal noise.*
   - Noises: ⟨ξ⟩=⟨η⟩=0, white Gaussian, variances 2γk_BT and 2Γk_BT, mutually uncorrelated (fluctuation–dissipation).
8. **Path decomposition** — Eq. (8): P([x,y]|Π) = P_init(x₀,y₀|Π) · P_tr((x₀,y₀)→[x,y]|Π).
9. **Initial (canonical) distribution** — Eq. (9): P_init(x,y|Π) = √(kh)/(2πk_BT) · exp[−βH(x,y;Π)].
10. **Initial energy** — Eq. (10): H(x,y;Π) ≡ (k/2)x² + (h/2)(y−x)², with β⁻¹ ≡ k_BT.
11. **Onsager–Machlup transition probability** — Eq. (11): P_tr = (√(γΓ)/(4πk_BTΔt))ᴺ · exp[−βS([x,y];Π)].
12. **Action functional** — Eq. (12):
    S([x,y];Π) = (1/4γ) Σᵢ Δt [ γ(x_{i+1}−x_i)/Δt + k(x_{i+1}+x_i)/2 + h((x_{i+1}+x_i)/2 − (y_{i+1}+y_i)/2) ]² − k_BT(k+h)τ/(2γ)
    + (1/4Γ) Σᵢ Δt [ Γ(y_{i+1}−y_i)/Δt + h((y_{i+1}+y_i)/2 − (x_{i+1}+x_i)/2) ]² − k_BT hτ/(2Γ).
    *Discretized (midpoint/Stratonovich) OM action for both beads.*
13. **Rewritten path prob** — Eq. (13): P([x,y]|Π) = √(kh)/(2πk_BT)·(√(γΓ)/(4πk_BTΔt))ᴺ · exp[−βF], with **Eq. (14):** F([x,y];Π) ≡ H(x₀,y₀;Π) + S([x,y];Π).
14. **WKB (exact quadratic) expansion** — Eq. (15): F = F([x̂([y];Π),y];Π) + ½ Σ_{i,j} (x_i − x̂_i)Λ_{ij}(x_j − x̂_j).
15. **Hessian of F** — Eq. (16): Λ_{ij} ≡ ∂²F/∂x_i∂x_j |_{[x]=[x̂]}.  (Exact because F quadratic in x̂.)
16. **Closed-form Hamiltonian** — Eq. (17):
    Hτ(Π)[y] = s([x̂,y];Π)/k_BT + (√(φ²+χ²))/2 − ln(Γ/4πk_BTΔt)/(2Δt) + c(x̂₀,y₀;Π)/τ,
    with **φ² ≡ (k+h)²/γ²**, **χ² ≡ h²/(γΓ)**, and s ≡ τ⁻¹S the time-normalized action.
17. **Boundary term** — Eq. (18): c(x̂₀,y₀;Π) ≡ −ln[ √(kh/(2πk_BT(k+h))) · 2(φ+√(φ²+χ²))/(φ+√(φ²+χ²)) ] + H(x̂₀,y₀;Π)/k_BT. (τ-independent → negligible at large τ.)
18. **Hidden-trajectory accuracy criterion** — Eq. (19): ⟨(x̂(t)−x(t))²⟩ ≪ 1, with ⟨·⟩ ≡ lim_{τ→∞} τ⁻¹∫_{−τ/2}^{τ/2} dt (·).
19. **Analytic MAP error** — Eq. (20): ⟨(x̂(t)−x(t))²⟩ = (1/βh*)[ 2/κ* − 1/√(κ*²+g*) ], with **κ* ≡ 1 + k*/h*** and **g* ≡ γ*/Γ***. Satisfied (small error) when k* ≪ h*.
20. **Ergodic τ→∞ Hamiltonian** — Eq. (A1): H_∞(Π) = −lim_{τ→∞}(1/τ)∫𝒟y P([y]|Π*) ln P([y]|Π) ≡ −⟨ln P([y]|Π)⟩*.
21. **Stationarity at truth** — Eq. (A2): ∂H_∞/∂Π|_{Π=Π*} = 0.
22. **Hessian of H_∞** — Eq. (A3): H_{ij} ≡ ∂²H_∞/∂Π_i∂Π_j|_* = ⟨(∂ln P/∂Π_i*)(∂ln P/∂Π_j*)⟩* (correlation-matrix form ⇒ non-negative-definite ⇒ Π* is a minimum).
23. **ε-expansion of Hτ** — Eq. (A4): Hτ(Π)[y] = φ₀(Π) + εφ₁(Π) + O(ε²), ε ≡ τ₀/τ (τ₀ = system characteristic timescale). **Eq. (A5):** φ₀(Π) = lim_{τ→∞}Hτ = H_∞(Π).
24. **Estimate expansion / perturbation** — Eqs (A6)–(A9): Π̂ = Π̂₀ + εΠ̂₁ + O(ε²) with ∂Hτ/∂Π|_{Π̂}=0 ⇒ ∂φ₀/∂Π|_{Π̂₀}=0 and (∂²φ₀/∂Π²|_{Π̂₀})·Π̂₁ + ∂φ₁/∂Π|_{Π̂₀}=0.
25. **First-order correction** — Eq. (A10): Π̂₁ = −[H⁻¹·∂φ₁/∂Π|_{Π=Π*}].
26. **Finite-τ estimate (τ⁻¹ scaling)** — Eq. (A11): Π̂ = Π* − ε[H⁻¹·∂φ₁/∂Π|_*] + O(ε²); since first-order term is τ-independent, error ∝ τ⁻¹. **Eq. (A12):** φ₁(Π) ≡ lim_{ε→0}[Hτ(Π)[y] − H_∞(Π)]/ε.

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Harmonic trap on hidden bead:** U_trap = (k/2)x² → force −kx. (k = trap stiffness, "stiffness of the harmonic potential.")
- **Linear coupling spring (linker):** U_spring = (h/2)(y−x)² → forces −h(x−y) on x and −h(y−x) on y. (h = linker/coupling stiffness.)
- **Viscous drag / friction:** −γẋ (hidden bead), −Γẏ (probe); γ, Γ friction coefficients.
- **Thermal (fluctuation–dissipation) noise:** white Gaussian ξ,η, variances 2γk_BT, 2Γk_BT; uncorrelated.
- Total mechanical energy (initial): H = (k/2)x² + (h/2)(y−x)² (Eq. 10). **Purely linear/harmonic** — no nonlinear or catch-bond terms; the authors note nonlinear/metastable potentials and elastic-network extensions are possible future work (WKB then approximate).

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** No — surface-mesh continuum cell model shares nothing with this 2-bead single-molecule inference toy. No cortex/membrane/turgor/junction content.
- **FF?** Weak/indirect. FF is fine-grained Brownian/Langevin filament dynamics with explicit friction, harmonic bonds, and thermal noise — the same overdamped Langevin + Onsager–Machlup machinery used here (Eqs 6–7, 11–12). Not a mechanism to implement, but the paper is a reference for (a) writing/validating overdamped Langevin bead-spring dynamics with correct FDT noise variances (2γk_BT), and (b) a possible *inference/calibration* tool: estimating hidden stiffness/friction of coarse-grained beads from observable trajectories. No force law or parameter here feeds an FF cortex.
- **TAG-validation?** No direct simulation validation target for ffn_cellsim's cell-mechanics gates — all numbers are nondimensional (units fixed k*=γ*=k_BT=1) toy-model results, not in-vitro biophysical measurements (tensions, moduli, motor speeds). Value to the KB is methodological (Bayesian hidden-parameter estimation, loss-of-precision transition, Onsager–Machlup/WKB), and as Miyazaki-lab provenance. Register as a **method/theory** source, not a quantitative validation oracle.
