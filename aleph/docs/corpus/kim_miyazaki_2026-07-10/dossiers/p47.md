## Summary
The paper asks how the actin cortex and the intrinsically **weak cis (lateral) binding** between cadherins jointly govern cadherin clustering at adherens junctions (AJs) — a quantitative, mechanistic gap left by prior experiments. The authors extend a previously published coarse-grained cadherin-cadherin model (Chen/Wu, ref [14]) into a **2D agent-based Brownian-dynamics model** of two apposed 1 µm × 1 µm membrane planes (periodic BCs) representing the AJ interface. Each cadherin is a diffusing disk (radius 2.5 nm) carrying one trans site and two cis sites (donor + receptor); binding is gated by distance + orientation cutoffs and fired stochastically via intrinsic on/off rates (p = 1 − exp(−kΔt)). The 3D actin cortex is projected to 2D line segments (length L_F = h_actin·tanφ) that both **corral** (reject cadherin crossing) and **tether** (bind cadherins with cad/actin on/off rates, immobilizing them). Four findings: (1) counter-intuitively, **weak cis affinity (high k_dis^cis) promotes LARGER clusters** at high cadherin density — small transient clusters seed slow growth because trapped central cadherins lose mobility; cluster lifetime scales ~[cad]². (2) F-actin **corralling reduces cadherin diffusivity to sub-diffusion** — higher [actin] shrinks mesh (sub-diffusive at all timescales), longer L_F forms closed non-escapable meshes (sub-diffusive only at long timescales), both shrinking clusters. (3) **Cad/actin tethering can facilitate OR inhibit clustering**: high affinity + small (20%) binding region → localizes cadherins, boosts cis+trans, large clusters; large (≥50%) binding region → cadherins strung along filaments as linear cis chains, trans dimerization depleted; intermediate affinity → cis vs actin competition for a limited cadherin pool → small unstable clusters. (4) F-actin **architecture/turnover** matters: vertical filaments (φ=75°) → small, punctate, uniformly-distributed clusters; parallel filaments (φ=15°) → larger, linear clusters; longer F-actin lifetime → more trans/cis and longer cluster lifetime (only when cad/actin binding is present). The model unifies apparently contradictory experimental observations (µm-sized clusters vs. ~50 nm clusters; low cadherin-actin colocalization) as different regimes of one mechanism.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** 2D **agent-based Brownian-dynamics** (Monte-Carlo-style stochastic reaction rules on top of Brownian displacement). Not a force-field MD — cadherins move by random diffusive steps and bind/unbind by probabilistic acceptance of intrinsic rates; F-actin is a **rigid immobile** projected line network (no deformation, no polymerization dynamics beyond stochastic turnover/replacement).
- **Geometry / domain:** Two flat planes (the two apposed cell membranes at the AJ), each **1 µm × 1 µm**, stacked in 3D but co-located in the 2D representation. **Periodic boundary conditions** in x and y. The 2D model = projection of a local 3D region onto the x–y plane.
- **Actin representation:** 3D cortex sub-membrane volume split into 3 depth regions — (i) cadherin **movement-obstruction** region (nearest membrane), (ii) cadherin **binding** region (deeper), (iii) **non-effective** region (ignored). F-actin projected to line segments; segment length L_F = h_actin·tanφ, where φ = filament tilt from membrane normal. Number of filaments adjusted to hold [actin] constant as φ varied.
- **Cadherin representation:** circular monomer, radius 2.5 nm, one trans site (center of column end), two cis sites (donor = red, receptor = white) on opposite lateral faces defining orientation θ. Trans-dimers diffuse together; cis-bonded pairs are **immobilized** (stop diffusing). Cadherins bound to F-actin are immobilized but may still rotate.
- **Time integration:** discrete time step Δt; per step each cadherin makes a random-direction displacement of fixed magnitude √(4DΔt) plus a restricted rotation about the membrane normal. Binding/unbinding events fired by comparing a uniform random number to p_ass / p_dis = 1 − exp(−kΔt). Simulations run to 100 s (reported snapshots) / until trans+cis event counts converge. Each condition averaged over **5 independent simulations**.
- **Algorithm outline (per step):** (1) attempt diffusive move; reject if it crosses an actin obstruction segment. (2) Check candidate partners within cutoff distance d_c and orientation Δθ < α_c; fire intrinsic association with p_ass. (3) On success, structurally align (trans → axes align, packing 90°; cis → axes set to column-diameter spacing, orientations synchronized, packing 180°). (4) Fire dissociation of existing bonds with p_dis. (5) Check cadherin↔F-actin binding-region proximity (< 3.5 nm) and fire cad/actin bind/unbind; refresh actin network per F-actin turnover lifetime.

## Governing Equations
1. **Bimolecular binding reaction:** A + B ⇌ C, with forward rate k₊ʲ and reverse rate k₋ʲ, j ∈ {trans, cis}. — overall association/dissociation of two cadherin monomers into a bound complex.
2. **Diffusion-limited decomposition:** A + B ⇌(k_diff / k_diff,b) A·B ⇌(k_ass / k_dis) C. — separates the reaction into a diffusion-controlled encounter (A·B) and the intrinsic (reaction-limited) binding step; A·B is the encounter state satisfying the distance + angle conditions.
3. **Intrinsic association probability:** p_ass^j = 1 − exp(−k_ass^j · Δt). — per-timestep probability of firing an intrinsic bond; the same functional form gives p_dis^j from k_dis^j.
4. **Projected F-actin length:** L_F = h_actin · tan φ. — 2D line-segment length from cortex effective-region thickness h_actin and filament tilt φ from membrane normal (vertical φ→0 gives short segments; parallel φ→90° gives long segments).
- **Diffusion step (in text, not numbered):** step size = √(4 D Δt) per timestep, random direction (2D Brownian).
- **Encounter conditions (in text):** bind eligible iff distance between binding sites d < d_c AND orientation difference Δθ < α_c (α_c = 30°). Cad/actin bind eligible iff cadherin-to-binding-region distance < d_c^{cad/actin} = 3.5 nm.
- **Cluster lifetime scaling (empirical result):** τ_cluster ∝ [cad]² (quadratic; Fig 2F solid line).

## Force Laws & Potentials
This model is **rule-based / kinetic, not force-based** — there is no explicit interaction potential (no LJ, no bending/stretching springs, no motor force-velocity). "Interactions" are geometric acceptance rules + stochastic rate constants:
- **Excluded volume / corralling:** hard rejection — a diffusive move that would cross an actin obstruction segment is discarded (infinite hard wall along filament, no soft potential). Cis-bonded/actin-bonded cadherins are pinned (zero mobility).
- **Trans bond:** formed if d < d_c^trans = 1.5 nm and Δθ < 30°, fired at rate k_ass^trans; post-bond structural alignment to packing angle θ_trans = 90°; trans-dimer diffuses as a unit.
- **Cis bond:** formed if d < d_c^cis = 3 nm and Δθ < 30°, fired at rate k_ass^cis; post-bond alignment to packing angle θ_cis = 180°, axes spaced at column diameter (5 nm); cis pair immobilized.
- **Cadherin–F-actin bond:** formed if cadherin within 3.5 nm of a binding-region filament segment, fired at k_ass^{cad/actin}; unbinds at k_dis^{cad/actin}; bound cadherin immobilized (may still rotate). Linker proteins (vinculin, α-catenin, EPLIN) are **implicit** — represented only through the three effective parameters {k_ass^{cad/actin}, k_dis^{cad/actin}, % binding region}. The catch-slip nature of the linker bonds is discussed but NOT explicitly modeled (constant k_dis used).
