## Summary
The paper asks whether mechanical stress in the plant cell wall is a plausible and sufficient cellular-scale cue to orient and pattern the cortical microtubule (MT) array — a hypothesis long correlated but never directly tested. The authors build a **2D discrete agent-based stochastic model** of cortical MTs (each MT = chain of 100 nm segments, three-state plus-end dynamics: growth/shrinkage/pause, plus rescue and catastrophe; slow constant minus-end shrinkage), with angle-dependent MT–MT collision rules (zippering < 40°, catastrophe/crossover > 40°). Local wall stress is coupled to MT dynamics through a **linear constitutive relation** in which the orientation-projected normal stress σ_MT (from the continuum stress-transformation equation) modulates ONE of four plus-end parameters (polymerization rate, depolymerization rate, catastrophe frequency, or rescue frequency). Realistic maximum-principal-stress fields are imported from a validated finite-element (FE) model of a growing Arabidopsis leaf trichome. Results: (i) anisotropic stress (≥ 1.5:1 threshold) aligns MTs along the principal-stress direction, with the order parameter reaching S_p ≈ 0.6–0.85; stress-modulated **polymerization rate and catastrophe frequency are the most potent** aligners (isotropic stress gives only random S_p ≈ 0.4–0.5). (ii) A time-varying stress field (instantaneous 90° rotation, or gradual rotation over 50–150 min) reorients an established array within ~50 min, with a transient homogenization (MT density ~halved) as bundles disassemble/reform — matching experimentally reported 10 min–2 h reorientation rates. (iii) A spatial stress-magnitude gradient plus a 2 µm isotropic apical zone reproduces the physiological trichome pattern: a microtubule-depletion zone (MDZ) at the apex, a dense transverse collar just below, and diminishing density toward the base — validated against confocal imaging of stage-4 Arabidopsis trichomes. Conclusion: stress-dependent modulation of plus-end MT dynamics is a plausible, sufficient feedback mechanism linking cell geometry/wall composition to cortical-array order.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** Discrete, agent-based, stochastic (Monte-Carlo-style) 2D simulation of cortical MT array. MTs modeled as **rigid polymers** (serially-connected 100 nm segments) confined to a 2D plane (justified because plant cortical MTs are membrane-tethered → 2D dynamics). NOT Brownian/Langevin dynamics — no explicit thermal force integration; dynamics are governed by stochastic state-switching rates + deterministic collision rules.
- **Implementation:** MATLAB R2020a, run on Purdue "Bell" supercomputing cluster (ITaP).
- **Time stepping:** Stochastic dynamic events (state switches, growth/shrink increments) updated **once per 0.001 min** (= 0.06 s). MT–MT collision checks performed **once per 0.05 min** (= 3 s). (Consistent with Allard-Wasteneys-Cytrynbaum 2010, ref [66].)
- **Simulation duration:** 100–200 min typical; reorientation runs to 350 min.
- **Domain / geometry:**
  - Square: 10 × 10 µm, periodic BC in both x and y (checked vs 20 × 20 µm — no finite-size effect).
  - Rectangular: 30 × 10 µm (x = long/cell-axis, y = transverse), PBC in y; in x, one of {catastrophe-inducing, reflective, repulsive, periodic(control)} BC.
- **Boundary conditions (x-edges of rectangular domain):**
  - *Catastrophe-inducing:* growing MT hitting boundary switches to shrinkage.
  - *Reflective:* MT reflects with angle = incidence angle.
  - *Repulsive:* MT pauses on contact with boundary.
  - *Periodic:* control only (not used with stress gradient, to avoid abrupt stress jumps across the seam).
- **Nucleation:** de novo at rate **10 µm⁻²·min⁻¹**, random location, independent of existing MTs. Unlimited free-tubulin pool assumed.
- **Minus end:** always slow constant shrinkage (no dynamic instability).
- **Plus end:** three-state dynamics (growth ↔ pause ↔ shrinkage) with rescue & catastrophe.
- **Collision rules (deterministic, angle-dependent):** contact angle < 40° → zippering (the incoming MT reorients to align with the barrier MT); > 40° → catastrophe OR crossover, with catastrophe probability set between 0.2 and 0.8. MT–MT spacing set to 25–50 nm (from MAP2/tau and carrot-MAP cross-bridge observations).
- **Stress coupling:** maximum-principal-stress values imported from a validated FE model of a polarized (trichome) plant cell (Yanagisawa 2015, ref [32]); spatially discretized onto mesh elements of the sim domain. Shear stress assumed negligible (per FE model). Stress modulates exactly ONE plus-end dynamic parameter at a time (linear constitutive law).
- **Analysis:** nematic order parameter S_p vs a reference direction θ_p; time constants τ_p (50%), τ_p,2 (75%), τ_p,3 (87.5% of steady state); local S_p(z) and MT segment density I(z) in 15 sub-regions (2 µm bins) along x.
- **Experiment (validation only):** live confocal imaging of stage-4 Arabidopsis thaliana trichomes, mCherry:MBD marker, spinning-disk (Yokogawa CSU-X1) on Zeiss Observer.Z1, 100× 1.46-NA oil objective, 561 nm excitation, Evolve 512 camera; z-stacks analyzed in ImageJ; 10–12 DAG.

## Governing Equations

**(1) Orientation-dependent local stress (continuum stress transformation, normal stress on an MT at angle θ):**
```
σ_MT = (σ_x + σ_y)/2  +  (σ_x − σ_y)/2 · cos(2θ)
```
Meaning: the normal stress projected onto an MT oriented at angle θ (relative to +x) given the in-plane normal stresses σ_x, σ_y; shear terms neglected. This scalar σ_MT is the input to the linear constitutive law that modulates a plus-end dynamic parameter.

**(2) Nematic order parameter S_p (extent of MT alignment):**
```
S_p = [ Σ_{i=1..N_MT} l_i ( cos²(θ_i − θ_p) − sin²(θ_i − θ_p) ) ]  /  [ Σ_{i=1..N_MT} l_i ]
```
equivalently S_p = Σ l_i cos[2(θ_i − θ_p)] / Σ l_i. Meaning: length-weighted alignment of all MT segments relative to reference direction θ_p; S_p = 1 → perfectly aligned to θ_p, S_p = 0 → isotropic, S_p → −1 → aligned perpendicular to θ_p (used as S_p,y after 90° stress rotation).

**(3) Linear constitutive relation (functional form given verbally, not as a numbered display eq):** each of the four mechanosensitive plus-end parameters {growth rate v_p^g, shrinkage rate v_d^s+, catastrophe frequency, rescue frequency} is a **linear function of σ_MT**. Direction of effect: increasing tensile σ_MT → ↑ growth rate, ↑ rescue frequency, ↓ depolymerization (shrinkage) rate, ↓ catastrophe frequency. (The authors note the true experimental relations, Akiyoshi 2010 ref [50], are closer to exponential; they deliberately use a linear approximation.)

## Force Laws & Potentials
This is a **kinetic/rate-based** model, not a force-field model — there are no explicit interaction potentials, bending moduli, or Langevin forces. "Force" enters only as **wall tensile stress** projected onto MT orientation (Eq 1), which then linearly biases stochastic transition rates (Eq 3). The MTs are treated as rigid (no bending energy), and MT–MT interactions are deterministic geometric rules (zippering/catastrophe/crossover) rather than pairwise forces. The proposed (unmodeled, conceptual) molecular sensor is a **catch-bond** integral-membrane receptor whose wall-dissociation rate decreases under higher tensile force (Williamson 1990 hypothesis, ref [45]; catch-bond concept ref [65]) — described qualitatively in the Discussion, not implemented.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Weak/indirect. DCM is a surface-mesh continuum cell model; this paper is a 2D cortical-MT ordering model with no membrane-mesh or turgor mechanics implemented (stress is imported, not computed). BUT it is a Taeyoon Kim lab microtubule model — the three-state MT dynamics rate constants and nucleation rate could inform DCM's microtubule compartment (the native cell uses `--microtubules`). Cortical MT ordering / stress-feedback is conceptually adjacent to cortex organization.
- **FF?** Moderate-to-yes (reference/parameter donor). FF is fine-grained filament-FEM. This paper's plus-end **three-state dynamic-instability rate constants** (f_gp=0.47, f_gs=0.97, f_pg=0.51, f_ps=0.24, f_sg=0.87, f_sp=0.23 min⁻¹; v_grow=3.69, v_shrink+=5.80, v_shrink−=0.53 µm/min; nucleation 10 µm⁻²·min⁻¹; 100 nm segment), the 40° zippering/catastrophe collision rule, and 25–50 nm inter-MT spacing are directly reusable literature values for an FF microtubule model. Note these are PLANT cortical MTs (Arabidopsis, Shaw 2003) — animal-cell values may differ, so treat as plant-specific.
