## Summary
The inverted emulsion (transfer) method builds giant liposomes (cell models) by centrifuging lipid-monolayer-coated water-in-oil droplets through an oil/water interface, where they acquire a second (outer) monolayer to become a bilayer vesicle; it is prized for high encapsulation efficiency of concentrated proteins under physiological buffer, but its **lamellarity** (number of bilayers) had never been quantified. The authors developed an epifluorescence-microscopy assay: liposomes were made with egg PC doped with 0.1 mol% fluorescent lipid (rhodamine-PE or Oregon-Green-PE), Z-scanned, and the peak membrane fluorescence intensity around the equatorial circumference was measured and plotted vs. diameter. Plots fall into **discrete quantized bands**; a single analytic curve I(r) (accounting for out-of-focus fluorescence leakage as a Gaussian of width σ) fits the lowest band, and integer multiples 2×,3×,4×,5× mark bi/tri/tetra/penta-lamellar populations (1.5×,2.5×,… dashed thresholds classify each vesicle). The lowest band was independently proven unilamellar by an α-hemolysin pore assay (pore only spans a single ~5.2 nm bilayer): adding α-hemolysin triggered inner-buffer salt exchange → encapsulated G-actin polymerized into PEG-depletion bundles in 94.3% of lowest-band vs 8.7% of higher-band liposomes. Result: the inverted emulsion method yields **~97% (>90% in every condition) unilamellar** giant liposomes — 98.0% vs 49.8% for the hydration method — and lamellarity is essentially independent of lipid concentration in oil (0.1–10 mM; vesicle count rises ~100× while unilamellar fraction stays ~92–98%), lipid composition (±20% DOPE/DOPG, ±50% cholesterol), and encapsulated cargo (50 µM F-actin, 25 mg/mL BSA, Xenopus egg extract).

## Model & Methods
- **Type:** Wet-lab in-vitro reconstitution + quantitative optical microscopy + one analytic geometric-optics fitting model. **No numerical simulation, no dynamics integrator.** The only "model" is a closed-form fluorescence-intensity-vs-radius curve (Eq. 1).
- **Liposome fabrication (inverted emulsion / transfer method, after Noireaux & Libchaber; Pautot):**
  1. Lipids dissolved in chloroform → dry film under vacuum overnight; stored ≤2 weeks.
  2. Film dissolved in 1 mL mineral oil at 80°C by repeated heat/vortex; bath-sonicated 90 min at 60°C, 60 W; vortexed, cooled, rested overnight dark; used ≤2 days.
  3. 200 µL lipid-oil chilled on ice >15 min; + 20 µL inner solution; vortex 30 s → water-in-oil emulsion; ice 5 min (inner monolayer self-assembly).
  4. 150 µL emulsion layered on 1 mL outer solution in glass tube; ice 5 min (outer monolayer at oil/water interface).
  5. Centrifuge **12,000 × g, 30 min, 4°C** → droplets pushed through interface, acquiring second monolayer → liposomes at tube bottom; oil + 500 µL buffer aspirated; 500 µL liposome suspension retained.
  6. Xenopus egg extract variant: 10 µL extract + 200 µL lipid-oil, gentle vortex 10 s; ice 5 min; 150 µL on 1 mL XB buffer; ice 5 min; **gentle 100 × g, 15 min, 4°C**.
- **Osmotic balancing:** inner solution +150 mM sucrose +350 mM glucose; outer +500 mM glucose so osmolality matches across membrane (inner slightly denser → liposomes sediment to coverslip).
- **Imaging (lamellarity):** custom inverted microscope, 100× PlanApo NA 1.40 oil, EM-CCD (Andor iXon3 DU-897E), 100 W Hg epifluorescence, motorized XYZ stage, LabVIEW control. Flow chamber (~8 mm gap, ~40 µL) on Pluronic-F127-passivated silicone coverslip. Liposomes settle >30 min; Z-scan bottom→top at **5 µm/s, 25 fps, 25±1°C**, 0.2 µm/frame slices.
- **Image analysis pipeline:** subtract camera background → flat-field correct by dividing by homogeneous 0.1 µM rhodamine-B image → compute cross-sectional area per Z-frame → pick max-area frame = equatorial plane → find center of mass → cast radial rays every 1° → take peak F.I. and its XY position per ray → circumference intensity ~homogeneous & Gaussian for clean vesicles (aggregate-bearing vesicles show sharp peaks, Fig. S1, discarded) → mean circumferential F.I. plotted vs diameter.
- **Lamellarity counting (separate scope):** Nikon DIAPHOT 300, 60× NA 1.40 Ph4, CCD; phase-contrast Z-scan −25→100 µm at **20 µm/s, 30 fps, 50 fields of view, 24±2°C**; spherical liposomes >2 µm auto-detected/counted in LabVIEW.
- **α-hemolysin unilamellarity assay:** encapsulate 10 µM G-actin (10% Alexa-488) + 10% w/v PEG-20k in G buffer; add high-salt buffer + α-hemolysin externally (final 0.25 mg/mL, osmotically balanced); pore forms only across a single bilayer → salt influx → actin polymerizes → PEG depletion force bundles filaments (visible). Score bundle presence after 120 min.
- **Statistics:** χ² test, Fisher's exact test, Z-test for lamellarity-fraction comparisons; ***p<0.001.

## Governing Equations

**(Eq. 1) Apparent equatorial membrane fluorescence intensity of a unilamellar liposome of radius r:**

I(r) = I₀ ∫_{−arccos((r−δ)/r)}^{+arccos((r−δ)/r)} exp( − r² sin²θ / (2σ²) ) · r dθ

- Meaning: the measured circumferential peak intensity vs vesicle radius, integrating the membrane fluorescence over the angular window that falls within one pixel of the equatorial focal plane, weighted by a Gaussian out-of-focus leakage kernel of width σ. Explains why intensity rises with diameter then plateaus (large vesicles: the in-focus arc length saturates relative to pixel/focal-depth geometry).
- Symbols: r = liposome radius; I₀ = fluorescence intensity of a unilamellar membrane **per unit surface area**; δ = pixel size of image (fixed 0.143 µm); σ = Gaussian width = out-of-focus fluorescence-leakage depth (focal-depth proxy); θ = angular coordinate of the radial ray. Integration limits ±arccos((r−δ)/r) define the arc within one pixel δ of the equator.
- Fitted free parameters: I₀ and σ (fit to lowest-intensity band). Result σ = 0.913 µm.

**(Eq. 1-kernel) Out-of-focus leakage weighting:** exp(−z²/2σ²), z = distance from focal plane. This Gaussian is the physical assumption underlying Eq. 1 (substituting z ≈ r sinθ gives the integrand).

**Lamellarity classification rule (not an equation but the decision law):** vesicle assigned lamellarity n if its F.I. lies between (n−0.5)·I(r) and (n+0.5)·I(r); i.e. below 1.5·I(r)=unilamellar, 1.5–2.5·I(r)=bilamellar, 2.5–3.5·=trilamellar, … (dashed thresholds at 1.5,2.5,3.5,4.5,5.5 ×I(r); solid multiples at 1,2,3,4,5 ×I(r)). Basis: each added bilayer adds one I₀-worth of fluorescence.

**(Eq. calibration) I₀ ∝ Iᵦ (linear):** the fitted I₀ is linearly correlated with the bulk fluorescence intensity Iᵦ of the lipid-oil mixture (measured by spectrophotometer) at low fluorescent-lipid fraction (≤~0.2 mol%) — allows lamellarity determination of a single vesicle without per-chamber cluster fitting (Fig. S8).

## Force Laws & Potentials
No mechanical force laws, potentials, or motor/bond kinetics are modeled — this is an imaging/geometry paper. Two physical-force mechanisms appear only as experimental tools:
- **Depletion (Asakura–Oosawa) force:** 10% w/v PEG-20k drives entropic depletion attraction that bundles polymerized actin filaments for visualization (ref. 46 = Asakura & Oosawa 1954). No functional form given.
- **α-hemolysin transmembrane pore:** heptameric pore, pore diameter ~2.8 nm (passes ions, blocks proteins), stem length ~5.2 nm ≈ single bilayer thickness — used as a molecular ruler for single-bilayer (unilamellar) detection. No energetics modeled.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Weak-yes (peripheral). DCM models a membrane + cortex shell; this paper is the canonical validation that inverted-emulsion GUVs are unilamellar and that actin cortices/actomyosin gels can be reconstituted inside cell-sized vesicles (cites Pontani/Sykes actin-cortex-in-liposome, Carvalho/Sykes cortical-tension GUVs). Useful as a **membrane/experimental-baseline reference** and to justify single-bilayer membrane assumptions, but contributes no mechanical parameters (no tension, modulus, or force law).
- **FF?** Weak-yes (peripheral). FF reconstitutes actin filaments/bundles; this paper encapsulates 50 µM F-actin and shows PEG-depletion actin bundling inside vesicles — an in-vitro confinement analog, and the Miyazaki-lab lineage relevant to FF's fine-grained actin. But no filament mechanics, persistence length, or kinetic constants are measured here.
- **TAG-validation?** No (not a mechanistic force/parameter source). The only quantitative outputs are an imaging-geometry fit (σ=0.913 µm, δ=0.143 µm) and lamellarity statistics — method/optics numbers, not physiological force/rate constants for the simulator. Register as a **method/reference** source (unilamellarity of inverted-emulsion GUVs; α-hemolysin pore metrics 2.8/5.2 nm) rather than a validation-gate target. Author-link: Makito Miyazaki corpus.
