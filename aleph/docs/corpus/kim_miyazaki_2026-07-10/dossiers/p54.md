## Summary
This is a systematic in-vitro study (Miyazaki lab) of how phospholipid composition controls the **production yield (number)** and **size distribution (diameter)** of cell-sized giant liposomes made by the **inverted emulsion method** (a.k.a. droplet transfer method; Pautot 2003) — the workhorse technique for encapsulating purified proteins/cell-free extracts into artificial cell models. Using natural egg PC (from chicken egg yolk) as the base membrane, they replaced a fraction of egg PC with lipids differing in net charge and hydrophilic-headgroup molecular weight, and quantified liposome number and diameter for two variants of the technique: **natural sedimentation** (gravity-driven droplet transfer) and **centrifugal sedimentation** (12,000×g). Main findings: (1) A **10% replacement with singly-negatively-charged lipids (DOPA, DOPG, DOPS) boosts yield ~9–14× (~10-fold)** in the natural method; DOPG shows the same ~10× peak at 10% in the centrifugal method (with a sharp non-monotonic optimum — further replacement collapses yield). (2) **Smaller headgroups** (PE, PA) slightly enhance yield in both methods. (3) **Larger headgroups** (Biotinyl-Cap-PE, DGS-NTA(Ni), PEG2000-PE) slightly enhance yield AND shift the size distribution toward **larger liposomes** in the natural method (probability of >10 µm liposomes correlates positively with additive head MW), but these head-size effects vanish under strong centrifugal force. (4) Water-in-oil droplet size distributions are unchanged by lipid composition → large-head lipids act by raising the droplet→liposome **transformation efficiency** of larger droplets, not by enlarging droplets. (5) For egg PC, **lower temperature raises yield** (~2× from 16→0 °C; opposite to POPC), and a **10-min monolayer incubation** is sufficient (0-min still gives ~75% of saturated yield). A mechanistic cartoon (Fig. 7) proposes three yield levers: abundant lipid supply, charge repulsion between the liposome bilayer and the interface monolayer, and water intrusion at the liposome "neck" facilitated by head-size (steric hindrance / negative-curvature stabilization).

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** purely **experimental in-vitro reconstitution** — no simulation, no governing dynamical model. Only two *empirical phenomenological fit curves* (exponential relaxation forms) are used to summarize temperature/time trends. Quantification is by optical microscopy + manual/automated liposome counting and sizing.
- **Technique:** inverted emulsion method. Water-in-oil (W/O) droplets stabilized by a lipid monolayer are transferred across an oil→water interface (which bears a second lipid monolayer), zipping the two monolayers into a bilayer → unilamellar liposome. Transfer driven by density mismatch (gravity = "natural sedimentation") or centrifugation (12,000×g = "centrifugal sedimentation").
- **Lipid-oil mixture:** lipids dissolved in dehydrated chloroform at 20 mM, mixed to target molar ratio, chloroform evaporated overnight in vacuum desiccator, dry film redissolved in 1 mL mineral oil at 80 °C/30 min then bath-sonicated 60 °C/60 W/90 min; stored dark overnight. **Total lipid in oil fixed at 1 mM.**
- **Natural sedimentation protocol (Figs. 2,3):** 96-well microplate (φ=6.7 mm). 50 µL outer solution → overlay 50 µL lipid-oil, incubate >10 min to build interface monolayer. Separately, 50 µL lipid-oil + 5 µL inner solution (with R18 dye) vortexed 30 s → W/O droplets. 20 µL emulsion skimmed from 2–3 mm below surface, gently placed on the monolayer; incubate >20 min horizontal, vibration-free → observe on inverted microscope.
- **Centrifugal sedimentation protocol (Figs. 4,5,6):** 200 µL lipid-oil incubated (various T or on ice) 15 min; +20 µL inner solution; vortex 30 s → droplets; incubate 5 min; skim 150 µL, layer onto 1 mL outer solution in glass tube preincubated; incubate to form interface monolayer (0–30 min); **centrifuge 12,000×g, 30 min** at set T (2 °C for Fig 4D/E; 4 °C for Figs 5,6). Remove oil + upper outer solution by aspiration; gently mix by 30 pipetting strokes (1 stroke/s; verified not to alter size dist., Fig. S2); perfuse 150 µL into a Pluronic-F127-blocked flow chamber; settle >30 min; observe.
- **Imaging / counting:** Z-scans. Bright-field (Figs 2,3,6): −10→100 µm at 1-µm steps, 5 (or 10) fields of view (172.9×172.9 µm² or 119.4×119.4 µm²), manual detection in Fiji/ImageJ. Phase-contrast (Figs 4,5): −25→100 µm at 0.67-µm steps, 40 or 200 fields (204.2×153.1 µm²), automated detection in custom LabVIEW program. **Only spherical liposomes with diameter >5 µm counted.**
- **Microscopes:** custom-built inverted (×40/0.60 Ph2), iXon3 EM-CCD (natural method); Nikon DIAPHOT 300 (×60 NA1.40 oil Ph4), CCD-300-RCX (centrifugal phase-contrast); Olympus IX73 (×60/1.40), iXon3 (Fig 6).
- **Statistics:** two-sided Welch's t-test (MATLAB). All experiments ≥2 replicates.
- **BCs / geometry:** cell-sized emulsion droplets in bulk mineral oil / aqueous phases; planar oil–water interface; no confinement geometry beyond the droplet/interface.

## Governing Equations
No physics governing equations (no Langevin/BD, no force law, no constitutive law). Only two empirical fitting curves for trend summaries:

1. **(Fig. 4B) Yield vs centrifugation temperature:**  `y = a·exp(−b·x)`, with **a = 68.6, b = 0.0855** (x = temperature in °C, y = liposomes per mm²). Monotone decrease of yield with rising T — meaning: colder → more egg-PC liposomes.
2. **(Fig. 4D) Yield vs pre-centrifugation incubation time:**  `y = c − a·exp(−b·x)`, with **a = 25.6, b = 0.249, c = 102** (x = incubation time in min, y = liposomes per mm²). Saturating rise plateauing near c≈102; at x=0, y≈76.4 ≈ 75% of plateau — meaning: 10-min monolayer incubation suffices.

## Force Laws & Potentials
None quantitative. Only a *qualitative* mechanistic hypothesis (Fig. 7) for what sets yield — three proposed "key factors," no functional forms:
- (i) Abundant lipid supply to the oil–water interface (cites Chiba 2014; yield rises with oil lipid conc. up to 5 mM, above which multilamellar contamination sets in).
- (ii) Electrostatic **repulsion between charged lipids** on the nascent liposome bilayer and on the interface monolayer (and among lipids in the oil), thinning the connecting "neck."
- (iii) **Water intrusion at the liposome "neck"** below the interface, facilitated by (a) steric hindrance of large headgroups or (b) stabilization of strong negative curvature by small headgroups — both narrow the neck and promote detachment.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Marginal-NO. DCM models the cell as a surface mesh (membrane + actomyosin cortex + turgor + nucleus). This paper is about *forming* protein-free lipid vesicles by droplet transfer — it has no cortex, no active mechanics, and no quantitative membrane-elastic parameters (no bending modulus, no tension). The only transferable concepts are qualitative membrane-curvature/headgroup notions (Fig. 7) and cell-sized (5–40 µm) vesicle geometry — not a DCM validation target.
- **FF?** NO. No cytoskeletal filaments, motors, crosslinkers, adhesion clutches, or any force law relevant to the Cytosim-style fine-grained engine. Nothing to port or parity-check.
- **TAG-validation?** Weak-YES for provenance/method cataloguing only. It supplies concrete Miyazaki-lab reconstitution protocol constants (inverted emulsion: 1 mM lipid-in-oil, 12,000×g/30 min, A50 buffer, sucrose/glucose density steps, cell-sized 5–40 µm liposomes, egg PC mean MW 770.123 Da) and empirical yield/size trends — useful as *methods/reference metadata* if the project ever reconstitutes an encapsulated cytoskeleton in a liposome, but it carries NO mechanistic simulator validation targets (no tensions, moduli, motor speeds, bond kinetics, or persistence lengths).
