## Summary

The paper asks how the **deformability (buckling) and cross-linker connectivity of microtubules (MTs)** regulate dynein-driven extension of an MT bundle that mimics an elongating neuronal axon — a question prior axon-outgrowth models ignored by treating MTs as infinitely rigid rods. The authors build an **agent-based Brownian-dynamics model**: MTs (coarse-grained into serially bonded cylindrical elements with extensional + bending stiffness + volume exclusion), transient cross-linkers (two-element, Bell force-dependent unbinding), and dynein-like motors (cargo end permanently on an MT plus end; walking end binds a neighboring MT and walks toward its minus end with force-dependent walk/unbind rates from Monzon 2018) confined in a long cylinder (radius 2 µm, length 20 µm, thickness w_dom = 0.6 µm). The moving boundary (growth cone/axon terminal) advances against a resisting force F_R only when the bundle pushes hard enough; bundle strain ε = ΔL_dom / 20 µm is the extension readout. **Key results:** (1) Without cross-linkers, ε peaks at ~100 s then declines as MTs near the extending end buckle (compressive energy falls, bending energy rises within ~20 s), while motor speed/force stay steady — so decline is buckling-driven, not motor fatigue. (2) ε_end depends on tubulin concentration C_MT (≈0 below 20 µM, then ~linear rise), MT length L_MT (rises then slightly falls), motor density R_M (rises, saturating due to limited plus ends), and domain thickness w_dom (inversely — narrow domain gives side support against buckling). (3) Bundle extension is **biphasic in cross-linking density R_X**, maximal at the reference R_X = 0.25 µm⁻¹: too few cross-linkers → MTs buckle easily (low Euler F_cr); too many → MT mobility is friction-throttled (motors feel higher force, walk slower). (4) Stiffer MTs (higher persistence length l_p = 3–10 mm vs reference 1 mm) resist buckling, raise ε_end, and **abolish the biphasic R_X dependence** at low R_X (though high-R_X friction penalty persists). The work provides a mechanistic buckling/connectivity account of axonal outgrowth.

## Model & Methods

- **Model type:** Agent-based / particle-based **Brownian (Langevin, inertialess overdamped) dynamics** of discrete cytoskeletal elements. This is the Taeyoon Kim cylindrical-element framework (same force-distribution scheme as Jung, Murrell & Kim 2015, ref 46).
- **Elements:** All species are built from **cylindrical elements** (rigid cylinders with nodes at ends):
  - **MT** = chain of serially connected cylindrical elements (coarse-grained bead-rod-like), equilibrium element length r₀,MT = 280 nm, diameter (r_c,MT) = 25 nm (MT diameter). Has extensional + bending stiffness → deformable/bucklable.
  - **Cross-linker** = two cylindrical elements joined at their center; two free ends bind MTs. Element length r₀,X = 20 nm. Has extensional stiffness + two bending stiffnesses (κ_b,X1 for the two-element internal angle θ₀,X1 = 0; κ_b,X2 for the element-to-bound-MT angle θ₀,X2 = π/2).
  - **Motor (dynein-like)** = two cylindrical elements ("cargo" + "walking" domains). Element length r₀,M = 10 nm. Extensional stiffness only, **no bending stiffness**. Cargo end permanently anchored to an MT plus end (≤ 1 motor per plus end); walking end binds another MT, walks toward its minus end, unbinds force-dependently.
- **Integrator / timestep:** Forward Euler update of positions from the overdamped Langevin velocity; **Δt = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ s**.
- **Domain / geometry / BCs:** Cylinder, radius 2 µm, initial length L_dom = 20 µm. One circular end **fixed** (stationary boundary; minus ends of some MTs clamped there). Opposite end is a **moving boundary** (growth cone/axon terminal) that advances (increasing L_dom) only when bundle pushing force exceeds resisting force F_R. Effective bundle thickness set by w_dom (default 0.6 µm ≈ axonal MT-bundle thickness). MT plus ends all initially point +z toward the moving end (uniform polarity).
- **Curvature metric:** per-node discrete curvature (Eq. 9); a node with curvature > **0.2 rad/µm** is counted as "curved"/buckled (threshold from Memet 2018, ref 49).
- **Energy accounting:** extensional (split into tensile vs compressive by sign of r − r₀) and bending energies tracked separately per species.
- **Algorithm / procedure:**
  1. **Bundle formation phase (1 s):** start from a monomer pool of MT elements; stochastic **nucleation** (draw one element into space) then **plus-end polymerization** (add elements); **no MT disassembly**. Cross-linkers and motors bind during this phase; motors do NOT walk yet. After element pool depletion, mean MT length ≈ 7 µm (reference).
  2. **Measurement phase (1 hr):** motors begin walking toward MT minus ends; ε, energies, curvature, motor speed/force measured.
- **Binding sites:** each MT element has **35 equispaced binding sites** (from ~8 nm tubulin-dimer size × 13 protofilaments); up to **13** cross-linkers or motors can occupy one site simultaneously. Walking displaces the walking-end binding site by **~8 nm** per step.
- **Statistics:** two-tailed unpaired t-test, MATLAB 2021b; n = 10–50 realizations per condition (per figure).

## Governing Equations

1. **Overdamped Langevin (force balance) — Eq. (1):**
   `F_i − ξ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_i^T = 0`
   Deterministic force + drag + thermal force sum to zero (inertialess) for element i.

2. **Fluctuation–dissipation for the stochastic force — Eq. (2):**
   `⟨F_i^T(t) F_j^T(t)⟩ = (2 k_B T ξ_i δ_ij / Δt) δ`
   White thermal noise variance set by drag ξ_i and temperature; δ_ij Kronecker delta, δ second-order (identity) tensor.

3. **Drag coefficient of a cylindrical element — Eq. (3):**
   `ξ_i = 3π μ r_{c,i} · (3 + 2 r_{0,i}/r_{c,i}) / 5`
   Approximate translational drag for a cylinder (Clift, Grace & Weber, ref 44); μ = medium viscosity, r_{0,i} = element equilibrium length, r_{c,i} = element diameter.

4. **Position update (forward Euler) — Eq. (4):**
   `r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (dr_i/dt) Δt = r_i(t) + (1/ξ_i)(F_i + F_i^T) Δt`

5. **Extensional (spring) harmonic potential — Eq. (5):**
   `U_s = ½ κ_s (r − r₀)²`
   κ_s extensional stiffness, r instantaneous element length, r₀ equilibrium length.

6. **Bending harmonic potential — Eq. (6):**
   `U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ₀)²`
   κ_b bending stiffness; θ, θ₀ instantaneous and equilibrium angles.

7. **Volume-exclusion (repulsion) potential between overlapping MT elements — Eq. (7):**
   `U_r = ½ κ_{r,MT} (r₁₂ − r_{c,MT})²   if r₁₂ < r_{c,MT};  0  if r₁₂ ≥ r_{c,MT}`
   κ_{r,MT} repulsion strength, r₁₂ minimal distance between two MT elements, r_{c,MT} = MT diameter threshold.

8. **Bell's-equation force-dependent cross-linker unbinding — Eq. (8):**
   `k_{u,X} = k⁰_{u,X} · exp( F_s x_{u,X} / (k_B T) )`
   Zero-force rate k⁰_{u,X}; F_s = spring force magnitude on the cross-linker element; x_{u,X} = force sensitivity (mechanical compliance / bond length). (Bell 1978, ref 47.)

9. **Discrete MT curvature — Eq. (9):**
   `curvature = 2 cos⁻¹( a_i · b_i / (|a_i| |b_i|) ) / ( |a_i| + |b_i| )`
   with a_i = r_{i+1} − r_i, b_i = r_i − r_{i−1} (three adjacent nodes). Threshold 0.2 rad/µm ⇒ "curved."

10. **Euler critical buckling load — Eq. (10):**
    `F_cr ∝ κ_{b,MT} / L_uns²`
    Critical compressive load ∝ MT bending stiffness / (unsupported length)². With cross-linkers, L_uns = spacing between adjacent cross-linking points; more cross-linkers ⇒ shorter L_uns ⇒ higher F_cr ⇒ less buckling.

11. **Persistence length ↔ bending stiffness — Eq. (11):**
    `l_p = κ_{b,MT} / (k_B T)`
    Used to convert the tuned MT bending stiffness to persistence length (reference l_p = 1 mm).

## Force Laws & Potentials

- **Extensional force:** F = −κ_s (r − r₀) (from Eq. 5) — applies to MT elements (κ_s,MT), cross-linker elements (κ_s,X), motor elements (κ_s,M — note the paper writes "r_{s,M}" for motor extensional stiffness, evidently a typo for κ_{s,M}).
- **Bending force/torque:** from Eq. 6 — MT κ_b,MT (θ₀,MT = 0); cross-linker κ_b,X1 (θ₀,X1 = 0) and κ_b,X2 (θ₀,X2 = π/2); motors have **no** bending stiffness.
- **Volume exclusion:** repulsive harmonic (Eq. 7), MT–MT only, activated when centerline distance < MT diameter (25 nm).
- **Cross-linker unbinding:** Bell slip-bond, k_{u,X} = k⁰_{u,X} exp(F_s x_{u,X}/k_BT) (Eq. 8; Fig. 1E shows k_{u,X} rising from ~1 s⁻¹ at F_X = 0 to ~4.5 s⁻¹ at F_X = 60 pN — a slip-bond, monotonic, super-linear).
- **Cross-linker binding:** constant rate k_{b,X}.
- **Motor cargo end:** permanent bond to MT plus end (unbreakable), ≤ 1 per plus end.
- **Motor walking end:** binds at constant rate k_{b,M}; walks toward minus end at force-dependent rate k_{w,M} (walking speed = k_{w,M} × 8 nm); unbinds at force-dependent rate k_{u,M}. Force-dependences of k_{w,M} and k_{u,M} adopted from Monzon et al. 2018 (mammalian cytoplasmic dynein multimotor assay, ref 48; Fig. 1F): walking speed sigmoidal, ~0 for large assisting/hindering negative F_M, plateauing at ~1.1 µm/s near F_M ≈ 0 to +3 pN; k_{u,M} near 0 until F_M ≳ 0 then rising to ~1 s⁻¹ (walking-end detachment increases under load); F_M defined positive when the motor is pulled toward the MT plus end.
