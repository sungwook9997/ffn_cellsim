## 1. Summary
This is a JoVE video-article protocol describing a 3D cancer cell spheroid invasion assay. Single-cell suspensions (500–1,000 cells per 20 µl drop) are aggregated into spheroids via the hanging-drop method over 72 hr, then embedded in a 1:1 mix of growth-factor-reduced basement membrane materials (Matrigel) and type I collagen, and cancer cell egress into the surrounding 3D matrix is monitored by inverted microscopy (20x) at 0/24/48 hr and quantitated in ImageJ as either maximal invasive distance or total invaded area. A panel of 13 cell lines was scored for sphere-forming ability and invasion: invasive lines (4T1, E0771, U-87 MG) showed strong egress; epithelial/less-aggressive lines (MCF-7, BT-474, MCF10DCIS) formed compact spheres but did not invade; mesenchymal MDA-MB-231 failed to form spheroids (attributed to reduced E/N/P-cadherin). The assay distinguishes collective (E0771) vs. individual/rapid (U-87 MG) invasion modes.

## 2. Problem & motivation
Cancer cell motility/invasiveness predicts metastatic potential, but most traditional assays (scratch-wound, transwell) are 2D and/or measure a single endpoint, missing 3D-relevant features like protease-dependent matrix remodeling and collective vs. single-cell migration. The goal is a streamlined, physiologically relevant 3D assay that can compare invasion across cell lines and under perturbations (drugs, coculture, altered ECM composition).

## 3. Methods / model
Purely experimental wet-lab protocol — no mathematical/physical model.
- **Cell lines:** 13 lines incl. breast (MCF-7, BT-474, MCF10DCIS.com, MDA-MB-231, SK-BR-3), mammary mouse (4T1, E0771), prostate (LNCaP, PC-3), pancreas (COLO 357 PL, PANC-1), epidermoid (A-431), glioblastoma (U-87 MG).
- **Spheroid generation:** hanging-drop, 20 µl drops, 500–1,000 cells/drop, 40 drops/lid, 37 °C, 72 hr (some lines 48 hr or >72 hr).
- **3D embedding matrix:** 100 µl basement membrane materials (Matrigel; laminin, entactin, type IV collagen) + 100 µl cold (4 °C) type I collagen + 40 µl spheroids → 4 cultures; 40 µl drops polymerized 30 min at 37 °C; basement membrane component neutralizes acidic collagen to render it gel-competent.
- **Readout:** inverted microscope, 20x objective, time points 0/24/48 hr; scale bar 200 µm.
- **Quantitation:** ImageJ Straight-line tool (radius / maximal invasive distance) or Freehand tool (invasive area), ROI Manager Tools plugin for image stacks; values reported as pixel measurements.
- **Controls/variants:** drug pretreatment in drops or matrix; stromal/endothelial coculture; tunable collagen:Matrigel ratio (e.g. reduce Matrigel to 1/3 to enhance collagen-driven invasion).

- Spheroid-forming + invasion scoring (Table 1, ordinal +/− scale, not physical units):
  - 4T1: sphere +++, invasion ++; E0771: +++ / +++; U-87 MG: +++ / +++.
  - MCF-7: sphere +++, invasion − (non-invasive); BT-474: ++ / −; MCF10DCIS.com: ++ / −.
  - MDA-MB-231: sphere − (does not form spheroids), invasion ?; SK-BR-3: − / ?; PC-3: − / ?.
  - PANC-1: sphere +, invasion ++; A-431 +++ / −; COLO 357 PL ++ / −; LNCaP + / −.
- Invasion timing (Fig 2, qualitative): U-87 MG invasion most pronounced by 24 hr (commences directly after implantation); 4T1 and E0771 maximal invasion later (24–48 hr); invasion typically concludes 24–48 hr after plating.
- Invasion modes (Discussion): E0771 = gradual/collective; U-87 MG = individual/rapid egress.
- Seeding density: 500–1,000 cells per 20 µl drop (Protocol 1.1.2). Less aggressive lines (BT-474, MCFDCIS) form smaller compact spheres, requiring adjusted seeding number.
- No length-scale invasion speeds (µm/h), moduli, or forces are reported — only ordinal scores and pixel-based ImageJ distances/areas.

## 7. Limitations & caveats
- Authors explicitly note it is a model system omitting in vivo complexity (stromal factors, orthotopic vs. ectopic microenvironment) and that some metastatic lines (A-431, COLO 357 PL) behaved against expectation.
- Quantitation is semi-quantitative (ordinal scores; pixel distances/areas without calibration to µm in the table), so cross-experiment physical comparison is not possible.
- No replicate statistics, no mechanistic measurement (no traction, no ECM stiffness, no protease rates) — gap of many scales vs. a fine-grained particle simulator.
- MCF-7 is reported as non-invasive here, which is a phenotype note rather than a measured quantity.
