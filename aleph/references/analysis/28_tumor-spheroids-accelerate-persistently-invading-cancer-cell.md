## 1. Summary
The authors tracked individual human glioblastoma (U87-MG) cells migrating through 3D Matrigel ECM in two configurations: cells escaping a tumor spheroid (gradient assay, GA) versus dispersed single cells (gradient-free assay, GFA). Using mean-squared-displacement (MSD) analysis with Fürth's persistent-random-walk (PRW) formula, they extract per-cell speed (S) and persistence time (P). They find that cells fleeing a spheroid move faster, straighter, and radially outward compared to gradient-free single cells. They reproduce both assays with a phenomenological stochastic model: a PRW (Ornstein-Uhlenbeck / Langevin) for GFA, and a biased PRW (BPRW) with a repulsive radial drift decaying as 1/|r| for GA. The conclusion is that the spheroid imposes an external, space-dependent repulsive gradient field that accelerates and directs invasion — not an intrinsic change in the cells themselves.

## 2. Problem & motivation
Glioblastoma is a lethal brain cancer whose mortality is driven by infiltrative invasion of healthy tissue. The question: is the outward, super-diffusive migration of cells leaving a tumor spheroid an intrinsic cell property, or is it imposed by an external gradient generated by the spheroid geometry/density? Disentangling intrinsic vs. environment-imposed motility matters for understanding metastasis and for designing migration-targeting anti-cancer assays/drugs (e.g. temozolomide screening).

## 3. Methods / model
- Cell line: U87-MG human glioblastoma, cultured in DMEM + 10% FBS + 1% pen/strep at 37 °C / 5% CO₂.
- Assays: GFA — ~3500 cells/well embedded in 65% growth-factor-reduced Matrigel (single dispersed cells); GA — spheroids formed from ~325 cells in U-bottom wells over ~72 h (diameter ~80 µm) then embedded in 65% Matrigel.
- Imaging: JuLI stage real-time recorder, bright-field, 10x objective, every 3 min (GA) / 5 min (GFA) for ≥24 h; 2D projection of 3D migration.
- Tracking: Ilastik machine-learning segmentation → TrackMate/Fiji center-of-mass trajectories, manual curation (TrackScheme). Tracks terminated/excluded for edge-crawling >15 min, total displacement <50 µm (removes dead/immobile), division, or merging. Stage-vibration noise checked with plastic phantom beads (deemed negligible).
- Analysis: time-averaged msd (Eq. 11) and ensemble MSD (Eq. 1), fit to Fürth's formula (Eq. 2/3) to extract S and P; angular-displacement θ(τ) (Eq. 5); angle ϕ between major axis of motion m and radial vector r (Eq. 6–7); radial displacement dr (Eq. 8); directionality D ∈ [0,1] (Eq. 9); radial velocity vr (Eq. 10).
- Model: normalized Langevin / Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process (Eq. 12–16). PRW (bias Φ=0) for GFA; BPRW adds a repulsive radial drift Φ(ti) ∝ δ R̃/|r|² · sin(ρ/2) · r̂ (Eq. 17), i.e. an effective bias field. Bias amplitude δ fit by minimizing NRMSE of ensemble radial velocity over N=1000 sims → δ=3. Cell is a single COM point; no cytoskeleton, no adhesions, no explicit ECM mechanics.

## 4. Key results (quantitative)
- Both GA and GFA cells super-diffuse (MSD slope > 1) at short delays, transitioning toward diffusive (slope→1) for τ > 200 min (Fig. 2A).
- Persistence time (mean ± SD of log values): GA ⟨P⟩ = 12.7 ± 1.0 min; GFA ⟨P⟩ = 16.6 ± 1.0 min; most cells P < 90 min; distributions log-normal (Fig. 2B, Table 1).
- Migration speed (mean ± SD): GA ⟨S⟩ = 1.81 ± 0.66 µm/min; GFA ⟨S⟩ = 1.15 ± 0.71 µm/min (Fig. 2C, Table 1). → spheroid cells ~57% faster.
- Angle ϕ between motion axis and radial vector: GA median 35°, GFA median 71° (GA cells follow radial outward direction, ϕ < 75°) (Fig. 3).
- θ(τ) peaks at 0° and 180° for all τ → bidirectional migration axis (forward + U-turns) (p.4, Supp. Fig. S1).
- Radial velocity: GA decays over first ~100 µm from spheroid then plateaus at ⟨vr⟩ ~ 0.4 µm/min; GFA constant ⟨vr⟩ ~ 0.2 µm/min (p.6, Supp. Fig. S3).
- Bias amplitude δ = 3 (dimensionless), gradient field ∝ −1/r² (Fig. 4, Eq. 17).
- Directionality: GA > GFA for t < 400 min, then GA falls to GFA levels once cells escape the field (Fig. 4B).
- Simulation settings: N = 1500 trajectories, n = 800 steps, dt = 0.5 min, minimal displacement d = 50 µm (p.8).
- Sample sizes: GA N = 177 trajectories; GFA N = 305 trajectories.

## 6. Relevance to ffn_cellsim  <-- MOST IMPORTANT
This is **(d) spheroid/multicellular-scale context** and largely **(f) tangential** for a fine-grained, mechanistic single-cell simulator. Plainly: the paper operates at exactly the abstraction level ffn_cellsim's architectural principle rejects. The cell is a single center-of-mass point driven by an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck/Langevin PRW with a lumped phenomenological bias term — no cytoskeletal filaments, no motor heads, no adhesion clutches, no explicit ECM cross-links, no traction. There is nothing here to import as a runtime mechanism, and the PRW/BPRW closed forms are exactly the kind of "paper model" the project forbids at runtime.

Where it has marginal value:
- **Spheroid-scale emergent validation anchor (weak).** If ffn_cellsim ever scales to a multi-cell spheroid-on-ECM regime (the project's downstream MCF7-spheroid overlay track, or any future glioblastoma analogue), the measured single-cell migration speeds (~1.2–1.8 µm/min) and persistence times (~13–17 min) are sanity-scale numbers an emergent simulation could be checked against. But this is glioblastoma U87-MG in Matrigel, NOT the project's MCF7/collagen-I/pV4D4 validation platform — so it is at best a loose cross-species/cross-line order-of-magnitude reference, not a contract oracle.
- **Conceptual framing for the spheroid gradient field.** The finding that a spheroid imposes an *external* radial repulsive gradient (density/stiffness/pH/O₂/secreted-factor origin) that biases and accelerates escaping cells is useful background for interpreting any multicellular emergent behavior — but it is a hypothesis about the microenvironment, not a mechanism the project would encode.
- **Migration-metric methodology.** The MSD/Fürth fitting, directionality D, radial velocity vr, and angle-ϕ analyses are standard, reusable post-processing definitions IF ffn_cellsim ever outputs cell trajectories to characterize emergent motility. This is a methods footnote, not a physics input.

## 7. Limitations & caveats
- **Scale gap:** whole-cell COM point particle vs. ffn_cellsim's explicit per-filament/per-motor/per-clutch resolution — no bridge between the two levels in this paper.
- **2D projection of 3D migration:** trajectories are 2D projections; cells leaving the focal plane are lost, biasing toward in-plane motion.
- **Lumped bias term:** the repulsive field origin is unidentified (could be stiffness, pH, O₂, nutrients, secreted factors); δ=3 is fit, not derived — a magic number by ffn_cellsim's standards.
- **Phenomenological model:** PRW/BPRW assume constant per-cell persistence and ignore mode-switching, population heterogeneity (acknowledged: fast sub-populations / "super-spreaders"), and matrix proteolysis/remodeling.
- **Cell line / ECM mismatch:** U87-MG glioblastoma in 65% Matrigel ≠ project's MCF7/collagen-I overlay target.
- **No mechanics:** no traction, modulus, force, or adhesion measurement of any kind.
