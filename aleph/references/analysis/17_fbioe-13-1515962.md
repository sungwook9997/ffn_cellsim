## 1. Summary
The authors build a hybrid discrete-continuous model in PhysiCell (v1.12.0) where cancer cells are off-lattice centre-based spherical agents and the ECM is a continuum density field (voxels storing local collagen density ρ ∈ [0,1], on top of the Metzcar et al. 2025 ECM framework). They simulate a pseudo-2D ("constrained 3D", a single z-slice) cancer spheroid embedded in collagen I and ask how ribose-induced collagen cross-linking (stiffening at 0/50/200 mM) changes spheroid growth and invasion. Cells move under cell-cell adhesion/repulsion + cell-ECM adhesion/repulsion velocities, degrade the ECM toward zero density, and proliferate with an inhibition function depending on neighbour count and local ECM density. Ribose is modelled phenomenologically as exponentially suppressing both the ECM degradation rate and the maximum cell-ECM migration speed. With one parameter set per cell line (low cell-ECM params for non-invasive MCF7, high for invasive HCC1954), the model reproduces the in-vitro observations of Jahin et al. (2023): ribose stiffening inhibits invasion/growth in invasive HCC1954 but leaves non-invasive MCF7 largely unchanged, and pan-MMP inhibition (GM6001) reduces invasive-cell growth most at 0 mM ribose. The key emergent prediction is a non-monotonic effect of ECM degradation rate (cell speed peaks at ρ = 0.5; too-fast degradation drops ρ below 0.5 and slows migration).

## 2. Problem & motivation
Metastasis is the leading cause of cancer death; collective invasion of solid tumours is strongly shaped by cell-cell and cell-ECM mechanics. ECM stiffness (here tuned non-destructively via ribose/threose non-enzymatic glycation, which cross-links collagen fibres without changing matrix architecture or ligand sites) has contradictory reported effects on invasion across studies. The authors want a computational model that isolates the effect of stiffness alone, replicates 3D in-vitro spheroid experiments, and gives a tunable, extensible framework to dissect which cell-ECM interaction parameters (degradation rate, migration speed, proliferation) drive growth vs. invasion.

## 3. Methods / model
- **Model class**: hybrid discrete-continuous, off-lattice **centre-based agent model** (PhysiCell) coupled to a **continuum ECM density** field (Metzcar et al. 2025 ECM addon); diffusion solver BioFVM (not heavily used here — no diffusible substrate included).
- **Geometry**: pseudo-2D / "constrained 3D" — a single layer of ECM voxels; cells move in x,y but not z, mimicking a microscopy z-slice of the 3D spheroid.
- **Cell**: each cell = one sphere of radius R (max volume V), position = centre. No cytoskeleton, no subcellular structure.
- **Governing rules**:
  - ECM remodelling: dρ/dt = −r_deg,rib · ρ (Eq 1); degradation rate ribose-dependent r_deg,rib = r_deg,0 · e^(−δ·rib) (Eq 2).
  - Total cell velocity v_i = v_i,cc + v_i,cm (Eq 3), split into cell-cell (built-in PhysiCell adhesion/repulsion) and cell-matrix (adhesion + repulsion).
  - Cell-ECM adhesion speed s_i,cma = 4·S_rib·ρ (Eq 4), with random unit direction; S_rib = S_0·e^(−σ·rib) (Eq 5).
  - Cell-ECM repulsion velocity ∝ ρ (Eq 6); total cell-ECM speed s_i,cm = 4·S_rib·ρ·(1−ρ) (Eq 7), maximal at ρ = 0.5 where s_i,cm = S_rib.
  - Direction-change is a Poisson process: P(change) = Δt_mech / T_per (persistence time).
  - Proliferation: P(division) ≈ r_div·f_IP(N_i, ρ)·Δt with inhibition f_IP = (1−ρ) for 0<N_i<N_max else 0 (Eq 8); daughter halves volume then regrows.
- **Time stepping**: mechanics Δt_mech = 0.1 min; phenotype (proliferation/volume) Δt_cell = 6 min (PhysiCell defaults).
- **Initial condition**: 200 μm spheroid (≈139 cells), homogeneous ECM ρ = 1 except ρ = 0 at spheroid location.
- **Statistics**: 10 stochastic replicates per condition; readouts = spheroid area growth relative to t0, cell count, Delaunay mean inter-cell distance (SciPy Delaunay). Run on BlueBEAR HPC (2×56-core Xeon Platinum 8570), 45 s–5 min per run.
- **Experimental anchor**: Jahin et al. 2023 in-vitro — MCF7 (non-invasive) and HCC1954 (invasive) spheroids (hanging drop, 200 μm) embedded in rat-tail collagen I + fibronectin, ribose 0/50/200 mM, imaged daily over ≥96 h, GM6001 pan-MMP inhibitor control.

## 4. Key results (quantitative)
- Cell-ECM speed is non-monotonic in ECM density, peaking at ρ = 0.5 where s_i,cm = S_rib (Eq 7, p.6/9).
- Excessively high degradation (r_deg,0 = 0.0128 min⁻¹) over-degrades ECM, drops ρ below 0.5, and inhibits migration/invasion (Fig 3, p.8).
- Chosen cell-line parameters (Section 3.2, p.9–10): **non-invasive MCF7** r_deg,0 = 0.0001 min⁻¹, S_0 = 0.1 μm·min⁻¹; **invasive HCC1954** r_deg,0 = 0.0032 min⁻¹, S_0 = 0.7 μm·min⁻¹. Shared r_div = 0.00072 min⁻¹ (PhysiCell MCF10A default), δ = 0.02 mM⁻¹, σ = 0.035 mM⁻¹.
- Effective ribose-suppressed invasive-cell values: S_50 ≈ 0.33 / S_200 ≈ 0.035 μm·min⁻¹ (Section 3.2); and r_deg,50 ≈ 0.001, r_deg,200 ≈ 0.00006 min⁻¹; S_50 ≈ 0.1, S_200 ≈ 0.0006 μm·min⁻¹ (Section 3.2, p.11).
- **MCF7 (non-invasive)**: spheroid area growth stays below 2 at 96 h for all ribose; cell count ~280 at 96 h for 0 mM (matches in-vitro nuclei count) (Fig 5C, p.10).
- **HCC1954 (invasive)**: 0 mM → growth ~6 at 96 h, count ~570 at 72 h; 50 mM → growth ~3.4, count ~370 at 72 h; 200 mM → growth ~1.8, count ~250 at 72 h (Fig 5D, p.11). Monotonic suppression with ribose, matching Jahin et al.
- **MMP inhibition (GM6001) for invasive cells**: simulated by lowering degradation to r_deg,0 = 0.0004 min⁻¹ (non-zero because mechanical remodelling persists). Growth at 72 h, 0 mM drops from ~4 (control) to ~2 (GM6001); 50 mM less affected; 200 mM control ≈ inhibited — reproducing the experimental trend (Fig 6, p.11–12).
- Higher S_0 + higher degradation → more single-cell detachment and multicellular protrusions; low values → compact rounded spheroids (Fig 3C, Fig 5, p.8–10).
- Sim variability is lower than in-vitro (no cell death; homogeneous initial ECM) (Discussion, p.13).

## 6. Relevance to ffn_cellsim
This is principally **(d) spheroid/multicellular-scale context** plus a partial **(a) validation oracle** at the wrong scale and a soft **(b) parameter source** for migration speeds and proliferation rates. It is explicitly NOT a mechanism reference for the fine-grained runtime: cells here are single spheres with no cytoskeleton, no motor, no explicit clutch, no filament — the antithesis of ffn_cellsim's per-filament/per-clutch fidelity. The runtime mechanisms (Bell-Evans, Hill, Stam-Hocky, Arp2/3, BAOAB) have zero overlap with this lumped-velocity agent model. Per the project's "closed-forms are oracles, never the runtime" rule, this paper's velocity laws (s = 4·S·ρ·(1−ρ), exponential ribose suppression) are exactly the kind of lumped/proxy mechanism ffn_cellsim deliberately avoids in its core.

Where it IS useful:
- **Direct experimental-overlay alignment**: the project's stated validation target is MCF7-spheroid-on-pV4D4/collagen-I (PI experimental overlay). This paper models the closely-related **MCF7 + invasive-line spheroid-on-collagen-I + ribose-stiffening** system (Jahin et al. 2023), so it is a strong literature companion / cross-check for the spheroid-scale (cohesion, growth, Delaunay inter-cell distance) behaviour that ffn_cellsim's multi-cell EXTEND track will eventually need to reproduce.
- **Cell-line phenotype contrast** (non-invasive MCF7 vs invasive HCC1954, with αv/β1 integrin & E-cadherin crosstalk cited via de Abreu Pereira 2022) maps onto ffn_cellsim's cancer cell-type KU v2 layer — useful as the "what should the emergent single-cell motility/adhesion phenotype roll up to at the spheroid scale" target.
- **ECM-stiffening axis** (collagen cross-linking → reduced remodelling & migration) is the macroscale phenotype that ffn_cellsim's mechanistic ECM cross-link bonds + Bell-Evans clutch dynamics should reproduce bottom-up; this paper gives the expected sign and magnitude of the spheroid-level response to validate against.
- **Order-of-magnitude migration speeds** (0.1–0.7 μm·min⁻¹ = 6–42 μm/h) and proliferation rate (~0.00072 min⁻¹ ≈ 1 division / ~16 h) are reasonable sanity-band anchors for cell-scale outputs, with the caveat that they are fit, not measured directly here.
- Methodologically it confirms the **non-monotonic density–speed coupling** (peak at intermediate ECM density) — a qualitative emergent feature ffn_cellsim's mechanistic clutch+remodelling could be checked against.

Bottom line: keep as a spheroid-scale validation companion for the multi-cell / EXTEND and PI-experimental-overlay tracks; do NOT mine it for single-cell cytoskeleton/clutch/motor mechanisms or constants.

## 7. Limitations & caveats
- **Scale mismatch**: whole cell = 1 sphere; no cytoskeleton, no motors, no explicit adhesion clutches, no filament-level mechanics — directly opposite to ffn_cellsim's mechanistic philosophy.
- **Pseudo-2D / constrained 3D**: all simulations are a single z-slice; cannot capture true 3D protrusion geometry or unconstrained z-motion (authors flag this).
- **Phenomenological ribose coupling**: ribose enters only as two empirical exponential suppression factors (δ, σ) on degradation and speed; no explicit collagen cross-link bonds, no stiffness modulus — stiffness is a proxy, density is the only ECM state variable.
- **ECM target is zero density** (cells always degrade toward ρ=0); authors note this may give wrong predictions at high degradation.
- **No cell death / anoikis, no nutrients/oxygen diffusion**: explains lower-than-experiment variability and the Delaunay-distance mismatch at high ribose; cell count never declines.
- **Parameters fit to one experiment** (Jahin et al. 2023); only 2 experimental time points (24 h, 72/96 h) limit dynamic validation.
- **No fibre orientation/alignment** (acknowledged as a planned extension via Metzcar 2025 / PhysiMeSS).

## 9. Notable quotes / citable claims
- "the total cell-ECM interaction speed is given by … s_i,cm = 4·S_rib·ρ·(1−ρ), which reaches its maximum at ρ = 0.5, where s_i,cm = S_rib." (Eq 7, p.6) — the non-monotonic density-speed law.
- "ribose-induced stiffening can significantly reduce ECM remodelling and confine cancer cell movement, inhibiting spheroid growth and invasion." (Abstract / Introduction, p.2–3).
- "increased ECM stiffness due to ribose-induced cross-linking inhibits spheroid invasion in invasive cells, whereas non-invasive cells remain largely unaffected." (Abstract, p.1).
- "we represent the collagen fibre matrix as a homogeneous density and treat the ribose as a separate quantity that directly impacts ECM remodelling and cell migration." (Discussion, p.12) — explicit statement of the lumped/proxy modelling choice.
- "all simulations and analyses were restricted to 2D … to mimic the original 2D data: thin z-slices (microscopy images) of 3D spheroids." (Discussion, p.13).
