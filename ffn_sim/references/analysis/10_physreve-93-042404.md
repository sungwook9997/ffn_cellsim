## 1. Summary
Fang & Lai build a coarse-grained, energy-minimization whole-cell model of an adherent eukaryotic cell to study traction-force-driven spreading from a suspended sphere to a flat adherent state. The cell is two nested triangulated spherical shells — cortical cytoskeleton (CSK, radius 10 μm, 1806 vertices) and nuclear envelope (radius 3.33 μm, 1806 vertices) — each governed by a Helmholtz free-energy functional (in-plane WLC+power-law, bending, global/local area, volume conservation), connected by explicit actin filaments (AFs), intermediate filaments (IFs, nucleus→cortex), and microtubules (MTs, centrosome→adhesion), with a repulsive cortex–nucleus potential. Spreading is driven by per-vertex outward "traction forces" at adhesion molecules that obey Coulomb-friction attach/detach rules on a rigid substrate; dynamics are Newtonian (mass-weighted vertices, membrane dashpot viscosity, explicit time step Δt = 2.5×10⁻⁸ s). They simulate AFM conical-tip nanoindentation (0–500/600 pN) at different traction levels and show cell stiffness rises with traction force (suspended → adherent), with the spreading-area increase (~20%, 535→671 μm²) matching the range of published traction-force-microscopy experiments, and the indentation curves matching their own AFM measurements on SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells (control vs trypsin-EDTA-detached). A Sneddon/modified-Hertz fit yields whole-cell Young's modulus dropping 5.124 → 1.008 kPa upon de-adhesion.

## 2. Problem & motivation
Most prior cell-mechanics models either treat the cell as a homogeneous (visco)elastic continuum (cannot resolve subcellular contributions) or model only suspended cells (e.g. spectrin-level erythrocytes — simple, no nucleus, no substrate). Adherent-cell morphogenesis is a dynamic process governed by mechanical interaction between the cell and its ECM/substrate via adhesion molecules, and existing adherent models gave limited quantitative information on subcellular reorganization, spreading-area-vs-traction-force relationships, or per-component force contributions. The authors want a discrete, subcellular-component-resolved model that reproduces spreading and the traction-force → stiffening relationship and connects whole-cell AFM mechanics to underlying component-level events.

## 3. Methods / model
- **Model class**: coarse-grained "spectrin-level"-style triangulated-membrane discrete cell model (after Li/Dao/Lim/Suresh erythrocyte models [17] and Discher–Boal–Boey [37]), solved by free-energy-based force evaluation + explicit Newtonian time integration. NOT a continuum FEM and NOT an all-atom/particle MD.
- **Geometry**: two triangulated spheres. Cortical CSK r = 10 μm; nuclear envelope r = 3.33 μm (one-third). Each shell = 1806 vertices, 5412 edges, mass lumped at vertices; whole-cell density 1.15× water = 993.68 kg/m³ (note: 1.15× water ≈ 1150 kg/m³; the stated 993.68 appears to be ~0.99× water — likely a typo/inconsistency in the paper).
- **Free energy** Eq.(1): U_total = U_in-plane + U_bending + U_area + U_volume + U_CSK + U_potential.
  - In-plane Eq.(2): worm-like-chain (WLC) attraction + power-law (POW, exponent m=2) repulsion per edge. WLC uses k_B T = 1.38065×10⁻²³ J/K, T = 298.15 K, L_C = 3·L_0, x_i = l_i/L_C, persistence length L_p.
  - Parameters k_POW, L_0, L_p calibrated from cell/nucleus shear modulus μ_0 via Eqs.(3)–(4) at equilibrium length x_0 = 1/3.
  - Bending Eq.(5): U = Σ k_b[1 − cos(θ_i − θ_0)], k_b = 2.77×10⁻¹⁹ J (cortex), 5.54×10⁻¹⁹ J (nucleus).
  - Area Eq.(6): global (k_ga k_B T/L_0² = 6.17×10⁻⁴ J/m²) + local (k_la k_B T/L_0² = 1.26×10⁻⁵ J/m²) constraints.
  - Volume Eq.(7): incompressibility, k_v k_B T/L_0³ = 703 J/m³ (cytoplasm), 2109 J/m³ (nuclear inclusion).
  - U_CSK Eq.(8): AFs and IFs as WLC springs; MTs as linear-elastic springs (E_mt = 1.2 GPa, A_mt = 190 nm²).
  - U_potential Eq.(9): tan-based repulsion between cortex and nucleus vertices when gap shrinks, k_potential = 5×10⁻¹⁴ J.
- **Spreading mechanics**: adhesion molecules at cortex vertices; on substrate contact, traction force points outward-normal to the leading edge. Attach/detach via Coulomb friction (μ = 0.1), adhesive force F_AD = 1 nN, critical detach force F_C = 10·F_AD. MTs generated centrosome→adhesion to stabilize nucleus; N_MT = half the number of substrate-contact vertices.
- **Integration**: conservative force F_C,i = −∂U_i/∂r_i (virtual work, Eq.10); dissipative dashpot F_D = η_m v (η_m = 0.051 N·s/m²); explicit velocity-update Newton Eqs.(12)–(14); Δt = 2.5×10⁻⁸ s.
- **Nanoindentation**: conical AFM tip, 15° half-angle, vertical deformation, modified-Hertz/Sneddon comparison. Indentation force 0–500 pN (Fig 5), up to 600 pN in nucleus comparison (Fig 12).
- **Experiment (validation)**: AFM nanoindentation on SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells, poly-D-lysine-coated 8 mm coverslip, day 3, room temp in HBSS; SNL-10 Bruker tip, k = 0.06 N/m, 1 Hz; control vs 0.25% trypsin-EDTA partial-detachment; n = 10 force curves, mean ± SD (Fig 8).

## 4. Key results (quantitative)
- Total traction force to maintain the adherent cell: **14.07 nN** (Fig 4d); traction-force magnitudes per adhesion are order-pN, total order-nN, comparable to experiment [6].
- Spreading states by input traction force (Table II): 0 nN = suspended, 25.54 nN = de-adhering, 47.04 nN = adherent. Spreading area 0 → 5.35×10⁻¹⁰ → 6.71×10⁻¹⁰ m² (i.e. 0 → 535 → 671 μm²); a 47% traction increase (25.54→47.04 nN) gives 20% spreading-area increase (535→671 μm²) — within the experimental range (Table I lists 18–51% area increase across smooth-muscle/fibroblast/endothelial cells).
- Cell stiffness increases monotonically with traction force / indentation (Figs 6,7e); de-adhesion softens the cell (Fig 8).
- Sneddon/modified-Hertz fit: whole-cell Young's modulus **5.124 kPa (adherent) → 1.008 kPa (de-adhering/drug-treated)** (Fig 10).
- Volume mildly decreases with spreading: 4.18 → 4.09 → 4.02 ×10⁻¹⁵ m³; surface area increases 1.25 → 1.54 → 1.67 ×10⁻⁹ m² (Table II).
- Actin concentration sweep (±80%, ±40%): higher actin → stiffer indentation response (polymerization-stiffening), via L_C = R(2 d_actin / (c_actin π))^0.2 (Fig 11).
- Cell-without-nucleus is more deformable than with-nucleus at 600 pN indentation (Fig 12).

## 6. Relevance to ffn_cellsim
This sits at the **mesoscale boundary** of what ffn_cellsim targets. It is useful in three concrete ways and explicitly off-target in one:

- **(b) Parameter source / (a) validation oracle — primary value.** Table III + Eqs.(2)–(9) give a self-consistent set of cortical-shell constants (shear modulus 6.3 μN/m; bending 2.77×10⁻¹⁹ J ≈ 67 k_B T; WLC L_0/L_C/L_p ratios 1 : 3 : 0.1; area/volume constraint magnitudes) that ffn_cellsim's **H.1 cortex** unit can use as cross-check anchors. The whole-cell Young's modulus 1–5 kPa and the traction-force-vs-spreading-area relationship (Table I/II) are clean **acceptance-oracle numbers** for the cell-spreading milestone and for any future indentation-response test — exactly the "closed-form/whole-cell model = validation, not runtime" role mandated by the project's architectural principle.
- **(d) Cell-spreading + EXTEND-track context.** It models cortex + **nuclear envelope** + cytoplasm-volume incompressibility + IF nucleus↔cortex tethers + MT compressive struts together — directly the H.8/9/10 EXTEND scope (membrane/nucleus/cytoplasm). The cortex–nucleus repulsion potential (Eq.9), the volume-conservation term (Eq.7), and the IF tether geometry are reusable design references for those additive units.
- **(e) Numerics caution.** Their explicit-Newton integrator with Δt = 2.5×10⁻⁸ s and a membrane dashpot is a useful reference point for ffn_cellsim's BAOAB integrator stability discussion, but it is a deterministic energy-minimizer with no thermal Langevin noise — a contrast, not a template.
- **Off-target aspects.** The "traction force" here is a **prescribed per-vertex outward force**, not an emergent motor-clutch readout — there is NO Bell-Evans bond, NO Hill force-velocity motor, NO Stam-Hocky minifilament, NO catch-bond, NO Arp2/3 branching, NO actin treadmilling. Adhesion is a 1-nN sticky vertex with Coulomb friction, the opposite of ffn_cellsim's mechanistic FA/clutch. The cortex is a triangulated continuum shell, NOT explicit semiflexible actin filaments with cross-links. So this paper is precisely the kind of **lumped/whole-cell model that ffn_cellsim refuses to use at runtime** — its worth to the project is as a parameter anchor and as a spreading/stiffness validation oracle, not as a mechanism reference for FA/motor/cortex dynamics.

Net: **Medium**. Strong for H.1-cortex parameter sanity, cell-spreading validation oracle, and EXTEND-track (nucleus/cytoplasm/IF/MT) scoping; weak/irrelevant for the fine-grained FA-clutch and motor mechanisms that are the project's core.

## 7. Limitations & caveats
- Traction force is **imposed** (input parameter), not generated by an actomyosin/clutch mechanism — so the model cannot predict force magnitudes from first principles, only their structural consequences.
- Deterministic free-energy minimization; **no thermal fluctuations / Langevin dynamics** despite using k_B T-scaled constants — k_B T enters only as an energy scale, not a stochastic forcing.
- Cortex and nucleus are **continuum triangulated shells** (1806 vertices), not particle-resolved filament networks; the ×40 mesoscale of ffn_cellsim is still far finer in mechanism (explicit filaments/bonds vs membrane elements).
- Internal inconsistencies/ambiguities: density "1.15× water = 993.68 kg/m³" does not multiply out (993.68 ≈ 0.99× water); shear-modulus units written as "μN" in Table III but "μN/m" in text (surface shear modulus, the text value is correct).
- AFs/IFs/MTs are simple springs with no turnover, no branching, no bundling dynamics; actin "concentration" enters only through a static contour-length formula, not polymerization kinetics.
- Validation cell line is **SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma**, not the project's MCF7-breast-cancer overlay target; numbers transfer only as order-of-magnitude anchors.
- Substrate is rigid and flat with no ECM compliance — no collagen-I / pV4D4 substrate physics.
