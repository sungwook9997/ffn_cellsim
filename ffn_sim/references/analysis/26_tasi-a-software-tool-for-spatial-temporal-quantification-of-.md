## 1. Summary
TASI (Temporal Analysis of Spheroid Imaging) is an open-source MATLAB software framework for end-to-end quantification of tumor-spheroid growth and invasion dynamics from time-lapse confocal microscopy. The pipeline (1) projects 4D (x,y,z,t) volumes to 3D (x,y,t) via a standard-deviation focal-plane filter, Gaussian-smooths in space and time, and segments spheroid boundaries with a spatio-temporal max-flow/graph-cut; (2) extracts morphology features (area, perimeter, eccentricity, intensity stats, a "complexity" shape index, a "core radius" and "invasive radius", branch-tip count via skeletonization, and isolated-cell count); (3) fits linear/quadratic/exponential temporal models by least squares; and (4) runs statistical tests (t-test, ANOVA) to compare experimental conditions. They validate it on H1299 non-small-cell-lung-cancer spheroids of three phenotypes — "leader", "follower", and "parental" — generated by the SaGA technique, showing TASI distinguishes invasive (leader, branching) from indolent (follower, near-spherical) dynamics with statistically significant differences in area, complexity, and perimeter model parameters.

## 2. Problem & motivation
3D tumor spheroids bridge 2D monolayer cultures and in-vivo mouse models and are increasingly used to study invasion and metastasis. Temporal (live) imaging captures the inherently dynamic invasion process, but most existing spheroid software targets static high-throughput screening, measures only basic size/shape, and lacks integrated modeling — and irregular, branching, dynamic neoplastic morphologies make automatic segmentation hard, pushing researchers to subjective manual segmentation. The paper aims to provide an objective, repeatable, extensible end-to-end tool that segments, featurizes, models, and statistically compares spheroid dynamics.

## 3. Methods / model
- **Model class:** image-processing / computer-vision pipeline + phenomenological curve fitting. NOT a mechanistic or physics-based model — there is no force, energy, or material-property model of the cells.
- **Imaging:** Leica SP8 confocal; H1299 NSCLC spheroids embedded in 3D matrix; imaged at x,y,z every 10 min for ≥14 h. 6 parental + 3 leader + 3 follower spheroids. Leader/follower/parental cells purified by the SaGA (SpAtiotemporal Genomic and cellular Analysis) technique (ref. 38, Konen et al. 2017).
- **Preprocessing:** 4D→3D projection via standard-deviation filter over focal planes Ip(x,y,t) = σz[I(x,y,z,t)]; 3D Gaussian smoothing in space+time.
- **Segmentation:** energy-minimizing spatio-temporal max-flow graph cut (continuous max-flow / min-cut, refs 40–42); optional 2D per-frame graph cut for long intervals.
- **Feature extraction:** complexity = Perimeter² / (4π·Area) (Eq. 1; circle = 1); core radius = largest inscribed circle centered at mask centroid; invasive radius = minimum enclosing circle including branches; branch number via morphological skeletonization + terminal-endpoint count; isolated-cell detection via connected-component labeling of small disconnected foreground islands.
- **Temporal modeling (Eq. 2):** linear y=a·t+b; quadratic y=a·t²+b·t+c; exponential y=a·e^(b·t); fit by least squares, reported with adjusted R².
- **Statistics:** Student's t-test (pairwise) and ANOVA (≥2 conditions) on fitted model parameters.
- **Outputs:** segmentation masks, feature images, CSV feature/parameter tables, time plots with 95% CIs.
- **Length/time scales:** spheroid-scale microscopy; scale bars 100 μm (Fig. 2); 10-min frames over ≥14 h. No sub-cellular resolution of cytoskeleton/adhesions.

## 4. Key results (quantitative)
- Temporal-model adjusted R² generally high, ranging 0.71–0.99 (p.7); branch number/complexity of leader spheroids poorly fit by any model, adjusted R² ≈ 0.58–0.90 (p.7, Table 2).
- Leader spheroids rapidly develop branches in the first ~5 h then plateau, while core radius keeps increasing (p.6–7).
- Follower spheroid branch number increases slowly/consistently over ~8 h, proportional to core radius (circumference); complexity stays ≈1 (spherical) at all times (p.7).
- Most significant cross-phenotype differences (Table 2): area (linear, ANOVA p = 3.40e-7); complexity (quadratic, pa = 9.88e-8, pb = 7.16e-10); perimeter (quadratic, pa = 7.96e-6, pb = 3.93e-9).
- Area best-fit linear (adjusted R² 0.990–1.0 across types); invasive radius best-fit quadratic (0.816–0.979); core radius best-fit linear (0.909–0.992) (Table 2).
- Eccentricity and single-cell count showed weakest / non-significant differences (eccentricity ANOVA p = 0.152 linear; single-cell count p = 0.0608) (Table 2).
- Imaging: 10-min interval, ≥14 h duration; 100 μm scale bars (Fig. 2).

## 6. Relevance to ffn_cellsim
This is a **tangential / spheroid-scale-context** paper. It is a (f) tangential image-analysis software tool with at most weak (d) spheroid/multicellular-scale context value. It is **off-topic for a mechanistic single-cell cytoskeleton+ECM simulator**:
- No mechanism: nothing about actin, myosin, Arp2/3, clutches, cadherins, ECM mechanics, integrators, or excluded volume — the targets of H.1–H.7 and the EXTEND track.
- No transferable parameters: it yields no forces, stiffnesses, off-rates, velocities, or moduli usable as oracles or anchors. It cannot serve as a (a) validation oracle, (b) parameter source, (c) mechanism reference, or (e) numerics/methods reference for HOOMD physics.
- Where it touches the project: it is a **measurement/analysis methodology** for the *experimental overlay* side. If ffn_cellsim ever simulates many cells to spheroid scale (well beyond the single-cell + multi-cell cohesion roadmap) and wants to compare emergent morphology to live imaging, TASI-style morphometrics (core vs invasive radius, branch-tip count, complexity index, A(t) growth fits) are the kind of *readouts* one would extract from simulation snapshots to match an imaging assay. It is conceptually adjacent to the PI MCF7-spheroid imaging overlay (A/A₀ = a + b/R + c/R² growth-curve fitting is the same genre as TASI's polynomial feature fits), but TASI uses NSCLC H1299 leader/follower spheroids, not the MCF7-on-pV4D4/collagen-I platform, and provides no biophysical parameters.
- Net: keep as context for "how spheroid time-lapse is quantified," not as a physics input. Relevance = Low.

## 7. Limitations & caveats
- 2D-only analysis: 3D z-stacks are projected to 2D before segmentation; surface area, true 3D skeletonization/branching, and z-motion are explicitly deferred future work (Discussion, p.7).
- No cell tracking: invasion is inferred from boundary morphology, not from tracked cell trajectories (future direction).
- Tiny n (3 leader, 3 follower, 6 parental spheroids; single H1299 system) limits generality.
- Phenomenological curve fits (linear/quadratic/exponential) are descriptive, not mechanistic — no causal/biophysical model.
- For ffn_cellsim: a giant scale gap — spheroid-image morphometrics vs. fine-grained per-filament/per-bond particle dynamics; nothing here constrains sub-cellular forces.

## 9. Notable quotes / citable claims
- "Most spheroid analysis software only measures basic size and shape features, which is insufficient to discriminate different patterns of invasion" (p.2).
- "The core radius was defined as the radius of the largest circle that can be inscribed within the spheroid mask... The invasive radius was defined from the minimum circle that can encompass the entire spheroid, including any invasive branches. These radii roughly capture growth due to proliferation and growth due to invasion" (p.3).
- "Leader spheroids rapidly develop branches in the first 5 hours of growth and then reach a plateau, although the core radius continues to increase" (p.6–7).
- "The complexity of follower spheroids remains close to 1 for all time points, suggesting a spherical morphology" (p.7).
