## 1. Summary
The authors build an "integrated cell migration model" that couples focal adhesion (FA) dynamics, cytoskeleton/nucleus remodeling, and actin-motor (myosin II) activity to predict how a single cell migrates on 3-D curved ECM surfaces (cylindrical lumens of an angiogenic conduit). The cell is represented as a double elastic membrane (outer = cytoskeleton/cell membrane, inner = nuclear membrane), each a triangular mesh of N = 98 nodes; the two are bridged by contractile actin stress fibers (SFs) running from FA-bearing integrin nodes to nuclear nodes. Integrins at the outer nodes stochastically bind ligands on the ECM (collagen I / fibronectin / laminin) via Monte Carlo bond formation/rupture, forming FAs whose force pulls the node toward the surface; the SFs shorten via myosin sliding to generate traction. Quasi-equilibrium ODEs (6×N) are integrated with a 4th-order Rosenbrock adaptive solver over ~10 h of cell time. Monte Carlo simulations reproduce that (a) migration speed is non-monotonic in lumen diameter/width with a maximum near ~20 µm width (~60–90×10⁻¹² m² cross-section), (b) narrow lumens force longitudinal elongation/straight tracks while wide lumens drive circumferential spreading and transverse migration, and (c) migration direction is persistent. The speed-vs-width curve matches Irimia & Toner microfluidic cancer-cell data (R² = 0.771).

## 2. Problem & motivation
Most prior migration models/experiments are 2-D planar; real cells (e.g. angiogenic stalk cells crawling inside MMP-cleaved lumens) move on curved 3-D ECM surfaces where surface curvature changes the integrin–ligand bond geometry and thus FA formation probability. The question: how do FA dynamics, cytoskeleton/nucleus deformation, and myosin motor activity combine on a curved surface to set migration speed, direction, and persistence? This matters for angiogenesis, tumor invasion through confined conduits, and wound healing.

## 3. Methods / model
- **Model class:** Lagrangian node-mesh mechanical model. Two coupled elastic triangular-mesh membranes (cell + nucleus), N = 98 nodes each. Stress fibers connect an outer integrin node to an inner nuclear node.
- **Governing equations:** Per node, inertial EOM (Eq 1–2) reduced under quasi-equilibrium to overdamped ODEs (Eq 3–4): Cᶜ dxᵢᶜ/dt = F_FA,i + F_E,iᶜ + F_SF,iᶜ ; Cⁿ dxᵢⁿ/dt = F_E,iⁿ + F_SF,iⁿ. Forces: frictional dissipative F_D = C|v|v (nonlinear, from bond-rupture energy release), focal-adhesion force F_FA = n_b,i·k_LR·(L_b − l)·n̂_R,i (Eq 6), stress-fiber force F_SF = (k_SF/N_SF)(d_SF − N_SF·L_SF⁻¹)·∂d/∂x (Eq 7a) with myosin sliding shortening each SF unit by 2v_m·Δt per step (Eq 8). Elastic membrane forces via virtual-work theory.
- **Bond kinetics:** Stochastic (Monte Carlo) integrin–ligand bond formation and rupture; bond root located where the integrin node's unit normal n̂_R,i intersects the ECM surface (Eq 5), valid only when gap h_p < critical height h_c = 300 nm. Curvature shortens the integrin–ligand distance on concave surfaces, raising bond probability — the central geometric mechanism.
- **Timescales:** F-actin full polymerization T_p = 180 s; complete disassembly ~1 s; myosin off during remodeling.
- **Numerics:** 4th-order Rosenbrock adaptive time-stepping (Δt ~10⁻³–10⁻² s), convergence <10⁻⁴, 6N unknowns; chosen over Runge–Kutta for speed on stiff system.
- **Experimental side:** hMVECs in a 3-D microfluidic collagen-gel device with VEGF gradient (20 vs 40 ng/mL); confocal live imaging every 30 min for 12 h; immunostaining of vinculin (FA), Rhodamine-phalloidin (F-actin SFs), Hoechst (nucleus). Validation against Irimia & Toner cancer-cell microfluidic-channel data.

## 4. Key results (quantitative)
- Migration speed is non-monotonic in conduit width: slowest at narrowest, peak near **20 µm width** (≈ 20–30 µm, **60–90×10⁻¹² m²** cross-section), then declines (Fig 4B, Fig 5A).
- Simulation vs. Irimia & Toner experiment: linear regression **R² = 0.771** (rectangular conduits, Fig 4C); R² = 0.719 for the gradient case (Fig S2C).
- Cylindrical lumens tested: 6, 8.8, 12, 15, 20, 30 µm diameter; max speed near **~10 µm diameter** (≈ capillary diameter) (Fig 5, S4B).
- At 6 µm diameter, **97% of integrin nodes** contact the wall → near-equal leading/trailing FA density → very slow migration.
- Cell body longitudinal lengths: **23 µm, 15 µm, 10 µm** for conduit diameters 6, 8.8, 12 µm (length roughly inversely proportional to cross-section) (Fig S4).
- Nuclear aspect ratio (semi-major/semi-minor): **1.91 ± 0.26** in lumen <25 µm vs **1.37 ± 0.19** in lumen >25 µm (n = 35 cells/case) (Fig 1E); narrowest lumen ⇒ most elongated nucleus.
- Short SFs beneath nucleus ~2–3 µm long; nucleus tethered to cell membrane via nesprin-1 links.
- Bond-rupture dissipation: two regimes — low |vᶜ| → long bond lifetimes, large force/energy release (slope gives Cᶜ); high |vᶜ| → immediate rupture, negligible power loss; fixed Cᶜ = 0.001 N s m⁻¹ approximates the low-speed (red-line) regime well (Fig 6).

## 6. Relevance to ffn_cellsim  ← MOST IMPORTANT
This is principally a **(b) parameter source** and a **(c) mechanism reference / conceptual cross-check**, with secondary value as **(d) spheroid/multicellular-scale context** (it is a single-cell-on-curved-ECM model, the migration analog of confined invasion).

- **FA / clutch (H.4/H.5):** This is a molecular-clutch-flavored FA model: integrin nodes stochastically bind ECM ligands (Monte Carlo), FA force F_FA = n_b·k_LR·(L_b − l) is a linear-spring bond ensemble, and bonds rupture with velocity-dependent dissipation. ffn_cellsim's clutch is more mechanistic (Bell-Evans force-dependent off-rates, explicit per-bond particles), so this paper is a **parameter anchor** (k_LR ≈ 1 pN/nm, l = 30 nm, h_c = 300 nm) and a **validation-oracle-adjacent** comparison for emergent traction/speed-vs-confinement, not a runtime mechanism. NOTE: their bond rupture is essentially geometric/energy-release, NOT Bell-Evans — explicitly the lumped option the project rejects; use only as a cross-check, not a copy.
- **motor/myosin (H.2/H.3):** SFs shorten via a constant myosin sliding rate v_m = 10 nm/s (Eq 8) — a linear-stall convenience, the abstracted option ffn_cellsim rejects in favor of Hill/Stam-Hocky. Still, v_m = 10 nm/s and E_SF = 230 kPa, A_SF ~250 nm radius are usable order-of-magnitude anchors for stress-fiber stiffness/contraction.
- **nucleus EXTEND (H.9):** Directly relevant — the double-membrane (cell + nucleus) coupled by SFs and nesprin-1 links, and the measured nuclear aspect ratios (1.91 vs 1.37) under confinement, are good targets/parameters for the additive nucleus compartment and the perinuclear-actin-cap/LINC tethering story.
- **ECM/collagen (H.7):** Collagen-I ligand surface densities (750 molecules/µm² ↔ 0.8 mg/mL via Gaudet 2003 ref 48) and the plating-concentration↔surface-density conversion are useful for ECM ligand-density setup.
- **numerics (methods):** They use a 4th-order Rosenbrock adaptive stiff-ODE solver, not Langevin/BAOAB — different paradigm from ffn_cellsim's particle dynamics; informative only as a contrast (their quasi-equilibrium overdamped reduction mirrors why ffn_cellsim cares about overdamped/Brownian regimes).
- **Off-target aspects:** The whole cell is ~196 nodes (N=98 ×2), no explicit filaments, no per-motor-head physics, no excluded volume — i.e. exactly the lumped paper-model class ffn_cellsim inverts. So it is NOT a runtime template; its value is parameters + emergent-behavior validation targets (speed-vs-confinement non-monotonicity, persistence, nuclear elongation).

## 7. Limitations & caveats
- Heavily coarse-grained: ~98 nodes per membrane, no explicit actin filaments, motor heads, or branched networks; SF is a 1-D spring with a prescribed shortening rate.
- Bond rupture/dissipation is geometric + energy-release, NOT a force-dependent (Bell-Evans) kinetic law; Cᶜ is a single fixed literature value justified post-hoc (Fig 6) and known to break down at high speed.
- Migration restricted to crawling *on* a surface; true embedded-in-3D-gel migration and MMP-driven matrix degradation are absent (flagged as ongoing work).
- Quasi-static / overdamped reduction discards inertia and thermal fluctuations except through stochastic bond kinetics; no Brownian dynamics.
- Validation is against published cancer-cell microfluidic data (Irimia & Toner) and the authors' own hMVEC imaging — cell-type heterogeneity (critical curvature varies by cell) is acknowledged but not resolved.
