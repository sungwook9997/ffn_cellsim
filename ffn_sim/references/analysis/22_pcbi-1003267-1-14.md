## 1. Summary
The authors build a deformable single-cell model whose surface is a triangulated, "rounded-triangle" mesh (icosahedron subdivisions → 642 or 2562 nodes). Each triangle is assigned a local curvature so adhesive contact with a substrate is computed via Maugis-Dugdale (MD) contact theory (the JKR↔DMT-bridging adhesion law), integrated by quadrature over each facet and distributed to nodes. The cell cortex (modeling the spectrin-phospholipid shell of a red blood cell, RBC) is a viscoelastic network: FENE springs for in-plane stretch, a power-law term for area incompressibility, bending stiffness between adjacent triangles, local/global area constraints, and a volume constraint, all integrated with an over-damped (inertia-free, low-Reynolds) first-order equation of motion solved by conjugate gradient + forward Euler. Validating against optical-tweezer RBC stretching (Suresh et al.) and relaxation data, they then reproduce the experimentally observed universal **r_contact ∝ t^(1/2)** early spreading power law (Cuvelier et al.; Hategan et al.). The central finding: the fast initial slope is a purely *geometric* effect dominated by **contact dissipation** (friction at the adhesion front), with no irreversible cortex deformation required; later slowing is due to rising cortex tension and dissipation in the stretching shell.

## 2. Problem & motivation
Initial cell spreading (first few minutes) is driven by passive adhesion-energy release from surface-molecule binding, not active stress fibers / focal adhesions. Across many cell types it universally follows r ∝ t^(1/2) (early) then ∝ t^(1/4) (later). The simplest cell exhibiting this — an osmotically swollen RBC — has a well-characterized elastic cortex, making it an ideal testbed to mechanistically dissect *which physical mechanism* produces the universal scaling. The motivation is both to explain the power law and to deliver a general deformable-cell contact framework usable for arbitrary smooth microenvironments and cell-cell interactions.

## 3. Methods / model
- **Model class**: mesoscale deformable-cell model — a closed triangulated surface (continuum cortex on a mesh), not explicit filaments. One cell = one mesh.
- **Mesh**: subdivided icosahedron projected to a sphere; 642 (4-fold) or 2562 (5-fold) nodes. Biconcave RBC obtained by relaxing a 60%-volume sphere. Each triangle carries a local curvature (encompassing sphere) computed from the discrete Laplace-Beltrami operator (Eq 14).
- **Contact**: Maugis-Dugdale theory (Eqs 3–13) applied per rounded triangle, treating local contact as sphere-sphere/sphere-plane. Hertz repulsion (Eq 5–6) + MD adhesive tension with cutoff h₀ (Eq 7–8, W = h₀·σ₀). Forces integrated by a 16-point degree-8 quadrature (Eq 15–17), then distributed to nodes conserving force + moment (Eq 19). JKR (Eq 13) and DMT are limiting cases.
- **Cortex elasticity**: FENE attractive spring (Eq 21–22) + power-law incompressibility (L=2, Eq 23–24); bending moment M = k_b·sin(θ−θ₀) between adjacent triangles (Eq 26); local + global area constraints (Eq 27–28); volume constraint (Eq 29).
- **Dissipation**: Kelvin-Voigt dashpot on each connection (Eq 25); substrate friction tensor with normal/tangential coefficients c_n, c_t; Stokes drag split per node for whole-cell drag in plasma.
- **Equation of motion**: over-damped Stokesian, C·v = F (Eq 30–32), C positive-definite, solved iteratively by conjugate gradient, advanced by forward Euler. Δt ≈ 6×10⁻⁶ s (Hategan match) or 50 µs (Cuvelier match).
- **Length/time scales**: cell radius ≈ 3.25 µm; total spreading time ~1 s; rim curvature radii ~200 nm (mesh) vs measured 125±40 nm.

## 4. Key results (quantitative)
- Early spreading follows **r_cc ∝ t^(1/2)** robustly; no clean second t^(1/4) regime, only a slowing (least pronounced for RBC; Fig 4a, Discussion pt 2).
- Stretching validation: axial diameter D_A up to ~16 µm, transversal D_T ~4–5 µm at 200 pN, matching Fedosov/Suresh data (Fig 2, p.2).
- Relaxation: cortex damping c ≈ 50 µPa·s reproduces measured RBC relaxation times of ~0.1–0.3 s (Fig 3, p.2).
- Effective contact angle of fully spread cell ≈ 65°, vs measured ~60° (Hategan; p.4).
- Max normal pressure at nodes (first stage) → force ~100 pN, comparable to validation stretching forces (p.6).
- Analytic balance: contact dissipation ∝ c_n·a²·(da/dt)², adhesive power ∝ W·a·(da/dt) ⇒ a ∝ √(2W/c_n)·t^(1/2) (Eq 2, p.8) — closed-form rationalization of the t^(1/2) law.
- Parameter sweeps (Fig 5): adhesion W and friction c_n set spreading *speed*; FENE k_s, max-stretch x_max, bending k_b, local area k_a set *final* contact radius; t^(1/2) initial law survives all reasonable parameter changes.
- Two experiments matched by changing only adhesion energy + friction constants (Table 1): Hategan W = 1 mJ/m²; Cuvelier W = 88 mJ/m².

## 6. Relevance to ffn_cellsim
This is primarily a **(b) parameter source + (c) mechanism reference + (d) cell-spreading/validation context**, with a secondary **(e) numerics** flavor — but it is NOT a fine-grained filament model, so it sits one coarse-graining tier above ffn_cellsim and is a "Medium" rather than "High."

- **Cell-spreading validation oracle**: The universal r ∝ t^(1/2) early law and the analytic balance a ∝ √(2W/c_n)·t (Eq 2) are exactly the kind of closed-form *acceptance oracle* ffn_cellsim wants for any future cortex-on-substrate spreading test (and aligns with the project's MCF7-spheroid-on-substrate spreading overlay context). Per the project rule, this t^(1/2) law would be a validation gate, never the runtime mechanism.
- **Cortex (H.1) parameter anchors**: cortex Young's modulus (~800 kPa, material stiffness of the shell), bending modulus k_b ~2.4×10⁻¹⁹ N·m, FENE stretch ~1 mN/m, cortex damping ~50 µPa·s, area/volume constraint stiffnesses. These are RBC-specific (spectrin, not actomyosin) so they are sanity-range references, not drop-in actin-cortex constants — useful to cross-check that ffn_cellsim's explicit-filament cortex lands in a physically plausible stiffness/bending band.
- **Adhesion / FA-clutch context (mechanism, lumped)**: Maugis-Dugdale/JKR adhesion energy W (1–88 mJ/m²) and effective range h₀ ~20 nm give order-of-magnitude adhesion-energy and interaction-range anchors. But note: this paper deliberately models *passive* adhesion as a continuum surface-energy contact law — the exact "lumped/abstracted" approach ffn_cellsim rejects at runtime (ffn_cellsim uses explicit Bell-Evans catch/slip clutches). So MD adhesion is a *contrast* example and an oracle, not a mechanism to port.
- **Numerics**: confirms over-damped, inertia-free, low-Reynolds Stokesian dynamics (C·v=F, conjugate gradient) as the right regime for cell mechanics — consistent with ffn_cellsim's BAOAB-limit overdamped integrator. Forward Euler here is the abstracted choice ffn_cellsim explicitly replaced with Leimkuhler-Matthews; this paper is a baseline against which to justify that upgrade.
- **Off-topic aspects**: the cortex is a continuum FENE shell (no explicit actin filaments, no myosin motors, no ECM cross-links, no Arp2/3, no individual clutch bonds, no thermal Langevin noise in production). The paper explicitly *excludes* active spreading (stress fibers, focal-adhesion reinforcement) — which is precisely the active machinery ffn_cellsim is built to resolve mechanistically. So it informs the passive/initial regime and provides oracles, but cannot supply the active-mechanism content.

## 7. Limitations & caveats
- Cortex is a coarse continuum shell (FENE + bending on a 2562-node mesh), explicitly RBC/spectrin — not actomyosin; no motors, no cross-link turnover, no explicit clutches. Two coarse-graining levels above ffn_cellsim's native-filament target.
- Adhesion is a deterministic surface-energy contact law (MD/JKR), not stochastic force-dependent bonds; the authors admit the "viscous friction constants" c_n, c_t are a first-order, time-scale-dependent lump and could NOT match both Hategan and Cuvelier curves with the same values.
- Active late spreading (polymerization, FA, stress fibers) is out of scope by design.
- Mesh-refinement dependence: 5-fold subdivision needed to capture final rim curvature; coarser mesh gives correct initial slope but wrong final radius.
- Forward Euler + fixed Δt; several friction constants are "fitted"/trial-run estimates (flagged in Table 1), violating ffn_cellsim's no-magic-number standard if borrowed uncritically.
- Substrate is a flat rigid plane; predicted radial inward substrate compression is only qualitatively compared to traction-force-microscopy (which concerns *late* spreading).
