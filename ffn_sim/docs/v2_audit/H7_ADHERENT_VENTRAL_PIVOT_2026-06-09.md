# H.7 — ADHERENT-VENTRAL operating-point pivot (DESIGN, pre-build)

**Date** 2026-06-09 · **Branch** `h7/full-cell-integration` · **Status** DESIGN (Sanity Gate
Protocol — design before first build). **PI directive (2026-06-09):** stop modelling a
SUSPENDED/floating cell; model the ADHERENT (substrate-bound) cell. The relevant force-bearing
structure for the platform is the **ventral stress-fiber + FA traction apparatus on a FLAT
ventral surface**, not the isotropic suspended cortex on a sphere.

## 1. The diagnosis (PI)

The cortex was built with two geometrically INCONSISTENT layers:
- **S1 (surface)** `surface_manifold` = a curved icosphere sphere-cap (radial = R) — SUSPENDED
  cell geometry.
- **B1 (filaments)** = a flat disk at z ≈ z_basal.

The "curvature option" (place filaments along the curved surface) was a **spurious choice that
existed only because S1 was curved while B1 was flat** — an artifact of the S1↔B1 mismatch, not
real physics. PI's correction: an **adherent/spread cell's ventral surface is FLAT** (pressed
onto the substrate; this is where FAs, ventral stress fibers, and the traction-bearing
filaments live); only the cell body / apical is rounded.

```
   ___round apical___        ← curved (cortex, apical/lateral)
  /                  \
 |      nucleus       |
  \__________________/
 ━━●━━●━━●━━●━━●━━●━━     ← VENTRAL = FLAT (FA + stress fibers + traction)
 ──────── substrate ───────
```

The curved sphere-cap is suspended-cell geometry → WRONG for traction/SF. **Fix: make S1 a flat
ventral surface, consistent with B1 — both flat, FA-anchored — and the "curvature" disappears.**

## 2. Why this matters to the active-γ / transmission saga (the key connection)

This session traced the active-γ transmission floor to two causes; the pivot resolves ONE and
leaves the OTHER:

- **RESOLVED by the pivot — the load-path / architecture half.** The suspended-sphere operating
  point FORCED an isotropic, densely-crosslinked cortex → inter-anchor load-path ~1.3 seg →
  transmission floor (`H7_ARCHITECTURE_LOADPATH`). But the adherent cell's traction structure is
  NOT the isotropic cortex — it is **ventral STRESS FIBERS: aligned contractile bundles anchored
  at FAs at both ends**. That is precisely the **long load-path + end-anchoring + axial
  coherence** the isotropic sphere structurally could not provide. We were measuring the wrong
  structure (isotropic cortex) at the wrong operating point (suspended) for the platform's
  observable (adherent traction). On the ventral SF, myosin tension builds along the fiber to the
  FA anchors → substrate traction — the physiological force path.
- **NOT resolved — the soft-coupling half (still a separate must-do).** The cross-bridge
  `k_head_actin = 1e-6` (vs canonical 1e-3, a 1000× unit slip) and crosslink `k_intra = 1e-7`
  unit-slips are geometry-INDEPENDENT — the same myosin/α-actinin parameterise the stress fiber.
  So the stiffness-unit-slip contract correction remains a separate PI-gated track regardless of
  the adherent pivot.

**Net: the pivot fixes the structure/operating-point error and dissolves the load-path puzzle;
the stiffness unit-slip fix proceeds in parallel.**

- **FA anchors**: on the flat ventral surface, anchoring SF ends to the substrate.

## 6. Structure: ventral stress fiber (the new traction unit)

A ventral SF = an **aligned actin bundle** spanning between two FAs, with:
- bipolar myosin minifilaments along it (contractile),
- α-actinin periodic crosslinks (the sarcomeric-like spacing; uses the existing catch/slip
  crosslinker — same parameters, hence the soft-coupling fix applies here too),
- both ends anchored at FA clutches (the load reacts at the substrate → traction).
Contrast with the isotropic cortex: ALIGNED (coherent axial contraction, no isotropic
cancellation) + END-ANCHORED (tension has a substrate reaction) → tension transmits along the
fiber. This is the architecture that makes traction emerge.

## 7. Measurement plan + sanity gates

- M-step 1 (cheap): build ONE ventral SF (aligned bundle, FA-anchored ends, myosin) on the flat
  ventral surface; measure the tension delivered to the FA anchors (= traction) vs myosin
  activity, and the load-path along the fiber. Compare to the isotropic-cortex transmission
  (expect: SF transmits, cortex doesn't) — directly tests §2.
- M-step 2: scale to a ventral SF array; measure aggregate substrate traction; compare to the
  platform traction magnitude.
- Sanity gates (CLAUDE.md): dimensional (tension [N] → traction stress [Pa]); boundary (no
  myosin → zero traction; rigid substrate → all tension reacts at FA); conservation (Newton-3 at
  FA anchors; total force = 0); sign (contractile → inward traction); measurement consistency (FA
  reaction == integrated fiber tension); physiological baseline (real cytoplasm viscosity, turgor
  ON, FA at physiological density — adherent baseline, NOT suspended-null).
- Bands LOCKED; success = traction EMERGES from the mechanism at literature parameters, not
  tuned to a band.
