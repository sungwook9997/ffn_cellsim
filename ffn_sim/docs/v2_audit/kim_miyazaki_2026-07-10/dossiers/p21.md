## Summary
The paper asks how the *extent of F-actin nucleation* (not motor activity) controls the storage vs. dissipation of internal mechanical energy in a growing cortical-actin-like network. Using a minimal, quasi-2D in-vitro reconstituted "model cortex" — purified G-actin + profilin + His-tagged formin (mDia1) tethered to an NTA-Ni model membrane, crowded to the surface by methylcellulose — the authors vary formin bulk concentration (10 nM, 100 nM, 1 µM) at fixed limiting total actin (2 µM dark + ~0.6 µM Alexa-568) and image growth, turnover (FRAP), curvature/energy, and viscoelastic relaxation (intensity autocorrelation). They find a **non-monotonic** response: at low nucleation, long (~100 µm) floppy filaments entangle into a low-viscosity, fast-turnover (τ₁≈1.7 min) isotropic material; at high nucleation, short (~5–10 µm) filaments form a dense gel with slower turnover (τ₁≈12.6 min); but at an **intermediate critical nucleation (100 nM formin)**, polymerization-induced bending energy is *not* relaxed by turnover — it accumulates ~100-fold above thermal energy (10⁻²⁰→10⁻¹⁹ J), forming vortex-like topological defects whose curvatures set in early and "template" further assembly, encoding a persistent structural (shape) memory. A stochastic filament-assembly model (finite shared G-actin pool, formin release) reproduces the length/number/turnover trends, and a continuum growing-elastica theory reproduces the maximal curvature at intermediate nucleation and the early curvature-lock templating.

## Model & Methods
Three coupled approaches:

**(1) Experimental (in-vitro reconstitution, quasi-2D "model cortex"):**
- Components: G-actin (2.0 µM dark + 0.64 µM Alexa-568), profilin, His-tagged formin mDia1, model supported lipid bilayer (SUVs on piranha-treated 25 mm coverslip in a Chamlide chamber). Formin tethered via His-tag ↔ NTA-Ni lipid.
- Crowding: 0.25% methylcellulose (MC, 14 kDa) confines protein to membrane → quasi-2D layer.
- Bilayer composition: 91% EPC / 8.6% NTA / 0.4% FITC-DHPE.
- Formin bulk concentration swept: 10 nM, 100 nM, 1 µM (the single control parameter).
- Polymerize ~1 h at 25 °C.
- Imaging: Nikon Ti-E + Yokogawa spinning-disk confocal, HQ2 CoolSnap CCD, 60×/1.4 NA oil objective, Metamorph software. Z-stacks for out-of-plane density.
- A microfluidic-channel geometry gives an xz side-view of the 1 µM dense gel.
- Measurements: single-filament elongation rate (subunits/s); network fluorescence growth curves; mesh size vs [formin]; FRAP turnover time τ₁ (10 µm circular bleach, 410 nm laser); radius of curvature R & bending energy of defects; director-field splay/bend/total Frank free-energy density; intensity-autocorrelation relaxation time τ₂; myosin-tracer MSD as independent relaxation check.

**(2) Stochastic filament-assembly model (agent-based; V.Y.):**
- Multiple filaments grow simultaneously from a fixed number of formin nucleators (N_f) sharing a finite conserved monomer pool (N_p; F-actin + G-actin conserved).
- Formin-bound filaments elongate fast until ~100 µm, then formin releases; released filament continues to elongate/depolymerize slowly; freed formin nucleates a new filament.
- Assembly rate ∝ available soluble monomers (diffusion-limited mimic: length produced ∝ bulk G-actin). Constant presumed disassembly rate; turnover time τ_sim from rate of change of filament length at long times.
- Outputs: filament count, mean length ⟨L⟩ vs time; equilibrium ⟨L⟩ vs N_f; τ_sim vs N_p; τ_sim over the (N_f, N_p) phase space. Pool size 10⁷ monomers for Fig 1 J–L.

**(3) Continuum growing-soft-filament theory (S.B./D.S.B., UCL):**
- Growing elastica: polymerization builds up a density-dependent growth pressure P acting on the surrounding network; competition between polymerization force and bending stiffness induces buckling at intermediate density/length; curved filaments then polymerize preserving curvature ("templating"). Predicts normalized curvature ⟨R⟩/⟨R_max⟩ maximal at intermediate N_f/N_p, and curvature reaching steady state before length equilibrates. (Full equations in SI Notes — not in this file.)

## Governing Equations
Equations referenced here point to the SI (S1–S7); the main-text file gives their meaning and the constitutive relations used:

1. **Single-filament elongation kinetics (formin two-mode):** fast formin-bound elongation ≈150 subunits s⁻¹, switching to slow profilin-actin elongation ≈10 subunits s⁻¹ upon release. (Piecewise elongation rate; Fig 1B,C.)

2. **Network growth (biphasic exponential):** fluorescence intensity I(t) grows fast in regime R₁ (0–20 min) then slows in R₂ (>20 min); attributed to G-actin pool depletion. (Fig 1E.)

3. **Assembly-rate law (stochastic model):** dL_i/dt ∝ [G-actin]_free (available soluble monomer), with total actin conserved: Σ L_i·(subunit density) + [G-actin] = const. Formin release at L ≈ 100 µm; constant basal depolymerization. (Meaning of Fig 1I–L / Experimental "Modeling Filament Assembly Kinetics".)

4. **FRAP recovery (turnover):** I(t) recovers as single-exponential with timescale τ₁ (Eq. S6, SI). Meaning: chemical turnover time of F-actin/membrane.

5. **Bending energy of a filament arc:** E_bend = (κ/2)·(L/R²) with κ = k_B T · l_p (bending modulus), estimated from measured radius of curvature R of defect inner surfaces (Eq. S2, SI). Meaning: curvature → stored elastic energy. (Fig 3C.)

6. **Frank free-energy (nematic director) distortion density:** f = ½K₁₁(∇·n̂)² + ½K₃₃(n̂×(∇×n̂))² — splay + bend terms of the director field n̂ over an actin bundle (Eqs. S3–S4, SI). Yields field energy density maps (Fig 3B,D). Frank constant K₃₃ estimated 1.38–4.6 pN.

7. **Growth-pressure buckling (continuum):** growth pressure P(ρ) is an increasing function of local filament density ρ; buckling occurs when P exceeds a bending threshold set by filament length/stiffness → normalized curvature ⟨R⟩/⟨R_max⟩ peaks at intermediate density (Fig 3H,I). (Full form in SI Notes.)

8. **Viscoelastic relaxation (intensity autocorrelation):** C(Δt) = ⟨I(t)·I(t+Δt)⟩ decays with relaxation timescale τ₂ (Eq. S7, SI). Meaning: mechanical/structural relaxation time from thermal fluctuations. (Fig 4C,D.)

9. **Turnover-vs-relaxation ratio:** τ₂/τ₁ measures the extent to which mechanical relaxation is driven by chemical turnover; τ₂/τ₁ ≈ 1 → dissipative (energy relaxed); τ₂/τ₁ ≫ 1 → decoupled (energy stored). Peaks at 100 nM. (Fig 4E.)

10. **Myosin MSD relaxation check:** MSD of embedded myosin tracers shows transition to fluid-like behavior at ~10 min, matching the 1 µM turnover timescale (Eq. S1, SI; Fig S3).

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Filament bending (elastica):** bending modulus κ = k_B T · l_p; energy ~ κL/(2R²). l_p(F-actin) ≈ 15 µm. Filaments with L ≫ l_p are floppy/thermal; buckling/curvature stores nonthermal bending energy.
- **Polymerization (growth) force / pressure:** density-dependent growth pressure P(ρ) from continued monomer addition against a sterically hindering network; drives buckling when steric hindrance is significant (intermediate/high density). Negligible at low density (no steric hindrance); at very high density filaments are shorter than l_p so remain straight.
- **Nematic distortion (Frank):** splay (K₁₁) + bend (K₃₃) elastic penalties on director field; K₃₃ ≈ 1.38–4.6 pN (order of a lyotropic liquid crystal).
- **Crowding / entanglement:** methylcellulose depletion force confines filaments to the membrane plane (quasi-2D) and raises baseline energy above k_B T (~10⁻²⁰ J vs 10⁻²¹ J).
- **Membrane tether:** formin His-tag ↔ NTA-Ni bond couples nucleation site (and filament barbed end while processive) to the bilayer; release upon reaching ~100 µm length.
