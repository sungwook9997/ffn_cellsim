## Summary
This paper asks *which specific feature of a catch-slip bond* lets a transiently cross-linked actin network simultaneously raise both its yield stress (σy) and yield strain (γy) — a combination normally forbidden by the stiffness-vs-extensibility trade-off. Using Taeyoon Kim's well-established agent-based Brownian-dynamics actin model (filaments, cross-linkers, and a myosin-II-like motor as cylindrical segments in a 30×30×1 µm periodic box), the authors run *in silico* bulk shear rheology (strain rate 0.001 s⁻¹) and single-motor contraction assays, comparing pure slip-bond cross-linkers against catch-slip-bond cross-linkers. The catch-slip unbinding rate is a sum of a slip (Bell) term that increases with force and a catch term that decreases with force (Eq. 1), parameterized by a zero-force lifetime τ0, a maximal lifetime τmax, and the force Fmax at which lifetime peaks (reference τ0=9.5 s, τmax=53 s, Fmax=14.7 pN, area-matched to the slip bond). The central finding: catch-slip cross-linkers *redistribute* under load — at low stress they turn over rapidly and "search," then accumulate ("settle down") in intermediate-force regions where their lifetime is maximal, reinforcing the load-bearing pathways. A systematic sweep of the three lifetime parameters shows the three knobs act differently: **large Fmax** keeps the high-force-bearing part continuously *turning over* (force-bearing filaments keep changing), giving relatively constant tangent modulus but high γy AND σy (the counterintuitive regime); **large τmax** freezes the force-bearing pathway and keeps recruiting cross-linkers onto it → higher σy and Ey but roughly unchanged γy; **large τ0** suppresses low-strain redistribution → high initial modulus E0 but small γy. Control tests exclude total-bond-lifetime and high-force-lifetime as the cause (Figs. S7–S8), isolating the *low-vs-intermediate force lifetime difference* as the essential feature. The advantage requires cross-linker turnover (5 µm hop; removing turnover flips catch-slip below slip) and high network connectivity (high CF and RCL). Finally, in active networks a single 512-head motor produces ~3–4× larger contractile stress with catch-slip cross-linkers (~90 vs ~25 Pa), because catch-slip cross-linkers arrive at the motor *before* filament aggregation (force-dependent redistribution precedes contraction).

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** Agent-based (particle/segment) model on **Brownian dynamics**; cylindrical-segment representation of filaments, cross-linkers, and motors. Established Kim-lab model (refs [24–27]). Full details + Table S1 in the (external) Supporting Information.
- **Representation:**
  - **Filament (F-actin):** serially connected cylindrical segments *with polarity* (barbed/pointed distinction for the motor).
  - **Cross-linker:** two cylindrical segments joined at a center point (a two-armed cross-link between a pair of filaments).
  - **Motor:** a fixed (immobile) backbone bearing **64 motor arms**; each arm = **8 myosin heads** in kinetic rates and stall force → **512 heads total per motor**, chosen to mimic a skeletal-muscle myosin-II thick filament (ref [28]). Parameters based on myosin II on actin. Motor backbone segments are fixed in space; filament and cross-linker segments move.
- **Integrator / dynamics:** Displacements of segment endpoints updated by the **Langevin equation** with **forward Euler** integration. (Timestep value is in Table S1 / SI — not in main text.)
- **Deterministic forces (3 types):** (i) bending forces maintaining equilibrium angles between segments; (ii) extensional/stretching forces maintaining equilibrium segment lengths; (iii) repulsive (volume-exclusion) forces between overlapping filaments. Functional forms are in the SI.
- **Domain / BCs:** 3D rectangular box **30 × 30 × 1 µm**. Periodic boundary conditions in all three directions during network *formation*; initial filament orientations random but perpendicular to z (quasi-2D slab). After assembly, PBC in **y** is deactivated, and filaments crossing the two y-normal boundaries are **severed and clamped** to those boundaries to create shearable walls.
- **Cross-linker kinetics:** bind to filament pairs at a constant on-rate; unbind force-dependently as slip or catch-slip (Eq. 1). After unbinding, a **"turnover" event**: the cross-linker disappears and reappears at a new location within a hop distance (default 5 µm; short-turnover variant 2 µm; or no-turnover variant where it stays on the other filament). *Note:* the authors emphasize that turnover is their distinguishing feature — AFINES [29], MEDYAN [30], ALENS [31], Cytosim [32] can do catch/catch-slip bonds but do **not** include cross-linker turnover.
- **Two assays:**
  1. **Bulk shear rheology** (passive, no motor): −y boundary fixed, +y boundary displaced in +x at constant strain rate **γ̇ = 0.001 s⁻¹** (linearly increasing shear strain); shear stress on +y boundary measured vs strain.
  2. **Motor contraction** (active): a single immobile 512-head motor at the network center generates internal contractile force; no external strain.
- **Yield point definition:** the strain at which the stress-strain slope deviates from the linear-regime slope by **>5%**.
- **Four extracted material metrics:** (i) initial modulus **E0** measured over the first 20 s; (ii) tangent modulus **Ey** measured over the 10 s before the yield point; (iii) yield stress **σy**; (iv) yield strain **γy**.
- **Statistics:** **n = 4** independent simulations per condition (unless noted). Mean ± s.d. ANOVA + Tukey for >2 groups; unpaired two-tailed Student's t-test pairwise. Significance ns p≥0.05, * p<0.05, ** p<0.01, *** p<0.001, **** p<0.0001.
- **Analysis constructs:** "critical cross-linkers" = the top 10% of cross-linkers by tensile force right before the yield point, tracked backward in time and compared to the global average; "critical filaments" = top 10% force-bearing filaments; filament/CL "overlap %" between time points measures how much the force-bearing set changes.

## Governing Equations

**(Eq. 1) — Force-dependent cross-linker unbinding rate (slip + catch-slip):**

```
             ⎧  k^{s0}_{u,CL}·exp( λ^{s}_{u,CL} |F⃗_{s,CL}| / k_B T )
             ⎪     + k^{c0}_{u,CL}·exp( −λ^{c}_{u,CL} |F⃗_{s,CL}| / k_B T )    if r ≥ r_{0,CL}
k_{u,CL} =  ⎨
             ⎪  k^{s0}_{u,CL} + k^{c0}_{u,CL}                                   if r < r_{0,CL}
             ⎩
```

- Meaning: the cross-linker off-rate is the **sum of a slip (Bell-Evans) term** whose rate grows exponentially with tensile force |F⃗_{s,CL}| and a **catch term** whose rate *decreases* exponentially with force. `r` is the cross-linker extension; below the rest length `r_{0,CL}` the bond feels no tensile load and the rate reduces to the force-free sum k^{s0}+k^{c0}.
- Symbols: `k^{s0}_{u,CL}`, `k^{c0}_{u,CL}` = zero-force off-rate constants for the slip and catch parts; `λ^{s}_{u,CL}`, `λ^{c}_{u,CL}` = force sensitivities (mechanical compliances, units length) for slip and catch parts; `k_B T` thermal energy. **Pure slip bond** ⇒ set k^{c0}_{u,CL} = 0.
- **Bond lifetime** τ(F) = 1/k_{u,CL}(F). The catch-slip lifetime curve rises from τ0 at F=0 to a maximum τmax at F=Fmax, then decreases (slip-dominated) at high force. The three phenomenological descriptors used throughout the paper — **τ0 (zero-force lifetime), τmax (maximal lifetime), Fmax (force at maximal lifetime)** — are a reparameterization of {k^{s0}, k^{c0}, λ^{s}, λ^{c}}.

**(Implied, in SI) — Langevin equation of motion** for each mobile segment endpoint: `ζ (dr/dt) = F_bend + F_ext + F_repulsive + F_thermal`, integrated by forward Euler. Drag coefficient ζ, bending/stretching stiffnesses, and thermal noise amplitude are in Table S1 (not in this PDF).

## Force Laws & Potentials
(Functional forms are stated qualitatively in the main text; explicit harmonic expressions live in the SI.)
- **Bending force:** restoring force keeping the angle between consecutive cylindrical segments near its equilibrium value (semiflexible bending stiffness ~ persistence length; not tabulated in main text).
- **Extensional (stretching) force:** restoring force keeping segment lengths near equilibrium (filament and cross-linker stretch stiffness).
- **Repulsive (volume-exclusion) force:** acts between overlapping filaments to prevent interpenetration.
- **Cross-linker unbinding force law:** Eq. 1 above (slip = Bell term e^{+λ^s F/kT}; catch = e^{−λ^c F/kT}).
- **Motor:** myosin-II-like force generation via 512 heads (walking/stall behavior); rates governed by crossbridge-cycle mechanochemistry (discussed conceptually, refs [45–47]); stall force per arm in Table S1.
