## Summary
The paper asks what minimal biophysical mechanism can simultaneously reproduce single-cell persistent random walk (PRW), contact inhibition of locomotion (CIL) between a few cells, and collective/ordered (nematic) migration of many cells on a 2D non-deformable substrate — without phenomenological momentum-transfer or explicit cell–cell repulsion. The authors build an over-damped, agent-based mechanical model in which each cell is a two-point "machine" (front + rear cell-points joined by an elastic spring), each point carrying a partial-donut adhesion region. The front region is subdivided into angular sections that stochastically activate/deactivate to represent dynamic lamellipodial protrusions; substrate points falling under an active region instantly become focal adhesions (FAs) that exert non-centripetal tensile (traction) forces, producing a constant-torque propulsion via a kinematic constraint relating angular and linear velocity. The linear system K u = f is solved each step (Intel Pardiso) and integrated by 4th-order Adams–Bashforth. Results: (1) cell polarity + finite-duration lamellipodia spontaneously give PRW — MSD log-slope ≈ 2 (ballistic) at short lag, decreasing toward 1 (diffusive) at long lag; final MSD slope is biphasic in lamellipodium duration T_F and decreases with wider front angular span θ_F. (2) With multiple cells, CIL emerges purely from *competition for shared substrate points* (each substrate point belongs to only the nearest cell-point) — no explicit repulsion, no momentum transfer — giving an "alternative view" of CIL as substrate-partitioning rather than elastic collision. (3) Cell density controls a phase transition at a critical density ≈ 2300–2400 cells/mm²: below it PRW dominates, above it CIL/jamming dominates, confining motion and producing anomalous, long-tailed speed distributions. (4) A "sensitivity to contact" parameter (contact-induced lamellipodium turnover) plus intermediate density and short T_F drives spontaneous *nematic ordering* (order parameters h_p, h_v rising to an asymptote after ~20 h), with polarity order always exceeding velocity order.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** Agent-based, force-balance / torque-balance **overdamped (inertia-neglected)** mechanical model on a 2D rigid substrate. NOT Brownian/Langevin thermal dynamics — stochasticity enters only through random lamellipodial-section activation. NOT a continuum/phase-field/Potts model.
- **Cell representation:** each cell = 2 cell-points (front "F", rear "R") joined by an elastic spring (equilibrium length r₀^FR). Polarity = unit vector rear→front. Each point has a partial-donut adhesion region (outer/inner radii, angular span).
  - Rear region: half-donut, angular span θ_R = 180°, small radii R_R,out / R_R,in, ALWAYS active.
  - Front region: larger radii R_F,out / R_F,in, tested angular spans θ_F ∈ {180°, 240°, 300°}; divided into (6, in the reported runs) angular sections; only ≤ max (=1 in reported runs) active at a time; each active section lasts a finite duration T_F, does not co-rotate with the cell body while active, then deactivates (by expiry, or by contact/low-substrate-count) and a new random section activates.
- **Substrate:** immobile points on an equilateral-triangle lattice; areal density tunable via triangle size. A substrate point under an active adhesion region instantly becomes an FA (no maturation delay). Each substrate point is assigned to only the SINGLE nearest cell-point → competition/partitioning → the CIL mechanism.
- **Propulsion:** FA points exert equal-magnitude, non-centripetal tensile forces F_c,i tangent to a circle (radius = ½ mean cell-point–FA distance) → net torque; front torque M_F ≫ rear torque M_R (polarity). Kinematic constraint v = Δ × ω couples linear and angular velocity.
- **Solver / integrator:** cell-list neighbor scheme each step; assemble sparse linear system **K u = f** from Eqs. 1, 2, 4; solve for all cell-point velocities via **Intel Pardiso** direct solver; update positions with **4th-order Adams–Bashforth** (Hayes 2011). Then update section activation/deactivation. Loop to final time.
- **Boundary/geometry:** 2D plane, non-deformable rigid substrate; multi-cell runs over ranges of cell density (140 → ~4000 cells/mm²) and simulated times of 15–60 h.
- **Compute:** 16–24 Intel CPU cores per run, parallelized with OpenMP; XSEDE Comet supercomputer.
- **Ensembles:** e.g. Fig 3c/d — 10 simulations per condition, 60 h each.

## Governing Equations
Transcribed faithfully (vectors bold in original; subscript I = cell-point index, i = FA index).

**(Eq. 1) Torque balance (front and rear cell-points), inertia neglected:**
M_F − γ ω_I^F + Σ_{i∈R_I^F} Δ_Ii^F × F_c,i = 0
M_R − γ ω_I^R + Σ_{i∈R_I^R} Δ_Ii^R × F_c,i = 0
— constant generated torque M is balanced by rotational drag (γ ω) plus the sum of moments of FA tensile forces about the cell-point; Δ_Ii = vector from cell-point I to the tangential point; γ = angular drag coefficient.

**(Eq. 2) Force balance (front and rear cell-points), inertia neglected:**
F_I^FR − α(v_I^F − v_I^R) − Σ_J β(v_I^F − v_J) − η v_I^F + Σ_{i∈R_I^F} F_c,i = 0
−F_I^FR − α(v_I^R − v_I^F) − Σ_J β(v_I^R − v_J) − η v_I^R + Σ_{i∈R_I^R} F_c,i = 0
— term 1: internal front–rear spring force; term 2: internal viscous drag between the cell's own two points (coeff. α); term 3: viscous drag versus other cells' cell-points J within cutoff r_crit (coeff. β) — this is the ONLY cell–cell coupling, an effective friction, not adhesion/repulsion; term 4: substrate + medium drag (coeff. η); term 5: sum of FA tensile forces.

**(Eq. 3) Front–rear spring (Hookean, along the bond):**
F_I^FR = −κ ( |r_I^FR| − r₀^FR ) · ( r_I^FR / |r_I^FR| )
— κ = spring constant, r₀^FR = equilibrium front–rear distance, r_I^FR = rear→front vector.

**(Eq. 4) Kinematic constraint linking linear and angular velocity:**
v_I^F = Δ_Ii^F × ω_I^F ,  v_I^R = Δ_Ii^R × ω_I^R
— for each cell-point a substrate point is randomly selected; this makes ω parallel to M and forces the FA forces F_c,i to be tensile (traction), reproducing cell self-propulsion.

**(Eq. 5) Assembled linear system:** K u = f
— K = coefficient matrix from relative positions of cell-points and substrate points; u = all unknown velocities; f = constant torques/forces (incl. Eq. 3 spring force). Solved per time step.

**(Eq. 6) Mean squared displacement (rear-point tracked):**
MSD(τ) = (1/N) Σ_I [ (1/(T−τ)) ∫₀^{T−τ} ‖r_I(t+τ) − r_I(t)‖² dt ]
— N cells, T total time, τ lag time.

**(Eq. 7) Logarithmic MSD slope:**
s = d ln MSD / d ln τ
— s = 2 ballistic, s = 1 diffusive, 1 < s < 2 persistent, s < 1 sub-diffusive.

**(Eq. 8) Ordering (nematic) tensor:**
Q = (1/(2 N_C)) Σ_I (3 u_I ⊗ u_I − I)
— u_I = a per-cell unit director; ⊗ = tensor product; I = identity; leading eigenvector of Q = preferred direction n.

**(Eq. 9) Scalar (Saupe) directional order parameter:**
h = (1/(2 N_C)) Σ_I [ 3 (u_I · n)² − 1 ]
— h = 1 perfect order, h = 0 no order. h_p uses polarity director (u_I = r_I^FR); h_v uses rear-point velocity (u_I = v_I^R).

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Front–rear elastic spring:** linear Hookean, F = −κ(|r|−r₀) r̂ (Eq. 3). Only internal structural potential.
- **FA traction (contractile) force F_c,i:** NON-centripetal tensile force, equal magnitude for all FAs of a given cell-point (magnitude re-solved each step from the coupled torque/force/kinematic system), directed tangent to a circle of radius = ½·(mean cell-point–FA distance). Emulates actin retrograde flow / myosin-driven traction. This is the propulsive force law but has no closed-form force–extension constant — it is a Lagrange-multiplier-like unknown fixed by Eqs. 1–2–4.
- **Constant generated torque M (M_F, M_R):** each cell-point generates a fixed torque (dimensionally an energy); M_F ≫ M_R sets polarity. Justified physically by the near-constant strain energy a single cell injects into a substrate (Gardel 2010).
- **Drag laws (linear, over-damped):**
  - Internal front–rear viscous drag: F = −α(v_F − v_R).
  - Cell–cell viscous drag (effective friction, within r_crit): F = −Σ_J β(v_I − v_J).
  - Substrate/medium drag: F = −η v.
  - Rotational drag: M_drag = −γ ω.
- **NO explicit cell–cell repulsion, NO area exclusion, NO cadherin adhesion, NO Bell/catch bond, NO thermal (Langevin) noise term.** Excluded volume and CIL are emergent from substrate-point partitioning.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM? — Partial/indirect.** Not a deformable-cell surface-mesh model (cells are 2-point machines, substrate is rigid), so no direct DCM physics import. BUT it is a Taeyoon-Kim collective-migration / CIL / nematic-jamming reference: conceptually relevant to DCM aggregate/monolayer behavior and to the "cell–cell contact = one junction, effective viscous friction" framing (matches project note that cadherin friction ≈ viscous drag). Useful as a qualitative comparison target for confluent/jamming regimes, not for parameter transfer.
- **FF? — Low direct, moderate conceptual.** No fine-grained actin filaments, no Bell/catch bonds, no Hill motor law — the traction force is a lumped constant-torque construct, exactly the kind of abstraction the FF engine replaces with mechanistic clutches/myosin. Relevant only as motivation (lamellipodium turnover, FA-under-protrusion traction, PRW emergence) and as a coarse behavioral oracle for FF crawl (PRW speed ~30–60 µm/hr ≈ 8–17 nm/s ballpark, comparable order to the FF crawl 28–45 nm/s note).
