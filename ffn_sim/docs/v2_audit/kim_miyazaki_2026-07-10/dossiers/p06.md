## Summary
This is a review of how **plant** cells generate **branched morphologies** (leaf trichomes, sclereids, root hairs, moss protonema, interdigitated pavement cells) by locally patterning the mechanical properties of a stiff outer **cell wall** under high **turgor pressure**, and how **finite-element (FE) modeling** of the cell wall combined with live-cell imaging has been used to dissect the underlying mechanics. The central biological question is: how do the **microtubule and actin cytoskeletons** locally tune cell-wall material properties (via cellulose-microfibril alignment and matrix secretion) to specify branch initiation sites and drive anisotropic, tip-biased diffuse growth? The review organizes the field around: (1) basic turgor-driven cell-wall expansion mechanics; (2) trichome branch initiation controlled by cortical microtubule collars, a microtubule-depleted zone (MDZ), SPK1→WAVE/SCAR→ARP2/3 actin nucleation, and a genetic hierarchy (GL1-GL3-TTG → endoreduplication → STI/BLT → ZWI/KCBP, FRA2, AN, KINESIN13A); (3) a validated **axisymmetric linear-viscoelastic FE shell model** of the trichome branch (Yanagisawa et al. 2015) that couples a matrix modulus to an anisotropic hyperelastic fiber network and, through iterated load–relax–remesh cycles, predicts wall-stress, strain and new-wall-material budgets that were experimentally validated; (4) root-hair-initiation FE models (Krupinski et al. 2016) coupling ROP/auxin patterning to local wall stiffness change; and (5) a family of **computational microtubule-array self-organization models** (Dixit&Cyr, Allard, Baulin, Deinum, Eren, Mace&Wang, Chakrabortty, Mirabet) in which angle-dependent zippering/bundling, collision-induced catastrophe, katanin severing at crossovers, boundary/geometry effects, and cell-wall-stress feedback jointly govern the emergence of ordered transverse cortical arrays. The take-home is that cell shape, cell-wall stress, and cytoskeletal patterning form a multi-scale feedback loop that integrated imaging + FE modeling is beginning to resolve.

## Model & Methods
This review does not introduce a new model; it describes several. The key modeling frameworks surveyed:

**(A) Trichome-branch FE model (Yanagisawa et al. 2015, the centerpiece — Fig. 4):**
- Model type: **axisymmetric linear-viscoelastic thin-shell finite-element** model of the cell wall.
- Geometry: initial shape = **prolate ellipse** (the branch bulge); axisymmetric.
- Constitutive law: a **matrix** (isotropic, modulus E0) **coupled with a network of anisotropic hyperelastic fibers** (fiber modulus k1, fiber angular dispersion κ). Fiber orientation/dispersion parameterized from measured cortical-microtubule angle distributions.
- Loading: internal pressure = **turgor pressure of epidermal cells** applied to the shell.
- Algorithm (iterated growth cycle): (1) load shell with turgor; (2) let wall **relax to equilibrium** (viscoelastic); (3) **re-mesh** the deformed geometry with a new FE grid; (4) **reset** wall material properties at corresponding locations to original values; repeat. This produces a sequence of shapes → tip-biased anisotropic diffuse growth with tapering.
- Validation: simulated vs. measured shape + local strain; the model *predicted* a threshold transverse-fiber-alignment required for observed anisotropic elongation, and the measured cortical-microtubule angle distribution matched the prediction. Also predicted a base→tip wall-thickness gradient later validated by EM.

**(B) Root-hair-initiation FE model (Krupinski et al. 2016 — Fig. 5a):**
- Model type: **standard isotropic elastic thin-shell** FE under continuous turgor pressure.
- Biochemical coupling: ROP activation modeled as a function of an **auxin gradient**; active-ROP converges to a patch at the basal end only when the auxin gradient is present.
- Mechanical experiment: a square wall patch with a small circular region of variable stiffness; **softening** the circle → circumferential max-principal-stress ring; **stiffening** the circle → radial star-like stress pattern (matches in-vivo microtubule orientation at root-hair site).

**(C) Cortical-microtubule-array self-organization models (Section 7–8):** agent/rule-based simulations of individual dynamic microtubules on a 2D or 3D cortical surface. Key ingredients across models: three-state dynamic instability (growth/pause/shrinkage) with treadmilling; angle-dependent collision outcomes — zippering/bundling below a threshold angle, collision-induced catastrophe above it, or crossover; katanin severing preferentially at crossovers; boundary-induced catastrophe at high-curvature cell edges; microtubule-bound nucleation (sideways ~45° branching, forward, backward). Geometries: 2D square domain (Allard, Mace&Wang, Deinum), 3D cylinder (Eren — Fig. 5b), realistic triangulated meshes from confocal images (Chakrabortty), symmetric ellipsoid (Mirabet).

No integrator, timestep, or explicit numerical scheme is transcribed in this review (they live in the primary papers).

## Governing Equations
This review paper transcribes **NO explicit governing equations** (no Langevin/BD equations, no bending/stretching potentials, no motor force-velocity, no Bell/catch bond rates, no constitutive PDEs written out). The mechanics are described only verbally and via the FE-model schematic (Fig. 4b). The only quasi-formal constitutive statement is:

1. **Wall constitutive model (verbal):** cell wall = matrix (modulus **E0**) coupled to a network of **anisotropic hyperelastic fibers** (fiber modulus **k1**, fiber angular dispersion **κ**), over a spatially varying **thickness t(position)**. Meaning: wall stiffness is anisotropic, set by fiber alignment relative to loading, and the matrix/fiber modulus ratio controls shape output.

2. **Turgor-driven stress scaling (verbal / order-of-magnitude):** for turgor pressure Π ≈ 0.5–1 MPa the resulting in-plane wall stress ≈ ~10 MPa (thin-wall pressure-vessel scaling implied: σ_wall ~ Π·R/t, not written explicitly). Meaning: wall stress is roughly an order of magnitude above turgor because the wall is thin relative to cell radius.

3. **Growth as iterated relax–remesh (algorithmic, not an equation):** shape_{n+1} = Remesh(Relax(shape_n under turgor)); material properties reset each cycle. Meaning: irreversible plastic-like growth is emulated by discrete viscoelastic-relaxation + remesh steps.

(No numbered equations exist in the source to transcribe faithfully; the above are the paper's only formal-model content.)

## Force Laws & Potentials
None given in closed form. Qualitative force/interaction elements mentioned:
- **Turgor pressure** as the sole growth-driving load on the wall (isotropic hydrostatic, Π ≈ 0.5–1 MPa).
- **Anisotropic hyperelastic fiber-reinforced-matrix** wall resistance (cellulose microfibrils = stiff aligned fibers embedded in pectin/matrix).
- **Angle-dependent microtubule collision rules** (zippering below threshold angle; catastrophe above threshold) — stated as rules, no potential/energy function transcribed.
- **Boundary/curvature-induced catastrophe** at sharp cell edges (geometric barrier), no functional form.
- Microtubule **persistence length ~several mm** implies a bending stiffness κ_MT = k_B T · L_p, but the paper only quotes L_p, not the potential.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** WEAK-YES (conceptual only). The Yanagisawa 2015 FE model — a **thin viscoelastic shell under internal turgor pressure that relaxes, remeshes, and grows** with anisotropic fiber-reinforced material properties — is structurally analogous to DCM's surface-mesh-under-turgor + remesh approach. The load-relax-remesh growth loop and turgor-as-driver are transferable *concepts*. But the regime is entirely plant (MPa turgor, MPa wall stress, cellulose-fiber anisotropy, no actomyosin cortical tension), so it offers modeling-pattern inspiration, NOT parameters or force laws for an animal DCM cell.
- **FF?** NO. No actin-filament fine-grained mechanics, no myosin force-velocity, no crosslinker Bell/catch kinetics, no adhesion clutches. The cytoskeletal content is plant **cortical microtubule array** self-organization (zippering/catastrophe/katanin) and ARP2/3 as a genetic regulator — not the Cytosim-style filament-FEM physics FF implements. Microtubule persistence length ~mm could in principle inform an MT module, but it is a generic textbook value here.
- **TAG-validation?** NO (not for this project's MCF7/animal targets). All quantitative numbers are plant-cell-wall (turgor 0.5–1 MPa, wall stress 5–12.6 MPa, strain 0.023–0.039, wall-material 3–5.3 nm/cycle, MT L_p ~mm). None are validation targets for the single-cell animal-mechanobiology gates. Could be registered as SourceEvidence only if the project ever adds a plant/turgor-shell or microtubule-cortical-array line, which is out of current scope.
- **Overall:** LOW relevance. A Taeyoon-Kim-co-authored **review** on plant cell-wall FE morphogenesis; useful mainly as a conceptual reference for turgor-shell + remesh growth loops and MT-array self-organization, with no directly reusable equations, force laws, or animal-cell parameters.
