## Summary
The paper asks how the specific architecture of the cortical actomyosin network emerges during RhoA-driven **pulsed contractions** and how that architecture supports contractile function, using the early *C. elegans* embryo (1–4 cell stage cortex). Combining single-molecule TIRF imaging + tracking of the diaphanous-family formin **CYK-1::GFP** (19,137 molecules / 5 embryos) with MSD-based anomalous-diffusion classification, the authors resolve two formin subpopulations: **superdiffusive/ballistic (α≈1.6) = elongating** formins that processively track growing actin barbed ends, and **subdiffusive (α≈0.3) = recruited/inactive** formins. Elongating formins move at **1.1–1.3 µm·s⁻¹ (~450 monomers·s⁻¹)**; a bulk turnover rate of **0.11 s⁻¹** (smPReSS) combined with actin turnover (0.05–0.15 s⁻¹) implies mean filament length **~6 µm**. Surprisingly, during a pulse elongating formins accumulate **~3.3 s before** recruited formins; a simple ODE kinetic model shows this "first-come-first-served" inversion emerges if **free barbed ends are the limiting, rapidly-depleted resource** and formins are in excess (barbed-end saturation → time-optimal response to RhoA). Single-molecule orientation analysis shows elongating formins point **radially outward** from the pulse center, i.e. the pulse builds a **polar "actin aster" with barbed ends pointing out**. Finally, using Taeyoon Kim's agent-based Brownian-dynamics actomyosin-network model (cylindrical F-actin / ACP / motor segments, Langevin dynamics, Bell's-law crosslink unbinding, parallel-cluster-model motors), they show that this **barbed-out polar architecture** transmits myosin-generated force farther, avoids collapse into disconnected aggregates, and yields more efficient network-scale contraction than the inverted (pointed-end-out) architecture (which instead drives myosin polarity-sorting to the center). Conclusion: RhoA converts a chemical gradient into a polar network via **kinetic (not mechanical) control** of assembly.

## Model & Methods
Two modeling layers plus the experiment:

**(A) Experimental (Robin lab).** Near-TIRF single-molecule fluorescence microscopy of *C. elegans* embryos; CRISPR CYK-1::GFP (endogenous) + CYK-1::GFP overexpression (GFP-RNAi-titrated to single-molecule density). Single-particle detection/tracking via Crocker–Grier / Kilfoil-lab Matlab. MSD fit to anomalous-diffusion model over τ<300 ms. Classify tracks (>15 frames / 750 ms) by exponent α. Drug perturbations: latrunculin A (10 µM, actin depoly), nocodazole (10 µg/mL, MT depoly), perm-1 RNAi to permeabilize eggshell. smPReSS photobleaching-depletion assay → bulk turnover + photobleach rate. Frame rate 20 fps, 50 ms exposures, pixel 107 nm, 15–60% laser (~1.6 µW·µm⁻²), localization ~60 nm.

**(B) Formin-recruitment ODE kinetic model (Fig 4, Fig S5).** Small deterministic reaction network for 4 species (inactive formin, recruited formin, elongating formin, free barbed ends) with periodic RhoA production. Model type = coupled ODEs; 2 free + 4 fixed parameters (fixed from experiment). RhoA waveform simulated as sin⁶(πt/30) (period 30 s). Robust to biochemical-scheme variants (Models 1/2/3).

**(C) Agent-based actomyosin-network mechanics model (Taeyoon Kim; Fig 6, Fig S7, Table S4).** This is the core mechanistic engine relevant to FF.
- **Model type:** agent-based **Brownian (Langevin overdamped) dynamics**; F-actin, motor (myosin II), and ACP (actin-crosslinking protein) are coarse-grained into **cylindrical segments** whose x,y,z positions update via the Langevin equation.
- **Forces:** deterministic bending + extensional (spring) forces maintaining equilibrium segment angles and lengths; a **repulsive** (volume-exclusion) force between neighboring F-actin segment pairs; thermal (stochastic) forces.
- **Actin dynamics:** nucleation → barbed-end polymerization → pointed-end depolymerization (treadmilling), each at constant rates.
- **ACP (crosslinker):** binds F-actin at constant rate; unbinds at a **force-dependent rate via Bell's law (Bell 1978)**.
- **Motors:** each of Na arms binds F-actin at constant rate and walks toward the barbed end (or unbinds) at **force-dependent rates set by the parallel cluster model (Erdmann & Schwarz 2012; Erdmann 2013)**; each arm represents Nh=4 heads.
- **Domain / BC:** thin cortex-like slab **20 µm × 20 µm × 0.1 µm**, **periodic BC in x,y only**; z-boundaries exert repulsive force (confinement). Self-assembled thin actin network at t=0.
- **Integrator / timestep:** explicit Brownian-dynamics update, **Δt = 1.15×10⁻⁵ s**.
- **RhoA-pulse implementation:** domain split into 16 subdomains (4×4 in x,y). Every 30 s one subdomain is randomly activated. In an activated subdomain a fraction ρf-enhanced barbed ends polymerize faster (factor ρf=10) for τf=10 s → F-actin elongated ~10 µm on average ("fast-elongating" fraction swept 0%→2%). Myosin turned on in same subdomain with delay dM=5 s for duration τM=15 s; motors self-assemble into thick minifilaments, contract, then disassemble. 5 runs per condition.
- **Inversion experiment:** elongate from **pointed ends** instead of barbed ends → inverted polarity → compare contraction/forces.

## Governing Equations
1. **Anomalous-diffusion MSD:** `MSD = 2·D·tᵅ` — classifies formin mobility; slope α on log-log; α<0.8 subdiffusive, 0.8<α<1.2 diffusive, α>1.2 superdiffusive; pure ballistic α=2, pure diffusive α=1, immobile α=0. Empirical peaks α≈0.3 and α≈1.6.
2. **Filament-length law (exponential competition of two Poisson processes):**
   `L_filament = V_elongation × τ_filament = V_elongation / (1/τ_actin + 1/τ_formin)`
   — the elongated filament length is the formin speed times the *effective* off-time, where the effective off-rate is the sum of the actin-monomer turnover rate (1/τ_actin) and the formin off-rate (1/τ_formin); i.e. filament grows until either the anchoring monomer disassembles or the formin detaches, whichever comes first (min of two independent exponentials).
3. **Simulated-formin orientation persistence (random-walk with persistence length, STAR Methods):**
   `θ_{n+1} = θ_n + X·cos⁻¹(exp(−R/(2·P_L)))`, with `X ~ N(0,1)`
   — turning-angle update for synthetic formin tracks; R = step length (mean 1.23 µm, SD 0.30 µm), P_L = persistence length = 15 µm (≈ actin persistence length). Reproduces observed outward polarity from kinetics alone.
4. **RhoA activation waveform (simulation input):** `RhoA(t) ∝ sin⁶(π·t / 30)` — sharp periodic pulses, period 30 s.
5. **Barbed-end half-life estimate (Discussion):** with bimolecular formin→barbed-end binding rate `k_on = 0.029 nM⁻¹·s⁻¹` and local formin concentration `[F]≈10 nM`, barbed-end lifetime `t½ = ln2/(k_on·[F]) ≈ 3 s` — self-consistency check of the barbed-end-saturation mechanism.
6. **Bell's law (crosslink/ACP unbinding, from Bell 1978, used in model C):** implied form `k_u = k_u⁰ · exp(F·x_u / k_B T)` — force-dependent ACP off-rate, with `k_u⁰ = 0.115 s⁻¹`, `x_u = 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m`.
7. **Formin ODE kinetic scheme (Fig S5A):** periodic production k₁ (RhoA) → recruited; recruited ⇄ inactive (k₃), recruited → elongating (k₄), elongating → inactive (k₂), free barbed ends produced/consumed (k₅/k₆). Deterministic mass-action ODEs (exact rate constants not tabulated in text; 2 free + 4 measured-fixed).

## Force Laws & Potentials (model C, from Table S4 stiffnesses)
- **F-actin extensional (spring):** harmonic, stiffness κ_s,A = 1.69×10⁻² N/m about equilibrium segment length r₀,A = 1.4×10⁻⁷ m.
- **F-actin bending:** harmonic-angle, κ_b,A = 2.64×10⁻¹⁹ N·m about θ₀,A = 0 (straight), from actin persistence length (Isambert 1995).
- **ACP extensional:** κ_s,ACP = 2.0×10⁻³ N/m (r₀,ACP = 2.35×10⁻⁸ m); **ACP bending:** κ_b,ACP = 1.04×10⁻¹⁹ N·m.
- **Motor backbone:** extensional κ_s,M1 = 1.69×10⁻² N/m; **motor arms:** κ_s,M2 = κ_s,M3 = 1.0×10⁻³ N/m; **motor backbone bending:** κ_b,M = 5.07×10⁻¹⁸ N·m.
- **Repulsive (excluded-volume) force:** stiffness κ_r = 1.69×10⁻³ N/m between neighboring F-actin segments.
- **ACP off-rate (Bell):** k_u = 0.115·exp(F·1.04×10⁻¹⁰/k_BT) s⁻¹.
- **Motor force-velocity / detachment:** parallel cluster model (Erdmann–Schwarz) — not closed-form in this PDF.
