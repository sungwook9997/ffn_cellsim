## Summary
The paper asks how F-actin **network architecture** (branched vs. bundled/linear) controls the *ab initio generation* of myosin-II active stress, as distinct from the well-studied problem of stress *transmission*. Thin "cortex-like" quasi-2D actin networks are reconstituted in vitro on an Egg-PC lipid bilayer, nucleated either by the formin **mDia1** (linear/bundled filaments, 83 or 830 nM) or by **Arp2/3** (branched/dendritic, 0.74 / 7.4 / 74 nM) at constant total actin (0.7 µM G-actin). Skeletal-muscle myosin-II (40 nM dimers) assembles into thick filaments within the network; F-actin displacement (strain via PIV) and the motions of individual thick filaments (via multi-particle tracking → MSD, MSDθ, van Hove, NGP, and two-point correlation Crr) are measured. Result: **mDia1/linear networks contract strongly and accumulate/transmit tension (Crr ~ 1/r, super-diffusive thick-filament motion, laser-ablation retraction), whereas Arp2/3/branched networks are non-contractile, accumulate no detectable tension (no ablation retraction), poorly correlate myosin motion (no 1/r), and cage thick filaments (sub-diffusive, suppressed rotation).** Cofilin de-branching partially restores motility (7× speed increase, MSD exponent 0.35→0.77). Thick-filament speed is size-dependent (∝1/L) in formin (crossbridge-number-limited) but size-independent and strongly reduced in Arp2/3 networks. A Langevin/Brownian-dynamics agent-based simulation (Taeyoon Kim lab) with tunable **volume-exclusion (VE)** shows myosin MSD drops as Arp2/3:actin ratio rises and that VE (steric confinement of bound, walking motors) is the dominant cause of the caging. Conclusion: branched "gel-like" F-actin geometrically encages myosin thick filaments, so myosin cannot be treated as an idealized force dipole — local geometry/density set the constraints on active-stress generation.

## Model & Methods
**Two coupled approaches.**

**(A) In-vitro reconstitution (primary).**
- Thin quasi-2D "cortex-like" F-actin nucleated *from* an Egg-PC + Ni-lipid (DGS-NTA) supported bilayer on a piranha-cleaned coverslip in a Chamlide chamber. Nucleators His-tagged to the Ni-lipid: HST-mDia1 (formin) OR His-VCA (WASp fragment) activating Arp2/3.
- Actin polymerized from 0.7 µM G-actin (15% rhodamine-labeled) + 2.1 µM profilin (mDia1 case), 0.25% methylcellulose (14 kDa) as depletant to keep the network thin/3-D. F-buffer pH 8.5 (raised from physiological 7.5 to suppress spontaneous nucleation). 1 h polymerization at RT.
- Myosin: skeletal-muscle myosin II (SKMMII, rabbit) 40 nM dimers added post-assembly → self-assemble into "pill"-shaped thick filaments. Also smooth-muscle myosin (SMM) and HMM controls.
- Imaging: structured-illumination super-resolution (DeltaVision OMX SR, SIM), 60× objective, pixel 0.08×0.08×0.125 µm, 1024², steady state (>30 min). Confocal (Andor) for dynamics: dt = 3 s.
- Measurements/algorithms: PIV (mPIV MATLAB, window 32, overlap 0.5) → strain ε=⟨∇·x_c⟩; myosin tracking (ImageJ peak-find + Crocker–Grier) → MSD, MSDθ, van Hove, NGP; two-point correlation Crr (custom MATLAB, r=3–20 µm power-law fit, 40 bins); micro-rheology with 0.5 µm streptavidin beads → G*(ω) via GSER; N2 pulsed-laser ablation (λ 337 nm / also 435 nm Micropoint, 30 µm line, 60–65% power) → retraction strain fit to Kelvin–Voigt; nematic order n via FFT director field (3.5 µm windows, 80% overlap); pore size via BoneJ; radial distribution g(r).

**(B) Agent-based simulation (Taeyoon Kim lab; Figs. 8, S14, Table S1).**
- **Model type:** agent-based **Langevin equation Brownian dynamics** (over-damped). Actin filaments, motors, and Arp2/3 are coarse-grained as **cylindrical segments**. Forces: bending, extensional (stretch), torsion, plus a **repulsive volume-exclusion (VE)** force between all elements.
- **Geometry/BCs:** rectangular domain **4 × 4 × 1.5 µm**; periodic BCs in x, y; repulsive wall in z (elements displaced past z-boundaries are pushed back). Network self-assembles from seed filaments + Arp2/3 (Arp2/3 permanently binds a mother filament, nucleates a daughter → branch). Seed filaments placed in **biased** (grows from z≈0 surface, mimicking bilayer growth → denser lower half) or **random** orientation.
- **Motors:** each motor has **64 arms**; equilibrium backbone length L_M ≈ 1.3 µm (1302 nm). Each arm binds F-actin, walks toward the barbed end, and unbinds at **force-dependent (load-dependent) rates**. In biased runs motors seeded near −z boundary (z≈0); in random runs near z≈0.75 µm (midpoint), to maximize network–motor interaction.
- **Protocol:** assemble network → let motors walk → record positions at regular interval → compute ensemble MSD. Runs with vs. without VE; sweeps of R_Arp2/3 and L_M. (Full equations/parameters are in Supplementary "Details of the agent-based simulation" + Table S1, not in this PDF.)

## Governing Equations
(Numbering follows the paper.)

1. **Nematic order parameter** — Eq (1): `⟨n⟩ = 2⟨cos²θ − 1/2⟩`, where θ = orientation difference between a central 3.5 µm F-actin window and its 8 neighbors; perfect local alignment ⇒ n = 1.

2. **Translational MSD (anomalous-diffusion form)** — Eq (2): `4 D_T t^α = (1/N) Σ_{i=1}^N ⟨(r⃗(0) − r⃗_i(t))²⟩`; D_T = translational diffusion coefficient, α = motion exponent (α<1 subdiffusive/caged, =1 diffusive, >1 superdiffusive/directed).

3. **Rotational MSD** — Eq (3): `2 D_θ t^α = (1/N) Σ_{i=1}^N ⟨(θ⃗(0) − θ⃗_i(t))²⟩`; D_θ = rotational diffusion coefficient; θ = angle of thick-filament long axis to x–y plane.

4. **Translational van Hove step size** — Eq (4): `Δr = sqrt[(x(t+1)−x(t))² + (y(t+1)−y(t))²]`.

5. **Angular van Hove step size** — Eq (5): `Δθ = θ(t+1) − θ(t)`. (Van Hove function = distribution of Δr, Δθ; Gaussian ⇔ random diffusive; non-Gaussian ⇔ trapped/persistent.)

6. **Non-Gaussian parameter (2-D)** — Eq (6): `NGP(τ) = 3⟨r⁴(τ)⟩ / (5⟨r²(τ)⟩²) − 1`; NGP∈(0,∞); 0 ⇒ Gaussian; larger ⇒ more anomalous. (Prefactor 3/5 is the 2-D form.)

7. **Autocorrelation double-exponential fit** — Eq (7): `a(1)·e^{−t/a(2)} + (1−a(1))·e^{−t/a(3)} + a(4)`; two time constants a(2),a(3) (larger = network mechanical property, smaller = monomer diffusion / F-actin serpentation); fit over first ~40% of curve.

8. **MSD logarithmic slope** — Eq (8): `α(τ) = d ln⟨Δr²(τ)⟩ / d ln τ` (local power-law exponent used for G*).

9. **Generalized Stokes–Einstein (GSER) modulus** — Eq (9): `|G*(ω)| ≈ k_B T / { π a ⟨Δr²(τ=1/ω)⟩ · Γ[1 + α(τ=1/ω)] }`; a = bead radius; assumes thermally driven (~k_BT), ignores polymerization/active terms.

10. **Two-point correlation (generic tensor)** — Eq (10): `D_{αβ}(r,τ) = ⟨ Δr_α^i(t,τ) · Δr_β^j(t,τ) · δ[r − R^{ij}(t)] ⟩_{i≠j}`; R^{ij}(t) = inter-filament distance; the projection onto the interparticle axis gives D_rr.

11. **Normalized two-point correlation** — Eq (11): `C_rr = ⟨ D_rr / ( sqrt[(dx_α)²+(dy_α)²] · sqrt[(dx_β)²+(dy_β)²] ) ⟩`; averaged over first 10 lag entries; power-law fit r = 3–20 µm, 40 bins.

12. **Kelvin–Voigt viscoelastic retraction (ablation fit)** — Eq (12): `ε(t) = (σ/E_v)(1 − e^{−t/τ_v})`, with viscoelastic time `τ_v = η_v / E_v`. Used to extract τ_v from post-ablation strain relaxation.

**Non-numbered but central:**
- **Strain / contraction:** `⟨ε⟩ = ⟨∇⃗·x⃗_c⟩` (divergence of PIV displacement field); contractile when ⟨ε⟩<0; observables ⟨ε⟩_max and strain rate d⟨ε⟩/dt.
- **Two-point correlation spatial decay:** `C_rr ~ 1/r^α` — fitted exponent α distinguishes continuum-like (α≈1, mDia1) from non-correlated (Arp2/3).
- **Radial distribution fit:** `g(r) = A e^{−r/λ}` (λ = characteristic correlation length).
- **Simulation force set (qualitative):** bending + extensional + torsional harmonic potentials on cylindrical segments + repulsive volume-exclusion; motor arm unbinding at force-dependent (Bell-type, load-dependent) rate. (Explicit functional forms are in the SI, not this PDF.)

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Myosin unbinding:** load-/force-dependent unbinding rate of motor arms (stated as "force-dependent rates"; the classic Bell/catch framing invoked via refs 54–56, incl. Tabatabai catch-bond). Functional constants are in the SI.
- **Segment mechanics (simulation):** harmonic bending, extensional (stretch), and torsional potentials on coarse-grained cylindrical segments; explicit spring constants in Table S1 (SI, not in this PDF).
- **Volume exclusion (VE):** repulsive contact force between all cytoskeletal elements representing steric confinement — the key knob toggled on/off in Fig. 8.
- **z-boundary:** repulsive wall force on elements displaced beyond the ±z domain boundaries.
- **Kelvin–Voigt element:** spring E_v ∥ dashpot η_v (macroscopic constitutive model for network retraction), Eq (12).
- **Nucleator coupling:** His-tag ↔ Ni-NTA lipid bond tethering nucleators (and hence network) to the membrane — attenuates contractility when present.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Partial/indirect. This is a fine-grained filament-scale study, not a surface-mesh continuum. Relevant to DCM only at the level of the actomyosin cortex tension source term: it shows branched-cortex geometry suppresses de-novo active-stress generation, so a lumped cortical-tension γ that ignores architecture is an oversimplification. Useful conceptual input, not direct parameters.
- **FF?** **YES — high.** Directly on the FF engine's turf: explicit fine-grained actin filaments (bending beams), Arp2/3 branches, formin-elongated bundles, myosin-II thick filaments as multi-arm (64-arm) walkers with force-dependent (Bell-type) unbinding, and — critically — **volume-exclusion (steric) confinement as the dominant mechanism** constraining myosin motion. The Taeyoon Kim agent-based model (Langevin BD, cylindrical segments, bending/stretch/torsion + repulsive VE, PBC box) is essentially the physics FF re-implements in Warp. Architecture→contractility mapping (mDia1 contracts, Arp2/3 caged), MSD exponents, C_rr~1/r, and the VE-toggle result are strong FF validation/behavioral targets.
