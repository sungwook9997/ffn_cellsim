## Summary
The paper asks how mechanical stretching tension and the actin-binding protein profilin jointly regulate the polymerization dynamics of individual actin filaments capped by the mammalian formin mDia1 (FH1-FH2 fragment, "mDia1ΔN3"). Using a dual-trap optical-tweezers "actin dumbbell" assay — one bead carries GST-immobilized mDia1 at the filament barbed end, the other an avidin bead anchoring the rhodamine-phalloidin-stabilized pointed end — the authors measured barbed-end elongation rate versus G-actin concentration and versus applied tension (0.13 pN single-trap "low" up to 3.5–5.5 pN double-trap "high"), for both ATP-G-actin and ADP-G-actin, and across profilin concentrations (0–6 µM). They find stretching tension **accelerates** mDia1-mediated elongation (opposite to the reported inhibition on yeast Bni1p), much more strongly for ADP-G-actin than ATP-G-actin, and that tension lowers the apparent critical concentration. A single transition-state-theory kinetic model (rapid equilibrium between open/closed = pre-/post-translocated FH2-dimer states, with a force-dependent closed-state probability p_c = p_c⁰·exp(−fd/k_BT), working distance d = 5.5 nm) reproduces both G-actin- and force-dependence with only kon, koff, p_c⁰ as fit parameters. Crucially, profilin's known **biphasic** effect (accelerates at optimal concentration, represses/depolymerizes in excess) becomes **mechanically switchable**: several-pN tension converts a depolymerizing filament (excess profilin) into a rapidly elongating one, an all-or-none response far exceeding the previously reported ~2× tension effect. Mechanistically, tension-enhanced FH2-dimer translocation simultaneously accelerates profilin-dependent polymerization and represses profilin-dependent depolymerization, so the acceleration effect (normally masked by depolymerization) becomes dominant.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** In-vitro single-molecule reconstitution (dual-trap optical tweezers, "actin dumbbell") + a minimal analytic chemical-kinetics model (transition-state / Bell theory), NOT a particle/agent simulation. No integrator/timestep — the "model" is a closed-form steady-state elongation-rate equation fit to data (Origin software global fit).
- **Assay geometry:** Individual actin filament tethered between two 1-µm polystyrene beads. mDia1 (FH1-FH2, residues 543–1192, GST-tagged) immobilized on one bead via GST–glutathione(BSA) chemistry; barbed end attached to it. Pointed-end region = rhodamine-phalloidin-stabilized, 10%-biotinylated short fluorescent actin fragment, bound to neutravidin/IC5-labeled bead via biotin-avidin. Unlabeled G-actin in solution polymerizes at the mDia1-bound barbed end (mDia1 tracks it via processive capping), lengthening the filament.
- **Force application:**
  - *Single-trap (low tension):* trap avidin bead, move stage at constant 15 µm/s; viscous drag on free mDia1 bead gives only ~0.13 pN.
  - *Double-trap (high tension):* trap both beads; hold avidin bead fixed (force sensor), step mDia1-bead trap away to hold tension ~constant. Force from avidin-bead displacement. Ranges: weak 1.0–2.0, moderate 2.0–3.5, strong 3.5–5.5 pN.
- **Measurement:** phase-contrast bead images at 30 fps; bead centroids via Particle-Track-and-Analysis ImageJ plug-in; elongation rate = slope of bead-to-bead distance vs time. Bead-rotation artifact (nonlinear-spring compliance) corrected by binning plots at fixed force windows (1.4–1.6 / 2.9–3.1 / 4.0–4.2 pN) over ≥3 pull-relax cycles.
- **Trap calibration:** F = 6πηrv (Stokes drag of bead moving through water at stage speed v). Trap stiffness measured 0.024 pN/nm (Fig. S3).
- **Conditions:** T = 26 ± 1 °C. Basic buffer 50 mM KCl, 10 mM imidazole-HCl pH 7.4, 1 mM MgCl₂, 1 mM EGTA, 50 µM CaCl₂; oxygen-scavenger (glucose oxidase/catalase/glucose), 10 mM DTT. ATP assay: +1 mM ATP. ADP assay: +1 mM ADP, 15 U/mL hexokinase + 25 µM Ap5A (ATP-depletion). ≥3 dumbbells per condition, ≥2 independent prep.
- **Algorithm outline of the kinetic model:** FH2 dimer at barbed end has open (polymerization-competent) and closed states in rapid equilibrium. Closed-state probability decreases exponentially with tension f applied to the translocating FH2 half over working distance d. Net elongation = association (only in open state) minus dissociation (only in closed/depolymerizable state). Global least-squares fit of Eq. 1 to low+high-tension elongation-vs-CA data → optimal kon, koff, p_c⁰ (Table 1); same values then predict the elongation-vs-force curves (Fig. 3 C,D).

## Governing Equations
**(Eq. 1) Stair-stepping-model elongation rate**
```
v_elong = v_poly − v_depoly
        = kon·C_A·[1 − p_c⁰·exp(−f·d/k_B·T)] − koff·p_c⁰·exp(−f·d/k_B·T)
```
Net barbed-end elongation rate: G-actin adds only while FH2 is open (prob. 1−p_c), dissociates only while closed (prob. p_c); tension lowers the closed-state probability. (Meaning: force-dependent balance of on/off giving the observed acceleration + critical-concentration drop.)

**(Force-dependent closed-state probability)**
```
p_c = p_c⁰ · exp(−f·d / k_B·T)
```
Closed-state occupancy shrinks (Boltzmann/Bell factor) under stretching tension f. d = 5.5 nm = distance the FH2 dimer-half moves in the closed→open transition. (Meaning: the single force-sensing element of the model.)

**(Open-state probability, plotted in Fig. 3 E,F)**
```
p_open = 1 − p_c = 1 − p_c⁰·exp(−f·d/k_B·T)
```
Fraction of time FH2 is polymerization-competent; →1 above ~3 pN.

**(Eq. 2) Stepping-second-model elongation rate**
```
v_elong = p_o·kon·C_A·[1 − p_depoly⁰·exp(−f·d/k_B·T)] − p_o·koff·p_depoly⁰·exp(−f·d/k_B·T)
```
Same physics but with an explicit gating factor p_o (open-state probability / "gating factor," isoform-dependent) and depolymerizable-open-state probability p_depoly⁰. (Meaning: generalized form; both poly and depoly occur only in the open state.)

**(Eq. 2′) mDia1 special case, p_o = 1 (not force-decreased)**
```
v_elong = kon·C_A·[1 − p_depoly⁰·exp(−f·d/k_B·T)] − koff·p_depoly⁰·exp(−f·d/k_B·T)
```
For mDia1 the gating factor is 1, so Eq. 2 collapses to a form identical to Eq. 1 with p_c⁰ ↔ p_depoly⁰. (Meaning: the two competing formin models are experimentally indistinguishable for mDia1; the paper fits with Eq. 1.)

**(Trap/drag calibration)**
```
F = 6·π·η·r·v
```
Stokes viscous drag used to calibrate optical-trap stiffness and estimate the ~0.13 pN single-trap tension (η water viscosity, r bead radius, v stage speed).

**(Eq. S, Fig. S3) Nonlinear dumbbell compliance from bead rotation**
```
dL/dF  = nonlinear function of F, fit with flexural rigidity EI = 33,900 pN·nm²
```
Force-dependent rotation of beads makes the dumbbell a nonlinear spring; compliance ΔL/Δf vs mean force fit by a single curve (Dupuis 1997 model) to subtract rotation from true elongation. (Meaning: measurement-artifact correction, and gives a bending-rigidity number.)

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Bell / transition-state force factor:** every rate that is force-modulated carries the Boltzmann-Bell factor `exp(−f·d/k_B·T)` with a single working distance **d = 5.5 nm**. This is the operative "force law": the FH2-dimer-half translocation is the force-sensitive step; tension biases it toward the open (polymerizing) state. (Bell 1978, ref. 22, is explicitly cited as the framework.)
- **No bending/stretching bond potential** in the runtime sense; the only mechanical constant besides d is the dumbbell flexural rigidity EI = 33,900 pN·nm² (Fig. S3), used solely to correct bead-rotation compliance, not as a filament model.
- **Trap force:** linear spring (stiffness 0.024 pN/nm) plus Stokes drag F = 6πηrv for calibration.
