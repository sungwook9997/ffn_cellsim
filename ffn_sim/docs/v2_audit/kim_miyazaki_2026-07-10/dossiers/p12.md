## Summary
This is a short (3-page) Commentary and Perspective written by Makito Miyazaki and Takahiro Kosugi to introduce a symposium at the 60th Annual Meeting of the Biophysical Society of Japan (Hakodate, 28–30 Sept 2022) organized within the JST PRESTO research area "Dynamic supra-assembly of biomolecular systems." Its thesis is that beyond "knowing and seeing" molecular components/structures/dynamics, the design principles of supramolecular assemblies (from nm-scale protein and DNA/RNA–protein complexes to µm-scale cytoskeleton, chromosomes, organelles, and liquid droplets) are best revealed by "creating and manipulating" them. It profiles six early-career speakers and their manipulation strategies: (1) Takahiro Kosugi — computational protein design of allosteric control into rotary motor V1-ATPase, regulating its rotation rate; (2) Takeshi Yokoyama — optogenetic control of ribosomes via an optogenetic tool fused to the rRNA core; (3) Taki Nishimura — mechanism of ATG3 amphipathic α-helix (AH-ATG3) curvature sensing driving LC3 lipidation in autophagy; (4) Yuka Iwasaki — reconstruction of piRNA/PIWI-guided heterochromatin formation and stepwise nuclear architecture change; (5) Shunsuke Shimobayashi — optogenetic study of biomolecular condensate (LLPS) nucleation kinetics, showing classical-nucleation-like physics tunable by biomolecular features; (6) Hideki Nakamura — synthetic-biology tools to manipulate/design/analyze intracellular structures (incl. ActuAtor force-generation tool). There are NO equations, force laws, parameters, or quantitative measurements in this article.
