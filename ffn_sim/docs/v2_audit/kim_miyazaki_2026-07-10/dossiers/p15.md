## Summary
The paper asks how actomyosin bundles (stress fibers, SFs) mechanically adapt to complex, time-varying loads at the molecular level, independent of cellular regulation/turnover. Experimentally, individual ventral SFs were isolated from A7r5 bovine aortic smooth muscle cells (membrane/cytosol/nucleus removed by hypotonic shock + detergent, leaving a contractile SF still attached to substrate), then stretched (to ~50% strain) and compressed back with a functionalized glass needle at various rates. SFs buckled after **rapid** compression and then **gradually recovered** to a straight shape; **slow** compression produced no buckling — a rate-dependent, protein-turnover-independent, intrinsically mechano-adaptive response that no simple spring-dashpot viscoelastic model (Kelvin-Voigt/Maxwell/SLS) can explain. To find the mechanism they used Kim's agent-based Brownian-dynamics model of a single contractile unit: two anti-parallel sets of actin filaments whose barbed ends are spring-tethered to opposite rigid boundaries, cross-linked by explicit ACPs (Bell-model force-dependent unbinding) and by explicit multi-arm myosin motors (parallel-cluster-model, PCM, catch-bond walking/unbinding). The model reproduced stretch-induced force relaxation (from motor/ACP unbinding under tension → permanent bundle lengthening), rapid-compression buckling, and gradual motor-walking-driven straightening. Parametric studies showed: (i) filament/bundle bending stiffness sets the buckling curvature but barely affects recovery time; (ii) compression rate sets contour length at end-of-compression, hence buckling severity and recovery time (buckling only when compression rate exceeds unloaded motor walking speed); (iii) recovery rate (bundle shortening) is governed by **motor walking speed** (tuned via the ATP-dependent unbinding rate k₂₀), NOT motor density; (iv) motor density (R_M) sets only the steady-state plateau tensile force after full recovery. Conclusion: intrinsic filament mechanics + load-free motor walking, not viscoelasticity, drive SF mechano-adaptation.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** Agent-based / coarse-grained Brownian dynamics (Langevin, inertia neglected / overdamped). Every cytoskeletal element (actin filament, ACP, myosin motor) is an explicit assembly of cylindrical segments joined by elastic hinges. This is the canonical Taeyoon Kim actomyosin-network model (refs [43–48]).
- **Integrator:** Euler (forward) integration of the overdamped Langevin velocity (Eq. S1/S4).
- **Timestep:** Δt = 1.15433×10⁻⁶ s (≈1.15 µs).
- **Domain / BCs:** Rectangular computational box 20×20×5 µm. Filaments initially all oriented along z. Barbed ends of half the filaments spring-tethered (stiffness κ_s,bnd) to the −z boundary (z=0); barbed ends of the other half spring-tethered to the +z boundary (z=5 µm) → one anti-parallel contractile unit (sarcomere analog). x,y start on a circle of diameter 27 nm (bundle cross-section). Boundary at +z is displaced to impose strain.
- **Geometry / assembly:** N_F = 50 actin filaments, each length L_f = 4.9 µm (≈ initial 5 µm bundle). During an assembly phase filaments are held fixed while ACPs and motors bind; then filaments/motors are released. Thermal fluctuation + inter-filament repulsion thickens the bundle. Note: only ~12 filaments of 7-nm diameter physically fit on the 27-nm circle; they intentionally overlap to guarantee uniform cross-linking. Real SFs have ~10–30 filaments per cross-section (their 50 is already ≥ physiological).
- **Loading protocol (reference):** (1) Stretch +z boundary at tensile strain rate ε̇_s = 0.1 s⁻¹ to ε = 0.5 over 5 s. (2) Hold at ε = 0.5 for 1 s (force relaxation/pausing). (3) Compress back to ε = 0 at compressive strain rate ε̇_c = −0.25 s⁻¹ over 2 s. (4) Hold at ε = 0 and quantify recovery. Recovery deemed complete when L_B − L_ee < 0.01 µm.
- **Observables/measurement protocol:** Tensile force F_B = average over 20 equispaced z cross-sections of the summed z-component of extensional forces on all segments (actin, motor arms, motor backbone, ACP) crossing each section (computed every 1 s). Curvature κ per Eq. (1), averaged over all filaments → bundle curvature K. End-to-end distance L_ee = z-distance between leftmost and rightmost actin endpoints. Contour length L_B = sum of two half-domain averaged filament lengths (Fig. S2B), excluding filament fragments split from the main bundle.
- **Dynamics implemented (fine-grained):** extensional + bending harmonic potentials, volume-exclusion repulsion, thermal (fluctuation-dissipation) noise, cylinder drag; ACP force-dependent unbinding (Bell); motor binding + PCM catch-bond walking/unbinding with a hard 30-pN slip cutoff.

## Governing Equations
1. **(Eq. 1) Local curvature:** κ = |r′ × r″| / |r′|³ — local bundle curvature at each filament endpoint from 1st/2nd derivative vectors of segment positions.
2. **(Eq. S1) Overdamped Langevin (force balance, inertia neglected):** F_i − ζ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_iᵀ = 0 — deterministic force + drag + thermal stochastic force balance for element i.
3. **(Eq. S2) Fluctuation-dissipation theorem:** ⟨F_iᵀ(t) F_jᵀ(t)⟩ = (2 k_B T ζ_i δ_ij / Δt) δ — variance of the stochastic force sets thermal noise (δ = 2nd-order identity tensor, δ_ij Kronecker).
4. **(Eq. S3) Cylinder drag coefficient:** ζ_i = 3π µ r_c,i · (3 + 2 r_0,i / r_c,i)/5 — approximate drag for a cylindrical segment of length r_0,i, diameter r_c,i in medium viscosity µ.
5. **(Eq. S4) Euler position update:** r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (dr_i/dt)Δt = r_i(t) + (1/ζ_i)(F_i + F_iᵀ)Δt.
6. **(Eq. S5) Bending harmonic potential:** U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ₀)² — restoring energy for hinge angle deviation.
7. **(Eq. S6) Extensional harmonic potential:** U_s = ½ κ_s (r − r₀)² — restoring energy for segment length deviation.
8. **(Eq. S7) Volume-exclusion repulsion:** U_r = ½ κ_r (r₁₂ − r_c,A)² if r₁₂ < r_c,A, else 0 — harmonic repulsion active only when inter-segment min distance r₁₂ < actin diameter (7 nm).
9. **(Eq. S8) Bell force-dependent ACP unbinding:** k_u,ACP = k⁰_u,ACP · exp( x_u,ACP |F⃗_s,ACP| / k_B T ) if r ≥ r₀,ACP, else k⁰_u,ACP — slip-bond off-rate rising exponentially with tensile spring force on the ACP (zero-force rate k⁰_u,ACP, force sensitivity/bond length x_u,ACP).
10. **(Motor binding rate, text):** k₊,M = 40·N_h s⁻¹ — per-arm motor binding rate.
11. **(PCM, described not closed-form):** motor walking (k_w,M) and unbinding (k_u,M) rates from the parallel cluster model — 3 myosin states, 5 transition rates; k₂₀ = post-power-stroke→unbound transition (ATP-dependent). k_w,M and k_u,M decrease with force on the longitudinal spring (catch-bond). Reference unloaded walking velocity ≈ 56 nm/s; stall force F_s ≈ 6·N_h pN = 24 pN. Hard slip cutoff: motor arm instantly unbinds if force > 30 pN (catch-slip approximation, ref [62]).

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Actin extensional:** harmonic (Eq. S6), κ_s,A = 1.69×10⁻² N/m, r₀,A = 140 nm.
- **Actin bending:** harmonic (Eq. S5), κ_b,A = κ*_b,A = 2.64×10⁻¹⁹ N·m (reference; = persistence length 9 µm), θ₀,A = 0. NOTE: reference simulations used 10× the experimental κ_b,A ("κ_b,A/κ*_b,A = 10") to get a clear buckling pattern; parametric sweep spanned ratios 1, 3, 10, 30, 100.
- **ACP:** two segments jointed at center; extensional κ_s,ACP = 2.0×10⁻³ N/m, bending κ_b,ACP = 1.04×10⁻¹⁹ N·m, arm length r₀,ACP = 23.5 nm, θ₀,ACP = 0. Force-dependent unbinding = Bell (Eq. S8).
- **Motor (myosin minifilament):** backbone of 7 identical segments (center = bare zone), N_a = 8 arms, each arm = N_h = 4 heads → 32 heads/motor. Backbone extensional κ_s,M1 = κ_s,A = 1.69×10⁻² N/m; backbone bending κ_b,M = 5.07×10⁻¹⁸ N·m (≫ κ_b,A so backbone stays straight); backbone segment length r₀,M1 = 42 nm. Arm = two-spring model: transverse spring κ_s,M2 = 1.0×10⁻³ N/m (equilib r₀,M2 = 13.5 nm), longitudinal spring κ_s,M3 = 1.0×10⁻³ N/m (equilib r₀,M3 = 0, enforces right angle arm↔actin). Walking/unbinding via PCM catch-bond.
- **Repulsion (volume exclusion):** harmonic (Eq. S7), κ_r = 1.69×10⁻³ N/m, cutoff r_c,A = 7 nm.
- **Boundary tether:** linear spring κ_s,bnd = 1.0×10⁻² N/m connecting filament barbed ends to rigid ±z boundaries.
- **Force distribution:** forces on an actin segment (from bound ACP/motor or repulsion) are split to its two endpoints weighted by proximity to the application point (Kim prior work).
