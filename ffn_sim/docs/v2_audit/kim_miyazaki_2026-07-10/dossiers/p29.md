## Summary
The paper asks how **growth-induced mechanical stress that feeds back to inhibit individual-cell growth** shapes the internal patterning and morphology of a proliferating 2D colony of elongated (spherocylindrical, rodlike bacteria/yeast-like) cells. They run large-scale **discrete particle simulations** (O(10⁵) growing, dividing spherocylinders on a 2D frictional substrate) in which each cell grows exponentially but with a rate exponentially damped by the local axial compressive stress (growth rate ∝ exp(−λσ)). Cell–cell overlap is resolved by a **constraint-based (Lagrange-multiplier / convex-optimization) contact algorithm** borrowed from granular / computer-graphics / inertialess-active-matter methods, avoiding stiff repulsive potentials. As the dimensionless stress sensitivity λ increases, the colony spontaneously develops **concentric ring patterns in cell size**, whose characteristic wavelength decreases with λ (∝λ⁻¹/²) and with radial distance. They then derive a **multiscale continuum theory**: a Smoluchowski (population-balance) equation for the cell-length distribution Ψ(x,ℓ,t) coupled to a **Darcy-like macroscopic flow** (ξu + ∇p = 0, ∇·u = g) with stress-inhibited volumetric growth g = (1/τ)e^{−λp}. The continuum model's single free parameter λ′ maps exactly to the discrete one, and its **analytical radially-symmetric solution** reproduces the ring wavelength (no fitting), the stress decrease with λ, and the crossover from exponential to **linear** long-time colony growth R(t) ∼ (2/λ)^{1/2} t. The central message: purely mechanical stress-growth feedback — accumulated anisotropically over many cell-cycle generations (an "inheritance" of resistance) — is sufficient to generate concentric size patterning, distinguishable from chemical/periodic pattern mechanisms by its radially-decreasing wavelength.

## Model & Methods
**Two coupled models, discrete + continuum.**

### Discrete (particle) model
- **Model type:** Agent-based, over-damped (inertialess) 2D simulation of N(t) spherocylindrical ("stadium") particles on a substrate.
- **Particle:** spherocylinder of constant diameter b; state = center of mass xₙ(t), orientation angle θₙ(t) (pₙ = [cosθₙ, sinθₙ]), length ℓₙ(t).
- **Growth & division:** each particle grows from length ℓ₀ to 2ℓ₀, then divides into two particles of length ℓ₀. Cell-size variation after division is neglected (adder/sizer variability ignored, refs [37,38]).
- **Dynamics:** overdamped; velocities set by contact forces/torques (Eqs. 6–7 below).
- **Contact resolution:** no-overlap enforced as a **nonlinear convex optimization** with non-negative Lagrange multipliers; constraints = (i) particles cannot overlap, (ii) non-contacting particles generate no force, (iii) collisions minimize the rate of frictional dissipation. This is a **complementarity / constraint-based** scheme (from granular matter + computer graphics [29–34], adapted to inertialess active matter [35,36] — Yan/Shelley aLENS-type). Avoids stiff repulsive potentials → allows larger time steps at the cost of an iterative solve.
- **Initial condition:** single cell of unit length ℓ₀, diameter b = ℓ₀/2.
- **Run termination:** until colony radius reaches R = 150 ℓ₀ → O(10⁵) particles.
- **Boundary/domain:** 2D free (unconfined) colony on a substrate with constant friction factor ξ.
- **Nondimensionalization:** length by ℓ₀, time by growth timescale τ, stress by τ/(ξℓ₀²). Sole free parameter: dimensionless growth sensitivity λ′ = (τ/ξℓ₀²)λ. (Primes dropped thereafter.)

### Continuum model
- **Model type:** multiscale continuum — a Smoluchowski/population-balance PDE for the length distribution coupled to a Darcy-like tissue-flow PDE.
- **Assumption:** orientational isotropy (particles isotropic in orientation), so axial compressive stress σᶜ → isotropic pressure p.
- **Fields:** Ψ(x,ℓ,t) = number density of particles of length ℓ ∈ [ℓ₀, 2ℓ₀) at x ∈ Ω(t); velocity field u(x,t); pressure p(x,t).
- **Solution strategy:** Because growth rate g = (1/τ)e^{−λp} is independent of Ψ, the mechanics Eqs. (2)–(3) decouple and are solved first; then the Smoluchowski Eq. (1) is integrated along spatial+length characteristics (Lagrangian flow map Φ). Radially-symmetric geometry admits **closed-form analytical solutions** for p(r), u(r), and mean length ℓ̄.
- **Boundary conditions:** free-boundary (moving domain Ω(t)); p = 0 on ∂Ω, boundary advected by u (Eq. 3); division BC on Ψ in ℓ (Eq. balancing flux at ℓ₀ and 2ℓ₀).
- **Nondimensionalization:** same scales as discrete; single free parameter λ′ identical to discrete model → direct micro↔macro parameter map, no fitting.

## Governing Equations
(dimensional forms as written; nondimensional after scaling by ℓ₀, τ, τ/ξℓ₀²)

**(Eq. 6a) Translational velocity (discrete):**
  dxₙ/dt = (1/(ξℓₙ)) Fᶜₙ
  — overdamped COM velocity; mobility ∝ 1/(friction × length); Fᶜₙ = total contact force.

**(Eq. 6b) Angular velocity (discrete):**
  dθₙ/dt = (12/(ξℓₙ³)) Tᶜₙ
  — overdamped rotation; rotational mobility ∝ 1/(ξℓₙ³) (rodlike drag); Tᶜₙ = total contact torque.

**(Eq. 7) Stress-inhibited growth (discrete):**
  dℓₙ/dt = (ℓₙ/τ) exp(−λ σᶜₙ)
  — each cell elongates exponentially (rate 1/τ) but damped by the axial compressive stress σᶜₙ via sensitivity λ. λ=0 or σᶜₙ=0 → pure exponential growth.

**(Eq. 1) Smoluchowski / population-balance (continuum):**
  DΨ/Dt + ∂(ℓ̇ Ψ)/∂ℓ = 0,  with D/Dt = ∂/∂t + u·∇
  — conservation of cell number in (space, length) with material derivative in the flowing tissue; ℓ̇ is the growth flux.

**(Eq. 1b) Growth flux (continuum):**
  ℓ̇(x,ℓ,t) = (ℓ/τ) exp(−λ p)
  — single-particle growth rate with σᶜ replaced by isotropic pressure p (orientational isotropy).

**(Eq. 1c) Division boundary condition:**
  (ℓ̇ Ψ)|_{ℓ=ℓ₀} = 2 (ℓ̇ Ψ)|_{ℓ=2ℓ₀}
  — flux balance: each dividing cell (at 2ℓ₀) produces two daughters (at ℓ₀), hence factor 2. (Rubinow maturation-type BC [18].)

**(Eq. 2) Darcy-like mechanics + growth constraint (continuum, x ∈ Ω(t)):**
  ξ u + ∇p = 0 ;  ∇·u = g
  — force balance: substrate friction balances pressure gradient (Darcy flow through friction); divergence of velocity = local volumetric growth rate g (source from proliferation).

**(Eq. 3) Free-boundary conditions (X ∈ ∂Ω(t)):**
  p(X,t) = 0 ;  ∂ₜX = u(X,t)
  — zero pressure at the colony edge; the boundary is advected with the flow.

**(Eq. 2b) Volumetric growth rate:**
  g = [ℓ̇]_ℓ / [ℓ]_ℓ = (1/τ) e^{−λ p},  with [f]_ℓ := ∫_{ℓ₀}^{2ℓ₀} f Ψ dℓ
  — length-averaged growth rate; equals the stress-inhibited exponential rate.

**(Eq. 4) Average cell length along characteristics:**
  ℓ̄(x,t) = 2^{ frac[ ∫₀ᵗ g(Φ⁻¹(x,t′),t′)/ln2 dt′ ] }
  where frac[z] ∈ [0,1) is the noninteger part of z ≥ 0, and Φ(x₀,t): Ω(0) ↦ Ω(t) is the Lagrangian flow map; ℓ̄ := [ℓ]_ℓ/[1]_ℓ.
  — explicit solution: mean length is 2 raised to the fractional part of accumulated (integrated) growth along the material trajectory. Encodes "inheritance" of accumulated growth resistance.

**(Eq. 4b) Uninhibited (λ=0) homogeneous solution:**
  ℓ̄(x,t) = 2^{ frac[t/ln2] }
  — cells divide synchronously at t = n ln2 (integer n).

**(Eq. 5) Analytical radially-symmetric solution (r = |x| ≤ R(t)):**
  For λ = 0:  p(r) = (R² − r²)/4 ;  u(r) = r/2
  For λ ≠ 0:  p(r) = (2/λ) ln( 1/(8c) − c λ r² ) ;  u(r) = 32 c² r / (1 − 8 c² λ r²)
  where c(R,λ) = [ (1 + λR²/2)^{1/2} − 1 ] / (2 λ R²)
  — pressure peaks at center, vanishes at edge; radial outward velocity. λ=0 recovers parabolic pressure.

**(Eq. 8) Long-time colony radius scaling (continuum):**
  λ = 0:  R(t) = R₀ e^{t/2}  (exponential)
  λ ≠ 0, λ^{1/2}R ≫ 0:  R(t) ∼ (2/λ)^{1/2} t  (linear asymptotic)
  — stress-sensitivity converts exponential colony expansion to linear at long times.

**(Initial colony radius):** R(0) = √(b/(ℓ₀ π)) — chosen so the initial colony area ≈ one particle's area.

**(Wavelength scaling):** final ring wavelength ∝ λ^{−1/2}; understood via rescaling r̃ = λ^{1/2} r making g(r̃) λ-independent; period of wavelength oscillation ≈ growth-cycle ln2 ≈ 0.7.

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **No smooth interaction potential.** Excluded volume is enforced by a **hard non-overlap constraint** (complementarity), not by a repulsive potential — deliberately, to avoid numerical stiffness. Contact forces Fᶜₙ and torques Tᶜₙ are expressed via **non-negative Lagrange multipliers** solving a convex minimization of the rate of frictional dissipation subject to: (i) no overlap, (ii) no force without contact (complementarity), (iii) minimal frictional dissipation.
- **Drag / mobility law:** overdamped, anisotropic-rod drag — translational mobility 1/(ξℓₙ), rotational mobility 12/(ξℓₙ³), with constant substrate friction factor ξ.
- **Growth "law" (the active driver):** exponential length growth ℓ̇ = (ℓ/τ)e^{−λσᶜ} — the sole active/energy-injecting process; the mechanical feedback is the exp(−λσ) damping.
- (No Bell/catch bonds, no motor force-velocity, no bending/stretching elasticity of the cell body — cells are rigid spherocylinders that only elongate by growth; body elasticity explicitly noted as NOT addressed, ref [57].)
