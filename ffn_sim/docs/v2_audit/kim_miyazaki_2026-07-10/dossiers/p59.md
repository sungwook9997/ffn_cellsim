## Summary
This is a bottom-up in-vitro reconstitution study asking whether cell shape and actomyosin activity alone — without any of the spatio-temporal regulatory signalling (RhoA, spindle, formin activation) of real cytokinesis — can direct contractile-ring assembly. Purified rabbit skeletal-muscle G-actin (10 µM typical) plus a bundling agent (methylcellulose depletion, or the passive crosslinker α-actinin) is encapsulated in cell-sized water-in-oil droplets (mean diameter 4.77 µm) stabilized by a DOPE phospholipid monolayer, and actin polymerization is triggered inside. The authors find that spherical confinement spontaneously nucleates a **single ring-shaped actin bundle that always sits at the droplet equator**, because a semiflexible actin filament of persistence length Lₚ ≈ 5–18 µm behaves as an elastic rod when the droplet radius is ≲ Lₚ and localizes at the equator to minimize bending/elastic energy. Ring formation probability falls monotonically with droplet size and vanishes at a critical droplet diameter R*droplet ≈ 15 µm (≈ Lₚ); compressing droplets between coverslips raises R* to ~20 µm and re-orients rings parallel to the plates (perpendicular to compression, minimal-curvature plane). Adding **double-headed myosin motors (myosin II HMM or myosin V)** promotes ring formation via "active diffusion" — dynamic remodelling / directional transport of antiparallel filaments — raising R* to ~20 µm at 5 µM HMM; the maximum ring-assembly time scales linearly with πR_droplet. Rigor/ADP HMM and single-headed S-1 do NOT promote assembly, confirming that two simultaneously-bound heads and motor turnover are required. A **spontaneous stepwise increase in myosin effective concentration** — driven by ATP depletion raising the fraction of bound heads, and quantified by the unifying parameter [myosin]_eff = [myosin]_bulk × (duty ratio)² — triggers ring contraction: slow contraction (weak) → fast contraction (strong) → disassembly. Contraction velocity is proportional to initial ring perimeter with a critical contractable perimeter P* ≈ 4.5 µm matching the mean actin filament length (5.4 µm), and the contraction rate per unit perimeter is ≈ 0.39 µm min⁻¹, matching cytokinetic rings in cells. Contraction proceeds without actin depolymerization (excess-phalloidin insensitive) and at constant ring volume. Myosin V works at ~100× lower concentration than myosin II HMM, exactly the ratio of the squared duty ratios (0.05² vs 0.5²), but rarely gives strong contraction because ATP depletion can raise its [myosin]_eff only ~4× vs ~400× for myosin II. α-actinin promotes assembly when balanced against myosin remodelling; myosin-II oligomerization (2 µM full-length filament myosin substituted into 25 µM HMM) drives complete contraction (R_ring → 0). Conclusion: mitotic cell rounding + actin polymerization + stepwise myosin-density increase and oligomerization are sufficient to program cytokinetic ring dynamics from self-assembly to complete contraction.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** EXPERIMENTAL in-vitro reconstitution (single-molecule/ensemble biochemistry + fluorescence microscopy). There is NO computational simulation, integrator or timestep — this is a wet-lab bottom-up assay. The "model" is a physical minimal system: purified proteins encapsulated in geometrically-controlled droplets.
- **Confinement geometry:** cell-sized water-in-oil droplets, spherical, stabilized by a DOPE (1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphoethanolamine) phospholipid monolayer at 1 mM lipid in oil. Mean droplet diameter 4.77 µm (n = 3152), analysed range 1–20+ µm. Droplets remain spherical > 4 h. Compressed-droplet variant: squeezed between two silicone-coated coverslips to gap height 4.44 ± 0.45 µm (quasi-2D confinement).
- **Boundary conditions:** neither actin filaments nor HMM show specific interaction with the water/oil (phospholipid) interface (Supp. Fig. 2b,d) — the boundary acts as a purely geometric/steric confinement, not a chemical template. Full-length myosin II and myosin V tails DO interact with lipid, so myosin V was tail-truncated (Arg-1091) to a myosin-V-HMM to minimize boundary interaction.
- **Assay composition (droplet final concentrations, typical):** 10 µM actin (5% mol/mol Alexa-546–phalloidin label), 0.375% (w/v) methylcellulose (1500 cP) OR 0% (crosslinker experiments), 0.5 mM ATP, ATP-regenerating system 40 mM phosphocreatine + 0.1 mg ml⁻¹ creatine phosphokinase, 2 mM Trolox (anti-bleach), 10 mM DTT, plus desired HMM / S-1 / myosin V / α-actinin. Buffer A50 = 50 mM HEPES-KOH pH 7.6, 50 mM KCl, 5 mM MgCl₂, 1 mM EGTA. Temperature 25 ± 1 °C.
- **Algorithm / protocol outline:**
  1. Mix ×8 E-mix (ATP-regen) + methylcellulose + Trolox + labelled phalloidin + motor/crosslinker → add to 100 µM monomeric G-actin, pipette-mix; t = 0 s defines start of polymerization.
  2. At 15 s, put 2 µl into 80 µl lipid–oil and vortex 30 s → cell-sized droplets (total 45 s to droplet formation; droplets taking ≠45 s discarded).
  3. Perfuse into sealed chamber, incubate 2.5 h at 25 °C, then confocal Z-scan; count droplets with a single uniform ring → ring formation probability (58–161 droplets/experiment; ×3 independent).
  4. Time-lapse (Z-scan every 2–5 min) for ring assembly time, contraction dynamics.
- **Proteins:** actin, HMM, S-1, full-length myosin II, myosin filaments — from rabbit white skeletal muscle. Recombinant mouse myosin V-HMM (+ human calmodulin) and recombinant human α-actinin I from Sf9/baculovirus. Myosin filaments prepared by KCl dilution 550→200→50 mM (final 5 µM filaments, submicron length).
- **Key measurements:** ring formation probability; ring assembly time; ring diameter R_ring (peak-to-peak of cross-sectional fluorescence / ellipse long-axis); ring/droplet diameter ratio R_ring/R_droplet; orientation angle θ; bundle width d (Gaussian fit); contraction rate; individual actin filament contour length (droplets burst into buffer, phalloidin-stabilized, JFilament 2D). Actin polymerization tracked by bulk pyrene fluorescence (ex 365 / em 407 nm).
- **Statistics:** unpaired two-sided Student's t-test + χ² test; 3σ method to classify contraction categories.

## Governing Equations
This is an experimental paper; the quantitative "laws" are scaling relations and analysis definitions, not dynamical equations.

1. **Effective myosin concentration (central unifying law):**
   [myosin]_eff = [myosin]_bulk × (duty ratio)²
   — Only motors with BOTH heads bound can translocate two adjacent filaments; probability ∝ (duty ratio)². Explains the ~100× (= (0.5/0.05)²) concentration offset between myosin V and myosin II HMM.

2. **Ring orientation angle:**
   θ = sin⁻¹(h_ring / R_ring)
   — h_ring = Z-axis distance between bottommost and topmost ring points; R_ring = ring diameter. Quantifies equatorial vs tilted ring placement.

3. **Ring-volume conservation (constant during contraction):**
   V ≡ π R_ring × (π d² / 4) = const.
   — Total actin in the ring conserved from formation until disassembly; R_ring shrinks while bundle width d thickens (Fig. 4e dashed curves).

4. **Bundle-width cross-section fit (two-Gaussian, Supp. Fig. 3c):**
   F(x) = A₁ exp[−2(x−x₁)²/d₁²] + A₂ exp[−2(x−x₂)²/d₂²] + B
   — x₁,x₂ = peak positions; d₁,d₂ = widths (= bundle width); A₁,A₂ = amplitudes; B = background. Used to measure the two-peak ring cross-section (ring seen edge-on).

5. **Confinement / elastic-rod criterion (verbal scaling law):**
   Successful ring assembly requires R_droplet ≲ Lₚ (persistence length). For R_droplet < Lₚ the filament is an elastic rod → localizes at equator to minimize curvature/bending energy; for R_droplet > Lₚ it is a flexible polymer → no ring. Critical droplet diameter R*_droplet ≈ Lₚ ≈ 15 µm (spherical), ~20 µm (compressed/quasi-2D).

6. **Contraction-velocity ∝ perimeter with critical unit:**
   (contraction rate) ∝ (initial ring perimeter − P*), with critical perimeter P* ≈ 4.5 µm (main) / 4.90 µm offset (Supp. Fig. 3d); P* ≈ mean actin filament length ⇒ effective contraction unit set by filament length.

## Force Laws & Potentials
No explicit force fields (experimental paper). The physical mechanisms invoked:
- **Depletion (Asakura–Oosawa) attraction** between actin filaments from methylcellulose (or PEG 20 kDa) exclusion: reduction of depletion volume increases entropy, lowers free energy → effective attractive bundling force. (Fig. 1a; refs 29–31.)
- **Semiflexible-rod bending energy** ~ (κ/2)∫(1/R)² ds with κ = k_B T Lₚ; filaments minimize curvature by sitting at the largest-radius equatorial great circle of the droplet. (Verbal; Lₚ = 5–18 µm.)
- **Motor active force / directional transport:** double-headed myosin binds two antiparallel filaments and slides them oppositely (Takiguchi 1991, ref 30) → "active diffusion" remodelling and, at high effective density, contractile force. Contraction requires connectivity percolation across the ring circumference (ref 47, critically-connected active gel). No functional Hill / Bell form is written; duty-ratio squaring (Eq. 1) is the operative motor law.
