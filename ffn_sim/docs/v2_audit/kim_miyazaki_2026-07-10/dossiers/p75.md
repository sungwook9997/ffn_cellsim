## Summary
The study asks whether advanced glycation end-products (AGEs), which accumulate in the extracellular matrix (ECM) in type-2 diabetes (T2DM), can promote hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) in the *pre-cirrhotic* (low-stiffness) liver by changing matrix **viscoelasticity** rather than stiffness. Using human liver samples, four mouse models (high-AGE diet HiAD; AGE-production inhibitor pyridoxamine PM; AGE-crosslink breaker alagebrium ALT-711; AGER1 reconstitution; hepatocyte RAGE knockout), reconstituted collagen/IPN 3D hydrogels, and an **agent-based fibrillar-collagen network simulation** (Taeyoon Kim), they show AGEs leave stiffness (storage/Young's modulus) unchanged but raise the loss tangent, hysteresis (viscous dissipation) and the *rate of stress relaxation* (lower τ½). Structurally, AGE crosslinking produces **shorter collagen fibres, tighter bundling, lower fibre–fibre angles and lower network interconnectivity/heterogeneity**; the simulation demonstrates causally that reducing fibril/bundle length or bundling angle (↑ heterogeneity, ↓ connectivity) accelerates stress relaxation under a fixed 20% shear strain. Faster-stress-relaxing ECM promotes HCC (more GS⁺MYC⁺ transformed foci, worse survival) via an **integrin-β1 → Tensin-1 (TNS1) → RhoA → YAP** mechanotransduction axis (molecular-clutch), driving proliferation, YAP nuclear activation (CTGF/CYR61 targets) and invadopodia formation; knockdown of TNS1 or integrin-β1, dd-TNS1 (PTB-domain deletion), ROCK/myosin-II inhibition, or dn-TEAD2 all block the phenotype. Conclusion: viscoelasticity (faster stress relaxation), independent of stiffness, is a pro-tumorigenic mechanical cue in NASH/T2DM-associated HCC.

## Model & Methods
**Computational model (the Taeyoon Kim relevant core):** Agent-based / coarse-grained **Brownian (Langevin) dynamics** of the discrete structure of a fibrillar collagen matrix.
- **Model type:** discrete cylindrical-segment representation. Three element species — **fibrils** (grey, "f"), **cross-linkers** (yellow, "xl"), **bundlers** (red, "bu") — each a cylindrical segment / spring network with extensional (κ_s) and bending (κ_b) stiffnesses.
- **Integrator / timestep:** motions of cylindrical elements updated at each time step via the **Langevin equation** with an **Euler integration scheme** (over-damped Brownian dynamics). Timestep value is in Supplementary Table 5 (not in this PDF).
- **Domain / BCs / geometry:** rectangular computational domain **20 × 20 × 5 μm** (x, y, z). **Periodic boundary conditions in x and z only** (not y). Fibrils are permanently bound to two rigid boundaries normal to y: +y boundary at y = 20 μm, −y boundary at y = 0 μm.
- **Loading protocol:** after matrix assembly, a **20% shear strain** is applied to the +y boundary in the x-direction while the −y boundary is fixed; the strain is then **held constant** and the resulting stress is recorded to measure **stress relaxation**.
- **Effective concentration:** effective collagen concentration computed from the specific volume of collagen (0.73 ml g⁻¹) = **3.65 mg ml⁻¹**.
- **Assembly algorithm:** nucleation of seed fibrils at random positions → elongation by segment addition up to **3 μm or 5 μm** fibril length, **no depolymerization**. (i) *Fibrillar (homogeneous fine mesh)* matrix: cross-linkers only, no bundlers — small mesh. (ii) *Long tight-bundle* matrix: cross-linkers + bundlers connecting fibrils in **parallel** into tight bundles (cross-linkers connect fibrils within a bundle or across bundles). (iii) *Short loose-bundle* matrix: bundlers connect fibril **ends** at a specific angle θ, giving loosely packed short bundles whose length ≈ fibril length.
- **Crosslinker vs bundler binding rules:** cross-linkers bind any part of two fibrils with **no cross-linking-angle preference**; **permanently** bound between fibrils but **unbind following Bell's law** (off-rate rises exponentially with force). Bundlers bind a pair of fibrils at a **specific maintained angle**; first binding site is always at a fibril **end**, second site is on a portion defined by two fractional boundaries b₁, b₂ ∈ [0,1] along the partner fibril — varying (b₁,b₂) tunes the resulting bundle length L_B.
- **Bundle-length control (ED Fig 5):** for fibril length 3 μm, (b₁=0, b₂=0.1)→L_B=3.8 μm; (b₁=0.2, b₂=1)→5.1 μm; (b₁=0.8, b₂=1)→8.6 μm. For fibril length 5 μm → L_B = 6.3, 9.8, 13.6 μm. Bundling angle swept θ = 0°, 5°, 10°, 30°.
- **Code:** https://github.com/ktyman2/liverCancer.git ; full parameters in Supplementary Table 5.

**Experimental methods (context / validation datums):** human & mouse liver AFM indentation (Bruker Resolve BioAFM; silicon-nitride cantilevers k = 0.01 N m⁻¹ and 0.06 N m⁻¹; borosilicate spherical tips diameter 10 μm and 5 μm; Hertz model); parallel-plate shear rheometry (ARES-G2 / AR2000EX, TA Instruments; 2% constant-strain dynamic sweep at 1 rad s⁻¹ for 600 s, then 10% step-strain stress relaxation for 600 s or 4 h); SHG two-photon collagen imaging (fibre length via CT-Fire, fibre–fibre angle via ImageJ); reconstituted collagen gels (3 mg ml⁻¹ AGE-modified type-I collagen), IPN 3D hydrogels (alginate + collagen or + reconstituted basement membrane rBM), AGE-BSA (glycolaldehyde 33 mM, 37 °C, 72 h); RNA-seq/KEGG/GSEA; CRISPR-Cas9 knockdown; PLA; western blot; RhoA-GTP pulldown.

## Governing Equations
The paper states the model form textually; explicit closed forms are in Supplementary Table 5/Methods (not printed here). Faithful transcription of what is given:

**(1) Over-damped Langevin update (integration of every cylindrical element):**
  ζ · dr_i/dt = F_i^{det}(r) + F_i^{thermal}(t) ,  advanced by an explicit **Euler** step r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (Δt/ζ)[F_i^{det} + F_i^{thermal}]
  — Brownian dynamics of fibril/crosslinker/bundler segment positions; ⟨F^{thermal}⟩=0 with fluctuation–dissipation setting its variance (thermal fluctuation of the cylindrical elements). *Meaning:* each segment relaxes under elastic + thermal forces at low Reynolds number.

**(2) Fibril extensional (stretching) potential:**  U_{s,f} = ½ κ_{s,f} (ℓ − ℓ_0)²  — Hookean spring keeping segment length near equilibrium ℓ_0.

**(3) Fibril bending potential:**  U_{b,f} = ½ κ_{b,f} (φ − φ_0)²  — angular spring keeping consecutive-segment bending angle near φ_0 (fibril rigidity / persistence).

**(4) Cross-linker extensional & bending potentials:**  U_{s,xl} = ½ κ_{s,xl}(ℓ−ℓ_0)² ;  U_{b,xl} = ½ κ_{b,xl}(θ−θ_0)²  — crosslink acts as spring between two fibril points; no preferred crosslinking angle (θ_0 unconstrained/free).

**(5) Bundler extensional & two bending potentials:**  U_{s,bu} = ½ κ_{s,bu}(ℓ−ℓ_0)² ;  U_{b,bu,1} = ½ κ_{b,bu,1}(θ_1−θ_{1,0})² ;  U_{b,bu,2} = ½ κ_{b,bu,2}(θ_2−θ_{2,0})²  — bundler enforces both a length and TWO angles (θ_1 about binding point b₁, θ_2 about b₂), maintaining fibrils in a specific parallel/angled bundle geometry.

**(6) Bell's-law crosslinker unbinding (force-dependent off-rate):**  k_off = k_off^0 · exp(F·x_b / k_B T)  — cross-linker detachment rate rises exponentially with the tension F it carries (bundlers are permanently bound; only cross-linkers unbind). This force-catalysed unbinding is the microscopic origin of stress relaxation.

**(7) Shear stress / relaxation observable:**  σ(t) at fixed γ = 20% ; stress-relaxation curve σ(t)/σ(0) → half-time τ½ (time for stress to fall to half its initial value). Lower τ½ = faster relaxation = higher viscoelasticity.

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Harmonic extensional springs** (κ_s,f, κ_s,xl, κ_s,bu): linear restoring force −κ_s(ℓ−ℓ_0).
- **Harmonic bending springs** (κ_b,f, κ_b,xl, κ_b,bu,1, κ_b,bu,2): restoring torque −κ_b(θ−θ_0).
- **Thermal (Brownian) force:** Gaussian white noise per fluctuation–dissipation.
- **Bell's law** for cross-linker off-rate (Eq. 6): the single kinetic/dissipative law; permanent bundler bonds + force-sensitive crosslink unbinding + fibril rearrangement give the emergent viscoelastic stress relaxation.
- (Numerical magnitudes of all κ, ℓ_0, θ_0, k_off^0, x_b, Δt, ζ are in **Supplementary Table 5**, not reproduced in this PDF.)
