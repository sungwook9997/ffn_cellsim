## Summary
The paper asks how two spatially distinct actomyosin structures — a **bulk (cytoplasmic) actomyosin meshwork** and a **surface-associated actomyosin cortex** — jointly set whether an intracellular body sits at the geometric center or off-center at the boundary. Using an in-vitro model of **metaphase Xenopus laevis egg extracts encapsulated in water-in-oil droplets** (natural phospholipid monolayer boundary, quasi-2D between PDMS-coated glass, nocodazole to remove microtubules, TMR-LifeAct to image F-actin), they show the extract self-organizes: initial network contraction sweeps organelles into a single **nucleus-like "cluster,"** then **periodic centripetal actomyosin waves (rings)** propagate from the boundary to the center. Cluster positioning is **two-state and droplet-size-dependent**: **large droplets (D ≳ 100 µm) center** the cluster, **small droplets (D ≲ 100 µm) push it to the edge**, with a sharp transition (control transition diameter Dc = 85 µm). They dissect the mechanism as a **tug-of-war**: (i) periodic actomyosin **waves push** the cluster centrally each wave period T (weakly size-dependent, T ≈ 46 s); (ii) stochastically maturing **actomyosin "bridges"** (percolated bulk network linking cluster to boundary) **pull** it outward, with a maturation time τp that is strongly (exponentially) size-dependent. Whichever timescale is shorter wins; their crossing sets Dc. A **percolation model** yields an analytical edge-probability p(D) (Eq. 2) that quantitatively fits the data and, via molecular perturbations, correctly predicts Dc shifts: **α-actinin** crosslinker (250 nM) raises Dc to 115 µm; **gelsolin** severing (300 nM, shorter filaments) lowers Dc to 60 µm; **formin mDia2** (30 nM, longer filaments) raises Dc to 138 µm. Surface modulation confirms the boundary interaction is the outward-force source: **PEG passivation** centers clusters; **membrane-anchored VCA→Arp2/3 activation** edges them (and even initiates droplet migration). Laser ablation of the outward bridges transiently re-centers the cluster.

## Model & Methods
**Experimental system (primary):** In-vitro reconstitution. Actin-intact metaphase Xenopus laevis egg cytoplasmic extracts encapsulated as water-in-oil droplets, boundary = monolayer of natural phospholipid (L-α-phosphatidylcholine from egg yolk in mineral oil). Quasi-2D confinement between two PDMS-coated glass slides; **aspect ratio h/Ddroplet fixed 0.3–0.6**; droplet diameters **16–330 µm** (encapsulation range ~10–350 µm). Nocodazole (100 µM final) suppresses microtubules; TMR-LifeAct (1 µM) marks F-actin; R18 marks organelles/lipid. Temperature raised 0→20 °C to trigger polymerization; imaging at 20 ± 1 °C, frames every 3 s (epifluorescence IX73, ×20 or ×50); confocal (CSU-X1, 561 nm) and **UV pulsed-laser ablation** (Explorer One 349 nm) for cutting rings/bridges; PIV (Fiji plugin) for flow fields. Surface variants: **PEG30-DPHS** passivation; **DGS-NTA(Ni) lipids + His-VCA** to anchor WASP-VCA and activate Arp2/3 at boundary. Filament length measured by rhodamine-phalloidin stabilization + NEM-HMM flow-chamber fixation + MATLAB skeletonization. Statistics: Kolmogorov–Smirnov test.

**Theory (secondary):** Two coupled analytical models.
1. **Bridge maturation = 1D network percolation.** N crosslinking sites in series between boundary and center; each site is "on" with prob 1/2 per turnover time τ; bridge forms when all N occupied simultaneously. Gives Eq. (1)/(2) and mean maturation time τp = 2^N τ.
2. **Wave period = active gel dynamics with F-actin growth** (based on Hiraiwa & Salbreux, PRL 2016, active stress from turnover). Wave period T = t0 = time to (i) grow contacting F-actin network, (ii) bind myosin/crosslinkers, (iii) begin ring contraction; predicts weak size dependence of T and the wave contraction velocity/profile (Supplementary Notes 1–2).

**Numerical simulation:** Stochastic simulation of cluster motion coupled to periodic waves + stochastic percolation; time-averaged DC-ratio (1/T0)∫₀^{T0}[d(t)/R]dt with T0 = 1800 s per droplet; edge if DC-ratio > 0.8, center if < 0.2 (thresholds differ from experiment because simulated clusters have no finite volume). Details in Supplementary Note 4.

## Governing Equations
1. **(Eq. 1) Bridge/percolation probability over interval T:**
   `p(N) = 1 − [ 1 − (1/2)^N ]^{T/τ}` — probability that all N in-series crosslinking sites are simultaneously occupied at least once during time T (i.e., a force-percolating actomyosin bridge forms). Meaning: symmetry-breaking (edge) probability from bulk-network percolation.
2. **(turnover time)** `τ = 1 / (koff + C0·kon)` — characteristic crosslinker turnover time; C0 = crosslinker concentration; kon, koff = binding/unbinding rates. (from Hu et al. 2010, ref 35; Wachsstock 1994, ref 36.)
3. **(site count)** `N ≃ R/L = D/(2L)` — number of crosslinking sites along the boundary-to-center path; R = droplet radius, L = typical F-actin contour length, D = 2R = droplet diameter.
4. **(Eq. 2) Edge-positioning probability vs droplet diameter:**
   `p(D) = 1 − [ 1 − (1/2)^{D/(2L)} ]^{T(D)/τ}` — analytical two-state edge probability; fitted to experimental edge-probability curves.
5. **(wave period)** `T = t0` — contraction period of the actomyosin ring equals the maturation time t0 of a new F-actin ring (growth + myosin binding + onset of contraction); weakly size-dependent, T(D).
6. **(mean maturation time)** `τp = 2^N τ`, with N ≃ R/L — expected number of turnover steps to occupy all N sites × τ; strongly (exponentially) size-dependent, τp ∝ e^R.
7. **(Eq. 3) Transition (critical) radius from τp = T:**
   `Rc / L = log₂( T / τ )` — critical radius where the two timescales cross; Dc = 2Rc. Using T ≈ 46 s, L = 5.7 µm, τ = 0.54 s → **Dc = 2Rc ≈ 73 µm** (vs measured 85 µm).
8. **(DC-ratio, polarity parameter)** `DC-ratio = d / Rdroplet` — cluster off-centering; d = distance droplet-center→cluster-centroid. Edge if DC-ratio > 0.5, center if < 0.2.

## Force Laws & Potentials
This paper does not use particle-level force laws (no Bell/Hill/bending potentials); mechanics are coarse-grained into **active gel stress** and **percolation-gated contractile force transmission**. Explicit force statements:
- **Centripetal force:** periodic actomyosin waves (contracting rings) generate a strong **lateral (hoop) contractile force**; UV ablation of a ring opens it in a few seconds (direct evidence of ring tension). Wave pushes cluster to center each period T.
- **Outward radial force:** once bulk F-actin **percolates** into an inter-connected "bridge" between cluster and boundary, **myosin contraction along the bridge pulls** the cluster to the edge; ablating the bridge lets the cluster relax back toward center, then it re-forms (F-actin density between cluster and nearest wall increases as bridge reassembles).
- **Force-percolation concept (refs 32–34):** myosin contractile force transmits over long distance only when enough crosslinking sites are occupied (force-percolation transition). Long filaments (large L) and many crosslinkers (small τ) raise percolation probability → larger Dc.
- **Surface interaction:** boundary Arp2/3 activation (VCA) increases bulk-network–boundary coupling (more outward force → edge); PEG passivation reduces it (→ center). Edge-positioning by boundary Arp2/3 even drives whole-droplet migration (inner force transmitted to outer substrate).

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Partial. Not a surface-mesh continuum cell, but the physics maps onto DCM themes: a bounded, cell-sized confinement with a contractile cortex/boundary and an intracellular body (nucleus-like cluster) whose position emerges from cortex-vs-bulk force balance. The **wave period T(D), contraction velocity v(D), and cluster-size law (Dcluster=0.25·Ddroplet)** are candidate qualitative benchmarks for a DCM confinement + nucleus-positioning demo; but there is no elastic modulus / turgor / node-face data to anchor DCM constitutive parameters.
- **FF?** Yes (conceptually strong). This is fine-grained actomyosin: explicit **crosslinker turnover (τ=1/(koff+C0kon), koff=0.66 s⁻¹, kon=1.2×10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹), F-actin length control (gelsolin/mDia2), Arp2/3 nucleation, myosin contractility, and force percolation**. The percolation/tug-of-war is exactly the emergent-network-connectivity regime an FF Cytosim-style engine could reproduce (long filaments + many crosslinkers → percolated contractile bridge). Rate constants and length scales (L~4–10 µm) are directly usable FF inputs/validation targets.
- **TAG-validation?** Yes. Clean quantitative targets with provenance: crosslinker kon/koff/τ (Wachsstock 1994), Xenopus actin ~20 µM & α-actinin ~1 µM (Wühr 2014), wave period ~46 s, velocity 1–5 µm/s, transition-diameter law and its analytic form Rc/L=log₂(T/τ). Good KnowledgeClaim candidates for a crosslinker-turnover / actin-length / percolation-threshold parameter, all DOI-anchored — but note egg-extract context (not MCF7), so register as reconstitution reference values, not cell-type-specific.
