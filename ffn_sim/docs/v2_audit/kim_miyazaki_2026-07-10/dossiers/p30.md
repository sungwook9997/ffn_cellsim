## Summary
The paper asks how a single cytoplasmic actomyosin system, confined in a cell-sized quasi-2D circular compartment, can spontaneously select between two distinct self-organized contractile patterns — a **steady inward actin flow** versus **ring-shaped periodic actin waves** — and what physical parameters control the transition. Experimentally, M-phase *Xenopus laevis* egg extracts (actin-intact cytoplasm, with Arp2/3 highly active) are encapsulated in water-in-oil droplets (200–300 µm diameter, chamber height 60 µm) covered by a phospholipid monolayer, and F-actin is imaged (TMR-Lifeact, confocal, midplane, 6 s/frame). Increasing myosin contractility with the phosphatase inhibitor Calyculin A drives a steady-flow → periodic-wave transition (waves appear above ~0.3 pM CalA, period T≈1.5 min set by polymerization not contractility); a panel of molecular perturbations (CalA↑σ, VVCA/N-WASP↑kp, Cytochalasin D↓kp, Y27632↓σ, CK666 Arp2/3 inhibition) shows the two knobs are **myosin contractility σact and F-actin polymerization rate kp**. To rationalize this, they build a continuum **active-fluid model** (momentum balance of active + viscous stress against friction, plus an F-actin mass-conservation/reaction-advection-diffusion equation with density-dependent active stress) embedded in a **phase-field** representation of the nondeformable circular droplet. The single dimensionless control parameter is the **Péclet number Pe = (ζΔµ)₀/(Dγ)** (effective contractility): steady flow at Pe=38, periodic waves at Pe=115, with the transition also driven by lowering surface polymerization ksurf_p and by the bulk-to-surface polymerization contrast αp = kbulk_p/ksurf_p (<0.5 needed for waves). Finally, imposing a spatial/temporal heterogeneity in the onset of contraction (a time delay τd across four quadrants) produces a **rotational actin wave**, stable only along the flow↔wave phase boundary, independent of the delay magnitude. The work unifies previously separate observations (steady flow vs. periodic waves vs. rotational waves) as one actomyosin system's phase behavior governed purely by mechanics.

## Model & Methods
**Two parallel methodologies.**

**(A) Experiment (in-vitro reconstitution):**
- Actin-intact cytoplasmic extracts from *Xenopus laevis* M-phase eggs (physiological protein concentrations; Arp2/3 highly active).
- Encapsulated into water-in-oil droplets: 0.4 µL extract injected into 14 µL lipid–oil mix (1 mM eggPC in mineral oil), tapped to a polydisperse emulsion. Droplets confined between PDMS-coated glass slide + coverslip; chamber height h=60 µm set by spacers → quasi-2D circular confinement, droplet diameter 200–300 µm.
- F-actin labeled TMR-Lifeact (final 1 µM); microtubules suppressed with nocodazole (final 100 µM).
- Confocal microscopy at droplet midplane, 561 nm channel, ×20 objective, 6 s/frame; room temp 20±1 °C.
- Molecular perturbations (to move along the two control axes): Calyculin A (myosin II phosphatase inhibitor → ↑σact), VVCA domain of N-WASP (→ activates Arp2/3 → ↑kp), Cytochalasin D (F-actin polym. inhibitor → ↓kp), Y27632 (ROCK/myosin phosphorylation inhibitor → ↓σact), CK666 (Arp2/3 inhibitor), SMIFH2 (nominal formin inhibitor, but also inhibits myosin ATPase — non-specific, discarded).
- Analysis: angular kymographs over ROI 0.45R<r<0.55R; Fourier transform of angle-averaged intensity → wave period; PIV (Fiji ImageJ plugin) for velocity fields.

**(B) Theory/Numerics (continuum active fluid + phase field):**
- Model type: continuum active-gel hydrodynamics (isotropic, orientation neglected) coupled to a scalar phase field for the droplet domain.
- Governing PDEs solved by **finite-difference** for density & phase fields (Eqs. 7,8) and **spectral method** for the velocity/force-balance (Eq. 6), marched explicitly.
- Grid/timestep: Δx = Δy = 10⁻¹, Δt = 10⁻⁵ (verified convergence at smaller values).
- Boundary condition: immobile, nondeformable circular droplet (advection term −v·∇φ dropped); no-flux for actomyosin gel imposed via an effective interfacial energy E(ρ,φ); membrane bending rigidity neglected.
- Nondimensionalization: length unit l=(η/γ)^{1/2}, time unit τ=η/(Dγ).
- Rotational-wave protocol: domain split into 4 quadrants; region 1 evolves from t=0, regions 2–4 held at initial value until t=τd (system-wide symmetry-breaking perturbation; small random noise was insufficient).

## Governing Equations
Dimensional forms:

1. **Momentum balance (Stokesian active fluid, inertialess):**
   ∇·(σ_vis + σ_act) = γ v   — divergence of total stress balances effective friction (cytoplasm+membrane), γ = friction coeff, v = gel velocity.  *(Eq. 1)*

2. **Viscous (dissipative) stress:**
   σ_vis = η[∇v + (∇v)ᵀ] + η_b (∇·v) I   — Newtonian viscous fluid; η dynamic viscosity, η_b bulk viscosity, I identity.  *(Eq. 2)*

3. **Active (contractile) stress, density-dependent:**
   σ_act = (ζμ)₀ f(ρ) I, with f(ρ)=ρ/(ρ₀+ρ)   — isotropic contractile stress ∝ saturating function of actomyosin density; (ζμ)₀ = effective myosin contractile stress per F-actin; ρ₀ constant. (Myosin density assumed ∝ F-actin density.)  *(Eq. 3)*

4. **F-actin mass conservation (reaction–advection–diffusion):**
   ∂ρ/∂t + ∇·(ρv) = D∇²ρ + kp − kd ρ   — advection by gel flow + diffusion (D effective F-actin diffusion) + polymerization source kp − depolymerization sink kd ρ.  *(Eq. 4)*

5. **Phase-field dynamics (droplet domain):**
   ∂φ/∂t = Dφ ∇²φ + Γφ U′(φ), with U′(φ)=φ(φ−1)[φ − 1/2 − α₀(V/V_tar − 1)]   — φ=1 inside / φ=0 outside droplet; Dφ ~ surface-tension-like stiffness; double-well potential U with volume-conservation term; V=∫dr φ, target V_tar; α₀ constraint stiffness. Boundary width ∝ (8Dφ/Γφ)^{1/2}.  *(Eq. 5)*

Nondimensionalized full set (length l=(η/γ)^{1/2}, time τ=η/Dγ; rescalings ρ/ρ₀→ρ, kp/(ρ₀τ)→kp, kd/τ→kd):

6. **Nondimensional force balance:**
   ∇²v + λ∇(∇·v) + ∇f = v, with λ = 1 + η_b/η = 1/3   — Stokes flow driven by active-stress gradient ∇f against unit friction.  *(Eq. 6)*

7. **Nondimensional F-actin transport (with no-flux energy term):**
   ∂ρ/∂t + Pe ∇·(ρv) = ∇²ρ + kp − kd ρ + ε_ρ ∇²(δE/δρ)   — Pe multiplies advection; last term enforces no-flux at droplet wall. Functional derivative: δE/δρ = ρ{(ρ²+β)⁻¹[(1−φ)²+β]}^{1/2}.  *(Eq. 7)*

8. **Nondimensional phase field:**
   ∂φ/∂t = Dφ ∇²φ + Γφ U′(φ).  *(Eq. 8)*

Auxiliary definitions:
- **Péclet number (effective contractility):** Pe = C l / D = (ζμ)₀ / (Dγ), with characteristic velocity C = (ζμ)₀ / (ηγ)^{1/2}.
- **No-flux effective energy:** E(ρ,φ) = ∫ dr {(ρ²+β)[(1−φ)²+β]}^{1/2}, β avoids singularities.
- **Bulk-to-surface polymerization ratio:** αp ≡ kbulk_p / ksurf_p (=0.1 baseline). kbulk_p acts at 0<r<0.9R; ksurf_p acts at 0.9R<r<R (ΔR=0.1R surface shell).
- **Wave-period measurement (experiment):** I(t) ≡ ∫_{0.45R}^{0.55R} ∫_0^{2π} I(r,θ,t) dr dθ; Ī(ω)=(1/2π)∫_0^{10min} I(t) e^{−iωt} dt; T_peak = 2π/ω_peak (T→∞ ⇔ steady flow).

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Active contractile stress:** σ_act = (ζμ)₀ · ρ/(ρ₀+ρ) · I — Michaelis–Menten-type saturation in density; isotropic (pressure-like) tensor.
- **Viscous stress:** Newtonian σ_vis = η[∇v+(∇v)ᵀ] + η_b(∇·v)I.
- **Friction:** external drag −γv (lumped cytoplasm + membrane friction) balances stress divergence.
- **Double-well phase-field potential:** U′(φ)=φ(φ−1)[φ−1/2−α₀(V/V_tar−1)] — fixed points φ=0,1; volume-conservation shift via α₀(V/V_tar−1).
- **Interfacial no-flux energy:** E=∫dr{(ρ²+β)[(1−φ)²+β]}^{1/2}, contributing chemical-potential term ε_ρ∇²(δE/δρ) that repels gel from the droplet wall.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM? YES.** This is a directly reusable continuum active-fluid + **phase-field** cortex/cytoplasm model at the exact scale DCM cares about (cell-sized confinement, membrane friction −γv, saturating density-dependent active stress σact=(ζµ)₀·ρ/(ρ₀+ρ), viscous stress, volume-conserving phase field). It offers a validated *pattern-selection* (flow vs. wave vs. rotational) target and the Pe=(ζµ)₀/(Dγ) control-parameter framing for cortex contractility. The nondeformable-droplet choice is a stepping-stone toward the deformable DCM surface; the double-well U with V/V_tar constraint parallels DCM's enclosed-volume/turgor treatment.
- **FF? PARTIAL.** The physics (myosin contractility + Arp2/3/formin polymerization + turnover controlling network self-organization) is exactly FF's mechanistic domain, and the perturbation map (CalA↑σ, VVCA↑kp, CytoD↓kp, Y27632↓σ, CK666) gives molecular-lever → emergent-pattern expectations a fine-grained filament sim could reproduce. But the paper itself is coarse-grained continuum, not filament-resolved, so it is a *target/benchmark* for FF rather than a parameter source. No Bell-Evans/Hill/crosslinker constants here.
- **TAG-validation? YES.** Clean quantitative validation targets: wave onset above ~0.3 pM CalA, wave period T≈1.5 min, steady-flow PIV speed ~0.7 µm/s, Pe thresholds (flow at 38, waves at 115), αp<0.5 wave criterion, rotational wave only at the flow/wave boundary. These are citable emergent-observable checks for a confined-actomyosin module. Provenance is clear (Miyazaki/Maeda lab, peer-reviewed PRR 2023).
