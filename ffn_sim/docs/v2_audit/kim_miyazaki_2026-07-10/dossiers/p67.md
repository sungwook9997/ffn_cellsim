## Summary
The paper asks whether, under the high prestrain/prestress typical of living cells, actin cross-linking proteins (specifically **filamin A**) preferentially **unbind** from actin or **unfold** their Ig domains — two mechanisms with different hysteresis, time-scales, and remodeling consequences. Using an **optical-tweezers force spectroscopy (OTFS)** single-molecule pulling assay in which one filamin cross-links a substrate-immobilized (biotinylated) actin filament to a second actin filament bound to a gelsolin-coated 1 µm bead, they measure force–extension curves at loading rates of 400–2000 pN/s (stage translated at 5 µm/s). They find **unbinding at 70 ± 23 pN and unfolding at 57 ± 19 pN** — remarkably similar critical forces — with unfolding identified by a ~28 nm sawtooth period (Ig-domain contour-length gain). A Hummer–Szabo (HS) kinetic model fit yields the intrinsic off-rate, transition-state distance, and molecular spring constant for both processes. Rupture force decreases sharply once the inter-filament pulling angle exceeds ~45° (shear/torsion weakens the bond), and rupture force rises with the log of loading rate with two distinct slopes (two energy barriers). A second **cross-linked F-actin network assay** (drag a trapped bead through a filamin/actin gel) reproduces the discrete force-drop transitions (20–56 pN), and a **3-D Brownian-dynamics actin-network simulation** (Kim's model, with Bell's-law unbinding) qualitatively reproduces the bead force curve and shows the abrupt drops arise from unbinding of ABPs near the bead. Conclusion: both unbinding and unfolding occur at similar forces and both are important for cytoskeletal rheology, remodeling, and strain-softening.

## Model & Methods
**Two experimental assays + one computational model.**

**(1) Single-molecule OTFS pulling assay.** Optical trap (1064 nm Coherent laser, 100× 1.40 NA oil objective; AOD-steered; back-focal-plane detection with a 975 nm beam on a PSD; 16-bit A/D; bead position sampled at 3–10 kHz). Trap stiffness 0.5–0.75 pN/nm. Bottom actin filament biotinylated & immobilized on streptavidin/biotin-BSA glass; top actin filament tethered to a 1 µm gelsolin-coated carboxylated polystyrene bead; the two filaments cross-linked by one filamin A. Sample stage translated at **constant 5 µm/s** relative to the fixed trap → loading rate 400–2000 pN/s. Force = bead displacement × trap stiffness. Position resolution <1 nm.

**(2) Cross-linked F-actin network assay.** Reconstituted gel: final actin 10 µM, filamin:actin molar ratio 0.01, gelsolin sets mean filament length ~5 µm; embedded 1 µm bead captured by trap; stage translated at constant 5 µm/s; resistive force = bead-to-trap-center distance × trap stiffness.

**(3) Computational model — 3-D Brownian-dynamics agent-based actin network** (methods of Kim, Hwang & Kamm 2009, ref 22):
- Network built by polymerization + cross-linking of uniformly distributed actin monomers with randomly dispersed ABPs forming an orthogonal (filamin-like) network.
- Actin concentration 12 µM; mean filament length ~1.5 µm; ABP:actin molar ratio 0.017; cubic domain width 5 µm.
- Filaments and ABPs move by **Brownian (Langevin overdamped) dynamics** with bending and extensional stiffness.
- After polymerization, all filaments are **severed and clamped at the z-surfaces (top/bottom)**; **periodic BCs** on x- and y-surfaces.
- A **1.2 µm microbead** is inserted by replacing all filaments in a central spherical region with a rigid sphere.
- Bead forced in **+x at constant 20 µm/s**.
- ABP unbinding follows **Bell's equation** with **k_off = 0.115 s⁻¹** and **x = 0.416 nm**; only extensional forces on ABPs are considered.
- Records total x-force on bead and each ABP's bound/free state vs. bead displacement.
- Critical-unbinding filter for analysis: (1) ABP-to-bead-surface distance <500 nm at unbinding, and (2) ABP within a cone about +x with included angle 2π/3.

**Kinetic analysis:** Hummer–Szabo (HS) model fit to rupture/unfolding force distributions to extract k_off, x‡ (transition-state distance), κ_m (molecular spring constant), and barrier height ΔG.

## Governing Equations
1. **Bell's equation (force-dependent off-rate)** — used in the BD simulation:
   k_off(F) = k_off⁰ · exp(F·x / k_B T)
   with k_off⁰ = 0.115 s⁻¹, x = 0.416 nm (simulation values). Meaning: unbinding rate increases exponentially with applied extensional force (slip bond).

2. **Free-energy barrier from HS parameters:**
   ΔG = ½ · κ_m · x‡²
   Meaning: barrier height equals the elastic energy stored in the molecular spring stretched to the transition-state distance. (Unfolding ΔG = 6.8 k_BT > unbinding 5.6 k_BT at high loading rate.)

3. **Loading rate definition:** r = dF/dt = slope of a linear fit to the force-vs-time trace just prior to rupture (400–2000 pN/s high regime; 5–50 pN/s low regime from Ferrer 2008).

4. **Dynamic-force-spectroscopy scaling (implied, Bell/Evans-Ritchie/HS):** rupture force increases linearly with ln(loading rate); a change in slope signals suppression of an outer energy barrier by force (two-barrier landscape).

5. **Ig-domain contour-length gain (unfolding period):** ΔL_c ≈ N_aa × d_aa = 96 amino acids × 3.5 Å ≈ 34 nm theoretical; measured period 28 ± 5 nm.

6. **Network force estimate (both assays):** F = (bead displacement from trap center) × (trap stiffness).

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Cross-link unbinding:** Bell slip-bond exponential off-rate (Eq. 1). Simulation constants k_off⁰ = 0.115 s⁻¹, x = 0.416 nm.
- **Filament mechanics (simulation):** actin segments carry **bending stiffness** and **extensional (stretching) stiffness** (harmonic; functional constants not tabulated in this paper — refer to Kim/Hwang/Kamm 2009).
- **ABP as a molecular spring:** linear spring constant κ_m (646 pN/nm unbinding, 266 pN/nm unfolding at high loading rate; 820 pN/nm unbinding at low loading rate) with a single sharp transition state at x‡.
- **Optical trap:** Hookean restoring force F = k_trap × Δx, k_trap = 0.5–0.75 pN/nm.
