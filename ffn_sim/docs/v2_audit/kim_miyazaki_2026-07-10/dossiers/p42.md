## Summary
The paper asks what causes the well-known **cyclic stress relaxation** of cross-linked actin networks under large oscillatory shear — a phenomenon previously attributed almost entirely to force-dependent unbinding of actin cross-linking proteins (ACPs). Using a well-established 3D agent-based Brownian-dynamics model of actin filaments (F-actins) + ACPs in a periodic cubic domain (L = 3 µm, PBC in x and y), the authors add **F-actin severing driven by compression-induced buckling** and compare stress buildup/relaxation with and without severing under bidirectional vs unidirectional cyclic shear (50% amplitude). Key finding: severing produces pronounced cyclic stress relaxation (matching in-vitro sinusoidal-strain experiments), whereas ACP unbinding alone does not. Severing occurs preferentially on filaments oriented ~135° (perpendicular to the ~45° "supportive framework"), i.e. outside the load-bearing framework, because those filaments buckle under compression at high strains; the resulting high local curvature triggers severing. Because severed filaments lie outside the current supportive framework, severing barely relaxes stress during the current strain-stiffening half-cycle but strongly relaxes stress when the strain reverses sign (the ex-buckled filaments would have become the supportive framework in the next half-cycle). This is why bidirectional cyclic strain shows relaxation but unidirectional does not. Severing-induced relaxation is strongest for **long filaments and high cross-linking density R_ACP** (up to ~70% drop), governed by Euler buckling F_buckle ∝ κ/L_c². Severing and ACP unbinding act **independently and orthogonally**: ACP unbinding occurs at ~45° (supportive framework, high-load ACPs |F̄_s,ACP| ≥ 8 pN) and relaxes stress within a half-cycle; severing occurs at ~135° and relaxes stress across cycles — the two do not interfere.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** 3D **agent-based Brownian dynamics** of a coarse-grained actin cytoskeleton — the canonical Taeyoon Kim model (F-actins as cylindrical segment chains connected by extensional + bending springs; ACPs as two-armed cross-linkers). Filaments and ACPs are explicit agents (particles/bonds). Full equations/parameters are in the SI; this Letter states the model is "well-established" and cites Kim, Hwang, Kamm 2009/2011 (refs 8, 13).
- **Domain / BCs:** Cubical domain, edge L_s = L_z = 3 µm (Figure 1). **Periodic boundary conditions in x and y** to emulate an infinitely large network. Shear applied in the x–z plane.
- **Loading protocol:** Cyclic shear strain, amplitude 50% (baseline), applied bidirectionally (±50%) or unidirectionally (0→+50%). Low strain rate. Period ~20 s (peaks at t ≈ 10, 30, 50 s per Figures 2a, 3, 5a). Also a parameter sweep of 20 combinations of average filament length ⟨L_f⟩ and cross-linking density R_ACP.
- **Severing mechanism:** Modeled as a **stochastic event** (eq S9, in SI) whose rate depends on local F-actin bending/curvature; buckling raises the bending angle and thus severing probability. Severing rate is a tunable input (Fig S2 shows dependence). Because it is stochastic, relaxation is strain-rate dependent (Figs S3a,b).
- **ACP unbinding:** Force-dependent (Bell-like) off-rate, k_u = k_u⁰·exp(...) (eq S8, in SI). Reference rate k_u⁰. At F_buckle = 4.1 pN the rate rises only 1.1×, i.e. buckling forces are too small to trigger significant ACP unbinding — so severing precedes unbinding on buckled filaments.
- **Observables:** Shear stress σ (measured via virial/segment forces), percentage drop in stress between successive positive-strain peaks (eq 1), severing-event frequency, orientation histograms of severed segments and of high-load unbinding ACPs, cross-linking distance distributions.
- **Integrator/timestep:** Not stated in the Letter (in SI). The Kim model conventionally uses Euler-integrated Langevin/Brownian dynamics with an implicit-solvent Stokes drag.

## Governing Equations
1. **Percentage drop in stress (eq 1):**
   drop in stress = (σ₁ − σ₂)/σ₁ × 100
   — cyclic-relaxation metric; σ₁ = stress at first maximal positive strain, σ₂ = stress at second maximal positive strain.

2. **Euler buckling force (eq 2):**
   F_buckle ∼ π²·κ_b,A·r_0,A / L_c²
   — minimum compressive force to buckle an F-actin segment of cross-linking span L_c; κ_b,A = bending stiffness parameter of actin, r_0,A = equilibrium (rest) length scale of an actin segment. F_buckle ∝ L_c⁻². (Rendered form: F_buckle ∼ π²κ_{b,A}r_{0,A}/L_c².) The paper uses this to explain why low R_ACP → long L_c → small F_buckle but also small curvature → few severing events, whereas high R_ACP → short L_c → large curvature on buckling → frequent severing.

3. **ACP force-dependent unbinding (eq S8, referenced, form in SI):**
   k_u = k_u⁰ · exp(F·λ/k_BT)  [Bell-type, exact form in SI]
   — worked value given: for L_c = 300 nm, F_buckle = 4.1 pN ⇒ k_u = 1.1·k_u⁰ (only 1.1-fold increase).

4. **Stochastic severing rate (eq S9, referenced, form in SI):**
   Severing modeled as a stochastic (Poisson) event with a curvature/bending-angle-dependent rate; not transcribed here (SI). Its magnitude sets the degree of relaxation (Fig S2) and its stochastic nature causes strain-rate dependence (Figs S3a,b).

(The extension/bending harmonic potentials for F-actin segments and the ACP arm potentials are in the SI and are the standard Kim-model forms; not reproduced in the Letter.)

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **F-actin buckling / bending:** Euler beam theory, eq 2 above; bending governed by κ_b,A (bending stiffness) — buckling at F ≥ F_buckle produces curvature that, if high enough, triggers severing.
- **ACP unbinding:** Bell-type slip-bond force-dependent off-rate (eq S8), reference k_u⁰; high-load threshold used in analysis |F̄_s,ACP| ≥ 8 pN.
- **F-actin severing:** Stochastic bending/curvature-dependent scission (eq S9) — the mechanistic novelty of the paper; severing removes a segment bond irreversibly (no rebinding, unlike ACP).
- Standard Kim-model harmonic extensional + bending springs for filament segments and two-arm ACPs (SI).

## Figures
- **Figure 1:** Schematic of the network — F-actins (cyan/red/blue) + ACPs (orange) in a 3 µm cubic domain, before and after shear. Red = diagonal ~45° filaments under extension forming the supportive framework; blue = ~135° filaments under compression, prone to buckle. PBC in x,y. Establishes the two orthogonal filament populations.
- **Figure 2:** (a) Stress vs time under bidirectional cyclic strain with severing (blue △, strongly relaxes after cycle 1) vs without severing (red ○, no relaxation); peak stress ~100–120 Pa. (b) % stress drop vs strain amplitude: bidirectional (red ○) rises to ~75% at 0.5, unidirectional (blue △) stays ~0. Inset: severing-event frequency peaks as strain nears ±peaks. (c) Orientation histogram of severed segments — peaks ~135°, perpendicular to the ~45° framework; inset defines angle φ on x–z plane. (d) Stress vs time under unidirectional cyclic strain — negligible relaxation despite severing being active. Conclusion: relaxation needs strain reversal.
- **Figure 3:** 3×4 schematic matrix (t = 0,10,30,50 s) for three conditions: bidirectional no-severing (top, no relaxation), bidirectional + severing (middle, red framework severed at negative strains → relaxation at 50 s), unidirectional + severing (bottom, negligible relaxation). Cyan arrows mark severing in buckled filaments. Mechanistic cartoon of the whole story.
- **Figure 4:** Phase maps over (R_ACP ∈ ~0.005–0.05, ⟨L_f⟩ ∈ 0.7–1.3 µm). (a) First-max stress: 0.1→>100 Pa, >3 orders sensitivity, increases with both. (b) % stress drop: highest at high R_ACP + long L_f (up to ~70%); negligible at top-left (long L_f, low R_ACP) despite high stress. (c) Severing frequency: tracks (b). (d) Cross-linking-distance distributions for the four corners — exponential, strongly set by R_ACP (longer L_c at low R_ACP). Explains buckling-vs-curvature tradeoff via eq 2.
- **Figure 5:** (a) Stress vs time at R_ACP=0.1 for severing-only (red ○), unbinding-only (blue △, low first-peak stress but maintained over cycles), and both (green ▽, looks like severing-only) — severing and unbinding do not interfere; peak ~150 Pa. (b) Orientation histogram: high-load ACP unbinding (black) peaks ~45° (supportive framework); severing (white) peaks ~135° (outside framework) — orthogonal, independent mechanisms.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Weak/indirect. DCM is a surface-mesh continuum with lumped cortex tension, not an explicit filament network — this paper's severing/buckling physics is sub-grid to DCM. It could inform an effective viscoelastic/stress-relaxation constitutive law for the cortex (cyclic softening ∝ strain amplitude), but not directly.
- **FF?** YES — strongly. This is the canonical Taeyoon Kim agent-based actin-network model, which is architecturally the same family as FF (explicit filaments as bending beams + explicit ACP cross-linkers with force-dependent Bell off-rates + buckling). Directly relevant to: (1) implementing F-actin severing via curvature/bending-angle-dependent stochastic scission (eq S9) as a fine-grained mechanism, (2) Euler-buckling F_buckle ∝ κ/L_c² for filament stability, (3) Bell-type ACP unbinding (eq S8) matching FF's Bell-Evans clutch/cross-linker kinetics, (4) the supportive-framework (~45°) vs buckled (~135°) load-partition physics. Severing is currently NOT in FF's mechanism set — this paper is the reference to add it mechanistically. NOTE: full equations + parameter values are in the SI, which is not in this file and should be obtained for a complete FF port.
- **TAG-validation?** YES — provides quantitative validation targets: peak shear stress ~100–150 Pa at 50% strain (R_ACP 0.032–0.1), cyclic stress drop 0→~70% scaling with amplitude/⟨L_f⟩/R_ACP, F_buckle=4.1 pN at L_c=300 nm, ~135°/~45° orthogonal orientation split, exponential cross-linking-distance distribution. These are literature datums for a KB SourceEvidence row (Kim-lab reconstituted-network rheology) and FF network-rheology gates. Sign/behavior checks (bidirectional relaxes, unidirectional does not) are good qualitative gates.
