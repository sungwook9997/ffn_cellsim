## Summary
The paper asks how the **viscoelastic (stress-relaxing) nature of the ECM** shapes the way a single contractile cell remodels its surrounding matrix and how the resulting stress builds up, propagates, and relaxes over time — a time-dependent question that prior elastic-only fiber-network models could not address. The authors build a 2.5-D **agent-based (Langevin / Brownian-dynamics) discrete-fiber model**: a single cell (membrane + always-contractile actomyosin cortex, coarse-grained as serially connected rectangular solid elements) sits at the center of a cylindrical collagen matrix (radius 100 µm, thickness 1 µm) built from serially connected cylindrical fiber elements interconnected by **explicit transient cross-linkers** whose unbinding follows a **force-dependent Bell's-law slip-bond**. Viscoelasticity is not lumped into a continuum modulus — it emerges mechanistically from the binding/unbinding turnover of the discrete cross-linkers. The cell's cortex (zero-rest-length springs) contracts radially+circumferentially, focal-adhesion-like permanent links transmit cortex force to the fibers, and the team measures radial stress σ(r,t), fiber displacement, net local deformation, and deformation non-uniformity as functions of four control knobs: cortical contraction stiffness k_s,c, fiber concentration C_f, cross-linker density R_xl, and the two Bell's-law parameters k_ub,0 and x_ub. **Key results:** (i) stronger contraction → larger peak stress (∝ k_s,c), longer-range transmission, and — counter-intuitively — *faster* stress relaxation (more cross-linker unbinding under high load); (ii) in the 2-D cylindrical geometry a fully-transmitted steady stress decays as **r⁻¹** (vs r⁻² in 3-D), used as a diagnostic that stress reached the boundary; (iii) higher matrix connectivity (higher C_f or higher R_xl) → higher, more sustained stress, more uniform deformation; (iv) faster cross-linker unbinding (higher k_ub,0 or x_ub) → faster relaxation, shorter-range transmission, smaller/less-uniform deformation; even with zero unbinding, >50 % relaxation still occurs (a poroelastic-like elastic-vs-viscous split); (v) matrix deformation is mostly **irreversible/plastic** (fibers barely retract when the cell is removed). In-vitro experiments with 3T3 fibroblasts in 3-D collagen-I gels confirm the trends: increasing collagen concentration, adding tTG covalent cross-links, and inhibiting myosin (ML-7 / blebbistatin) all reduce remodeling, and low-density gels remodel non-uniformly — matching the simulations. The work reframes **tensional homeostasis** as achievable even in viscoelastic/viscoplastic matrices.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** Discrete, **agent-based Langevin (overdamped Brownian) dynamics**; deterministic + drag + stochastic (thermal) forces, **inertia neglected**. Effectively 2.5-D: membrane/cortex nodes carry only (x,y); fiber and cross-linker nodes carry (x,y,z).
- **Integrator / timestep:** **Euler (forward) integration** of the overdamped Langevin equation. Timestep not stated in the main text (in ESI Table S1).
- **Domain / geometry / BCs:** Cylindrical solid domain, **radius 100 µm, height/thickness 1 µm**. Cell at center, **initial cell radius 10 µm**, matrix outer radius 100 µm. Thin quasi-2-D slab (1 µm thick).
- **Constituents:**
  - **Cell** = membrane + actomyosin cortex, each coarse-grained as serially connected rectangular solid elements (1 µm height), formed as a circle.
  - **Matrix fibers** = serially connected cylindrical elements, average fiber length ≈ 10 µm; assembled by nucleation (appearance of one element) + polymerization (element addition at both ends) during the first 200 s.
  - **Cross-linkers** = two cylindrical arms, connect pairs of fibers **transiently**; bind at sites spaced every **100 nm** along fibers with **no contact-angle preference**.
  - **Focal-adhesion-like links** = **permanent** springs between fiber ends and cortex nodes (either fiber end may link).
- **Force ingredients:** bending + extensional (stretch) forces on fibers, cross-linkers, membrane, cortex; membrane–cortex mutual attraction (proximity maintenance); repulsion between membrane elements and matrix (fibers + cross-linkers) to keep matrix out of the cell interior; cortex zero-rest-length contractile springs; FA harmonic springs.
- **Protocol:** (1) build membrane+cortex circle; (2) 0–200 s assemble fibers + form transient cross-links + FA links; (3) at t = 200 s begin cell contraction (radial + circumferential); run ~1 h simulated time; measure σ(r,t), displacement, deformation.
- **Measurements:**
  - **Stress(r):** identify all fiber+crosslinker chains crossing the lateral surface of a cylinder of radius r (r = 10,20,…,90 µm); sum the **radial component** of spring forces; divide by lateral surface area (∝ r).
  - **Fiber displacement:** initial→final position vector projected onto the radial direction.
  - **Net local deformation:** difference in mean fiber displacement between adjacent 10-µm annuli.
  - **Non-uniformity:** SD of fiber displacement within an annulus, normalized by the mean.
  - **Tensed vs bent fibers:** ratio of final/initial end-to-end distance (>1 = tensed/straightened, <1 = buckled); orientation = acute angle between projected element and the cell-center→element-midpoint line (0° = radial, 90° = circumferential).
  - **Irreversibility:** reload final tensed matrix WITHOUT a cell; measure outward retraction vs the original inward contraction displacement.
- **Compute:** XSEDE / Comet supercomputer (SDSC).
- **In-vitro method:** 3T3 mouse fibroblasts (LifeAct/RFP-Lifeact actin label) encapsulated in 3-D collagen type-I gels at 1–5 mg mL⁻¹; covalent cross-linking via **tissue transglutaminase (tTG, guinea-pig liver, 500 µg mL⁻¹**); myosin inhibition via **ML-7 (25 µM)** or **blebbistatin (10 µM)**; imaging by **confocal reflectance microscopy**; ImageJ quantification (normalized reflectance intensity vs distance from cell edge); stats in GraphPad Prism (one-way ANOVA / unpaired t-test).

## Governing Equations

**(Eq. 0 — implied) Overdamped Langevin update (Euler):**
For each element, drag·velocity = deterministic force + stochastic (thermal) force, inertia neglected:
ζ (dx/dt) = F_det + F_stochastic  →  x(t+Δt) = x(t) + (Δt/ζ)(F_det + F_thermal).
*Meaning:* Brownian-dynamics equation of motion; velocity set balance-of-forces each step (specific ζ and noise variance are in ESI Table S1).

**(Eq. 1) Focal-adhesion / fiber–cortex link harmonic potential:**
U_s,fc = ½ · k_s,fc · (r_fc − r_0,fc)²
*Meaning:* permanent FA-like spring keeping fiber–cortex link length r_fc near equilibrium r_0,fc; k_s,fc is extensional stiffness. r_0,fc chosen longer than membrane thickness so the link is exempt from membrane–matrix repulsion.

**(Eq. 2) Force-dependent cross-linker unbinding (Bell's law, slip-bond):**
k_ub = k_ub,0 · exp( x_ub · |F⃗_s,xl| / (k_B T) )   if r ≥ r_0,xl
k_ub = k_ub,0                                        if r < r_0,xl
*Meaning:* cross-linker off-rate rises exponentially with the tensile spring force |F⃗_s,xl| it bears (slip bond); under compression (r < r_0,xl) it unbinds at the zero-force baseline rate. k_ub,0 = zero-force off-rate, x_ub = force sensitivity length, k_B T = thermal energy, r_0,xl = crosslinker equilibrium length. This is the **mechanistic origin of matrix viscoelasticity** in the model.

**(Implied) Cortex contractile spring:** cortex elements carry a spring force with **zero equilibrium length**, U_cortex = ½ k_s,c · r², so the cortex is always contractile (tension along the cell surface); k_s,c sets contraction strength.

**(Implied) Stress measurement:** σ_radial(r) = [ Σ (radial component of chain spring forces crossing cylinder r) ] / (lateral area ∝ r). Steady-state fully-transmitted stress ⇒ σ ∝ **r⁻¹** in the 2-D cylindrical geometry (vs r⁻² in 3-D), since lateral area ∝ r.

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Extensional (stretch) potential** on fibers/cross-linkers/membrane/cortex: harmonic, U = ½ k_s (r − r_0)² (form as Eq. 1; cortex has r_0 = 0).
- **Bending potential** on fibers/cross-linkers/membrane/cortex: harmonic in bending angle (standard Kim-lab worm-like form, U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ_0)²; constants in ESI). Fiber buckling under compression emerges from this.
- **FA link:** harmonic, Eq. 1, permanent, stiffness k_s,fc.
- **Cross-linker binding:** stochastic binding to 100-nm-spaced sites, no angular preference; unbinding via Bell slip-bond Eq. 2.
- **Cortex contraction:** zero-rest-length harmonic spring, stiffness k_s,c (the primary "activity" knob).
- **Membrane–cortex attraction:** short-range attraction keeping adjacent membrane and cortex elements proximal.
- **Membrane–matrix repulsion:** repulsive (excluded-volume) force preventing fibers/cross-linkers from entering the cell interior.
- **Thermal noise:** Gaussian stochastic force (fluctuation–dissipation with drag ζ).
