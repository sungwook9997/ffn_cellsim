## Summary
The paper asks how the **persistence length (Lp)** — i.e., bending rigidity / orientational flexibility — of self-propelled filaments governs collective motion, phase transition, and pattern formation. Using an in-vitro **microtubule–kinesin-1 gliding motility assay** in the presence of the depletant methylcellulose (MC), the authors engineer three MT groups with distinct Lp (softer GTP-MTs 0.65 mm, stiffer GTP-MTs 1.93 mm, GMPCPP-MTs 3.54 mm) by tuning nucleotide (GTP vs GMPCPP) and growth rate (tubulin 30 vs 100 µM); faster growth → more lattice defects → softer MTs. At fixed high surface density (ρ_MT ≈ 5.5 filaments/µm²) and identical average length (9.6 µm) and gliding velocity (601 nm/s), the three groups form **distinct patterns**: softer → transient local streams (nematic order S ≈ 0.54 at 50 min), stiffer → large-scale dense bundles (S ≈ 0.62), GMPCPP → highly nematic arrays (S ≈ 0.80, reaching nematic state by 10 min). The central mechanistic finding, established via **binary-collision assays** at low density (0.022 filaments/µm², 4 MC concentrations 0–0.4 wt%): the *probability* of alignment (polar ~50%, antipolar ~50%, crossover ~70%) and the collision-induced orientation change (dispersion η_d = 16.3±5.4°) are **independent of Lp and MC** — they depend only on the incoming angle θ_in. What differs is the **durability (coupling/alignment-duration time) of aligned bundles**, which increases with larger Lp and higher MC (depletion force), because tip thermal fluctuation ∝ 1/Lp. Thus longer Lp → more durable alignment → seed bundles survive long enough to recruit filaments (stiffer) or elongate nematically (GMPCPP), whereas soft seeds disintegrate. An **agent-based Brownian-dynamics simulation** (Lp = 0.63 vs 1.93 mm) reproduces this: bundle formation times are equal (p>0.05) but disintegration times differ (p<0.05) — stiffer MTs form larger (N_B > 130), longer-lived (>200 s) bundles vs softer (N_B < 45, <20 s). Conclusion: **Lp-dependent durability of alignment (not alignment probability) determines the phase transition and pattern formation**, linking "orientational noise" (∝1/Lp) to "alignment durability," a link absent from existing Vicsek-type models.

## Model & Methods
Two complementary approaches.

**(A) Experimental — in-vitro MT–kinesin gliding motility assay.**
- Tubulin purified from porcine brain, TAMRA-labeled; kinesin-1 (His6-tagged, residues 1–465aa) from E. coli.
- Three MT groups polymerized under conditions in Table S1: GTP or GMPCPP nucleotide × tubulin 30 or 100 µM (growth-rate control). All polymerized 37 °C, 30 min, stabilized with 20 µM paclitaxel. Lp measured via clamped-free-cantilever thermal-fluctuation mode analysis (Eqs 1–3, method from ref 35).
- Assay chamber: piranha-cleaned glass, air-plasma 5×5 mm central window; streptavidin (2 mg/mL) nonspecific coat → biotin-kinesin-1 (0.154 mg/mL, 5 min incubation) → casein block (0.3 mg/mL) → 25 µM MTs in motility buffer.
- Buffers: BRB80 (80 mM PIPES, 1 mM EGTA, 1 mM MgCl2, pH 6.8); motility buffer = BRB80 + 5 mM ATP + 0–0.4 wt% MC + 20 µM paclitaxel + O2 scavenger (36 mg/mL catalase, 216 mg/mL glucose oxidase, 25 mM D-glucose). All assays at 25 °C.
- Imaging: Olympus IX73 epifluorescence, 60× oil objective, 50 ms exposure, 1 frame/s, 300 s clips, ~60 min total (until ATP depletes).
- Collective-motion assay: ρ_MT ≈ 5.5 filaments/µm². Binary-collision assay: ρ_MT ≈ 0.022 filaments/µm², 4 MC concentrations.
- Analysis: nematic order S (ImageJ Directionality plugin, Eq 4), skewness Sk (Eq 5–6), PIV velocity fields (OpenPIV, 6 s lag, 32×32-px window), tip tracking via Mark2 for θ_in/θ_out, ImageJ AnalyzeSkeleton for bundle length, band-pass denoise + binarize for WB/DB. Density via 1:500 labeled:unlabeled dilution. Stats: Steel–Dwass, two-way ANOVA, Mann–Whitney–Wilcoxon.

**(B) Computational — agent-based Brownian-dynamics model.**
- Built on the **Langevin equation** ("Brownian dynamics"). Details in SI Text "Brownian Dynamics" + Table S7 (not in main text).
- MT filaments = **serially connected cylindrical segments** with polarity (plus/minus ends).
- Per-timestep displacement from Langevin eq. **Deterministic forces:** (i) extensional (stretching), (ii) bending, (iii) repulsive excluded-volume force between overlapping MT filaments, (iv) a **propelling force oriented toward the pointed (minus) end** mimicking surface kinesin (SI Fig S6a).
- Domain: **2D, 100 × 100 µm**. MTs nucleated by placing one segment at random position/orientation, then elongated by segment addition (polymerization). Nucleation rate k_n,MT varied at constant polymerization rate k_+,MT to set the target average MT length to match experiment.
- Two Lp values simulated: 0.63 and 1.93 mm (matching softer and stiffer GTP-MTs).
- Bundle detection: cluster of N_B ≥ 20 filaments = a bundle; formation time = time for N_B/2 → N_B; disintegration time = time for N_B → N_B/2 (SI Fig S6b). Snapshots at 1000, 1500, 2000 s.

## Governing Equations
Transcribed from the Methods (Data Analysis + Lp measurement). SI-only BD equations are noted as absent.

**(Eq 1) Cantilever mode decomposition of a fluctuating MT tip:**
`y(s) = Σ_{n=1}^∞ sqrt(1/L) · a_n · W_n(s/L)`
Meaning: the transverse shape y(s) of a MT clamped at one end (gold-stripe-immobilized) and free at the other is expanded in clamped-free beam normal modes with amplitudes a_n; L = MT length, s = arc length from clamped end.

**(Eq 2) Clamped-free (cantilever) beam mode shape:**
`W_n(s/L) = [ (cosh q_n − cos q_n) / (sin q_n + sinh q_n) ] · [ sin(q_n s/L) − sinh(q_n s/L) ] + cos(q_n s/L) − cosh(q_n s/L)`
Meaning: the nth spatial eigenfunction of an Euler–Bernoulli cantilever; q_n are the eigenvalue roots.

**(Eq 3) Persistence length from mode-amplitude variance (equipartition):**
`L_p = (1 / var(a_n)) · (L / q_n)^4`
Meaning: equating thermal energy with bending energy, Lp is inferred from the variance of the nth mode amplitude a_n. q_n = 1.875 (n=1), 4.695 (n=2), 7.855 (n=3), (n−0.5)π for n≥4.

**(Eq 4) Nematic order parameter:**
`S = (1/N) · sqrt( [ Σ_{i=0}^{180} f_i cos 2θ_i ]^2 + [ Σ_{i=0}^{180} f_i sin 2θ_i ]^2 )`
Meaning: global nematic ordering of gliding MTs; N = total MT pixel count, θ_i = orientation-angle bin (0–180°), f_i = frequency of MTs at θ_i. S=0 isotropic, S→1 perfectly nematic.

**(Eq 5) Skewness (bundling metric):**
`Sk = (1/N) · Σ_{n=1}^{...} [ (I_n − Ī) / σ ]^3`
Meaning: third standardized moment of the MT-pixel intensity distribution; larger Sk = brighter belt-like bundles = higher bundling level. Sk=0 at homogeneous start.

**(Eq 6) Intensity standard deviation (used in Eq 5):**
`σ = sqrt( (1/N) · Σ_{n=1}^{...} (I_n − Ī)^2 )`
Meaning: σ = SD of MT-pixel intensities; I_n = pixel intensity of each MT, Ī = mean MT-pixel intensity.

**(SI-only, referenced but not printed in main text):** the explicit Langevin/Brownian-dynamics update equation, the extensional and bending potential forms, the excluded-volume repulsion, and the kinesin propelling-force functional form (SI Text "Brownian Dynamics," "Propelling Force," Table S7, Fig S6a) — NOT available in this file.

## Force Laws & Potentials
Only qualitatively described in the main text (functional forms are in the un-provided SI):
- **Extensional (stretching) force** along MT backbone segments — keeps segment lengths near rest length.
- **Bending force** — resists MT curvature; sets Lp (Lp = κ/k_BT with bending modulus κ). Simulation Lp inputs = 0.63 and 1.93 mm.
- **Excluded-volume repulsion** — repulsive force between overlapping MT filaments (steric).
- **Propelling (motor) force** — constant-magnitude force oriented toward the MT pointed/minus end, mimicking surface-anchored kinesin-1 that drives gliding at ~601 nm/s.
- **Depletion force (experimental, MC-induced)** — attractive bundling force between adjacent MTs; magnitude **0.11 pN at 0.3 wt% MC** (from ref 15/53); suppresses tip thermal fluctuation, increasing alignment durability. Compared to single-kinesin **stall force 5–7 pN** (refs 54,55) — i.e., depletion force is far weaker than motor force, so its contribution to promoting alignment (vs durability) is argued to be minor.
- **Thermal-fluctuation of MT leading tip** ∝ 1/Lp — the "orientational noise" source; suppressed by both larger Lp and larger MC depletion force.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** No (weak/indirect). This is 2D filament collective gliding on a surface, no cell shell / turgor / cortex-tension / junction. No direct DCM mechanism. Only tangential: MT bending rigidity concepts if a nucleus-anchoring MT compartment were modeled.
- **FF?** **Yes.** Directly relevant to the FF fine-grained filament engine (Cytosim-style beams + motors). The agent-based model is exactly FF's paradigm: filaments as serially connected segments with **extensional + bending + excluded-volume + motor propelling forces** under Langevin dynamics. Provides concrete MT parameters for an FF microtubule compartment: **Lp = 0.65–3.54 mm** (→ bending modulus κ = Lp·k_BT), **gliding velocity 601 nm/s**, **kinesin stall 5–7 pN**, **depletion force 0.11 pN @0.3 wt% MC**, MT length 9.6 µm, tip-fluctuation ∝ 1/Lp. Also a clean validation reference for an agent-based filament-collision/alignment implementation (alignment probability determined by incoming angle; alignment *durability* set by Lp).
- **TAG-validation?** **Yes.** Rich quantitative targets suitable for KB SourceEvidence / Parameter rows: MT persistence lengths, gliding velocity, single-kinesin stall force, MC depletion force, bundle geometry (L_B/W_B/D_B), nematic-order and skewness trajectories, collision-outcome probabilities, and simulation bundle-size / lifetime numbers. DOI verified in-text (10.1021/acsnano.2c05593). Note: this is a Kim-lab agent-based-BD + Yokokawa-lab in-vitro paper — good cross-check for FF motor/filament parameters, but the core biology (MT–kinesin gliding on a coverslip) is not the MCF7 single-cell mechanobiology target, so register as reference/parameter provenance, not a single-cell validation gate.
