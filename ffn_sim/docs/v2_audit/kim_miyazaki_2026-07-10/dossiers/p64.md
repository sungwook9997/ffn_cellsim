## Summary
The paper asks whether molecular-scale changes in the actin monomer — driven by which nucleotide (ATP, ADP, or the intermediate ADP-Pi) and which/how-many divalent cations (Mg²⁺ or Ca²⁺, at the single high-affinity site or saturating all six/low-+intermediate-affinity sites) are bound — propagate all the way up to alter the rheology (strain-stiffening) of a crosslinked actin network. It builds a **bottom-up three-scale pipeline**: (1) atomistic **equilibrium molecular dynamics** (GROMACS, 11 monomer configurations of a 13-monomer F-actin repeat unit) to obtain conformational changes; (2) a filament-scale **elastic network model (ENM) + rotation-translation-block (RTB) normal-mode analysis** on 150 nm filaments to extract bending (persistence length lₚ) and extensional (kₛ) rigidities from mode frequencies; (3) a network-scale **Brownian-dynamics agent-based crosslinked-actin model** (Kim et al. 2009a,b; Kim 2014) in a 3 µm periodic box, sheared at 0.1 s⁻¹, feeding the RTB-derived kₛ and k_f into cylindrical filament segments. Findings: cation binding at the low-/intermediate-/high-affinity sites compacts F-actin (diameter −~15%), changes inter-/intra-strand subdomain distances and dihedral-angle variability, and shifts lₚ modestly (from ~3.5 to ~4 µm, up to 10% change in ATP-F-actin, 6% in ADP-F-actin). These modest filament-rigidity changes have **essentially no effect on network strain-stiffening when compliant crosslinkers (filamin A, α-actinin; kₛ,ACP = 0.002 N/m) are used** (p = 0.88), but a **statistically significant effect with rigid crosslinkers (scruin-like; kₛ,ACP = 0.2 N/m)** (p = 0.03). When the much larger lₚ change from the discrete "stiffness" Mg²⁺ site (Kang et al. 2012: lₚ 2.1→12.7 µm) is fed in, curves differ even with soft ACPs (p < 0.01), and with stiff ACPs the highest-lₚ stress is ~3× the lowest.

## Model & Methods
Three coupled models, each consuming the smaller-scale output (bottom-up):

**(1) Atomistic MD (angstrom / ns scale).**
- Model type: all-atom equilibrium molecular dynamics.
- Software/force field: GROMACS 4 (Hess et al. 2008), GROMOS G53a6 force field (Oostenbrink et al. 2005); SPC water; VMD for inspection; MATLAB for structural analysis.
- System: 13-monomer F-actin repeat unit (microfilament model of Grudinin & Redon 2010 / Deriu et al. 2012), placed in a rectangular box 13 nm × 13 nm × 37.5 nm, long axis ∥ z. G-actin coords from 2zwh.pdb (Oda 2009); DB loop in ADP configs rebuilt from 1j6z.pdb (Otterbein 2001); 6 Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ positions from 1j6z.pdb; ATP coords from 1atn.pdb (Kabsch 1990).
- BCs: periodic in xyz ("infinite" filament); ≥2 nm between filament and periodic images in x,y.
- Protocol: 5000 steps steepest-descent minimization → ~50 ps position-restrained MD (NPT, backbone restrained k = 1000 kJ mol⁻¹ nm⁻²) → **NVT production 300 K, 12 ns**.
- Electrostatics: particle-mesh Ewald, 1 nm short-range cutoff; LJ cutoff 1 nm. Virtual-site + LINCS constraint solver → **timestep 4 fs**.
- 11 configurations: ATP-G-actin, ADP-G-actin, ADP-Pi-G-actin, ATP-1Mg²⁺, ATP-1Ca²⁺, ATP-6Mg²⁺, ATP-6Ca²⁺, ADP-1Mg²⁺, ADP-1Ca²⁺, ADP-6Mg²⁺, ADP-6Ca²⁺.
- Analysis observables: filament diameter/radius; inter-/intra-strand distances between subdomain (SD1–SD4) mass centers; nucleotide-cleft width (two definitions); dihedral angle. Reported as mean ± SD over the 13 monomers (SD = heterogeneity metric).

**(2) Filament ENM + RTB normal-mode analysis (150 nm / mode scale).**
- 13-monomer MD output configs (at 12 ns) tiled by rigid z-translations into 150 nm filaments.
- Atomic model → **Elastic Network Model**: nodes = Cα atoms; springs = harmonic potentials, **spring constant 1 kcal/mol·Å²**, connecting nodes closer than **cutoff 1.2 nm** (Atilgan 2001; Doruker 2000).
- **RTB (rotation-translation block)** method (Durand 1994; Tama 2000/2001/2005): rigid blocks = the 4 functional subdomains (SD1–SD4) per monomer, preserving subunit topology while reducing DOF (filament has ~20,000 Cα, slow low-frequency modes ~10⁻¹ cm of interest).
- Filament treated as homogeneous isotropic rod (shear negligible in deflection). Bending & extensional rigidities computed from specific mode frequencies (equations in Supplementary Info — not in this PDF). n = 10 simulations per condition (error bars = standard error).

**(3) Network-scale Brownian-dynamics agent-based crosslinked-actin model (µm / s scale).**
- The Kim-lab engine (details in Kim et al. 2009a; Kim 2014; Supplementary Info).
- F-actin = series of cylindrical segments, **140 nm long, 7 nm diameter**, connected by elastic hinges; extensional stiffness kₛ and bending stiffness k_f (from RTB) maintain segment length and parallel alignment.
- ACPs (actin crosslinking proteins) = two cylindrical segments, **23.5 nm long, 10 nm diameter**, serially connected by elastic hinges; permanent crosslinks between F-actin pairs, no preferred crosslinking angle, binding to sites every 7 nm on the actin segment.
- Dynamics: **Langevin equation** with stochastic (thermal), drag, and deterministic forces (bending + extensional + repulsive volume-exclusion between actin segments).
- Domain: cubic box, **width 3 µm**, periodic in all directions during assembly. Self-assembly yields network: average filament length **1.2 µm**, actin concentration **20 µM**, relative ACP density **R_ACP = 0.01**.
- Shear test: F-actins crossing the z-boundaries are severed and permanently clamped, PBC deactivated; top z-boundary translated at constant shear rate **0.1 s⁻¹**, bottom fixed. Stress = (sum of forces on clamped filaments)/(z-boundary area).
- Two ACP extensional stiffnesses compared: **kₛ,ACP = 0.002 N/m (compliant; mimics filamin A / α-actinin)** and **0.2 N/m (rigid; mimics scruin)**.

## Governing Equations
The paper is largely descriptive; explicit closed forms live in the (absent) Supplementary Info. Transcribed/reconstructed governing relations:

1. **ENM harmonic potential (pairwise, filament level).**
   V_ij = ½ k (|r_ij| − |r_ij⁰|)² for node pairs with |r_ij⁰| < R_c, with **k = 1 kcal/mol·Å²** and cutoff **R_c = 1.2 nm**. — Meaning: elastic spring network approximating filament elasticity for normal-mode analysis.

2. **RTB / normal-mode eigenproblem (implied).**
   Diagonalize the projected (block) Hessian, K φ = ω² M φ; bending & extensional rigidities of the equivalent isotropic rod are read from selected low-frequency mode frequencies ω (explicit mapping in Supplementary Info). — Meaning: mode frequencies → EI (bending) and EA (extensional) rigidities.

3. **Persistence length ↔ bending rigidity (elastic-rod / ideal-chain theory, used to back-out kₛ,k_f from measured lₚ).**
   lₚ = κ / (k_B T), κ = EI (flexural rigidity). — Meaning: converts the Kang et al. 2012 measured lₚ (2.1–12.7 µm) into network-model bending/extensional inputs.

4. **Network Langevin equation of motion (agent-based, per segment).**
   0 = F_stochastic + F_drag + F_det, with F_det = F_bending + F_extensional + F_repulsion(excluded volume). Overdamped Brownian dynamics; F_drag = −ζ v (Stokes drag on cylindrical segment). — Meaning: governs displacement of actin/ACP segments.

5. **Extensional (stretching) potential, actin & ACP segments.**
   V_s = ½ kₛ (ℓ − ℓ₀)². — Meaning: maintains equilibrium segment length; kₛ,actin from RTB, kₛ,ACP = 0.002 or 0.2 N/m.

6. **Bending potential, actin & ACP hinges.**
   V_b = ½ k_f (θ − θ₀)². — Meaning: keeps adjacent actin segments parallel (θ₀ = 180°) and ACP arm–filament angle near right angle; k_f,actin from RTB, k_f,ACP1 = 1.45×10⁻²⁵ N·m², k_f,ACP2 = 5.8×10⁻²⁵ N·m².

7. **Macroscopic shear stress.**
   σ = (Σ F_clamped) / A_z, with imposed strain rate dγ/dt = 0.1 s⁻¹. — Meaning: network stress from forces on clamped boundary filaments; strain-stiffening = σ(γ) diverging above critical strain ~0.5.

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Stretching (harmonic):** V_s = ½ kₛ(ℓ−ℓ₀)². Actin segment ℓ₀ = 140 nm. kₛ,ACP ∈ {0.002 (compliant), 0.2 (rigid)} N/m.
- **Bending (harmonic angular):** V_b = ½ k_f(θ−θ₀)². k_f,ACP1 = 1.45×10⁻²⁵ N·m² (parallel alignment of ACP arms), k_f,ACP2 = 5.8×10⁻²⁵ N·m² (ACP-arm/F-actin ≈ right angle). Actin k_f from RTB.
- **Excluded-volume / repulsion:** repulsive force between actin cylindrical segments (volume-exclusion; functional form in Kim et al. 2009a — not transcribed here).
- **Thermal / drag (Langevin):** Gaussian stochastic force + Stokes drag on segments (overdamped BD).
- **ENM spring:** harmonic, k = 1 kcal/mol·Å², cutoff 1.2 nm (filament-level normal-mode model only).

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** No (weak). This is molecular/filament/network-rheology, not surface-mesh continuum cell mechanics; no membrane/cortex-tension/turgor content. Only tangential: strain-stiffening σ(γ) of crosslinked actin could inform a cortex constitutive law, but not directly usable in DCM.
- **FF?** **Yes (strong).** This is a Taeyoon Kim-lab Brownian-dynamics agent-based crosslinked-actin engine — the direct conceptual ancestor of FF's fine-grained Cytosim-style model: cylindrical actin segments (140 nm × 7 nm) with harmonic stretch/bend + excluded volume, ACPs as two-arm crosslinkers (kₛ,ACP, k_f) binding every 7 nm, Langevin overdamped dynamics, periodic 3 µm box, shear at 0.1 s⁻¹. Provides concrete FF parameters (actin segment kₛ/k_f from lₚ≈3.5–17 µm, ACP compliant 0.002 vs rigid 0.2 N/m, k_f,ACP 1.45/5.8×10⁻²⁵ N·m², R_ACP=0.01, 20 µM, 1.2 µm mean length) and a validation target: nonlinear strain-stiffening with critical strain ~0.5 and crosslinker-stiffness-dependent divergence.
- **TAG-validation?** **Yes.** Strong quantitative validation targets for FF crosslinked-network rheology: lₚ 3.5–4 µm (baseline) up to 12.7 µm (stiffness site); extensional kₛ ~0.055–0.075 N/m; ACP stiffnesses; network stress ~200–2500 Pa at 60% strain; ~3× stress ratio across lₚ; p-value regime (soft ACP insensitive, rigid ACP/scruin sensitive). Candidate KnowledgeClaim/Parameter rows for the crosslinker + filament-rigidity contract. Note the several magic-number-flavored ACP/segment constants trace to Kim et al. 2009a — cite that, not this paper, as the primary source for those.
