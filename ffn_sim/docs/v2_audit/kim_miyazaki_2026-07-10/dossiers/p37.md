## Summary
This paper asks how the stiffness of an adhesive substrate regulates the speed at which a neuronal growth cone establishes strong substrate-cytoskeletal coupling and advances. The authors adapt the classic 1-D Chan–Odde / Bangasser motor-clutch model to large *Aplysia californica* bag-cell growth cones, adding three new mechanistic features: (1) stiffness-dependent **reinforcement** at both the clutch level (adding clutches at rate k_add when a bound clutch bears force ≥ threshold F_t = 10 pN, Mekhdjian reinforcement law) and the motor level (number of NMII motors n_m increases with substrate stiffness); (2) a **retrograde F-actin flow threshold of 20 nm/s** (an 80% reduction from the 100 nm/s unloaded rate) that defines the moment of strong coupling; and (3) two new output observables — the **latency time t_l** (time for actin flow to decay to 20 nm/s) and the **substrate deformation Δx_sub** at that time. The model is simulated with the Gillespie stochastic simulation algorithm (direct method) in Python 3.8, and its motor number n_m and reinforcement constant k_add0 are optimized against previously published force-calibrated microneedle (ConA-coated) experiments on *Aplysia* growth cones (Athamneh et al. 2015) at substrate stiffnesses from 2.5 to 106 pN/nm. The added reinforcement converts the previous model's unstable oscillating steady state into a stable steady state (constant substrate deformation), matching the adaptive experimental behavior. Key result: the latency time is **biphasic** in substrate stiffness with a minimum (~2 min) at **4 pN/nm** (vs 14 min at 2.5 pN/nm, stabilizing ~8–9 min at high stiffness), the substrate deformation is highest at the softest substrate (1.6 µm at 2.5 pN/nm) and stabilizes ~0.8 µm above 14 pN/nm, and the system is least sensitive to parameter changes at 4 pN/nm — implying 4 pN/nm is an optimum stiffness. Latency time depends on both clutch- and motor-related parameters, whereas substrate deformation depends only on motor-related parameters. Conclusion: *Aplysia* growth cones respond faster and more efficiently to soft than to stiff substrates.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** 1-D stochastic agent-based motor-clutch model (single filopodium coupled to an elastic substrate). Deterministic mechanical force balance (springs) + stochastic reaction kinetics for clutch binding/unbinding/reinforcement. Descendant of Chan & Odde (2008) and Bangasser et al. (2013), with a reinforcement term from Mekhdjian et al. (2017).
- **Stochastic engine:** Gillespie (1977) direct-method Stochastic Simulation Algorithm. Three reaction channels (unimolecular, h_j = 1): clutch binding (rate k_on), clutch unbinding (rate k_off, force-dependent), clutch reinforcement / addition (rate k_add). Time step Δt drawn from exponential of total propensity; reaction selected by second uniform random number.
- **Mechanical geometry:** actin bundle (filopodium) moves rearward (retrograde flow) at velocity v_f pulled by n_m myosin motors (stall force F_s each). Bound clutches are springs (K_clutch) linking the moving actin to an elastic substrate spring (K_sub). Force is shared equally among all bound clutches. Free-body diagram in Supplemental Figure 1 (not in file).
- **Initial condition:** unloaded actin flow v_u = 100 nm/s, no clutches bound.
- **Termination / measurement:** simulation runs until retrograde actin flow attenuates to 20 nm/s (= 80% reduction), defining latency time t_l; substrate deformation Δx_sub recorded at t_l.
- **Language / implementation:** Python 3.8. Sensitivity analysis via common-random-numbers finite-difference scheme (Rathinam et al. 2010): same two random-number streams for baseline and perturbed parameter, sensitivity = (perturbed − unperturbed)/h. Custom Python 3.8 script for means/SD. Code available upon request.
- **Ensembles:** n = 40 simulations per substrate stiffness for the experiment comparison (Fig 7); n = 20 trajectories per condition in Fig 6; k_add0 optimization used 40 simulations scanning k_add0 ∈ [0.1, 1].
- **Experimental method (validation data, mostly re-used from Athamneh 2015):** *Aplysia* bag-cell neurons cultured on PLL-coated glass in L15 + artificial sea water, 14 °C, used 1 d after plating. Stiffness-calibrated glass microneedles (pulled 5 µl capillaries; stiffness via Polytec MSA-400 scanning laser Doppler vibrometer, thermal-vibration first flexural resonance fit to single harmonic oscillator, per Lozano 2010) coated with 100 µg/ml Concanavalin A (ConA, binds apCAM). Needle placed on growth-cone P domain; time-lapse DIC imaging (Nikon TE2000, 60×/1.4 NA + 1.5×, Cascade II CCD, MetaMorph), frames every 10 s. Needle displacement tracked with ImageJ TrackMate (Difference-of-Gaussians). Actin retrograde flow + boundary velocities from MetaMorph kymographs (4×4 low-pass → Laplace → 3×3 low-pass filtering). Traction force from needle deformation × stiffness.

## Governing Equations
All equations from the Materials and Methods (pages 10–11), transcribed from the rendered pages.

**(Eq 1) — Gillespie time step:**
Δt = −ln(r₁) / ( Σⱼ₌₁³ hⱼ cⱼ ) = −ln(r₁) / ( Σⱼ₌₁³ cⱼ )
where r₁ ~ U[0,1], cⱼ ∈ {k_on, k_off, k_add}, hⱼ = 1 (all unimolecular). *Meaning:* stochastic waiting time to the next reaction event.

**(Eq 2) — time advance:**
t_k = t_{k−1} + Δt
*Meaning:* clock update after event k (k = 0 … total events).

**(Eq 3) — reaction selection:**
cⱼ / ( Σⱼ₌₁³ cⱼ ) ≤ r₂ , r₂ ~ U[0,1]
*Meaning:* selects which of the three reactions (binding / unbinding / reinforcement) fires this step.

**(Eq 4) — clutch force (Hookean spring):**
F_clutch(i)(t_k) = K_clutch · ( x_i(t_{k−1}) − x_sub(t_{k−1}) )
*Meaning:* force on clutch i = clutch stiffness × (clutch/actin position − substrate position) from previous step.

**(Eq 5) — force-dependent clutch off-rate (Bell 1978 model):**
k_off(i)(t_k) = k_off0 · exp( F_clutch(i)(t_k) / F_b )
*Meaning:* bound clutch unbinding accelerates exponentially with load; F_b = bond rupture force scale (slip bond).

**(Eq 6) — adhesion reinforcement rate (Mekhdjian et al. 2017):**
k_add(t_k) = k_add0 · ( Σ_{i=1}^{n_eng} a_(i)(t_k) ) · f_available(t_k)
with the indicator
a_(i)(t_k) = 1 if F_clutch(i)(t_k) ≥ F_t ; 0 if F_clutch(i)(t_k) < F_t
and the available-fraction
f_available(t_k) = ( n_c,max − n_c(t_k) ) / n_c,max
*Meaning:* new clutches are added at a rate scaled by k_add0, the count of engaged clutches carrying force ≥ threshold F_t, and the fraction of the clutch pool still available. n_eng = engaged clutches; n_c,max = max clutch pool; n_c = current available clutches.

**(Eq 7) — retrograde actin flow (linear motor force-velocity):**
v_f(t_k) = v_u · ( 1 − K_sub · x_sub(t_{k−1}) / ( n_m · F_s ) )
*Meaning:* actin flow decreases linearly from unloaded v_u as the elastic load (K_sub·x_sub) approaches the total myosin stall force n_m·F_s (Hill-like linear approximation).

**(Eq 8) — clutch/actin position update:**
x_i(t_k) = x_i(t_{k−1}) + v_f(t_k) · Δt
*Meaning:* each bound clutch's actin attachment point advances (retrograde) by flow × time step.

**(Eq 9) — substrate position (force balance of clutch springs vs substrate spring):**
x_sub(t_k) = K_clutch · ( Σ_{i=1}^{n_eng} x_i(t_k) ) / ( K_sub + n_eng · K_clutch )
*Meaning:* substrate displacement from equilibrium of the substrate spring against the n_eng engaged clutch springs; substrate deformation Δx_sub = x_sub.

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Clutch spring (linear elastic):** F_clutch = K_clutch·(x_actin − x_sub), Eq 4. K_clutch optimum ≈ 0.3 pN/nm.
- **Substrate spring (linear elastic):** substrate reaction K_sub·x_sub; K_sub is the swept control variable (2.5–106 pN/nm).
- **Bell slip-bond off-rate:** k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b), Eq 5. Exponential force-weakening (no catch-bond term; authors explicitly note no evidence for catch bonds in apCAM–ConA–F-actin here).
- **Linear force-velocity motor law:** v_f = v_u·(1 − F_load/F_stall,total), with F_stall,total = n_m·F_s, Eq 7. Single-motor stall force F_s = 6 pN.
- **Reinforcement law (Mekhdjian):** k_add ∝ (engaged clutches above F_t) × (available fraction), Eq 6. Threshold-gated positive feedback; force threshold F_t = 10 pN.
- **Equal force sharing:** total load n_m·F_s is distributed equally over all bound clutches (stated in Discussion — adding clutches lowers per-clutch load, lengthening bond lifetime).
