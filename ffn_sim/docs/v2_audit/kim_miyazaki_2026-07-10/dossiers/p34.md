## Summary
This is a broad, narrative REVIEW of computational modeling in multiscale mechanobiology, organized by biological length scale from single molecules to multicellular tissues. The central thesis is that mechanical signals — force, stiffness, deformation — are generated, propagated, and transduced at every scale in an integrated "mechanobiological circuit," and that computational models coupled to empirical data are the tool needed to connect these scales. The authors survey four modeling paradigms (Table 1: molecular dynamics; coarse-grained agent-based; continuum-based; rule-based) and walk through the biology scale by scale: (1) atomistic — steered-MD unfolding of force-sensing proteins (talin, filamin A, fibronectin FnIII), cryptic-binding-site exposure, catch/slip bonds; (2) actin cytoskeleton — reconstituted and simulated crosslinked actin-network rheology, strain-stiffening via bending→stretching transition, motor-driven active stiffening, percolation/connectivity phase transitions, transient-crosslink-governed softening/creep/stress-relaxation; (3) cell–environment interface — integrin/cadherin clustering driven by mechanics + binding kinetics + glycocalyx (Paszek, Jamali agent-based models); (4) ECM mechanics — discrete crosslinked-fiber models of fibrous ECM, tension-driven fiber alignment enabling long-range force transmission (Wang, Ma, Abhilash); (5) integrated single-cell models with explicit stress fibers, focal adhesions, protrusions, and nucleus (Borau voxel-FE; Kim et al. force-balance migration model); (6) multicellular emergent behavior — Cellular Potts Model, self-propelled particles, DCIS cancer-progression models. It closes by flagging two under-explored frontiers: stochastic/non-thermal mechanical signaling (~10 s processivity motor-driven fluctuations) and nucleus/chromatin mechanotransduction. Because it is a review, it contains NO original governing equations, parameter tables, or quantitative simulation results of its own — the quantitative content is a handful of literature facts cited from primary papers, plus schematic figures.

## Model & Methods
This paper does not implement a model; it classifies and reviews modeling methods. Table 1 is the methodological backbone:

| Model type | Scale | Representative refs | Limitations | Advantages |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Molecular dynamics (incl. steered MD, atomistic Monte Carlo) | Atoms and molecules | 1–3 | Small spatiotemporal domain; no long-term biological behavior; computationally costly; equilibrium info approximated from nonequilibrium (steered) runs | High resolution, physics-based down to atomistic level |
| Coarse-grained agent-based (e.g. Brownian dynamics of actin/ACP networks) | Large molecular complexes, cytoskeletal & ECM networks, single & collective cells | 4–7 | May not reproduce all biologically relevant phenomena at small or large scales | Can simulate many interacting components at the scale of interest |
| Continuum-based | Single cells and small tissues | 8–10 | Limited resolution of discrete biological components | Large physical domain; underlying physical principles directly experimentally testable |
| Rule-based (e.g. Cellular Potts, self-propelled particles) | Collective cells and large tissues | 11–13 | Mostly phenomenological; assumptions hard to reconcile with physical principles | Simple rules → complex biologically-mimicking patterns; emergent behaviors not attainable by other methods |

Specific methods surveyed (all cited from primary literature, not run here):
- **Steered molecular dynamics (SMD)** — e.g. NAMD (ref 1) applied to talin H1–H12 rod (ref 2), filamin rod domain (ref 3), fibronectin FnIII (ref 84).
- **Brownian dynamics of crosslinked actin networks** — Kim/Hwang/Lee/Kamm (ref 4, 47); actin filaments as teal chains, ACPs as discrete crosslinks; prestrain applied, oscillatory strain, stress-supporting subnetwork emerges.
- **Multiscale MD→Brownian coupling** — Bidone et al. (ref 30): cation/nucleotide binding tunes actin bending rigidity → network strain-stiffening.
- **Agent-based integrin clustering** — Paszek (ref 72): springs (integrin, ligand, glycocalyx) + stochastic force-dependent binding kinetics; Jamali (ref 74): + ligand clustering/mobility, integrin homo-oligomerization, membrane crowdedness.
- **Discrete fibrous-ECM networks** — Wang (ref 79), Ma (ref 81), Abhilash (ref 80): crosslinked-fiber networks; two fiber families (aligned along principal stretch + isotropic); hyperelastic isotropic + nonlinear anisotropic aligned component.
- **Integrated single-cell models** — Borau voxel-FE (ref 108, contractile force per voxel ∝ substrate stiffness, stochastic voxel-move probability); Kim et al. (ref 110/114) force-balance migration model with lamellipodial protrusions, contractile stress fibers, kinetic focal adhesions (ligand-conc-dependent binding, force-dependent unbinding), nucleus.
- **Multicellular** — Cellular Potts Model (CPM, effective-energy/Hamiltonian minimization on a lattice; refs 11,12,120,122); self-propelled particles / stochastic interacting particles (refs 5,6,121); hybrid CPM+FE endothelial durotaxis (ref 13); DCIS breast-cancer CPM (Boghaert, ref 12).

## Governing Equations
This review presents essentially no original equations in the running text. The only explicit force expressions appear as annotations in the Fig. 1c schematic of the Kim et al. single-cell migration model (adapted from ref 110), reproduced here faithfully:

**(1)** Viscous drag on integrin node *i*:
  F_D,i^c = −C_c · v_i^c
  — drag force on the *i*-th integrin node equals a drag coefficient C_c times the node velocity v_i^c (Stokes-type linear drag, opposing motion).

**(2)** Nodal force balance at the *i*-th integrin node (implied by the labeled arrows in Fig. 1c; "3D force balance calculations are performed at each integrin node"):
  F_SF,i^c + F_E,i^c + F_FA,i^c + F_L,i^c + F_D,i^c = 0
  — sum of stress-fiber force (F_SF), elastic/cytoskeletal force (F_E), focal-adhesion force (F_FA), ligand/adhesion force (F_L), and drag (F_D) on each node is zero (quasi-static force balance governing node motion). Superscript *c* denotes the cell frame; exact functional forms of F_SF, F_E, F_FA, F_L are defined in the primary ref 110, not in this review.

No bending/stretching potentials, Bell/catch-bond rate laws, Hill force-velocity relations, or continuum stress-balance PDEs are written out in this paper — they are only referenced qualitatively (e.g. "slip bonds, catch bonds," "bending→extension transition," "force-dependent binding/unbinding probabilities").

## Force Laws & Potentials
No explicit functional forms with constants are given in this review. Force-law CONCEPTS invoked (each traceable to a cited primary source, for downstream retrieval):
- **Linear viscous drag** on cell/adhesion nodes: F_D = −C_c v (Fig. 1c, Kim ref 110).
- **Slip vs. catch bonds** for adhesion/crosslink off-rates — force-dependent unbinding kinetics (refs 27 Kong, 28 Thomas/Vogel/Sokurenko).
- **Strain-stiffening of semiflexible actin networks** — bending-dominated → stretching-dominated regime transition (refs 4, 29 Storm nonlinear elasticity, 32 Onck, 43 Huisman).
- **Force-dependent binding kinetics** for integrin–ligand clustering (springs + stochastic on/off rates; refs 72, 74).
- **Effective Hamiltonian / energy minimization** for CPM (adhesion, membrane tension, volume-exclusion energy terms; refs 11, 12, 122).
- **Contractile-force–substrate-stiffness coupling** — cell stress scales with substrate rigidity up to a plateau (Borau voxel model ref 108, based on rigidity-sensing model ref 63; empirical refs 91, 109).
