## Summary
The paper asks how the **mobility of myosin motors** — a continuous property between the two extremes probed by experiments (fully immobilized motors in gliding/motility assays vs. freely diffusing motors in reconstituted gels) — shapes the contractile behavior of actomyosin networks. Using a well-established Kim-lab **agent-based Brownian-dynamics model** (Langevin dynamics of coarse-grained cylindrical segments for F-actin, ACP cross-linkers, and motors) in a very thin quasi-2D domain (10×10×0.1 µm, PBC in x,y), the authors tune motor mobility by varying the **motor drag coefficient ζ_M** over 5 decades and independently vary the **ATP-dependent unbinding rate k₂₀** of myosin heads (which sets stall force and affinity via the Parallel Cluster Model, PCM). The central finding is a **dichotomy by motor connectivity**: (i) **one-arm motors** (bind only ONE F-actin, like myosin I) produce network contraction/heterogeneity that is **biphasic** — maximal at an *intermediate* mobility — because low mobility lets a motor stably pull F-actin toward its fixed position (many small clusters) while high mobility lets the motor slide along/aggregate with F-actin (few large clusters) but exert lower force; (ii) **two-arm motors** (bind a PAIR of F-actins, like myosin II/V/VI and thick filaments) produce contraction/heterogeneity that **monotonically increases with mobility** — because a motor bridging antiparallel F-actins pulls them together without needing viscous drag as a reaction anchor ("two ropes, both hands"), so free motors still exert substantial force and their number rises with mobility. The optimal one-arm mobility shifts with k₂₀ (lower stall force → aggregation matters more → higher optimal mobility). The work implies cells can tune actomyosin architecture by regulating motor mobility (e.g. calcium/calmodulin control of myosin I; membrane-anchoring of cytokinesis nodes).

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** Agent-based / coarse-grained Brownian dynamics (Langevin equation, overdamped) — the canonical Kim-lab actin-network model (refs 29, 31–33). F-actin, ACP, and motor are all represented as chains of **cylindrical segments** connected by extensional (spring) and bending (angular) potentials; excluded volume via repulsive segment-segment force.
- **Integrator:** Brownian/Langevin dynamics (segment velocity ∝ force/drag + thermal noise). Explicit timestep (value in SI). For **two-arm motor** simulations a **4-fold smaller timestep** was used for numerical stability. Actin endpoint velocities sampled **every 2 s**.
- **Domain / BCs:** Very thin quasi-2D slab **10 × 10 × 0.1 µm**. **Periodic boundary conditions in x and y**; in **z**, repulsive forces confine segments that cross the two z-boundaries (walls). This thinness makes F-actins and motors at similar (x,y) able to interact regardless of z, mimicking a thin cortex (100–200 nm) where severe volume exclusion is avoided.
- **Initialization:** F-actins self-assemble from actin segments (nucleation + polymerization). ACPs bind to form cross-links between filament pairs. Motors (one or two arms) placed at random (x,y,z). A fraction of motor arms initially bind without walking; after the network forms, motors start walking.
- **Motor kinetics:** Each motor arm binds ONE binding site on an actin segment at rate **40·N_h s⁻¹** (N_h = number of myosin heads per arm). Walking rate k_{w,M} and unbinding rate k_{u,M} come from the **Parallel Cluster Model (PCM)** (Erdmann–Schwarz, refs 35,36): 3 mechanochemical states, 5 rates (Fig S1a). Both k_{w,M} and k_{u,M} **decrease under higher applied force** (catch-bond behavior of myosin, refs 37,38) and are **proportional to k₂₀**. **Stall force is inversely proportional to k₂₀.** N_h = 8 (one-arm) / N_h = 4 (two-arm) chosen so maximal motor force (= N_h × stall force × #arms) stays comparable across motor types.
- **ACP kinetics:** ACPs bind at a constant rate, unbind force-dependently via **Bell's law** (ref 34).
- **Actin:** No turnover after polymerization (no severing/capping/branching) — an explicit simplification.
- **Mobility knob:** Motor drag coefficient **ζ_M** varied over ~5 decades; F-actin and ACP drag coefficients held fixed. Higher ζ_M → less displacement per step under equal force → lower mobility.
- **Analysis metrics:** network heterogeneity Q_A (Eq. 1, NG=20 grid), F-actin average speed (|v_i,A| averaged over endpoints & time), fraction of bound motors, average motor arm force, stress on network.

## Governing Equations
1. **Langevin / Brownian dynamics of a segment** (stated in words, standard form): overdamped motion, segment velocity governed by
   ζ · dr/dt = F_ext + F_bend + F_rep + F_thermal
   — each cylindrical segment moves against a drag force ∝ (velocity × drag coefficient); F_actin/ACP drag fixed, motor drag = ζ_M is the varied control. *Meaning:* force balance sets displacement per step; ζ_M sets motor mobility.

2. **Extensional (stretching) potential** maintaining equilibrium segment length via extensional stiffness k_s (harmonic spring, indicated by "springs" in Fig 1a). Distinct stiffnesses k_{s,A} (actin), k_{s,ACP} (ACP), k_{s,M} (motor). *Meaning:* enforces equilibrium bond lengths.

3. **Bending potential** maintaining equilibrium angle between adjacent segments via bending stiffness k_b (harmonic angular, "bent arrows" in Fig 1a). Distinct k_{b,A} (actin), k_{b,ACP} (ACP). *Meaning:* sets filament/crosslinker flexural rigidity; k_{b,A} corresponds to persistence length L_p = 9 µm at reference.

4. **Repulsive (volume-exclusion) force** between overlapping actin segments. *Meaning:* excluded-volume / steric repulsion.

5. **Bell's law for ACP unbinding** (ref 34): k_off = k_off,0 · exp(F·λ / k_BT) (force-dependent bond rupture). *Meaning:* cross-links rupture faster under load.

6. **PCM mechanochemical rates** (refs 35,36): k_{w,M}(F) and k_{u,M}(F), both ∝ k₂₀ and both DECREASING with applied force F (catch-bond); stall force ∝ 1/k₂₀. *Meaning:* motor walking and detachment kinetics as function of load and ATP-dependent rate.

7. **Heterogeneity of F-actin spatial distribution, Q_A** (Eq. 1 in paper):
   Q_A = (1/2N_G) · [ Σ_{i=1}^{N_G} std( ρ_A^{i,j} |_{j=1..N} ) + Σ_{j=1}^{N_G} std( ρ_A^{i,j} |_{i=1..N} ) ]
   where ρ_A^{i,j} = number of actin segments in grid cell (i,j), N_G = number of grids per direction (=20). *Meaning:* averaged SD of actin density across grid rows and columns — quantifies morphological clustering/heterogeneity.

8. **F-actin average speed:** average of |v_i,A(t)| (endpoint velocities, sampled every 2 s) over all endpoints and time. *Meaning:* how fast motors displace filaments.

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Segment extensional spring:** F = k_s (r − r_0), stiffnesses k_{s,A}, k_{s,ACP}, k_{s,M} (values in SI Table S1).
- **Segment bending:** angular harmonic with k_{b,A}, k_{b,ACP}; k_{b,A} tuned to L_p(actin) = 9 µm.
- **Excluded-volume repulsion:** repulsive-only force between overlapping actin segments (and z-wall repulsion).
- **Motor drag law:** F_drag = ζ_M · v; ζ_M is the swept control variable (reference ζ*_M = 8.10×10⁻⁸ kg/s).
- **Motor active force:** generated via PCM walking; maximal motor force = N_h × stall force × (#arms); stall force ∝ 1/k₂₀; catch-bond load dependence of k_{w,M}, k_{u,M}.
- **ACP unbinding:** Bell's law exponential force dependence.
- **Two-arm antiparallel force:** motor bridging antiparallel F-actins pulls both together requiring no external drag anchor (mechanistic crux of Fig 6b).

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Weak/indirect. This is a fine-grained filament-level model, not a surface-mesh continuum. However, it is a source of **cortex-scale contractility phenomenology** (motor mobility → tension vs. clustering) that could inform how DCM's lumped cortical-tension γ emerges; not a direct DCM parameter source.
- **FF?** **YES — strongly.** This IS a Cytosim/Kim-lab-style fine-grained actomyosin model: cylindrical-segment F-actin with bending (L_p=9µm) + extensional stiffness, ACP cross-linkers with **Bell's-law** off-rate, myosin motors with **PCM catch-bond** walking/unbinding, excluded volume — the exact mechanistic vocabulary of the FF engine. Directly relevant to FF motor mobility (drag ζ_M), one- vs two-arm motor connectivity, binding rate 40·N_h, stall∝1/k₂₀, and the antiparallel-pair contraction mechanism. The reference walking velocity 140 nm/s (nonmuscle myosin II) is a usable FF motor calibration datum.
