## Summary
This is a physics-education (outreach) article describing a 3-hour distance-learning workshop, "Let's explore the small world inside a cell!", run twice in 2020 for 27 motivated Japanese elementary-school students (grades 4–6), during COVID-19 via Zoom. The pedagogical goal was to have children trace the full research process (set a question → quantitative analysis → hypothesis → verification with a simple model) using Brownian motion as the vehicle. In section 1, students used the open-source Tracker software to track 1-µm plastic beads suspended in water (recorded at 25 fps, 100× objective, CCD) by clicking the bead center every 0.20 s for 20 s (100 points), then computed the square-root of mean-square displacement (√MSD) and observed the diffusive √t scaling (contrasting a car's ballistic ∝t motion). In section 2, students ran a hands-on 2D random-walk dice game (6-direction die on an A4 grid sheet, measuring displacement from origin every 10 rolls) and reproduced the same √N scaling, verifying the water-molecule-collision hypothesis for Brownian motion. In section 3, the lecture bridged to intracellular protein dynamics and molecular motors (kinesin/myosin) that rectify thermal collisions using ATP. Post-workshop surveys showed the content was rated "difficult but interesting" (interesting mean 4.69/5, difficulty mean 3.27/5, n=26). The only physics equation is the standard 2D diffusion law MSD = 4Dt.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** No computational/physics simulation model. Two hands-on empirical activities: (1) experimental particle-tracking video microscopy of Brownian beads; (2) a physical 2D random-walk realized with a six-sided die on a printed hex/grid sheet. The only theoretical "model" invoked is the textbook 2D Einstein diffusion relation.
- **Experimental setup (bead movie):** 1-µm-diameter plastic particles suspended in water; optical microscope, 100× objective lens, CCD camera, frame rate 25 fps. Different movies sent to each family to avoid duplicate particle sets.
- **Tracking protocol:** Manual center-clicking in Tracker every 0.20 s (= every 5 frames at 25 fps) for 20 s → 100 time points per trajectory. Origin placed at lower-left of image to avoid negative numbers (elementary students had not learned negatives/coordinates). Defocused (out-of-plane, Z-motion) beads still tracked at center. √MSD in Fig. 4 computed from 20 tracking points.
- **Dice random-walk protocol:** A4 game sheet; start at center double-circle; a die roll (faces 1–6) selects one of 6 movement directions (hexagonal step directions per Fig. 6 rule diagram). Every 10 rolls, measure straight-line distance token→start with a ruler, enter into spreadsheet. Goal circles set 7 steps from start (gamification). Fig. 8 √MSD computed from 20 independent dice experiments; sample number n declines with trial number N because tokens reaching the goal drop out (n = 20→18→14→13→8 across N = 0,10,20,30,50).
- **Integrator / timestep / BCs:** Not applicable (no numerical solver). Effective sampling interval Δt = 0.20 s; total observation window 20 s.
- **Analysis tools:** Tracker (open source, tracker.physlets.org); Google Spreadsheet for data aggregation; custom data-aggregation/analysis tool built by YY. Model fitting = √MSD vs t curve (solid line in Figs 4a, 8).

## Governing Equations
Only two equations appear (both on page 4), the standard isotropic 2D diffusion relations:

1. **2D mean-square displacement (Einstein diffusion):**
   ⟨[x(t) − x(0)]² + [y(t) − y(0)]²⟩ = 4·D·t   — (Eq. 1)
   Meaning: ensemble-averaged squared displacement of a Brownian particle in 2D grows linearly in time; slope = 4D (D = diffusion coefficient). ⟨·⟩ = ensemble average; x(t), y(t) = particle X,Y positions.

2. **√MSD scaling:**
   ⟨[x(t) − x(0)]² + [y(t) − y(0)]²⟩^(1/2) ∝ t^(1/2)   — (Eq. 2)
   Meaning: root-mean-square displacement scales as √t (diffusive), i.e. "four times as long to go twice as far" — the pedagogical contrast against ballistic ∝t motion of a car. For the dice game the analogous relation is √MSD ∝ N^(1/2) (N = number of rolls, playing the role of time t).

No bending/stretching potentials, no motor force–velocity law, no bond-kinetics (Bell/catch), no viscoelastic constitutive law, no Langevin equation of motion is written out. D itself is never numerically evaluated (kept qualitative for the young audience).
