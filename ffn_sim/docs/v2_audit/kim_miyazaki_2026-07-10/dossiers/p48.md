## Summary

The paper asks **how a branched lamellipodial actin network sustains a stable-yet-dynamic steady state with continuous retrograde flow**, and how that state is robustly maintained/recovered under environmental perturbation. The authors build a **fully agent-based, filament-resolved Brownian-dynamics model** (the Taeyoon-Kim cytoskeleton framework) of a quasi-2D lamellipodium: explicit F-actin (cylindrical serially-connected segments with barbed/pointed polarity), Arp2/3 complex (branch nucleator, 70° branch), actin cross-linking proteins (ACP, permanent), and non-muscle myosin II motors (backbone + 8 arms, each arm = 4 heads, parallel-cluster-model mechanochemistry), all on an elastic triangulated substrate to which endpoints transiently link as **nascent focal adhesions** (force-dependent Bell-model unbinding). F-actin undergoes de-novo nucleation, barbed-end polymerization (leading edge only), pointed-end depolymerization (rear only), and **angle-dependent (buckling-driven) severing** everywhere. Running 300 s per condition in a 5×2.5×0.1 µm domain, they identify a reference condition that holds a steady retrograde flow for >200 s (flow ~20–30 nm/s), then vary one parameter at a time. Core finding: the steady state is set by **(i) a balance between assembly at the front and disassembly (depolymerization + buckling-induced severing) at the rear**, and **(ii) a competition between myosin contractile force and FA frictional resistance**. F-actin **severing is essential** — it disassembles the myosin-compacted actin arc/bundle at the rear so the network stays homogeneous and the flow stays continuous; too little severing stalls contraction (bundle traps motors), too much fragments filaments before motors can act (biphasic). Depolymerization, Arp2/3 density, ACP density, polymerization rate, motor density R_M, myosin ATP-unbinding rate k20, and FA-region size A_FA each show a threshold/intermediate window. Finally they show **multiple modes of dynamic steady state**: a network that fails (e.g., too-low motors) can be rescued by lowering FA friction, and a network that fails from too-fast depolymerization can be rescued by raising flow speed (more motors or less FA) — the flow/friction competition and the assembly/disassembly balance are coupled knobs.

## Model & Methods

- **Model type:** Agent-based, filament-level **Brownian dynamics** (overdamped Langevin, inertia neglected). Cytoskeletal elements = cylindrical segments defined by endpoints. This is the Taeyoon-Kim "CytoSim-class" fine-grained framework (same lineage as refs 62, 74, 77, 78 — Bidone/Jung/Kim).
- **Integrator:** Explicit **Euler** update of positions from Langevin velocities (Eq S4).
- **Timestep:** Δt = **1.15 × 10⁻⁵ s**.
- **Simulated time:** 300 s per condition (steady state assessed after ~100–200 s).
- **Domain / geometry:** thin slab **5 µm (x) × 2.5 µm (y) × 0.1 µm (z)** mimicking flat lamellipodium.
- **Boundary conditions:** periodic in x; **repulsive** at +y (leading edge) and both z walls; **sticky** at −y (lamellipodium/lamella interface). No deformable membrane (simplification — retrograde flow driven solely by rear motors, not polymerization ratchet).
- **Substrate:** 2D triangulated mesh at z = 0, node z-positions fixed; chain length (mesh edge) r₀,sub = 50 nm; effective substrate stiffness κ_eff ≈ 2.4×10⁻⁴ N/m (soft).
- **Spatial zoning (Fig 1C):**
  - Assembly (barbed-end polymerization) region: y = **2.125–2.5 µm** (near +y leading edge).
  - Disassembly (pointed-end depolymerization) region: y = **0–0.375 µm** (near −y).
  - Motor activity region: y = **0–0.1875 µm** (near −y). Motor drag ×1000 to prevent drift.
  - FA link-formation region: y = **0.325–0.675 µm** (reference A_FA = 0.35; region size scaled by A_FA). (Fig 2B caption cites 0.81–1.69 µm in a differently normalized figure crop.)
  - Flow-speed measurement region: rectangle near +y boundary.
- **Algorithm outline:** (1) self-assemble branched network from seed filaments nucleated at −y, barbed ends biased +y, elongate to ≤0.98 µm, branch via Arp2/3 (70°), cross-link via ACP; ~90% of actin used, ≥10% kept as free monomer pool (closed system, monomer recycled). (2) Turn on motor walking → rear contraction → retrograde flow. (3) Continuous turnover: barbed polymerization at front, pointed depolymerization at rear, angle-dependent severing everywhere (dominant at rear due to buckling). Free segments recycled to front. (4) Nascent FAs form/break as force-dependent elastic links.
- **Measurement:** flow speed every 2 s (y-velocity of front F-actins); F-actin density every 1 s (40 y-bins, normalized to N_A/40); **heterogeneity = coefficient of variation of density along y**; total substrate force = Σ link spring forces. Visualization via VMD.

## Governing Equations

1. **(S1) Overdamped Langevin (force balance, no inertia):**
   `F_i − ζ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_i^T = 0`
   — deterministic force + drag + thermal force sum to zero on endpoint i.

2. **(S2) Drag coefficient (cylinder approximation, Clift-Grace-Weber):**
   `ζ_i = 3π μ r_c,i · (3 + 2 r₀,i / r_c,i) / 5`
   — μ = medium viscosity; r₀,i = segment length, r_c,i = segment diameter.

3. **(S3) Fluctuation–dissipation (thermal force):**
   `⟨F_i^T(t) F_j^T(t)⟩ = (2 k_B T ζ_i δ_ij / Δt) δ`
   — Gaussian white noise scaled by drag and temperature; δ = 2nd-order (identity) tensor.

4. **(S4) Euler position update:**
   `r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (dr_i/dt) Δt = r_i(t) + (1/ζ_i)(F_i + F_i^T) Δt`

5. **(S5) Extensional (stretching) potential:**
   `U_s = ½ κ_s (r − r₀)²`
   — harmonic spring maintaining equilibrium segment length r₀.

6. **(S6) Bending potential:**
   `U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ₀)²`
   — harmonic angle spring maintaining equilibrium angle θ₀.

7. **(S7) Torsional potential:**
   `U_t = ½ κ_t (φ − φ₀)²`
   — harmonic torsion maintaining equilibrium torsional angle φ₀ (used for Arp2/3 planarity).

8. **(S8) Repulsive (volume-exclusion) potential between actin segments:**
   `U_r,A = ½ κ_r,A (r₁₂ − r_c,A)²  if r₁₂ < r_c,A ;  0  if r₁₂ ≥ r_c,A`
   — one-sided harmonic repulsion; r₁₂ = inter-element distance, r_c,A = actin diameter.

9. **(S9) Angle-dependent F-actin severing rate:**
   `k_sev = k_{0,sev} · exp(λ_sev θ)`
   — severing rate rises exponentially with local bending angle θ (buckling-driven); k_{0,sev} = zero-angle rate constant, λ_sev = angle sensitivity. (Adapted from Kim ref 61; reproduces McCullough 2011 severing-angle distribution.)

10. **(S10) Force-dependent FA-link (nascent focal adhesion) unbinding rate — Bell model:**
    `k_u,C = k⁰_u,C · exp(λ_u,C F_s,C / k_B T)  if r ≥ r₀,C ;  k⁰_u,C  if r < r₀,C`
    — slip-bond: unbinding accelerates with link spring force F_s,C only under tension (r ≥ equilibrium length r₀,C); k⁰_u,C = zero-force rate, λ_u,C = force sensitivity.

**Additional (in-text) rate rules (not separately numbered):**
- Motor arm binding rate = **40·N_h** per arm (N_h = 4 heads/arm).
- Motor walking rate k_w,M and unbinding rate k_u,M set by the **parallel cluster model** (Erdmann–Schwarz, refs 48,49; benchmarked in Kim ref 78): 3 head states, 5 transition rates; k20 = ATP-dependent unbinding (post-power-stroke → unbound). Both k_w,M and k_u,M decrease with smaller k20 or larger load (catch-bond assumption).

## Relevance flags

- **DCM?** Weak-yes (peripheral). This is filament-resolved, not surface-mesh continuum.
