## Summary
The paper asks whether **pulsed contraction** of the actomyosin cortex — the reversible, transient accumulation-then-scattering of F-actin and myosin seen during embryogenesis and tissue morphogenesis (e.g. Drosophila apical constriction, C. elegans polarization) — can emerge from **purely mechanical/dynamic cytoskeletal behavior**, without the spatiotemporal RhoA biochemical oscillator that experiments have implicated. Using a well-established agent-based Brownian-dynamics model of a very thin (10×10×0.1 µm) 2D actomyosin network of explicit cylindrical F-actin segments, myosin thick-filament motors (backbone + 4 arms × 8 heads, parallel-cluster-model force-velocity/unbinding), and force-dependent (Bell's law) actin cross-linking proteins (ACPs), they sweep actin turnover rate (k_{t,A}), ACP density (R_ACP), mean filament length (⟨L_f⟩), and — critically — angle-dependent buckling-induced F-actin **severing** (rate k_{s,A}=k⁰_{s,A}·exp(θ_{s,A}/λ_{s,A})). Key findings: (1) increasing F-actin **turnover (treadmilling)** monotonically suppresses cluster formation / heterogeneity (force relaxation counteracts motor force generation); (2) network **connectivity** (tuned by R_ACP or ⟨L_f⟩) gives maximal contraction at *intermediate* values (three regimes: small clusters → single large cluster → interconnected bundles); (3) at a *subtle balance* between motor force generation and turnover-driven relaxation, **weak pulsed contraction** with small nascent clusters emerges in a very narrow regime (short ⟨L_f⟩≈1 µm, intermediate k_{t,A}≈70 s⁻¹, low/intermediate connectivity) — but the clusters are too small (<size) and short-lived (<50 s) vs. the in-vivo ~2–4 µm, ~40–100 s pulses; (4) adding **angle-dependent F-actin severing** (buckling-induced, occurs preferentially inside contracting clusters) lets large clusters disassemble quickly, producing **strong pulsed contraction** with physiologically realistic cluster size and duration; k⁰_{s,A} and λ_{s,A} independently tune the size/lifetime and small-vs-large populations of pulses. Conclusion: **both** local force relaxation via severing **and** global relaxation via treadmilling turnover are necessary; severing alone shortens filaments irreversibly and cannot sustain large pulses. Mechanistically, pulsed contraction need not require RhoA biochemistry — mechanical severing+turnover suffice; RhoA likely acts as a pacemaker while turnover/severing do the actual disassembly.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** Agent-based (coarse-grained particle) **Brownian dynamics** of a 2D (quasi-2D) actomyosin network. Well-established Kim-lab model (refs 12,16,17,21). All cytoskeletal elements are explicit cylindrical segments — F-actin, myosin motors, and ACPs.
- **Governing dynamics:** Langevin equation in the inertialess (overdamped) limit, integrated by **Euler** scheme. Stochastic thermal force set by the fluctuation-dissipation theorem (ref 22, Underhill-Doyle bead-spring coarse-graining). Deterministic forces = bending + extensional (harmonic, maintaining equilibrium angles and segment lengths) + repulsive volume-exclusion between neighboring segment pairs.
- **F-actin representation:** serially connected cylindrical segments, each **140 nm** long, with polarity (barbed / pointed end).
- **Motor representation:** myosin II thick filament — a backbone with **four arms**, each arm = **eight myosin heads**. Each arm binds F-actin at constant rate, walks toward the barbed end at a force-dependent rate (walking slows with tensile force; stops above stall force), and unbinds at a rate inversely proportional to applied force. Force-dependent walking + unbinding rates from the **parallel cluster model (PCM)** (Erdmann & Schwarz, refs 24,25).
- **ACP representation:** two cylindrical segments. Bind F-actin at constant rate with no preferred cross-linking angle; **unbind at a force-dependent (Bell's law) rate** that rises exponentially with tensile force (ref 23).
- **Domain / BCs:** very thin computational box **10 × 10 × 0.1 µm**, **periodic** in x and y only; in z the domain walls exert repulsive forces on elements displaced beyond the boundary. Network self-assembles at t=0 from F-actin, ACPs, and motors.
- **Actin dynamics (turnover):** F-actin nucleates from one polarity segment in a random in-plane direction. Polymerization = adding a segment (barbed end only); depolymerization = removing a segment (pointed end only) → **treadmilling**. Polymerization rate constant k_{+,A} set equal to depolymerization k_{-,A}, giving dynamic equilibrium at **1 µM** actin. Turnover rate k_{t,A} ≡ k_{-,A}. Mean length ⟨L_f⟩ tuned by ratio of nucleation rate k_{n,A} to k_{t,A}, spanning **0.7–5.0 µm** (smaller than ~20 µm reconstituted networks; comparable to cell cortex).
- **F-actin severing (optional):** remove one segment; segment chosen via sum of bending angles at its two ends θ_{s,A}; severing rate from Eq. 1 (angle-dependent, empirically motivated by ref 30 cofilin severing data). Authors note results are insensitive to the specific severing-model choice.
- **Analysis:** motor speed ⟨v_M⟩ every 1 s (Eq. 2, 3); network heterogeneity Q_A from SD of segment-density map on N_G×N_G grid (N_G=10–20); radial distribution g(r) (Eq. 4, r 50 nm–1 µm, dr=50 nm); cluster identification by density+duration thresholds with cluster-tracking across frames (ignore clusters <20 s); motor persistency ⟨cos φ⟩ velocity autocorrelation (Eq. 5, τ=1 s, averaged 10–20 s).
- **Fixed conditions:** actin concentration C_A = 60 µM; motor density R_M = 0.04 (fixed across all runs).

## Governing Equations

**(Eq. 1) Angle-dependent F-actin severing rate**
$$k_{s,A} = k^0_{s,A}\,\exp\!\left(\frac{\theta_{s,A}}{\lambda_{s,A}}\right)$$
Severing rate of an actin segment = zero-angle base rate k⁰_{s,A} times an exponential in the summed bending angle θ_{s,A} at the segment's two ends, scaled by insensitivity λ_{s,A} (larger λ_{s,A} ⇒ severing needs larger bending angle). Meaning: buckled (highly bent, small-radius) filaments sever fastest — captures buckling-induced fragmentation.

**(Eq. 2) Ensemble-average motor speed at time t**
$$\langle v_M(t)\rangle = \frac{1}{N_M}\sum_{i=1}^{N_M} v_{i,M}(t) = \frac{1}{N_M}\sum_{i=1}^{N_M}\frac{|\,\mathbf{r}_{i,M}(t)-\mathbf{r}_{i,M}(t-1)\,|}{1\ \text{s}}$$
Mean scalar speed of all N_M motors from center displacement over a 1 s window (x,y only; z ignored as domain is thin). Meaning: quantifies how fast motors translocate; sampling interval fixed at 1 s to average out thermal noise.

**(Eq. 3) Time-averaged motor speed over [t₁,t₂]**
$$\langle v_M\rangle_{t_1\to t_2} = \frac{1}{t_2-t_1}\sum_{t=t_1}^{t_2}\langle v_M(t)\rangle$$
Time average of the ensemble-average speed over a chosen interval (e.g. early 0–10 s vs late 50 s–end).

**(Eq. 4) Radial distribution function of actin segments**
$$g(r) = P(r)/(2\pi r\,\delta r\,\rho_a)$$
P(r) = probability of finding actin-segment pairs separated by r…r+δr; ρ_a = mean density of the 1%-sampled segments. g(r)≈1 everywhere ⇒ homogeneous network; high peaks at small r ⇒ heterogeneous/clustered. r: 50 nm–1 µm, δr=50 nm.

**(Eq. 5) Motor-velocity persistency (autocorrelation)**
$$\langle\cos\phi(t)\rangle = \frac{1}{N_G^2}\sum_{i=1}^{N_G}\sum_{j=1}^{N_G}\cos\phi_{i,j}(t) = \frac{1}{N_G^2}\sum_{i=1}^{N_G}\sum_{j=1}^{N_G}\frac{\mathbf{v}^{i,j}_M(t)\cdot\mathbf{v}^{i,j}_M(t+\tau)}{|\mathbf{v}^{i,j}_M(t)|\,|\mathbf{v}^{i,j}_M(t+\tau)|}$$
Grid-averaged cosine between per-grid mean motor velocity at t and t+τ (τ=1 s), averaged over 10–20 s. ⟨cos φ⟩→1 ⇒ highly persistent directed motion = signature of clustering/contraction.

*(The bending, extensional, volume-exclusion harmonic potentials, the Bell's-law ACP off-rate, the PCM motor walking/unbinding laws, and the Langevin stochastic-force expression are stated in words in the main text but their explicit functional forms + constants live in the Supporting Materials and Methods / Table S1, not reproduced in the main article.)*

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **Bending force:** harmonic on segment-junction angles, maintaining equilibrium angle (functional form in SI).
- **Extensional force:** harmonic on segment length, maintaining equilibrium segment length (140 nm) (SI).
- **Volume exclusion:** repulsive force between neighboring segment pairs (SI).
- **Thermal/stochastic force:** Langevin noise from fluctuation-dissipation theorem (ref 22).
- **ACP unbinding — Bell's law (ref 23):** unbinding rate increases exponentially with tensile force on the ACP (k_off = k⁰_off·exp(F·x/k_BT form; constants in SI).
- **Motor walking (force-velocity):** walking rate decreases with tensile force per head; halts above the **stall force**; from parallel cluster model (refs 24,25).
- **Motor unbinding:** rate inversely proportional to applied force (PCM).
- **Severing:** exponential-in-bending-angle law, Eq. 1.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Low-to-moderate. DCM is a surface-mesh continuum cortex; this paper is a filament-resolved network, so it does not map directly to DCM's lumped cortical-tension node model. But it informs the *phenomenology* DCM's cortex should ultimately emulate (pulsed cortical tension, turnover-set homogeneity) and warns that turnover/severing set whether the cortex aggregates vs stays smooth.
- **FF?** **Yes, strongly.** This IS the FF class of model — Cytosim/agent-based fine-grained actomyosin: explicit cylindrical F-actin segments (140 nm), myosin thick filaments (4 arms × 8 heads) with Hill/PCM-style force-velocity + force-dependent unbinding, ACPs with Bell's-law off-rate, treadmilling turnover (barbed polymerize / pointed depolymerize), and **angle-dependent buckling-induced severing (Eq. 1)** — all directly implementable as FF mechanisms. The Kim-lab model is a sibling/oracle for FF's cortex; severing law Eq. 1 and the PCM motor are concrete algorithms to port. Parameter ranges (⟨L_f⟩ 0.7–5 µm, k_{t,A} 15–240 s⁻¹, R_M=0.04, C_A=60 µM) are FF-relevant.
- **TAG-validation?** **Yes.** Quantitative validation targets: in-vivo pulse size 2–4 µm and duration 40–100 s; motor speeds ~0.6–1.8 µm/s; the emergent claim that pulsed contraction requires the balance of motor force generation vs turnover+severing relaxation. Provenance-worthy KB claims: severing law (Eq. 1) empirically from McCullough 2011 (ref 30); PCM motor from Erdmann-Schwarz (refs 24,25); Bell's law (ref 23). Note most numeric constants (spring stiffnesses, stall force, rates) live in Table S1 of the SI, not this main file — flag SI as needed for full parameterization.
