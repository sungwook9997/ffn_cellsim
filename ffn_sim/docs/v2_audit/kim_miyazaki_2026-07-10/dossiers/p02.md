## Summary
The paper asks which molecular event of actin cross-linking proteins (ACPs) — **unbinding** (bond rupture) or **unfolding** (Ig-domain extension) — dominates the dynamic (nonlinear, time-dependent, plastic) rheology of cross-linked actin networks. Using a coarse-grained Brownian-dynamics agent model (extending Kim, Hwang & Kamm 2009, ref 17) of actin monomers/filaments cross-linked by V-shaped filamin-like ACPs, they endow each ACP arm with BOTH a force-dependent unbinding rate and a multi-step force-dependent unfolding rate, each described by Bell's equation, plus rebinding/refolding. Bulk oscillatory/shear rheology is applied to a 2.8 µm cubic box. A time-rescaling ("k⁰·t_d fixed") argument lets them reach experimental (seconds) timescales by scaling zero-force rate coefficients. Across three protocols — strain-stiffening (constant shear-rate ramp), stress relaxation (hold strain at fixed stress), and plastic deformation (shear then free-release) — the conclusion is uniform: at physiological/experimental effective strain rates (γ̇_eff ≈ 0.1 s⁻¹) **unbinding, not unfolding, governs the rheology**. Unbinding caps stress at experimental levels (~10–100 Pa), produces stress relaxation to a finite ~1 Pa plateau (rebinding sets the plateau; without rebinding stress → 0), drives network reorganization / plasticity (γ ~ t^x with x = 0–0.4, ≈0.17 matching in vitro), and highly loaded ACPs (>20 pN) rupture mainly from the ~25% "supportive framework" that bears most load and rebind to *different* filaments. Unfolding alone merely delays stress rise without changing topology, so it cannot relax stress or produce plastic deformation.

## Model & Methods
- **Model type:** Coarse-grained **Brownian (Langevin overdamped) dynamics** agent-based simulation of a cross-linked actin network. Explicit particles/segments for actin monomers→filaments and V-shaped ACPs. Extends refs 17 (Kim/Hwang/Kamm 2009, PLoS Comput Biol) and 18 (Kim/Hwang/Kamm 2009, Exp Mech).
- **Network generation:** Brownian dynamics on actin monomers, filaments, and ACPs, assuming first-order **irreversible** bond formation during polymerization. ACPs are filamin-like V-shaped and form **orthogonal** cross-links between filaments during polymerization. After growth, a **coarse-graining procedure** increases length- and timescales for subsequent runs (per ref 17).
- **Mechanics:** Actin filaments and ACPs characterized by **bending** and **extensional** stiffnesses; **thermal fluctuations** included. **Steric (excluded-volume) interactions exist only between actin filaments** (filaments cannot pass through each other); ACP-ACP and ACP-filament sterics not modeled.
- **Rheology protocol:** Bulk rheology — oscillatory or ramp **shear strain applied to the top surface of a cubic box, bottom surface fixed** (per ref 17). Box width **2.8 µm**; small box chosen because bulk rheology of this box reproduces microprobe rheology (ref 17). A single **geometrically identical network** is reused across all parameter variations to isolate effects.
- **Timestep:** Implied Δt ≈ 6.17 ns (from "10 time steps = 61.7 ns" used for the ACP relaxation/extension window detection). Sub-nanosecond BD timestep pre-coarse-graining.
- **Boundary/geometry:** Cubic simulation domain, width 2.8 µm; top-surface shear, bottom fixed; free-release of top boundary in the plastic-deformation protocol.
- **Long-time scaling ("k⁰t_d invariance"):** Because true stress relaxation is > seconds but BD Δt is ns, they rescale the zero-force rate coefficients k⁰_ub and k⁰_uf (collectively k⁰) by a factor n and scale time by (k⁰)⁻¹. Runs with the same product **k⁰·t_d** (t_d = duration of strain application or relaxation window) collapse onto a single universal rheological curve — validated in Fig. 2 for both strain-stiffening (A) and stress relaxation (B). Effective shear rate γ̇_eff = γ̇/n. NOTE: this scaling is NOT applied to the oscillatory G'/G'' runs (Fig. S4), because thermal fluctuation/diffusion are not accelerated and contribute at high frequency.
- **Protocols run:**
  1. Strain-stiffening: constant shear-rate ramp γ̇ = 10 s⁻¹, n = 1–100 → γ̇_eff = 10/n.
  2. Stress relaxation: pre-shear to τ₀ = 5 Pa (rate 100·k⁰_r), then hold γ constant 5 s.
  3. Plastic deformation: shear at γ̇_eff = 10, 1, 0.1 s⁻¹ up to γ = 1.0, then free-release top boundary.
  4. Oscillatory (Supporting): G', G'' vs k⁰_ub at 10 Hz, prestress 10 Pa; and G'(τ₀) vs prestress with various R.

## Governing Equations
Transcribed faithfully (LaTeX-ish). Equation numbers match the paper.

**(1) ACP-arm force–extension law (series worm-like chain + compression spring):**
$$
F_s(r_{12}) =
\begin{cases}
-\dfrac{k_BT}{p}\left[\dfrac{(2l_{0,i}-r_{12}-r_0)(r_{12}-r_0)}{4\,l_{0,i}^2\left(1-\frac{r_{12}}{l_{0,i}}\right)^2\left(1-\frac{r_0}{l_{0,i}}\right)^2}+\dfrac{r_{12}-r_0}{l_{0,i}}\right] & \text{if } r_{12}\ge r_0\\[2ex]
-\kappa_{s,ACP}\,(r_{12}-r_0) & \text{if } r_{12} < r_0
\end{cases}
$$
Meaning: tension in one ACP arm as a function of its current length r₁₂; extended regime is a worm-like-chain-type restoring force (Marko–Siggia-like form, persistence length p, contour l₀,i for the i-th unfolded state), compressed regime is a linear spring with stiffness κ_s,ACP. (Sign convention as printed; magnitude |Fs| enters the rate laws.)

**(2) Unfolding rate (Bell's equation):**
$$
k_{uf} =
\begin{cases}
k^0_{uf}\,\exp\!\left(\dfrac{\lambda_{uf}\,|F_s|}{k_BT}\right) & \text{if } r_{12}\ge r_0\\[1ex]
k^0_{uf} & \text{if } r_{12} < r_0
\end{cases}
$$
Meaning: force-accelerated (Bell) domain-unfolding rate; each event increments unfolding index i by 1 and raises the arm's max extension to l₀,i.

**(3) Unbinding rate (Bell's equation, different constants):**
$$
k_{ub} =
\begin{cases}
k^0_{ub}\,\exp\!\left(\dfrac{\lambda_{ub}\,|F_s|}{k_BT}\right) & \text{if } r_{12}\ge r_0\\[1ex]
k^0_{ub} & \text{if } r_{12} < r_0
\end{cases}
$$
Meaning: force-accelerated bond-rupture rate of an ACP arm from a filament; slip-bond Bell form. Simultaneous unbinding of both arms prohibited; on unbinding the arm fully refolds (i→0, r₁₂→r₀).

**(4) Two-regime power-law stress–strain fit:**
$$
\tau(\gamma) = c_1\gamma^{x_1} + c_2\gamma^{x_2}
$$
with c₁ = 2.5, c₂ = 500, x₁ = 0.4, x₂ = 4. First term dominates low-γ (bending-dominated), second dominates high-γ (stretch-dominated). K = dτ/dγ is its derivative.

**(5) ACP cross-link decay under constant strain:**
$$
N_{ACP} = N^0_{ACP}\,\exp(-2k^0_{ub}t)
$$
Meaning: number of intact cross-linking ACPs decays exponentially; factor 2 = either arm can unbind. Kinetics scheme [actin-ACP] →(k⁰_ub) [actin] + [ACP]. Force effect on N_ACP negligible at low stress (τ < 5 Pa).

**(6) Stress-rate constitutive ODE (relaxation):**
$$
\frac{d\tau}{dt} = (a\tau - b)\frac{dN_{ACP}}{dt}
$$
Meaning: each intact-ACP unbinding drops stress by ∝τ (=aτ); rebinding limits the drop by constant b.

**(7) Closed-form stress relaxation:**
$$
\tau(t) = \frac{b}{a} + \left(\tau_0 - \frac{b}{a}\right)\exp\!\Big[a N^0_{ACP}\big(\exp(-2k^0_{ub}t)-1\big)\Big]
$$
Meaning: integrated relaxation curve. Without rebinding (b=0): fit a = 0.006, τ→0. With rebinding: a = 0.006, b = 1.8 Pa → finite plateau b/a ≈ 300... (note: plateau observed ~1 Pa in Fig. 5; b/a = 1.8/0.006 = 300 sets the argument scale, the observed ~1 Pa plateau is the actual relaxed value).

**(8) Free-relaxation force balance (plastic deformation):**
$$
\frac{d\gamma}{dt} \sim F_r
$$
Meaning: during free release, strain-rate ∝ restoring force F_r (balanced by viscous drag on clamped actin segments).

**(9) Restoring-force strain law + ODE:**
$$
\frac{d\gamma}{dt} = c\,\exp(-11.5\,\gamma)
$$
with empirical F_r ~ exp(11.5γ) dependence (except very high γ).

**(10) Closed-form plastic strain decay:**
$$
\gamma(t) = \frac{\ln\{\exp(11.5\,\gamma_0) + 11.5\,c\,t\}}{11.5}
$$
Meaning: temporal strain profile during free relaxation; γ₀ = 1.0. Fits: c = 0.11, 0.015, 0.0005 for γ̇_eff = 10, 1, 0.1 s⁻¹.

## Force Laws & Potentials
- **ACP arm tension:** Eq. 1 — extended-regime worm-like-chain (persistence length p = 0.33 nm; contour length l₀,i state-dependent), compressed-regime linear spring κ_s,ACP·(r₁₂−r₀). Saw-tooth force-extension emerges from sequential unfolding (Fig. 1), peaks 100–200 pN at ~30 nm intervals (matching filamin A AFM, ref 7).
- **Unfolding kinetics:** Eq. 2 — Bell slip law; zero-force rate k⁰_uf, compliance λ_uf = 6×10⁻¹⁰ m; multi-step (up to N_uf), each step adds 30 nm to max extension.
- **Unbinding kinetics:** Eq. 3 — Bell slip law; zero-force rate k⁰_ub, compliance λ_ub = 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m.
- **Actin filament mechanics:** bending stiffness κ_b,A and extensional stiffness κ_s,A (values from ref 17; reduced by up to ×0.1 in the c₁/c₂/x₁/x₂ sensitivity study — κ_b,A controls c₁, x₁ [bending regime]; κ_s,A controls c₂, x₂ [stretch regime]).
- **Steric:** excluded-volume repulsion between actin filaments only.
- **Thermal:** stochastic Langevin thermal forces on all beads.

## Relevance flags
- **DCM?** Weak/indirect. DCM is surface-mesh continuum (cortex tension, turgor); this paper is a fine-grained network model, not a continuum cell. But its emergent constitutive outputs (τ = c₁γ^{0.4} + c₂γ⁴ strain-stiffening; ~10 Pa relaxed stress; ~1 Pa plateau) could inform an effective cortex viscoelastic constitutive law if DCM ever needs a nonlinear rheology closure. Low priority.
- **FF?** **YES — high.** This is the methodological ancestor of the FF Cytosim-style engine's crosslinker layer. It gives explicit, literature-anchored **Bell-Evans unbinding/unfolding kinetics for ACPs** (k⁰_ub,r = 0.115 s⁻¹ from Ferrer 2008 [same Ferrer source the KB uses for α-actinin k_off0], λ_ub = 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m; k⁰_uf,r = 3.0×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹, λ_uf = 6×10⁻¹⁰ m), a WLC+multi-step-unfolding force-extension arm model (p=0.33 nm, r₀=105 nm, l₀,i=140+30i nm), first-order irreversible bond formation, orthogonal V-shaped filamin cross-links, excluded-volume between filaments, and the key mechanistic result that **unbinding (not unfolding) governs network rheology at physiological strain rates** — directly relevant to FF's Bell-Evans crosslinker off-rate design and to whether FF needs to model domain unfolding at all (answer for typical rates: no, unbinding dominates).
- **TAG-validation?** **YES.** Multiple quantitative validation targets suitable for KB registration / gate anchoring: the two-regime power-law strain-stiffening (c₁=2.5, c₂=500, x₁=0.4, x₂=4; K~γ³), stress-relaxation plateau ~1 Pa (rebinding-set) and ~10 Pa network endpoint, plastic power-law exponent ≈0.17 matching in vitro (Gardel 2006), stress cap >100 Pa rarely seen in vitro, and the Bell rate constants. Note Ferrer 2008 (ref 6) is the k⁰_ub,r=0.115 s⁻¹ provenance — cross-check against the existing KB Ferrer-anchored α-actinin off-rate (0.066 s⁻¹) as these are related single-molecule ACP rupture data.
