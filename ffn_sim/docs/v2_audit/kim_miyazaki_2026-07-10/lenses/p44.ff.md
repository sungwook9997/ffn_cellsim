# FF lens — p44

**Paper:** Bidone, Jung, Maruri, Borau, Kamm, Kim (2017) *Morphological Transformation and Force Generation of Active Cytoskeletal Networks*, PLoS Comput Biol 13(1):e1005277, DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005277.

**Why this paper is uniquely load-bearing for FF.** This is the Taeyoon-Kim-lab canonical agent-based Brownian-dynamics actomyosin model — the SAME physics class as FF/Cytosim: explicit actin filaments as bending beams (harmonic κ_s, κ_b), ACP cross-linkers with a **Bell slip-bond off-rate**, NM-II minifilaments as multi-arm bipolar motors with **catch-bond PCM kinetics**, excluded volume, treadmilling/turnover, overdamped Langevin + Euler. It is effectively a peer implementation of the FF target. Nearly every constitutive law and constant here maps 1:1 onto an FF module, several supersede or confirm FF anchors we already carry (Ferrer filamin, Isambert ℓ_p), and the network→bundle condensation is a directly reproducible FF validation scenario for our open **cortical-tension γ / contraction / bundle-formation** problems.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### 1a. Crosslinker → load-sharing sustainability law (HIGH priority — directly on FF's open γ/contraction problem)
- **FF module:** cross-linker (α-actinin / filamin bonds), Bell-Evans off-rate.
- **Change:** adopt the paper's *load-sharing → sustainability* mechanism as an emergent FF observable, not a parameter. The core finding: **max tension ∝ both R_M (motor) and R_ACP (crosslinker) density, but tension SUSTAINABILITY ∝ R_ACP and ∝ 1/R_M.** Mechanism: many ACPs share the motor-generated load → smaller per-ACP force |F_s,ACP| → slower Bell slip-bond unbinding k_u,ACP = k⁰·exp(λ|F|/kT) → stable bundle; few ACPs → each overloaded → runaway unbinding → bundle disintegrates into aggregates and tension relaxes to ~0.
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF's γ-floor / contraction-instability work has been chasing a *force-magnitude* deficit. This paper says the sustainability of network tension is governed by the **crosslinker off-rate feedback loop**, which FF already has all the ingredients for (Bell-Evans + explicit bonds). It predicts that adding crosslinker density (not more myosin) is the lever for *sustained* cortical tension — a testable, mechanistic alternative to raising myosin force. This is a first-class candidate mechanism for the γ-generation/floor open problem.

### 1b. Buckling-driven contraction is REQUIRED for tension (HIGH)
- **FF module:** filament bending beam (κ_b, ℓ_p), excluded volume.
- **Change:** treat filament buckling as the *primary tension-generation channel* in isotropic networks, and instrument it. The paper's control (100× κ_b,A → buckling suppressed → tension collapses to near-zero even with plenty of ACPs and motors at stall) proves buckling asymmetry — not motor sliding alone — converts myosin work into net contractile tension. FF already resolves filaments as bending beams, so this is native; we just need the **buckling diagnostic** (below) and to confirm FF reproduces the collapse when κ_b is raised.
- **Why it beats current FF:** confirms FF's fine-grained bending representation is *necessary* (not gold-plating) — a coarse straight-rod model cannot generate cortical tension. Gives FF a decisive mechanistic test: contraction must vanish under ℓ_p→∞. Note the paper's own duty-ratio control (S3) rules out the confound that stiff filaments merely lowered motor duty ratio (see §5), so the buckling attribution is clean.

### 1c. Depolymerization-inhibition (protection) channel on turnover (MEDIUM)
- **FF module:** actin turnover / treadmilling (barbed growth + pointed shrink).
- **Change:** add a **crosslinker/motor-bound protection factor** to depolymerization: bound ACPs/motors locally slow depolymerization. Eq 9: `k_d,A = k⁰_d,A·(1 − ξ_d,A)`. Physiological worked mechanism: with selective protection (intermediate ξ_d,A≈0.6), the *unbound* pointed-to-first-crosslink stub (which contributes least to connectivity) depolymerizes fastest; freed monomer re-polymerizes at barbed ends → extends motor walking runway → sustains tension. This is a mechanistic turnover model, not a lumped decay rate.
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF's barbed-end growth kernel currently has no state-dependent protection of bound regions. Adding it makes turnover *couple* to the crosslinker/motor occupancy field — the fine-grained option — and reproduces the non-monotone sustainability-vs-turnover result (best at intermediate protection).

### 1d. Polarity sorting as a distinct bundling channel (MEDIUM)
- **FF module:** myosin minifilament walking + filament orientation field.
- **Change:** recognize two distinct bundling routes: (i) **buckling of antiparallel pairs** (isotropic nets, generates tension) and (ii) **polarity sorting of parallel/antiparallel pairs by motor walking** (biased-orientation nets, bundles form but tension ≈ 0 or compressive when buckling suppressed). FF should be able to produce both and correctly report near-zero tension in the polarity-sorted, buckling-suppressed case.
- **Why it matters for FF:** directly relevant to lamellipodium→transverse-arc geometry and to any FF crawl/protrusion scenario where filament orientation is biased. Warns that "a bundle formed" ≠ "tension generated" — a validation subtlety FF viewers must not conflate.

### 1e. Future channel flagged: buckling-induced severing (LOW, roadmap)
- Paper explicitly defers **buckling-induced filament severing** (their ref 34) to future work. FF should keep this on the turnover/fragmentation roadmap: a curvature-threshold severing channel on the bending beam, complementary to the Matsuda-2024-style fragmentation already noted in FF's open list.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

Overdamped Langevin (FF already uses constrained BAOAB; these are the constitutive pieces):

- **Eq 1 (EOM):** `F_i − ζ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_i^T = 0`. Maps to FF per-node force balance; F_i deterministic, F_i^T thermal.
- **Eq 2 (FDT):** `⟨F_i^T(t) F_j^T(t)⟩ = (2 k_B T ζ_i δ_ij / Δt)·δ`. FF's BAOAB thermostat already satisfies this; use as the noise-amplitude cross-check.
- **Eq 3 (cylinder drag):** `ζ_i = 3π μ r_{c,i}·(3 + 2 r_{0,i}/r_{c,i}) / 5`. **Port directly** — this is the per-segment cylinder drag from segment length r₀ and diameter r_c (Clift/Grace/Weber). Cleaner than a bare Stokes sphere for FF's rod segments. μ = medium viscosity (FF must use the physiological cytoplasm η, ~65 Pa·s, NOT the paper's in-vitro μ — see §6).
- **Eq 5 (stretch):** `U_s = ½ κ_s (r − r₀)²` → force `κ_s(r−r₀)`. FF already Hookean on segments.
- **Eq 6 (bend):** `U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ₀)²`, θ₀=0. FF angular bending; κ_b,A set to ℓ_p=9 µm (see §3, note vs FF's 17 µm).
- **Eq 7 (excluded volume):** one-sided harmonic `U_r = ½ κ_r (r₁₂ − r_{c,A})²` for `r₁₂ < r_{c,A}`, else 0; r₁₂ = min inter-segment distance. FF uses LJ-repulsive; this harmonic-repulsion is an alternative EV kernel — keep FF's LJ but note their cutoff = segment diameter r_{c,A}.
- **Eq 8 (ACP Bell slip-bond off-rate) — PORT AS-IS:**
  ```
  k_u,ACP = k⁰_u,ACP · exp( λ_u,ACP · |F_s,ACP| / k_B T )   if r ≥ r₀,ACP
          = k⁰_u,ACP                                          if r < r₀,ACP  (compression: no acceleration)
  ```
  with `F_s,ACP = −∇U_s,ACP` (tensile arm force). Symbol map: k⁰_u,ACP → FF crosslinker zero-force off-rate; λ_u,ACP → FF Bell reactive compliance (x_β); |F_s,ACP| → FF bond tension magnitude. **Note the branch:** unbinding only accelerates under tension, not compression — FF should replicate this gate.
- **Motor kinetics (PCM, Erdmann–Schwarz refs 20,21):** walking k_w,M and unbinding k_u,M **both decrease with load** on the longitudinal arm spring `F_s,M4 = −∇U_s,M4` → catch-bond. Unbinding assumed ~∞ if F_s,M4 points toward the pointed end (motor detaches if pushed backward). This is a *catch-bond* motor; FF's Hill force-velocity is the F-v envelope but the load-dependent **detachment** (duty-ratio) must come from a PCM-style catch-bond, matching the paper. Unloaded v ≈ 140 nm/s, stall ≈ 5.7 pN, reference k_u,M = 0.049 s⁻¹.
- **Eq 9 (depoly inhibition):** `k_d,A = k⁰_d,A·(1 − ξ_d,A)`, ξ_d,A∈[0,1]. Port as the protection channel (§1c).
- **Force distribution rule:** forces from bound ACPs/motors on a segment are distributed to the segment's barbed and pointed **end nodes** (Kim 2015 ref 16). FF should match this node-distribution to keep parity with the oracle.
- **Microscopic force readouts (validation instrumentation):**
  - Eq 10: `f^max_ACP = F_s,ACP · û` (projection on barbed-end unit vector û; + toward barbed end).
  - Eq 11: `f^max_M = (F_s,M4 · û)/N_h` (per-head motor force = longitudinal spring force / heads-per-arm).
  Sign convention: positive = toward barbed end; a negative f^max_ACP sustains a positive f^max_M and vice-versa.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenario)

**Scenario "network→bundle condensation" (a clean FF contraction test):**
1. **Domain:** 3D thin slab **4 × 8 × 0.5 µm**, periodic BC in **y** only; x- and z-walls exert repulsive confinement. (Thin quasi-2D — good for a cheap FF smoke test but see §6 re native scale.)
2. **Assembly:** self-assemble a homogeneous network — actin (C_A=40 µM, mean length 1.56 µm), motors bound but **not walking**, ACPs cross-linking. Optionally bias nucleation direction to make anisotropic (±x/±y) nets mimicking lamellipodia.
3. **Trigger:** at t=0 start motor walking → watch condensation into a single bundle spanning y.
4. **Sweep axes:** motor density R_M, crosslinker density R_ACP (5×5 grid), filament bending (κ_b,A vs 100×κ_b,A to toggle buckling), initial orientation (isotropic / +x / +y / +x±y), turnover (k_t,A, ξ_d,A).
5. **Measurements:**
   - Macroscopic tension: 10 regularly-spaced y-cross-sections; sum normal component of extensional forces of all constituents crossing each; average over the 10.
   - Compaction metric σ_x = std-dev of actin x-positions; **compaction time** = time when d(σ_x)/dt exceeds 0.01×(mean d(σ_x)/dt during first 5 s).
   - Buckling frequency: count filaments with end-to-end/contour < 0.6 at least once.
   - Micro forces f^max_ACP (Eq 10), f^max_M (Eq 11) at peak tension.
   - Antiparallel-pair fraction vs time (for oriented nets).

This maps almost verbatim onto an FF `--scenario network_bundle` driver; the periodic-y slab is the reconstitution geometry, the R_M×R_ACP grid is the sweep.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **In-vitro reconstitution vs living cortex.** The paper's medium viscosity μ is a purified-assay value; FF's **physiological baseline HARD rule** requires the ~65 Pa·s MCF7 cytoplasm in Eq-3 drag, not water/in-vitro μ. Use the paper's *drag form* (Eq 3) but our *physiological μ*. Do not import their μ.
- **Model-relative densities.** R_M, R_ACP are dimensionless relative densities, not concentrations. Never KB-register them as literature concentrations; use only as sweep ratios. Flag before any Parameter row.
- **Coarse scale ≠ our native scale.** Their r₀,A=140 nm segment + 4×8×0.5 µm thin slab is a small quasi-2D reconstitution. Per the **NATIVE+full-compartment HARD rule**, any FF conclusion must be reconfirmed on the native 38000-filament cortex (Nc≈266k) — the thin-slab bundle run is a **dev smoke test / oracle-parity scenario only, labelled non-authoritative**. The bundle scenario is 3D-thin; the cortex is a closed shell — geometry differs, so treat tension *magnitudes* (nN) as scenario-specific, not a cortical-γ number.
- **ℓ_p conflict (9 vs 17 µm).** Isambert-1995 9 µm here vs FF's 17 µm anchor. This changes the buckling critical force. Keep FF's native ℓ_p; only drop to 9 µm for a parity reproduction of this paper. Surface to PI which is our physiological setpoint (do not silently switch — no gate-fitting).
- **k⁰_u,ACP identity.** 0.115 s⁻¹ is the **filamin-A** off-rate; FF's re-anchored **0.066 s⁻¹** is **α-actinin**. Both from Ferrer 2008. Register as the filamin crosslinker; do not overwrite α-actinin. Mislabeling would be a citation-integrity error.
- **Catch-bond motor vs Hill.** Their motor detach is a PCM *catch-bond* (k_u decreases with load), distinct from a pure Hill F-v curve. FF already mandates the mechanistic multi-head Stam-Hocky minifilament; ensure the **load-dependent detachment** (duty ratio) is catch-bond, not a lumped constant off-rate — otherwise FF won't reproduce the sustainability law.
- **Euler Δt=1.5e-5 s is theirs, not ours.** FF uses constrained BAOAB; use their Δt only as a CFL sanity anchor for the stiff bond springs, not as our step.
- **No fitting.** The R_ACP sustainability lever is a *prediction to test*, not a knob to tune FF's γ to a target. If FF reproduces the law emergently, good; if not, surface the discrepancy — do not adjust k⁰_u,ACP or λ to force it.
