# FF lens — p69

**Paper:** Jung, Murrell & Kim (2015), *F-actin cross-linking enhances the stability of force generation in disordered actomyosin networks*, Comp. Part. Mech. 2:317–327, DOI 10.1007/s40571-015-0052-9.

**One-line relevance:** This is essentially an FF-class model built by the Taeyoon Kim lab — explicit F-actin bending beams + two-armed Bell-slip cross-linkers + bipolar myosin minifilaments with catch-bond walking — run as a *thin cortex-like slab* clamped to a stress sensor. It hands FF (a) a directly portable cross-linker/myosin force-law + parameter set, (b) the single mechanistic result our cortical-γ / stress-relaxation program most needs: **passive cross-linker density and off-rate, not motor count, set whether motor-generated tension is *sustained* vs. dissipated**, and (c) a self-contained validation scenario (σ_max, sustainability S, G'/G'', critical frequency) at exactly our fine-grained scale.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cross-linker (α-actinin/filamin module): make ACP a *transient molecular clutch*, not a permanent bond
The core physical claim (§3.4): a cross-linker is **effective friction** between two F-actins whose magnitude is governed by its zero-force off-rate `k0_u` and by how much force it currently carries. Motors reach their stall force **only if the actin they pull is transiently anchored** by ACPs long enough for force to build by walking; if ACPs release too fast (high `k0_u` or force-accelerated unbinding), actin glides through the motor and no large force accumulates. This is the mechanism that converts "motors present" into "tension sustained."
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF already has Bell-Evans cross-linker off-rate, but this paper elevates it from a bookkeeping detail to *the* control variable for cortical tension sustainability. It predicts a specific, testable emergent behavior — a **sustainability metric S** that FF can measure and that discriminates "stress that holds" from "stress that relaxes." Directly relevant to our open γ-floor / stress-relaxation problems.

### M2 — Myosin: model the minifilament as a *temporary cross-linker* during the loading transient
Motors "accumulate large stress quickly by behaving as temporary cross-linkers" (Abstract, §3.2). Because a bipolar TF with 64 heads spends time bound to two filaments, early stress buildup (t → σ_max, fast) is dominated by motors *acting like extra cross-links*, and only the *long-term* holding (S, slow) is set by passive ACPs. This two-timescale split (fast σ_max generation vs. slow S maintenance) is a mechanism FF can reproduce and use to diagnose which lever controls which regime.
- **Why it beats current FF:** gives FF an explicit account of *why* raising myosin raises peak tension but can *lower* sustained tension (motors overload ACPs → force-accelerated ACP unbinding → remodeling). Our γ program keeps asking "add more myosin?"; this paper says past R_M ≈ 0.06 more motors *destabilize* the network. That is a mechanism, not a fit.

### M3 — Myosin arm kinetics: **catch-bond** walking + unbinding (both rates DECREASE with load)
Motor heads are modeled as catch bonds (refs Veigel 2003, Uemura 2004): `k_w,M` (walking) and `k_u,M` (unbinding) both *decrease* with higher force on the arm — opposite to the ACP slip bond. This is what lets `f_M^max` be predominantly *positive* (toward barbed end) and lets motors dwell under load to reach stall.
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF's myosin currently uses Hill force–velocity + duty ratio. The **explicit load-decreasing unbinding (catch behavior)** is the piece that makes a minifilament a persistent load-bearer rather than detaching under tension. Adopt catch-bond `k_u,M(F)` alongside Hill so the minifilament *holds* isometric tension — a prerequisite for the sustained-γ target.

### M4 — Emergent network remodeling as a readout channel (not a bug)
Force-accelerated ACP unbinding drives **large-scale remodeling**: mesh coarsening + motor aggregation, which *dissipates* the elastic energy motors built. The paper makes these first-class *observables* (motor nearest-neighbor distribution → aggregation; per-column density decay constant → mesh size). FF should instrument the same two metrics so "the cortex is relaxing/coarsening" becomes measurable rather than a visual impression.
- **Why it beats current FF:** turns our qualitative "cortex collapsed" (which the native-scale rule warns is often an artifact) into two quantitative time series that distinguish *mechanistic* remodeling from *structural-instability* artifact.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### (Eq. 1) Cross-linker force-dependent unbinding — Bell slip bond (α-actinin/filamin)
```
k_u,ACP = k0_u,ACP · exp( λ_u,ACP · |F_s,ACP| / (k_B T) )     if r ≥ r0,ACP   (arm stretched)
k_u,ACP = k0_u,ACP                                            if r <  r0,ACP   (arm compressed)
```
Symbol → FF state:
- `F_s,ACP` = extensional (axial) force on the ACP arm spring → FF crosslinker bond tension `|F_bond|`.
- `λ_u,ACP` = force sensitivity (units of length; = x_β/? — here quoted directly as a length) → FF Bell `x_β` / `F_b` mapping: `F_b = k_B T / λ`. With `λ = 1.04e-10 m` and `k_B T = 4.14e-21 J` (300 K), `F_b = k_B T/λ ≈ 39.8 pN`.
- `r0,ACP` = ACP arm rest length; the **compression gate** (`r < r0` → zero-force rate) is a detail FF should copy: only *tensed* cross-links accelerate their unbinding; compressed ones do not. FF likely applies Bell symmetrically today — this asymmetry is a small mechanistic upgrade.

### Myosin arm binding / walking / catch-bond (§2.3)
- Free-arm **binding** rate: `k_bind = 40 · N_h  s⁻¹` = `320 s⁻¹` at `N_h = 8`. Maps to FF myosin-head attachment rate per arm.
- **Walking** step: each event slides the arm to the next binding site toward the barbed end, `Δ ≈ 7 nm`. After the barbed end, next walking event slides the arm *off*. Maps to FF myosin `step = 7 nm`, barbed-directed, with fall-off boundary condition.
- **Catch-bond load dependence** (Fig. S1, functional form deferred to Table S1 / Kim 2015 BMMB ref 26): `k_w,M(F) ↓` and `k_u,M(F) ↓` as arm force ↑. Calibrated so **unloaded velocity ≈ 140 nm/s** and **stall force `f_M^stall ≈ 5.7 pN`** (per head). FF should reproduce these two calibration anchors with its Hill + catch scheme.

### Stress sensor / sustainability protocol (§2.5)
```
σ(t) = E · ε(t)                         E = 3e4 Pa (elastic x-boundary)
σ_max = max_t σ(t)
S = ⟨σ⟩ / σ_max,   ⟨σ⟩ = time-avg of σ from t(σ_max) to t = 100 s
f_M^max   = (F_s,M4 · û) / N_h          (per-head motor force at σ≈σ_max)
f_ACP^max = F_s,ACP · û                 (ACP force at σ≈σ_max; û = axial unit vector toward barbed end)
```
Directly implementable as an FF measurement harness: clamp cortex filaments to an elastic boundary of known E, integrate, read strain → σ(t) → (σ_max, S).

### Viscoelastic moduli (§2.5)
Apply small **5% sinusoidal strain** to +x boundary (−x fixed), sweep frequency 0.1–10³ Hz, extract storage `E'` / loss `E''`, phase delay `δ = tan⁻¹(E''/E')`. Critical frequency = where δ is minimal (network most elastic).

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenario)

**Scenario "cortex-slab stress sensor" (FF-portable):**
1. **Preassembly at physiological operating point:** self-assemble F-actin from `C_A = 25 µM` G-actin in a thin slab 8×8×0.5 µm, PBC in x & y, z-walls purely repulsive → confine to slab. Grow minifilament TFs (nucleate bare zone → symmetric backbone, arms bound but *no walking during assembly*). Bind ACPs to form functional cross-links. Reference: `R_M = 0.014`, `R_ACP = 0.1`, ⟨L_f⟩ ≈ 1.44 µm. **Start from this assembled, cross-linked, pre-tensioned state — never a bare relaxed lattice** (matches our physiological-baseline HARD rule).
2. **Clamp:** deactivate PBC in x; sever filaments crossing x-boundaries and irreversibly clamp their ends to the boundary. Keep PBC in y (infinite network). x-boundaries = elastic substrate, E = 3×10⁴ Pa.
3. **Run 100 s**, turn on myosin walking, read σ(t) = Eε(t).
4. **Sweeps:** R_M (motor density), R_ACP (crosslinker density), k0_u,ACP (off-rate ×0.1–1000). Each produces (σ_max, S) + morphology metrics.
5. **Rheology overlay:** after σ_max, apply 5% sinusoidal strain, sweep 0.1–10³ Hz → E'(ω), E''(ω), δ(ω).
6. **Universality control:** repeat in a 3×3×3 µm cubic domain — result should be invariant (validates that FF's cortex slab and a 3D chunk share the mechanism).

This is a self-contained, single-cell fine-grained scenario. It maps onto FF's cortex module directly (filaments + crosslinkers + minifilaments + clamp boundary). **Caveat vs. our native rule:** this slab is *purified-network scale* (8×8×0.5 µm), not the full MCF7 R=7.5 µm cortex with membrane/nucleus/turgor. Use it as a **calibration/validation sub-scenario** for the crosslinker+myosin force laws, then confirm any *conclusion* on the native full cell.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Purified in-vitro network ≠ living MCF7 cortex.** No membrane, no turgor, no nucleus, no continuous turnover (only the assembled state + 100 s). σ_max ≈ 600 Pa and E ~ 10²–10⁵ Pa are *reconstituted-network* numbers; treat as **cross-check targets for FF's crosslinker/myosin force laws**, not as the cell's cortical tension γ. Our γ is a *line tension* (N/m) from a pressure-borne shell; this paper's σ is a *bulk stress* (Pa) of a clamped slab — do not equate them without the thickness conversion (γ ≈ σ·h, h≈slab thickness). Keep γ generation anchored to the pressure-borne osmoregulation route already resolved (FF_STAGE6V/6T/6Z), and use p69 to validate that *given* a network, motors+ACPs produce the right stress and rheology.
- **2D-ish thin slab vs native 3D full cortex.** The mesh-size metric explicitly ignores z (underestimates absolute mesh; captures relative change only). Paper's own 3D cubic control (Fig S5) shows the mechanism is dimension-robust — good — but reproduce σ_max/S in FF's **native full-compartment** cortex before drawing any conclusion, per the HARD native-scale rule. The slab is a dev/calibration sub-scenario, labelled non-authoritative.
- **Isoform:** k0_u = 0.115 s⁻¹, λ = 1.04e-10 m are **filamin A** (Ferrer 2008), not α-actinin (our current re-anchor 0.066 s⁻¹). Register as a distinct crosslinker isoform; do not overwrite. Native cortex has both — an FF choice, PI-gated.
- **Euler overdamped integrator + timestep deferred.** Their integrator is plain Euler (dt in Table S1 / ref 26, not in this paper). FF uses constrained Leimkuhler–Matthews BAOAB — strictly better; no need to adopt their integrator. Only port the *force laws and rates*, not the numerics.
- **R_M/R_ACP are molar ratios, not densities.** R_M counts all 64 heads/TF. When mapping to FF, convert ratios → absolute number of minifilaments/crosslinkers at MCF7 cortex density (KB-3.18 ~100/µm²); do not import R_M/R_ACP as-is.
- **No fitting.** Every constant is single-molecule-sourced (Ferrer 2008) or geometric; the σ_max ∝ R_M^0.6 exponent is *emergent output*, not an input — so reproducing it is a genuine test, not a tautology.
