# DCM lens — p58

**Paper:** Negi, Sakamoto, Ienaga, Miyazaki, Maeda — "Myosin-Driven Advection and Actin Reorganization Control the Geometry of Confined Actomyosin Gel." *Nano Lett.* 2025, 25, 17979–17987. DOI 10.1021/acs.nanolett.5c02558.

**One-line orientation for DCM.** This is a *continuum active-fluid + phase-field* model of a purified Xenopus-extract actomyosin gel contracting inside a rigid microwell — the geometric inverse of a DCM cell (bulk gel shaped BY a boundary, not a cortex shaping an enclosed volume). It is NOT a parameter source for MCF7 physiology and its Pe / k_p^surf are nondimensional model-internal knobs, not physiological setpoints. Its genuine value to DCM is **one transferable mechanism** (myosin-driven advective redistribution of cortex density → spatially non-uniform surface tension), **one governing-equation template** (a turnover-plus-advection mass balance you can run ON the surface mesh), and **a small set of emergent cross-check numbers** (cortical flow speed ~1.2 µm/s, pulsatory period ~101 s, corner/curvature stress-concentration). Everything else is regime-mismatched. Honest DCM relevance: **moderate-low (~2.5/5)**.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Advection-driven cortical-tension redistribution (the one strong lever)
**DCM module:** actomyosin cortex (`γ` field on the surface mesh).
**Change:** DCM presently carries surface tension γ as a *spatially uniform scalar* (interphase ≈0.15 mN/m, pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2). This paper's central result is that **myosin contractility advects actomyosin density**, and where density piles up, contractile stress piles up — the network self-organizes an inhomogeneous density/tension field (Eqs 1–2, Fig 3). Port this as a **per-face cortex-density field ρ_f** advected by the tangential cortical flow, with a **local tension law γ_f = γ_0 · (ρ_f/ρ_0)** (density-proportional active stress, exactly the paper's σ_act ∝ ρ assumption). γ then becomes a *dynamical surface field*, not a constant.
**Why it beats current DCM:** the known-open problem "cortical-tension γ generation" is currently a magnitude-only floor. This gives γ **spatial structure from an actual transport mechanism** rather than a uniform prescribed value — it lets DCM produce cortical-flow-driven tension gradients (needed for bleb nucleation, cytokinetic-furrow tension asymmetry, and polarity) at the fine-grained, mechanistic altitude the project's hard rule demands. It is the mechanistic upgrade of a lumped constant.

### M2 — First-order turnover as the cortex-density source/sink
**DCM module:** cortex density field (feeds M1).
**Change:** close the ρ_f dynamics with the paper's turnover terms: source k_p (polymerization/nucleation) minus sink k_d·ρ_f (depolymerization), Eq 2. This makes cortex density a *steady-state balance of turnover against advective concentration* instead of a fixed quantity — the physiological picture (actin cortex turns over on ~10–30 s).
**Why:** DCM's cortex is currently a static tension; adding turnover lets the density (hence γ) relax back after a perturbation and reach a *dynamic* steady state, which is what a living cortex does. It is additive and default-off-safe but MUST be initialized at the physiological turnover point, not zero.

### M3 — Curvature / corner stress-concentration → tension concentration at high-curvature mesh regions
**DCM module:** cortex tension + contact/confinement response.
**Observation to adopt (Discussion, p.7):** at sharp boundary features (corners, flat-to-curved transitions) the myosin force — always ~normal to the local surface — changes direction abruptly, causing **actin accumulation and small outgrowths at corners**. Mechanistically this is density advection focusing where surface curvature is discontinuous. In DCM under confinement or cell–cell contact, the M1 advection field will *automatically* concentrate ρ_f (hence γ_f) at high-|H| mesh regions — no extra rule needed. Flag this as an **expected emergent signature** to look for and validate, not a new force.
**Why:** gives DCM a mechanistic prediction for where cortex thickens under confinement/contact, relevant to confinement-response and aggregate-corner behavior.

### M4 — Pulsatory (oscillatory) cortical flow regime
**DCM module:** cortex dynamics.
**Change:** the paper's model is built on Kumar–Bois–Jülicher–Grill pulsatory active fluids (ref 31) and observes **periodic inward actin waves (T ≈ 101 s)**. Once M1+M2 are on, DCM's coupled advection–turnover cortex can exhibit the same pulsatory instability (contraction concentrates ρ → higher stress → faster contraction → depletion → relaxation). This is the mechanistic origin of cortical pulses/oscillations DCM currently cannot produce.
**Why:** unlocks pulsatile-cortex phenomenology (relevant to division-ring pulsing, bleb cycling) as an *emergent* outcome, not scripted.

> **Not adoptable:** the phase-field boundary ϕ (Eq 3) — DCM already represents the boundary explicitly as the triangulated mesh; the phase field is a continuum crutch DCM does not need. The rigid-well confinement is a *scenario* (§4), not a mechanism.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

**Target representation:** cortex-density scalar ρ_f defined per face (or per node), advected by tangential cortical velocity **u** (the in-surface component of nodal velocity), with turnover. This is the paper's Eq 2 restricted to the 2-manifold surface.

**(Eq 2, ported) — cortex-density advection–turnover on the mesh:**
```
∂ρ/∂t + ∇_s·(ρ u)  =  D_s ∇_s²ρ  +  k_p  −  k_d ρ
```
- `ρ` → per-face cortex areal density `ρ_f` (kg·m⁻² or filaments·µm⁻²), replaces the implicit uniform cortex mass behind γ.
- `∇_s·`, `∇_s²` → surface divergence / Laplace–Beltrami on the DCM mesh (already have mesh operators for κ_m bending).
- `u` → tangential projection of nodal velocity (in-surface cortical flow). In DCM this is the surface-drift field; the advective term `∇_s·(ρ u)` is exactly the "myosin-driven advection" of the title.
- `D_s` → cortex surface-diffusion constant (small; sets flow-vs-diffusion competition = the paper's Pe).
- `k_p` → polymerization/nucleation source (per-face; can be surface-uniform for a cell, unlike the well's k_p^surf>k_p^bulk split which is a confinement artifact).
- `k_d ρ` → first-order depolymerization sink; `1/k_d` = cortex turnover time (physiological ~10–30 s).

**(Eq 1, ported) — density-dependent active surface stress:**
```
γ_f = γ_0 · (ρ_f / ρ_0)          [local cortical tension from local density]
σ_act ∝ ρ_f  (myosin density ∝ F-actin density, paper's closure)
```
- `γ_0`, `ρ_0` → physiological reference tension (0.15 mN/m interphase) at reference density ρ_0. This makes the existing DCM γ the ρ_f=ρ_0 special case — **backward-compatible**: uniform ρ ⇒ uniform γ ⇒ today's model.
- The paper's `λ = 1 + η_b/η = 1/3` (bulk/shear viscosity ratio, ref 32) governs the flow field's compressibility; if DCM adds cortex surface viscosity this ratio is the closure — but DCM's flow **u** comes from the mesh force balance, so Eq 1's full Stokes form is replaced by DCM's node dynamics. Do NOT port Eq 1 verbatim; only port its stress→density closure.

**Dimensionless group to preserve (design check, not a magic number):**
```
Pe = (ζΔμ)_0 / (D γ)          advective/diffusive time-scale ratio
```
For DCM this becomes `Pe_DCM = |u|·L / D_s` with `L` a cortex length scale — a *derived* diagnostic of whether advection (M1) dominates diffusion, NOT a tunable. Use it to confirm the mesh run sits in the advection-dominated regime the biology occupies (Pe~50 in the paper).

**Do NOT port:** Eq 3 (phase field) — DCM's mesh is the boundary.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce (DCM scenarios)

**P1 — Cell-sized rigid confinement response.** Place a DCM cell (R≈7.5 µm) inside a rigid confining wall (sphere, then non-spherical: hemisphere, square/box, wedge) as a fixed contact boundary; relax from the physiological resting checkpoint. Reproduce the paper's headline: **boundary asymmetry transfers into cortex/shape asymmetry** via M1 advection. Observables: cortex-density map, γ_f map, roundness R=m/M of the deformed cell silhouette. This is directly usable as a DCM **confinement-response** validation scenario the project lists as open.

**P2 — Corner/curvature stress test.** Confine in a square/wedge box; check M3 — does ρ_f (hence γ_f) concentrate at corners with small outgrowths? Curvature-discontinuity → tension-concentration is the mechanistic prediction.

**P3 — Contractility vs turnover sweep (mechanistic, not fit).** Vary myosin activity (raises effective advection, paper's Pe/CalA analogue) and turnover k_p (polymerization, paper's CytoD↓ / VCA↑ analogue) and confirm the *qualitative laws*: **↑contractility ⇒ tighter/smaller high-density core; ↑polymerization ⇒ larger, rounder-vs-shaped response.** These map onto DCM myosin and cortex-turnover config, giving a mechanistic-perturbation test battery.

**P4 — Pulsatory-cortex protocol.** Run M1+M2 to steady state and watch for the inward-wave / pulsation instability (M4); measure period and flow speed against §5.

> **Aggregate/compaction relevance (weak):** the paper's "area set by well *volume*, not shape" (Fig 2) is a continuum-gel result — it does NOT transfer to DCM's cadherin-junction compaction problem (different physics). Do not cite it for the loose→compact aggregate RATE question.

## 6. RISKS / caveats (regime mismatch and how to adapt)

1. **Purified extract ≠ living MCF7.** Xenopus egg-extract actomyosin is a reconstituted bulk gel with **excess free G-actin and no monomer conservation** (explicit in the paper, p.5). A living MCF7 cortex is monomer-limited and membrane-anchored. **Adapt:** when porting Eq 2's turnover, impose the physiological cortex density ρ_0 as the steady-state setpoint (k_p = k_d·ρ_0), i.e. initialize at the physiological operating point — do NOT copy the paper's unbounded-source assumption.

2. **Bulk contractile gel ≠ thin surface cortex.** The paper's cluster is a 3D bulk gel that collapses to the well center; a DCM cortex is a *thin shell on a closed surface enclosing turgor pressure*. The "collapse to a dense cluster" does NOT happen in DCM (turgor + enclosed-volume constraint resist it). **Adapt:** port only the *tangential* advection–turnover on the shell (M1/M2); the enclosed-volume constraint and Π₀≈40 Pa stay ON as the physiological baseline. Never let the ported density dynamics override the volume constraint.

3. **2D model, 3D reality (paper's own caveat, p.7).** The phase-field model is 2D and produces the cluster instantly; real accumulation is a slow 3D upward+lateral flow. DCM is already 3D — this is an *advantage*: DCM can capture the 3D flow the paper's model cannot. But the paper's *quantitative* shape numbers come from the 2D model, so treat them as qualitative for DCM.

4. **Rigid external well ≠ cell-cell / free cell.** The confinement is a hard passive wall. Using P1 as a DCM scenario is fine (rigid contact BC), but do NOT conflate "wall shapes gel" with DCM's cadherin-junction cell-cell mechanics — different interface physics. The compaction-RATE open problem is untouched by this paper.

5. **Dimensionless knobs are not physiology.** Pe=50, k_p^surf=1.5, λ=1/3 are model calibration, not measured cell constants. Porting them as-is would violate the no-magic-number / physiological-baseline rules. Only the *measured emergent* numbers (§5) and the *mechanistic structure* (§1–2) transfer; the fit parameters do not.

6. **No explicit crosslinker/motor bond kinetics.** This is a continuum model — no Bell/catch-bond, no explicit motor heads. It is the OPPOSITE altitude from DCM's fine-grained-mechanistic hard rule at the molecular level, but it operates at the *surface-continuum* altitude which is exactly where DCM's cortex tension already lives. So M1/M2 are altitude-appropriate for the DCM cortex field specifically (not for FF filament-level).

### Bottom line for DCM
Adopt **M1 (advective γ redistribution) + M2 (turnover source/sink)** as an additive, backward-compatible upgrade turning DCM's uniform cortex tension into a mechanistic density-transport field; use **P1–P2** as a new confinement-response validation scenario; cross-check the emergent cortex against **flow ≈1.2 µm/s** and **pulse period ≈101 s**. Keep all physiological baselines (turgor, enclosed volume, γ_0, ρ_0 from KB) ON and derive k_p/D_s rather than tuning them. Skip everything phase-field, bulk-collapse, and dimensionless-knob.
