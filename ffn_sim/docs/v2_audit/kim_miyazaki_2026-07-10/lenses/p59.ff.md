# FF lens — p59

**Paper:** Miyazaki, Chiba, Eguchi, Ohki, Ishiwata (2015) *Cell-sized spherical confinement induces the spontaneous formation of contractile actomyosin rings in vitro.* Nature Cell Biology 17(4):480–489. DOI 10.1038/ncb3142.

**One-line for FF:** A canonical bottom-up actin+myosin+crosslinker reconstitution *at exactly our scale* (cell-sized spherical confinement, R_droplet≈2.4–10 µm vs our MCF7 R=7.5 µm) whose central emergent result — a single equatorial contractile ring self-organizing from confined semiflexible filaments, contracting at constant volume with velocity ∝ perimeter — is a **pure emergence benchmark** for FF's Cytosim beams + Bell-Evans crosslinkers + duty-ratio myosin. Nothing here is a lumped law we adopt as a runtime mechanism; almost everything is a *validation target* or a *parameter anchor* that our fine-grained engine should reproduce from first principles.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### 1.1 Filament (bending beam) — confinement-driven equatorial localization is an *emergence test*, not a new mechanism
FF already has the right ingredients: semiflexible actin beam with bending κ = k_B·T·ℓ_p, excluded volume, membrane containment. The paper's result — a filament with ℓ_p ≳ R_confine behaves as an **elastic rod** and localizes at the droplet **equator (largest great circle)** to minimize bending energy ∫(κ/2)(1/R)²ds — is precisely what FF's beam+containment physics should produce *spontaneously* with NO new code. This is the strongest use of this paper: run FF cortex filaments inside a rigid spherical shell of radius R and check that long filaments (ℓ_p≈17 µm ≫ R=7.5 µm) preferentially sit at the equatorial plane. If FF does NOT reproduce equatorial localization, our bending stiffness or containment coupling is wrong. **Why it matters:** this is a clean, parameter-free check that our ℓ_p and beam discretization actually encode semiflexible-rod elasticity at the R∼ℓ_p regime the cortex lives in.

### 1.2 Crosslinker — depletion (Asakura–Oosawa) bundling as an explicit attractive channel
The paper bundles actin with **methylcellulose depletion** (0.375% w/v, 1500 cP) OR the passive crosslinker **α-actinin**. FF currently has explicit α-actinin/filamin bonds but (likely) no depletion-attraction term. Two options, both mechanistic:
- **(preferred, mechanistic-consistent)** keep bundling via explicit α-actinin catch/slip bonds (already in FF) — the paper shows α-actinin alone *can* substitute for methylcellulose to make rings (R*=10 µm without MC). This is the FF-native route.
- **(optional)** add an explicit short-range Asakura–Oosawa depletion potential between filament segments when modeling the crowded cytoplasm — a genuine physical force (excluded-volume/entropy), not a lumped proxy. Only if we ever want to model crowding-driven bundling directly.
**Why it beats current FF:** the paper demonstrates the *balance* between passive crosslink bundling and myosin remodeling controls ring assembly — too much α-actinin (rigor-like) kills assembly, added HMM recovers it (Fig 6a,b). This is a fine-grained crosslinker↔motor competition FF can reproduce and should, because it constrains our α-actinin bond density vs myosin ratio.

### 1.3 Myosin — "active diffusion" requires TWO heads bound + turnover (rejects single-head / rigor proxies)
Hard mechanistic constraint the paper establishes: **single-headed S-1 does NOT promote assembly; rigor/ADP HMM (no turnover) does NOT; only double-headed, ATP-turning-over HMM/myosin-V does.** Ring formation needs a motor that (a) binds two antiparallel filaments simultaneously and (b) cycles. This directly validates FF's choice of the **Stam–Hocky multi-head bipolar minifilament** with duty cycle over any single-spring or rigor-crosslink motor proxy. **Actionable:** FF's myosin binding update must require ≥2 heads engaged on distinct filaments before it can exert relative sliding — the paper is the experimental warrant that a 2-head requirement is not an implementation detail but the mechanism. If FF ever falls back to a 1-head slip proxy for speed, this paper says that proxy will fail to produce contraction.

### 1.4 Myosin — stepwise increase in *effective* motor density as an ATP-depletion-driven trigger
The contraction cascade (form → slow/weak → fast/strong → disassemble) is triggered by a **spontaneous stepwise rise in the fraction of bound heads** as ATP depletes (ADP accumulation raises duty ratio). FF models ATP implicitly. We do NOT need to adopt ATP-depletion as a runtime driver (that's a reconstitution artifact of a closed droplet), BUT the *lesson* is mechanistic: **contraction onset is a percolation/connectivity threshold in bound-motor density**, not a smooth ramp. FF should exhibit a threshold in myosin-engaged density below which the network only remodels ("active diffusion") and above which it contracts. This is a check on FF's motor duty-ratio × density coupling.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### 2.1 Effective-motor-concentration law (the central unifying equation)
```
[myosin]_eff = [myosin]_bulk × (duty ratio)²
```
- **Meaning:** only motors with BOTH heads bound simultaneously can translocate two adjacent filaments; P(both bound) ∝ r², where r = single-head duty ratio.
- **FF mapping:** in FF, `[myosin]_bulk` → minifilament number density along the cortex; `duty ratio r` → per-head fraction-of-cycle-strongly-bound already in the Hill/duty model. The *effective* number of load-bearing cross-links per minifilament ∝ (n_heads choose 2)·r² for an n-head minifilament. This is a **prediction FF should reproduce**, not a law to hardcode: with FF's explicit heads and per-head duty cycle, the (duty ratio)² scaling must *emerge*. Concretely: myosin V (r≈0.5–0.7) promotes rings at ~100× lower concentration than myosin II HMM (r≈0.04–0.05), matching (0.5/0.05)²=100. FF minifilaments with per-head r should reproduce this 100× offset when we vary duty ratio — a direct motor-model validation.

### 2.2 Contraction velocity ∝ perimeter, with a filament-length-set critical unit
```
(contraction rate) ∝ (P_initial − P*),   P* ≈ 4.5 µm ≈ mean filament length (5.4 µm)
rate per unit perimeter ≈ 0.39 µm·min⁻¹  (main) / 0.31 (SI)
```
- **FF mapping:** the contractile unit is set by filament length ℓ_fil — a ring shorter than one filament cannot contract (no antiparallel overlap to slide). In FF this maps to: net contractile strain rate ∝ (number of antiparallel overlaps around the loop) ∝ (P − ℓ_fil). **This is an emergent observable**, not a force law to insert. FF with distributed filament lengths (exponential 1–10 µm, mean ~5 µm) should reproduce P*≈mean-length and a per-perimeter rate near 0.39 µm/min.

### 2.3 Confinement / elastic-rod criterion
```
ring assembly succeeds iff  R_confine ≲ ℓ_p
critical droplet diameter R* ≈ ℓ_p ≈ 15 µm (spherical), ~20 µm (quasi-2D compressed)
```
- **FF mapping:** compares directly to our MCF7 R=7.5 µm and actin ℓ_p≈17 µm → R < ℓ_p, so FF cortex filaments are firmly in the "elastic rod, equatorial-localizing" regime. Encapsulated as a scenario: sweep shell radius R and confirm ring/equatorial-localization probability drops toward zero as R→ℓ_p.

### 2.4 Ring-volume conservation during contraction (constant-actin-mass constraint)
```
V ≡ π·R_ring · (π·d²/4) = const     (R_ring shrinks ⇒ bundle width d thickens)
```
- **FF mapping:** contraction WITHOUT depolymerization — the paper shows contraction is phalloidin-insensitive (excess phalloidin does not change rate). So in FF, a pure motor-sliding contraction (no filament turnover) should conserve total filament mass: as the loop shrinks it must thicken. Good invariant check for FF's contraction: measure ∫(density) around the ring stays constant.

### 2.5 Orientation-angle observable
```
θ = sin⁻¹(h_ring / R_ring)      (h_ring = Z-span of ring; equatorial ⇒ θ≈0)
```
- FF diagnostic for equatorial-localization scenario.

### 2.6 ATPase turnover rates (for closing the myosin energy budget)
```
ATPase ≈ 4.82 µM·s⁻¹ (0.48 s⁻¹/head) @ 5 µM HMM ;  7.89 µM·s⁻¹ (0.16 s⁻¹/head) @ 25 µM HMM
```
- Per-head cycling rate 0.16–0.48 s⁻¹ under contraction load — a sanity anchor for FF's myosin cycle rate (turnover-per-head) in the strongly-bound-fraction regime. Note this is the *loaded/ATP-limited* rate, not v_max.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenario spec)

**Scenario "confinement-ring" (new FF validation scenario, single-cell scale):**
- **Geometry:** rigid or membrane spherical shell, radius R swept over {2.5, 5, 7.5, 10, 15} µm. Purely geometric/steric boundary (paper: no chemical templating; actin & HMM do not bind the water/oil interface). FF: use excluded-volume containment only, no adhesive boundary.
- **Fill:** actin filaments polymerized *inside* from monomer/seed (paper: polymerization must start after encapsulation — pre-polymerized actin gives <10% rings). FF analog: nucleate/grow filaments from within the confined volume, not inject a pre-formed network. This tests barbed-end growth + confinement together.
- **Bundling:** α-actinin explicit crosslinkers (FF-native) OR a depletion term; balance density against myosin.
- **Motor:** bipolar myosin minifilaments (Stam–Hocky), duty-ratio r≈0.05, density swept 0→optimum.
- **Compressed variant:** quasi-2D slab confinement (gap ~4.4 µm) → ring should reorient into the minimal-curvature plane (parallel to plates). Good anisotropy check for FF containment.
- **Readouts:** ring-formation probability vs R; R_ring/R_droplet; orientation angle θ; contraction rate vs perimeter; volume conservation.

**Why valuable:** this is a *self-organization* scenario — no template, no pre-imposed ring. It stresses FF's emergent behavior (does a single equatorial contractile bundle form?) rather than a prescribed-geometry relaxation. It is at our exact length scale (cell-sized), unlike bulk rheology papers. Directly relevant to the open FF problems: **buckling/condensation symmetry-breaking** and **confinement-driven self-organization**, and secondarily **network contraction**.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for FF observables

Ranked by how cleanly FF can hit them:

1. **Equatorial localization of long filaments** — a filament with ℓ_p ≫ R sits at the largest great circle. Parameter-free; if FF fails this, bending stiffness or containment is wrong. (Fig 2c,f)
2. **R_ring/R_droplet = 0.96–0.97** — the emergent ring hugs the inner cortex. (Fig 2c)
3. **Confinement threshold R* ≈ ℓ_p ≈ 15 µm** — ring-formation probability → 0 as R→ℓ_p; sweep R and reproduce the monotonic falloff. (Fig 2d)
4. **Contraction rate ≈ 0.39 µm·min⁻¹ per µm perimeter**, linear in (P − P*). "Similar to cytokinetic rings in cells" — so this doubles as a *living-cortex* anchor, not just in-vitro. (Fig 4f)
5. **Critical perimeter P* ≈ 4.5 µm ≈ mean filament length 5.4 µm** — emergent from FF's length distribution. (Fig 4f)
6. **Constant-volume contraction** V=πR_ring·πd²/4 = const, phalloidin-insensitive (no depolymerization) — FF motor-only contraction must conserve filament mass and thicken the bundle as it shrinks. (Fig 4e)
7. **Duty-ratio² concentration scaling** — myoV vs myoII ring-promotion offset = (0.5/0.05)² ≈ 100×; collapse onto one curve when plotted vs [myosin]×r². A clean FF motor-model check if we ever instantiate a high-duty motor. (Fig 5)
8. **Two-head + turnover requirement** — single-head (S-1) and rigor/ADP motors produce NO assembly/contraction (P>0.1 vs control). FF: disabling the 2nd head or freezing the cycle must abolish contraction. (Fig 3a)
9. **Orientation flip under compression** — ring goes parallel to plates (minimal-curvature plane) in quasi-2D. (Fig 2g)

All DOI-anchored (10.1038/ncb3142), experimentally measured → high-confidence for KB registration as ValidationGate/Parameter rows.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Purified in-vitro vs living cortex (regime gap).** Rabbit skeletal-muscle actin/myosin-II HMM in a bare droplet at 25 °C, µM concentrations, methylcellulose crowding. Our cortex: MCF7 non-muscle myosin IIA, 37 °C, ~100 filaments/µm², membrane+turgor+nucleus. Do NOT import the paper's absolute µM concentrations as FF densities — our cortex operates at a different (denser, membrane-bound) point. **Adopt ratios/scalings/thresholds, not absolute concentrations.** Keeps the physiological-baseline rule intact (we stay at our 100/µm², 37 °C setpoint).
- **HMM ≠ bipolar filament.** The paper mostly uses HMM (soluble double-headed fragment) as a minimal 2-head crosslinker; only 2 µM full-length filament myosin drives *complete* contraction (R→0). FF already uses full bipolar minifilaments (the more physiological choice) — so FF should, if anything, contract *more* readily than HMM. Compare FF to the **full-length-myosin** result (complete contraction, 44% of rings), not the HMM-only weak-contraction numbers, when benchmarking our minifilament.
- **ATP-depletion driver is a closed-droplet artifact.** The stepwise [myosin]_eff rise comes from ATP running out in a sealed droplet. Our cortex has ATP-regeneration (physiological). Do NOT model ATP depletion as the contraction trigger; instead treat the *bound-fraction threshold* it reveals as the transferable physics. Adopting the depletion ramp would violate physiological-baseline (we'd be starting from an unphysical energy-limited state).
- **Muscle actin ℓ_p range (5–18 µm) vs our 17 µm.** The paper's cited range is broad and consistent with ours; the low end (5 µm) reflects labeling/measurement scatter, not a reason to lower FF's ℓ_p. Keep ℓ_p≈17 µm (Gittes).
- **2.5 h assembly, minutes contraction — timescale.** Assembly took hours in-vitro (diffusion-limited, dilute). FF at native scale should not be expected to match the *absolute* assembly time (different density/growth kinetics); the transferable target is the *contraction rate per perimeter* (0.39 µm/min) and the *scalings*, which the authors explicitly note match living cytokinetic rings.
- **No explicit force field in the paper.** All "laws" are scalings/definitions; there is no Hill/Bell/off-rate form to transcribe. So nothing here overrides FF's existing Bell-Evans/Hill mechanisms — this paper is an *emergence benchmark + parameter anchor*, not a mechanism replacement. Its highest value to FF is as a self-organization validation scenario at our exact confinement scale.
