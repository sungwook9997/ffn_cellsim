# DCM lens — p68

**Paper:** Li, Szymanski & Kim (2023), "Probing stress-regulated ordering of the plant cortical microtubule array via a computational approach," *BMC Plant Biology* 23:308. DOI 10.1186/s12870-023-04252-5. Taeyoon Kim lab (agent-based cytoskeleton).

**One-line fit for DCM.** This is a 2D agent-based *plant* cortical-microtubule ordering model — no membrane mesh, no turgor mechanics, stress is *imported* not computed. It is **weak/indirect** for DCM as a surface-mesh cell engine. BUT it carries one transferable idea worth extracting cleanly and one parameter set worth registering: (a) a **stress→cytoskeleton feedback law** (wall normal stress projected onto filament orientation biases plus-end dynamics → emergent nematic order along principal stress), which is the conceptual template DCM lacks for making its cortex/MT compartment *anisotropic and stress-responsive* rather than isotropic-γ; and (b) three-state MT dynamic-instability rate constants for DCM's `--microtubules` compartment. Everything here must be adapted across a large regime gap (plant, 2D, 3–15 MPa walls, imported stress) — see Risks.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### 1a. Stress-projection → anisotropic cortex/MT remodeling (the core transferable lever)
- **DCM target module:** cortex surface-tension field γ (per-face) and/or the microtubule compartment (`--microtubules`), specifically the direction/anisotropy of cortical reinforcement.
- **Change:** DCM currently carries γ as an (effectively) isotropic scalar per face (interphase ≈0.15, mitotic ≈1.5 mN/m). This paper's central mechanism is: resolve the local in-plane stress tensor onto each filament's orientation (Eq 1), and let that projected normal stress **bias filament plus-end kinetics** (↑polymerization / ↓catastrophe under tension) so filaments preferentially survive and grow *along the maximum-principal-stress direction*. The emergent result is a nematic array aligned to principal stress with order parameter S_p ≈ 0.6–0.85.
- **Why it beats what DCM does now:** DCM's cortex has no mechanism by which membrane/cortex load *directionally* reorganizes the reinforcing network — anisotropic remodeling (e.g. cortex stiffening along a stretch axis, MT alignment under confinement) is currently absent. Since DCM *already computes* the per-face membrane stress tensor (from K_A area elasticity, κ_m bending, and turgor Π₀), it has exactly the input this feedback needs — unlike the paper, which had to import stress from an external FE model. This closes a real gap: stress-guided cortical anisotropy is the mechanistic ingredient behind confinement response, durotaxis-like polarization, and cortex reinforcement.
- **Fine-grained caveat (hard rule):** the *fine-grained* version lives in the **FF** engine (explicit filaments with real plus-end catch/kinetics). At the DCM surface-mesh level this must be a **continuum coarse-graining** of that feedback — a per-face nematic order tensor Q evolving toward the local principal-stress direction — not a lumped fudge. Adopt it in DCM only as the mesh-scale image of the FF mechanism, gated against FF.

### 1b. Two-regime, angle-thresholded filament interaction (informs remesh/bundling, not core)
- **DCM target:** remesh / cortex crosslink representation.
- **Change:** the collision rule (contact angle <40° → zippering/bundling with reorientation; >40° → catastrophe/crossover, P_cat 0.2–0.8) is a compact model of how encounter angle sets bundle vs. sever. Low priority for DCM (it lives at FF/filament scale) but noted as the microstructural origin of the anisotropy that DCM would represent as a Q-tensor.

### 1c. Transient homogenization during reorientation (a dynamical signature to preserve)
- **DCM target:** any stress-guided cortical-anisotropy dynamics we add.
- **Observation to reproduce:** when the principal-stress direction rotates, the array does not smoothly rotate — it **disassembles (density ~halves, S_p → 0) then re-forms** along the new axis over ~50 min. Any DCM Q-tensor relaxation law should exhibit this order-parameter dip through zero, not a rigid rotation, or it is unphysical.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (to the surface-mesh / continuum level)

### Eq (1) — orientation-projected normal stress (DIRECTLY portable to DCM faces)
```
σ_MT(θ) = (σ_x + σ_y)/2  +  (σ_x − σ_y)/2 · cos(2θ)
```
- **As written:** normal stress on a filament at angle θ to +x, given in-plane principal normal stresses σ_x, σ_y (shear neglected).
- **DCM mapping:** for each surface face *f*, DCM already has (or can assemble) the in-plane 2×2 membrane stress tensor in the face's tangent frame; diagonalize to (σ₁, σ₂) with principal direction e₁. Replace (σ_x, σ_y) → (σ₁, σ₂) and θ → angle of a candidate reinforcement direction relative to e₁. This is the standard Mohr's-circle transform — DCM can compute it per-face from K_A/κ_m/Π₀ stresses **without importing an FE field** (advantage over the paper). σ_MT is then the scalar that drives the feedback in §2/Eq(3).

### Eq (3) — linear constitutive feedback (port as a continuum order-tensor evolution)
- **Paper form (verbal):** each plus-end parameter is a *linear* function of σ_MT; increasing tensile σ_MT ⇒ ↑growth rate, ↑rescue, ↓depolymerization, ↓catastrophe. Authors note the true relation (Akiyoshi 2010) is closer to exponential; they use linear as an approximation.
- **DCM-level port (recommended):** do NOT port per-filament rates into DCM (that is FF's job). Instead define a per-face nematic order tensor **Q_f** and relax it toward the principal-stress direction with a stress-set anisotropy:
  ```
  dQ_f/dt = (1/τ_Q) [ S_target(Δσ) · (e₁⊗e₁ − ½I)  −  Q_f ]
  ```
  where Δσ = (σ₁−σ₂)/(σ₁+σ₂) is the local stress anisotropy, e₁ the principal direction, τ_Q the reorientation time (set from the paper's ~50 min, or its underlying MT lifetime ~2–3 min at FF scale), and S_target(Δσ) a monotone map calibrated so S_target→0.4–0.5 at Δσ≈0 (isotropic floor) and →0.6–0.85 at strong anisotropy. **Magic-number guard:** S_target must be *derived* from the FF filament model's steady-state order under the same stress, not tuned to a DCM gate. Q_f then modulates cortical reinforcement direction (e.g. anisotropic γ or an added in-plane anisotropic membrane stiffness).
- **Symbol map:** σ₁,σ₂ ← DCM face membrane stresses (from K_A, κ_m, Π₀); e₁ ← principal-stress eigenvector; τ_Q ← reorientation time; Q_f ← new per-face state; S_target ← FF-calibrated order function.

### Eq (2) — nematic order parameter S_p (port as a DCM *observable*, not a force)
```
S_p = Σ_i l_i cos[2(θ_i − θ_p)] / Σ_i l_i
```
- **DCM use:** validation/reporting metric. On a DCM surface, define S_p per region as the largest eigenvalue of the length-weighted Q-tensor of whatever reinforcement/MT field DCM carries; report it against the paper's ranges. Also usable as the emergent-anisotropy readout when validating §1a.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce (as DCM scenarios)

- **Anisotropic-stress alignment test:** impose a controlled principal-stress ratio (10:1 demo, 2:1 physiological) on a patch and measure the steady S_p of the reinforcement/MT field. DCM analog: apply an anisotropic membrane stretch (or asymmetric confinement) to a cell region and check that the added Q-tensor field aligns to the stretch axis with S_p in the paper's band. Directly tests §1a.
- **Reorientation protocol:** rotate the principal-stress direction 90°, either instantaneously (at t=100 min) or gradually (over 50/100/150 min), and track S_p,y(t) from +1→−1. DCM analog: rotate the applied anisotropy and confirm the ~50 min reorientation with a transient S_p→0 homogenization (density dip ~½). Tests §1c.
- **Spatial stress-gradient + isotropic apical zone:** 30×10 µm domain, stress magnitude decreasing along the long axis + a 2 µm isotropic tip zone → produces a depletion zone at the tip, a dense transverse collar below, declining density toward the base. DCM analog is more of a *pattern-formation* demo than a single-cell scenario; lower priority, but relevant if DCM ever models a polarized/elongated cell where cortex density should track a stress gradient.
- **Confinement adjacency:** the "stress sets cortex anisotropy" template is the mechanism DCM would want for its **confinement-response** open problem (cortex reinforcing along the confinement-induced stretch). Reuse §1a there.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Kingdom / wall regime mismatch (largest).** This is a *plant* cell with a stiff cellulose wall at 3–15 MPa tensile stress; MTs are membrane-tethered and confined to 2D by that wall. MCF7 has no wall, a soft actomyosin cortex, and cortical stresses in the Pa–kPa range (γ≈0.15–1.5 mN/m over R≈7.5 µm ⇒ ΔP≈few tens of Pa). **Do not port the 3–15 MPa magnitude or the wall-tethering-to-2D assumption.** Adapt by using the *dimensionless* stress-anisotropy ratio Δσ and the *form* of the feedback, calibrated to DCM's own physiological membrane stresses — never the absolute plant values.
- **Cytoskeleton mismatch.** These are microtubules (plant cortical array), not the actomyosin cortex that carries DCM's γ. The stress-feedback *template* generalizes, but if DCM applies it to the actin cortex the rate constants (§3) do not transfer — they belong only to DCM's MT compartment. Keep the two uses separate.
- **Imported vs computed stress.** The paper imports a static FE stress field; it does not close the mechanical loop (no feedback of MT order back onto wall stress within the model). DCM *does* compute stress and would close the loop (Q-tensor → anisotropic stiffness → altered stress). That is an *improvement* but also a stability risk: the two-way coupling can run away. Guard with the physical τ_Q relaxation and the isotropic S_p floor; validate against FF, not against a tuned gate.
- **Linear constitutive approximation.** Authors deliberately linearize a relation that is really ~exponential (Akiyoshi 2010). For DCM's continuum port, do not enshrine linearity as physics — derive S_target(Δσ) from the FF filament model's actual steady-state order-vs-stress curve so the DCM law inherits the correct (likely saturating/exponential) shape. **Magic-number rule:** the map must be grid-invariant and FF-derived, not fitted.
- **2D vs 3D.** The whole model is 2D (justified by wall confinement). DCM is a 3D closed surface. The stress-projection (Eq 1) generalizes to 3D as the tangent-plane 2×2 restriction per face, which is fine, but "principal stress direction" on a curved surface must be handled per-face in the local tangent frame — a nontrivial but standard extension.
- **Physiological-baseline compliance.** Any DCM adoption must start from the resting turgor-pressurized cell (Π₀ ON, γ at its physiological value) and let the anisotropy field emerge from the *already-physiological* stress state — not from a zero-stress bag. The paper's own baseline (physiological FE stress) is consistent with this in spirit; the failure mode to avoid is initializing DCM's stress at zero and asking the feedback to bootstrap order.
- **Net priority.** For DCM this is a **secondary conceptual donor**: one portable equation (Eq 1), one adaptable feedback template (→ per-face Q-tensor), and a plant-MT parameter set for the `--microtubules` compartment. It does not touch DCM's higher-priority open problems (compaction RATE / T1 rearrangement, division ring, membrane–cortex coupling) except tangentially via confinement-response anisotropy.
