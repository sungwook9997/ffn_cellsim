# FF lens — p42

**Paper:** Jung, Murrell, Kim (2016) "F-Actin Fragmentation Induces Distinct Mechanisms of Stress Relaxation in the Actin Cytoskeleton," *ACS Macro Lett.* 5, 641–645. DOI 10.1021/acsmacrolett.6b00232.

**One-line for FF:** This is the canonical Taeyoon-Kim agent-based actin-network model — architecturally the SAME family as FF (explicit F-actins as bending beams + explicit ACP cross-linkers with Bell-type force-dependent off-rates + Euler buckling). It adds one mechanism FF does NOT currently have: **curvature/buckling-triggered stochastic F-actin severing (fragmentation)**, and shows it is the dominant, orthogonal source of *cyclic* stress relaxation — distinct from ACP unbinding. This is the direct reference to add a mechanistic severing/turnover channel to FF and to validate FF's network rheology against a like-for-like model.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### (A) F-actin severing via buckling-induced curvature — the headline lever [NEW MODULE for FF]
- **FF module:** `filament` / `turnover` (new fragmentation channel on the bending beam).
- **Change:** Add a per-segment stochastic scission event whose rate rises with the local F-actin **bending angle / curvature**. A segment bond is removed **irreversibly** (unlike an ACP unbind, which can rebind). This splits one filament into two shorter filaments, updating the topology (new barbed/pointed ends → become substrates for the existing growth/turnover kernels).
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF today has NO severing. Filament removal/turnover is either absent or lumped. Severing is a *fine-grained mechanistic* process (matches the HARD rule) that (i) generates **cyclic / large-strain stress relaxation** that ACP unbinding alone cannot produce, (ii) creates new filament ends that feed formin/Arp2/3/growth kinetics, and (iii) is the physical mechanism behind cortical **stress relaxation, turnover, and fluidization** — one of FF's listed open problems (network contraction & stress relaxation; filament turnover/fragmentation). The paper proves severing and ACP unbinding are **independent, orthogonal channels** (severing at ~135° / outside supportive framework; unbinding at ~45° / inside it), so adding severing does not double-count anything FF already has.
- **Coupling to buckling (already available in FF):** severing is gated by curvature that comes from **Euler buckling** of compressed segments. FF filaments are explicit bending beams, so buckling is already emergent — the new code only needs to read the local bending angle and fire a Poisson event.

### (B) Bell-type ACP unbinding — CONFIRM/ALIGN, do not re-add
- **FF module:** `crosslinker` (α-actinin/filamin) — already Bell-Evans in FF.
- **Change:** None mechanistically; the paper *confirms* FF's existing Bell slip-bond off-rate for cross-linkers, and gives the important quantitative sanity result that **buckling forces (~4.1 pN) barely perturb the ACP off-rate (only 1.1×)**. Use this to ORDER the two channels: on a buckling filament, severing fires *before* significant ACP unbinding. Good sign/sequence check for FF.

### (C) Supportive-framework load partition — emergent validation, not a new mechanism
- The ~45° stretched "supportive framework" vs ~135° compressed/buckled population is an **emergent** load-partition FF should reproduce for free (explicit beams under shear). Use as a structural/orientation validation, not something to code.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### Scheme 1 — Buckling-triggered severing (eq S9 form; curvature-gated Poisson)
The Letter states severing is "modeled as a stochastic event (eq S9)" with rate increasing with local bending. The exact S9 form is in the SI (NOT in this file — flag to obtain). The Letter's physics fully constrains the intended scheme; port as a curvature-dependent Poisson rate:

```
P(sever segment i in dt) = 1 − exp(−k_sev,i · dt)
k_sev,i = k_sev0 · g(θ_i)            # θ_i = local bending angle at segment i
```
where `g(θ)` is monotonically increasing in bending angle (Letter: "increase in local bending enhances severing probability by destabilizing bonds between adjacent subunits," citing De La Cruz 2015, ref 21). A defensible mechanistic choice consistent with the De La Cruz curvature-severing literature is a curvature-linear or curvature-power rate, e.g. `g(θ_i) = (θ_i/θ_ref)^p` or `∝ κ_local`. **Do NOT tune `k_sev0` to hit a relaxation target** (magic-number rule) — set it from a severing-rate constant (obtain from SI parameter table / De La Cruz kinetics) and treat the relaxation extent as a *prediction*.

**Symbol → FF state map:**
- `θ_i` → local angular bend at bead `i` (already computed for the bending potential κ).
- `k_sev0` → new config constant, `filament.sever_rate0` [s⁻¹], provenance = SI table / De La Cruz 2015.
- event action → delete segment bond `i`, promote the two new free ends to (barbed/pointed) end particles; hand to growth/turnover.
- Poisson draw per segment per step (matches FF's per-step stochastic clutch/xlink updates; GPU-resident, one uniform draw per segment).

### Scheme 2 — Euler buckling threshold (eq 2) — for the severing gate & sanity check
```
F_buckle ≈ π² · κ_b,A · r_0,A / L_c²          (eq 2)
```
- `κ_b,A` = actin segment bending stiffness (FF: bending modulus from ℓ_p = κ/k_BT ≈ 17 µm actin).
- `r_0,A` = actin segment rest length scale.
- `L_c` = cross-linking span (distance between consecutive bound ACPs along a filament) → in FF, distance between crosslinker attachment points on a filament.
- **Use:** F_buckle ∝ L_c⁻². Short cross-linking span (high crosslink density R_ACP) → large F_buckle but, once buckled, **high curvature** → frequent severing. Long span (low R_ACP) → easy buckling but **low curvature** → few severing events. This tradeoff is the mechanism FF should reproduce, and it tells you severing is strongest for **long filaments + high crosslink density**.
- **Worked datum:** `L_c = 300 nm ⇒ F_buckle = 4.1 pN` (use as a numeric sanity check on FF's beam bending: shear-compress a 300 nm-span segment, confirm buckling onset ≈ 4 pN).

### Scheme 3 — Bell ACP unbinding (eq S8; confirms FF crosslinker)
```
k_u = k_u0 · exp(F·λ / k_BT)          (Bell/slip, eq S8 form)
```
- Matches FF's existing Bell-Evans crosslinker off-rate `k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b)` (identify `F_b = k_BT/λ`).
- **Quantitative check for FF:** at `F = F_buckle = 4.1 pN`, `k_u = 1.1·k_u0` (only +10%). So FF's crosslinkers must NOT unbind appreciably at buckling-scale forces; high-load unbinding requires the supportive-framework tensions (`|F̄_s,ACP| ≥ 8 pN`).

### Metric — cyclic stress drop (eq 1)
```
drop_in_stress (%) = (σ₁ − σ₂)/σ₁ × 100        (eq 1)
```
- `σ₁` = shear stress at first maximal positive strain; `σ₂` = at second maximal positive strain. This is the FF network-rheology observable to compute under cyclic shear.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (as an FF scenario)

A clean, self-contained **reconstituted-network rheology scenario** for FF (separate from the cortex/whole-cell runs — an in-vitro cross-check, NOT a cortex claim):

- **Geometry:** cubic box, edge 3 µm, **PBC in x and y** (z is the shear-gradient direction). FF supports periodic box; add a Lees-Edwards-style or affine boundary shear in the x–z plane.
- **Contents:** random isotropic F-actin network of explicit bending beams + explicit ACP cross-linkers (α-actinin-like two-arm bonds) at ratio `R_ACP`.
- **Sweep axes:** `⟨L_f⟩ ∈ [0.7, 1.3] µm` × `R_ACP ∈ [0.005, 0.05]` → 20 combinations (Fig 4 phase map).
- **Loading:** cyclic simple shear, amplitude 50% (also 10–50%), **bidirectional (±50%)** and **unidirectional (0→+50%)** as the two arms of the decisive control; low strain rate; period ≈ 20 s.
- **Channels to toggle (4 conditions, matching Fig 5a):** severing-only, unbinding-only, both, neither — verify orthogonality/non-interference in FF.
- **Measure:** shear stress σ(t) via virial/segment forces (FF already has a virial stress path used for DCM σ_vm and network σ); cyclic drop (eq 1); severing-event frequency vs strain phase; orientation histograms of severed segments and of high-load-unbinding ACPs (φ on x–z plane).

This scenario is valuable because it validates FF's **network-rheology + severing** on a system with an analytic-ish expectation and a like-for-like agent model, before trusting severing in the native 266k cortex.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for FF observables

Qualitative (sign/behavior gates — cheap, decisive):
- **Bidirectional cyclic shear relaxes; unidirectional does NOT** (Fig 2a vs 2d). Decisive control for "severing needs strain reversal."
- **Severed segments peak at ~135°** (perpendicular to framework); **high-load ACP unbinding peaks at ~45°** (Fig 2c, Fig 5b) — orthogonal orientation split.
- **Severing + unbinding ≈ severing-only** relaxation (do not interfere, Fig 5a).
- Severing frequency **peaks as strain approaches ±peaks** (Fig 2b inset).

Quantitative targets:
- Peak shear stress at 50% strain: **~100–120 Pa** (R_ACP=0.032), **~150 Pa** (R_ACP=0.1). (Figs 2a, 5a)
- Cyclic stress drop vs amplitude (bidirectional): rises **0 → ~75%** as amplitude 0.1→0.5; unidirectional stays **~0** (Fig 2b).
- First-max stress spans **>3 orders of magnitude** (~0.1 → >100 Pa) across the (⟨L_f⟩, R_ACP) sweep (Fig 4a).
- Max cyclic stress drop **up to ~70%** at long filaments + high R_ACP; **negligible** at long-filament/low-R_ACP corner despite high stress (Fig 4b, S4).
- `F_buckle = 4.1 pN` at `L_c = 300 nm`; ACP off-rate rises only **1.1×** there (eq 2, eq S8).
- Cross-linking-distance distribution **exponentially decreasing, ~0.1–2 µm**, set by R_ACP (Fig 4d).
- Relaxation **decreases as strain rate increases** (stochastic origin; Figs S3).

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **In-vitro purified network, NOT a living cortex.** This is a reconstituted α-actinin/filamin F-actin gel under external oscillatory shear — no myosin-generated internal stress, no membrane/turgor, no polymerization/depolymerization. FF's native run is a turgor-pressurised, myosin-active 266k-particle cortex. **Adopt severing as a MECHANISM; validate its rheology in the dedicated reconstituted scenario (§4); do NOT quote the 100–150 Pa / 70% numbers as cortex targets.** The paper itself flags motor-stress + (de)polymerization coupling as future work — that coupling is exactly FF's regime, so FF can go *beyond* the paper, but the validation numbers stay in-vitro.
- **Short filaments (⟨L_f⟩ 0.7–1.3 µm) vs FF cortex (exponential 1–10 µm).** The sweep uses short filaments; FF cortex is longer and denser. Severing on long cortical filaments will be *more* impactful (paper: longer F-actin → more relaxation). Keep the reconstituted scenario at the paper's lengths for validation; use FF's physiological exponential 1–10 µm for cortex runs.
- **External shear vs internal myosin stress.** The paper drives buckling with imposed shear. In the cortex, compression/buckling is driven by myosin contraction. The severing gate (curvature-dependent) is the same; the *loading source* differs. Ensure severing reads local curvature regardless of load origin, so it fires correctly under myosin-driven buckling too — this is the physiologically-relevant extension.
- **Irreversibility + topology churn.** Severing is irreversible and changes filament count/ends every step → must be GPU-resident and not stall the Warp graph. New ends must feed the existing growth/turnover/formin kernels so the network doesn't monotonically fragment (physiological steady state = severing balanced by nucleation/growth). Running severing WITHOUT a compensating polymerization channel will artificially fluidize the cortex — enable both at physiological rates from t=0 (physiological-baseline rule).
- **SI dependency.** The exact severing rate law (eq S9), the Bell exponent form (eq S8), and the numeric `k_sev0`, `κ_b,A`, `r_0,A`, `k_u0`, timestep, drag are in the **Supporting Information PDF, which is not in the provided text** — obtain the SI before finalizing the port to avoid guessing a magic number. The Letter constrains the *form* and gives 4.1 pN / 1.1× / ~45°/135° / stress numbers, which is enough to scaffold and sanity-check.
- **NO gate-loosening / NO fitting.** The relaxation extent (0–70%) must emerge as a *prediction* from κ, R_ACP, ⟨L_f⟩, and a physiologically-sourced `k_sev0` — not by tuning `k_sev0` to hit 70%. If FF can't reach the band with a sourced rate, surface to PI, don't retune.
