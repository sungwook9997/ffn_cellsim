# FF lens — p17

**Paper:** Prigent Garcia, Pinard, Plancke, Li, Suman, Bourdon, Gally, **T. Kim**, Robin (2026) — *Kinetic Control of Out-Of-Equilibrium Dynamics in the RhoA Signaling Cascade Shapes Actomyosin Contractility.* bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.02.08.704688 (preprint, not peer-reviewed).

**Why FF cares:** the paper's third leg is a Taeyoon-Kim agent-based Brownian-dynamics actomyosin network (Jung/Murrell/Kim 2015; Li 2017; Kim 2009; Mak 2016) that is *the same physics class as FF* — cylindrical filament segments under Langevin dynamics with harmonic bending + extensional potentials, excluded-volume repulsion, **Bell's-law** ACP off-rate, and **parallel-cluster-model** motor detachment/force-velocity, with motors self-assembling into bipolar thick filaments (minifilaments). Everything Kim's model does, FF already does with more fidelity (Bell-**Evans** vs Bell; Stam-Hocky multi-head vs PCM; explicit Hill f-v). So this paper is not a new mechanism library — it is (a) a **new emergent-mechanics control variable** (the actin-crosslink-before-motor *timing delay* that non-monotonically maximizes contractile force), (b) a **concrete pulsed-cortex activation protocol** FF can run as a scenario, and (c) **in-vivo single-molecule turnover rate constants** for the myosin minifilament and formin elongation that FF can adopt/cross-check at native scale.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Timing/delay between filament elongation and motor engagement is a first-class contractility lever (the paper's headline result). **NEW to FF.**
- **FF module:** cortex scenario orchestration coupling `formin` (barbed-end elongation) → `crosslinker` (α-actinin/filamin binding, network percolation) → `myosin` (minifilament self-assembly + engagement).
- **Change:** add a per-activation-event **delay `d_M`** parameter between the onset of formin-driven elongation and the onset of myosin minifilament assembly/engagement. Currently FF turns motors on with the network as-is; this makes the *order of operations* explicit.
- **Why it beats current FF:** the sim result (Fig 5) is that net deployable contractile force is **non-monotonic in the delay** and peaks at an intermediate value (~5 s): with **zero delay** motors engage before filaments have cross-linked into a percolated, load-bearing network → premature local collapse into disconnected aggregates → *less* net force; with **too long** a delay the elongation/turnover window has partly relaxed → also less. This is a genuine emergent mechanism (network connectivity must precede motor loading), exactly the kind of thing FF's fine-grained connectivity can resolve and a lumped active-gel cannot. It directly informs FF's open problems of **network contraction / premature-collapse** and **γ generation** (a network that collapses before percolating generates little sustained tension).

### M2 — Disassembly-dominated (k_off-modulated) myosin turnover as the pulse-shaping channel. Refines FF myosin minifilament turnover.
- **FF module:** `myosin` minifilament bind/unbind kinetics.
- **Finding:** in vivo the myosin pulse shape is set predominantly by **modulating k_off** (Fig 3D: constant k_app + sinusoidal k_off reproduces the sharp in-vivo peaks; sinusoidal k_app + constant k_off gives only a smooth phase-shifted sine that does *not* match). Myosin regulation is **disassembly-dominated**; actin regulation is **assembly-dominated** (k_app 5× swing). 
- **Why it matters for FF:** FF should treat minifilament detachment rate (not just recruitment) as the dynamically-modulated quantity when it drives pulsatile cortex; a static k_off will not reproduce sharp contraction pulses.

### M3 — Actin substrate must precede motor loading (the ~0.5 s F-actin→myosin lead). Sequencing constraint.
- **FF module:** myosin engagement gate.
- **Finding:** F-actin accumulates **~0.5 s before** myosin (statistically significant, Fig 4A-B); myosin needs an actin substrate to bind. Consistent with and mechanistically underpins M1: motors can't load a network that isn't there yet.
- **FF use:** this is emergent in FF already (a motor head can only bind an existing filament), but it validates that the *0.5 s* lead should fall out of the assembly kinetics, not be imposed.

### M4 — RhoA-gated *local, transient, stochastic* activation → pulsatile cortex. **NEW protocol for FF.**
- **FF module:** cortex activation field / scenario driver.
- **Change:** partition the cortex patch into subdomains; stochastically activate one at a time on a fixed interval, applying a **transient formin-elongation boost** then a **delayed transient motor-assembly window**, then disassemble motors to non-binding monomers. This produces emergent **pulsed contraction** (period ~30 s) rather than a globally-clamped active cortex.
- **Why:** FF currently drives motors globally/steady; a local-transient-stochastic protocol is the physiologically-correct way to get pulses and to test M1 at native scale.

*(Not adopted: Bell's-law ACP and parallel-cluster-model motors are the paper's crosslinker/motor kinetics — FF's Bell-**Evans** k_off and Stam-Hocky/Hill myosin are strictly higher-fidelity, so use them as a **cross-check oracle**, not a downgrade. See §6.)*

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### Kinetic scheme K1 — leaky-integrator turnover (per tracked species; the master equation)
Eq. 5 (Methods p.24):
```
dN/dt = k_app − k_off · N
```
- `N` = number of bound molecules in a tracked cortical region (FF: count of engaged myosin minifilament heads, or bound crosslinkers, or F-actin subunits in a patch).
- `k_app` [molecule·s⁻¹] = effective binding influx = `k_on·[ROCK]·[Myo_T]` (linear in upstream drive). FF map: minifilament nucleation/recruitment rate onto the patch.
- `k_off` [s⁻¹] = first-order detachment rate. FF map: minifilament disassembly / head-cluster unbinding rate.
- Discrete (Eq. 6): `N_{t+1} − N_t = (k_app − k_off·N)·dt`.

### K2 — instantaneous set-point ("signaled" concentration)
Eq. 3: `[Myo⁰] = k_app / k_off` — the concentration the patch relaxes to if rates froze; FF observable = steady bound-head count = influx/outflux. Useful as an FF *diagnostic* of how far the driven cortex is from its kinetic equilibrium.

### K3 — step-change relaxation (state1→state2)
Eq. 7: `N(t) = N_eq² + (N_eq¹ − N_eq²)·exp(−k₂ t)`, with `N_eq = k_app/k_off` and relaxation time `1/k₂` = `1/k_off` of the new state. FF map: after a step in activation, bound-head count relaxes exponentially with time constant `τ = 1/k_off`.

### K4 — high-pass / frequency-response attenuation
Eq. 4 (Supp. Note 1): sinusoidal upstream forcing at angular frequency ω is attenuated/phase-shifted by
```
1 / sqrt(1 + ω²/k_off²)
```
- Slow drive (ω ≪ k_off): cortex tracks near-equilibrium. Fast drive (ω ≫ k_off): cortex lags, sits far out of equilibrium.
- **FF use:** a *predictive* validation — drive FF's minifilament assembly sinusoidally at frequency ω and confirm the emergent bound-head (and hence tension) amplitude rolls off as `1/√(1+ω²/k_off²)` with the corner at the *measured* `k_off ≈ 0.03–0.10 s⁻¹` (corner period ~10–40 s, i.e. right at the ~30 s pulse period — the cortex is deliberately operated near its kinetic corner).

### K5 — formin barbed-end elongation boost (agent-based recipe)
In an activated subdomain, a random fraction of barbed ends elongate at rate boosted by factor **ρ_f = 10** for duration **τ_f = 10 s** → **~10 µm** added length; equivalently the paper states the boosted elongation rate as **1.2 µm/s held ~10 s → filaments reach ~12 µm** (rate from Costache 2022). FF map: `formin` (mDia1) processive ratchet — set the *boosted* elongation velocity to **1.2 µm/s** and apply it as a transient ρ_f=10 multiplier over a gated 10 s window.

### K6 — bell's-law crosslinker off-rate (cross-check form)
ACP unbinds at `k_off = k_off⁰ · exp(F·λ / k_B T)` (Bell 1978). FF already uses Bell-Evans `k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b)` (F_b = k_BT/λ); the paper's form is the same law → use to cross-check FF's α-actinin/filamin off-rate against Kim's network rheology, not to replace it.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenario)

**Scenario "pulsed-cortex-patch" (Kim protocol):**
1. **Geometry:** thin slab **20 × 20 × 0.1 µm**, periodic BC in x,y, repulsive walls in z. (Note: this is a *flat cortex patch*, not the full MCF7 sphere — see §6 for the native-scale adaptation.)
2. **Init:** self-assemble a thin actin + crosslinker network to steady state at physiological density *before* any activation (physiological-baseline rule).
3. **Partition** into 16 (4×4) subdomains.
4. **Loop (every 30 s):** pick one subdomain at random →
   a. boost a random fraction of its barbed ends: elongation ×ρ_f=10 (→1.2 µm/s) for τ_f=10 s (→~10–12 µm filaments);
   b. wait delay **d_M** (sweep **0 / 5 / 10 s**);
   c. self-assemble myosin minifilaments in that subdomain, active for **τ_M=15 s**, contracting the local network;
   d. disassemble motors to non-binding monomers.
5. **Sweep** d_M ∈ {0,5,10 s}; measure the observables in §5.

This is a clean, self-contained FF regression scenario for **network contraction, premature-collapse, and pulsed-tension generation** — the actin-must-percolate-before-motors-load hypothesis (M1) is directly testable.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **In-vitro/agent-model vs living MCF7 cortex.** Numbers are from *C. elegans* embryo cortex (2-cell, interphase) and a 20×20×0.1 µm **flat periodic patch**. FF's native basis is the MCF7 spherical cortex (R=7.5 µm, ~38000 filaments, Nc≈266k, `--from-resting --microtubules`). **Adapt, don't transplant:** run the pulsed-activation protocol as a *cortex-patch sub-scenario* for mechanism discovery (M1/M4), but any conclusion (force-vs-delay optimum, γ contribution) must be **re-confirmed on the native full-compartment MCF7 cell** before it is reported (HARD native-scale rule). The 20×20 flat slab is a dev-only smoke geometry.
- **Absolute force scale is model-specific.** The ~30–40 nN is Kim's coarse-grained patch with his stiffness constants; FF uses Ferrer link stiffness and Stam-Hocky heads, so match the **shape / non-monotonicity / peak location (≈5 s)**, not the absolute nN. Do not tune FF constants to hit 38 nN (no-fitting rule).
- **Species/isoform.** NMY-2 (worm non-muscle myosin II) is a low/intermediate duty-ratio motor (p.19); this is consistent with FF's Stam-Hocky minifilament but the exact duty ratio is worm-specific. Use as qualitative constraint, keep FF's Hill/duty parameters from their own lit anchors.
- **Bell vs Bell-Evans, PCM vs Stam-Hocky.** The paper's crosslinker/motor kinetics are the *lower-fidelity* forms FF deliberately supersedes (mechanistic-over-lumped rule). Treat Kim's model strictly as a **cross-check oracle** (validation/oracles) — never KB-register Kim's constants as FF runtime values, and never downgrade FF to PCM/plain-Bell.
- **k_off ≈ 0.03–0.10 s⁻¹ is the one number to register**, but it is an *effective* single-molecule cortical detachment rate folding substrate availability into k_app (p.18); it is not a pure catch/slip head off-rate. Register it as an in-vivo minifilament *turnover* datum with that caveat, not as the motor-head Bell-Evans k_off0.
- **Preprint, not peer-reviewed** (bioRxiv, posted 2026-02-10). Cite with that flag; keep verdict pending in any KB row (per citation-integrity gate).
- **Undepleted-pool linearization (Eq. 2) holds only because contraction is ~5%/pulse.** FF's local density can change more under strong contraction; don't assume the leaky-integrator linearity if FF drives the patch far past ~5% strain.
