# FF lens — p68

**Paper:** Li, Szymanski & Kim (2023), "Probing stress-regulated ordering of the plant cortical microtubule array via a computational approach," *BMC Plant Biology* 23:308. DOI 10.1186/s12870-023-04252-5.
**One-line:** A 2D agent-based stochastic model of plant cortical microtubules (three-state plus-end dynamic instability + angle-dependent zippering/catastrophe collisions) in which orientation-projected wall tensile stress linearly biases plus-end kinetics, producing nematic MT alignment along the principal-stress axis.

**Scope note for FF (honest framing).** This is a *kinetic* model of PLANT cortical MTs — no Langevin forces, no bending energy, no explicit crosslinker/motor. Its FF value is therefore concentrated: (a) a literature-anchored **three-state MT dynamic-instability rate set** for FF's `--microtubules` compartment, (b) an **angle-dependent MT–MT collision scheme** (zippering vs catastrophe/crossover) that FF currently lacks, (c) a genuinely mechanistic **stress→plus-end-kinetics feedback loop** that maps cleanly onto FF's already-computed per-filament forces (a real mechanotransduction lever, not lumped), and (d) MT **validation targets**. It is NOT a cortical-actin rheology, contraction, or motor paper — do not import it as such. Species caveat throughout: Arabidopsis cortical MTs (Shaw 2003), not animal MTs.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Microtubule module: explicit three-state plus-end dynamic instability (FF `--microtubules`)
**Change.** Replace any static / two-state (grow–shrink) or fixed-length MT representation in FF's microtubule compartment with an explicit **three-state plus-end** stochastic machine: {growth ↔ pause ↔ shrinkage} governed by six inter-state transition frequencies, plus a **constant slow minus-end shrinkage** (no minus-end dynamic instability). Each MT is a serial chain of 100 nm segments (FF beads); the plus-end bead adds/removes segments per state.
**Why it beats current FF.** FF's mechanistic charter demands every filament be explicit; MTs in the native cell (`--microtubules`) are currently the least-developed compartment. A three-state machine with measured rates gives emergent MT length distribution and lifetime (the paper reproduces 2–4 µm lengths, 2.2–3.0 min lifetimes) instead of a prescribed length — the fine-grained option. Pause state is the physiologically correct third state animal-cell two-state models omit.

### M2 — Angle-dependent MT–MT collision resolution (zippering vs catastrophe/crossover)
**Change.** Add a deterministic geometric collision rule between MT filaments: on contact, compute contact angle θ_c; if θ_c < 40° → **zippering** (incoming MT reorients to align along the barrier MT → bundling); if θ_c > 40° → **catastrophe** (with probability P_cat) **or crossover** (probability 1−P_cat). MT–MT interaction range = 25–50 nm (cross-bridge spacing).
**Why.** This is the self-organization engine — it converts random nucleation into aligned bundles, i.e. it is the mechanistic origin of cortical-array order. FF's excluded-volume LJ handles steric repulsion but has no *bundling/reorientation* rule; this supplies the emergent nematic ordering that pure EV cannot produce. It is the MT analogue of what α-actinin does for actin, but for the semiflexible/near-rigid MT regime where alignment is collision-driven.

### M3 — Stress→plus-end-kinetics feedback (mechanotransduction, cortex-MT coupling)
**Change.** Couple the local mechanical stress that FF *already computes* per filament/per membrane-patch to MT plus-end kinetics: project the in-plane stress onto each MT's orientation (Eq. 1), then bias ONE plus-end parameter linearly (Sec. 3). Increasing tensile σ_MT → ↑ growth rate, ↑ rescue freq, ↓ shrinkage rate, ↓ catastrophe freq → MTs preferentially survive/grow along the high-tension axis → nematic alignment ∥ principal stress emerges.
**Why it beats a lumped alternative.** This is a *mechanistic* mechanotransduction feedback: no imposed order-parameter target, no direction hard-coded. In FF, unlike this paper (which *imports* stress from an external FE model), the cortex/membrane stress is native output — so the loop closes self-consistently (stress → MT order → cortex mechanics → stress). The paper explicitly proposes the molecular sensor is a **catch-bond** wall-anchor whose dissociation rate *drops* under tension (Williamson 1990 hypothesis) — which is exactly FF's existing Bell/catch-bond vocabulary (Rakshit KU-4.2). FF can implement the sensor mechanistically as a catch-bond MT–membrane anchor rather than a phenomenological stress-rate law. **This is the single most transferable idea in the paper.**

### M4 (concept only) — Catch-bond directional stress-sensor as the plus-end modulator
The paper argues (Discussion, p10) the modulation acts through an immobile wall-coupled receptor with catch-bond kinetics, and that the array senses **stress, not strain**, and that the coupling is largely **growth-status-independent** (side + end contact, ~300 nm² patch, 13 protofilaments). For FF: model MT–cortex/membrane tethering as explicit catch-bonds; longer bond lifetime under tension → longer MT residence → alignment. Beats a bare "stress modulates rate" constitutive law because it is particle-level.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### Eq. (1) — Orientation-projected normal stress on an MT at angle θ
```
σ_MT(θ) = (σ_x + σ_y)/2  +  (σ_x − σ_y)/2 · cos(2θ)
```
Symbol map to FF: σ_x, σ_y = in-plane principal stresses of the local cortex/membrane patch (FF already computes per-patch virial/membrane stress; take the 2D in-plane projection tangent to the surface manifold). θ = angle of the MT segment tangent relative to the local principal (+x) frame. Shear τ_xy neglected (paper's FE said negligible; FF should verify per-patch before dropping it). Output σ_MT is the scalar fed to Sec. 3.

### Eq. (2) — Nematic order parameter S_p (an FF observable, see §5)
```
S_p = ( Σ_i l_i · cos[2(θ_i − θ_p)] ) / ( Σ_i l_i )
```
Length-weighted alignment of all MT segments vs reference θ_p. S_p=1 aligned ∥ θ_p, 0 isotropic, −1 ⊥. Use as the MT-order readout in FF cortex diagnostics.

### Sec. 3 — Linear constitutive modulation of ONE plus-end parameter (kinetic scheme)
Functional form (verbal in paper; implement as): for modulated parameter X ∈ {v_p^g, v_d^{s+}, f_cat, f_res},
```
X(σ_MT) = X_0 · (1 + α_X · σ_MT)        (linear; paper's deliberate approximation)
```
with signs: **d(v_p^g)/dσ > 0, d(f_res)/dσ > 0, d(v_d^{s+})/dσ < 0, d(f_cat)/dσ < 0.** Only one modulated at a time in the paper; the two *most potent aligners are polymerization rate (v_p^g) and catastrophe frequency (f_cat)* — port those first.
**FF upgrade (do not blindly copy the linear form):** the paper flags (p14, ref Akiyoshi 2010) that the *true* force–kinetics relation is **exponential**, i.e. Bell-Evans-like:
```
X(σ_MT) = X_0 · exp(± σ_MT · A_contact / k_B T)   with A_contact ≈ 300 nm², tension scale ~10 pN
```
Since FF's charter mandates mechanistic over proxy, FF should implement the **exponential Bell-Evans form** (consistent with FF's existing k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b) crosslinker law), NOT the paper's linear convenience approximation. The 10 pN / 300 nm² sensitivity scale is the anchor.

### Three-state transition scheme (Fig 5A)
Plus end: growth →(f_gp)→ pause, growth →(f_gs)→ shrinkage, pause →(f_pg)→ growth, pause →(f_ps)→ shrinkage, shrinkage →(f_sg)→ growth (rescue), shrinkage →(f_sp)→ pause. Poisson events at rate f per step. Minus end: constant shrink at v_d^{s−}. Nucleation: Poisson in area at ρ_nuc, random position + orientation, MT-independent, unlimited tubulin pool.

### Collision scheme (Fig 5B)
On MT–MT contact within 25–50 nm at angle θ_c: θ_c<40° → zipper (set incoming segment orientation = barrier orientation); θ_c>40° → draw u~U(0,1): u<P_cat → catastrophe (plus end → shrinkage), else crossover (pass through, no state change).

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOLS to reproduce (FF scenarios)

1. **Isotropic vs anisotropic stress alignment (Fig 1).** 10×10 µm patch, PBC both axes, nucleate at ρ_nuc=10, run 100 min. Impose σ_x=σ_y (control) vs σ_x<σ_y at 10:1 (demo) and 2:1 (physiological). Modulate one plus-end parameter. Expect S_p → 0.4–0.5 (isotropic) vs 0.8–0.85 (anisotropic, polymerization/catastrophe modulated). FF adaptation: run on a flat cortex membrane patch with real per-patch stress instead of imposed σ.
2. **Dynamic reorientation (Fig 2).** Establish aligned array (100 min), then rotate principal stress 90° either instantaneously or gradually over 50/100/150 min. Expect ~5 min depoly of old array, transient homogenization (S_p→0, density halved) at ~20 min, complete reorientation ~50 min. Direct test of the feedback loop's dynamics.
3. **Physiological stress gradient (Fig 3).** 30×10 µm rectangle, PBC in y, catastrophe-inducing BC at x-edges, apex→base stress-magnitude gradient + a 2 µm isotropic apical zone. Reproduces MDZ (sparse apex) + dense transverse collar + declining base density. FF adaptation: a genuine confinement/gradient self-organization scenario for the cortex-MT compartment.
4. **Boundary-condition sweep** {catastrophe-inducing, reflective, repulsive, periodic}: use catastrophe-inducing at physical cell boundaries in FF (growing MT hitting membrane → shrink) — the physiologically correct plus-end fate at a wall.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Plant, not animal.** All DI rates are Arabidopsis cortical MTs. Animal-cell (MCF7) MT dynamics differ (faster catastrophe, centrosomal nucleation vs de novo). Use these as a *scaffold* and flag species; do not present plant rates as MCF7 physiology. FF's `--microtubules` should carry a species tag and ideally an animal-MT rate set for production; plant rates are a validated placeholder / cross-check.
- **Kinetic-only, no forces.** The paper has no bending energy, no Langevin, MTs rigid. FF must NOT drop its mechanistic force treatment to match — instead *graft* the DI kinetics + collision rules onto FF's existing beam MTs. Keep FF bending κ / ℓ_p from a proper MT-rigidity source (not this paper).
- **Linear constitutive law is an admitted approximation.** Paper itself cites the true relation as exponential (Akiyoshi 2010). Per FF's no-proxy charter, implement the **Bell-Evans exponential** stress→rate coupling (10 pN / 300 nm² scale), not the linear form — otherwise FF would import a lumped approximation the source flags as wrong.
- **Stress is imported, not computed, in the paper.** FF's advantage is it computes cortex stress natively — but that means FF must be careful to project the *correct* in-plane component onto the surface manifold (Eq 1 assumes 2D membrane-tethered MTs). Verify shear is actually negligible per-patch in FF before dropping τ_xy; the paper's justification is an external FE result, not a general truth.
- **2D membrane-confined vs FF 3D.** Cortical MTs are 2D by biology (membrane-tethered); FF's cortex is a 3D shell. Apply the DI+collision+alignment on the tangent plane of the surface manifold (FF already has `surface_manifold` in `common/`), not in bulk 3D.
- **P_cat = 0.2–0.8 is swept, not fixed** — good (avoids a magic number), but FF must report it as a swept range and derive the operating value, not pick one to pass a gate.
- **Native-scale rule.** Any FF MT-ordering conclusion must be reconfirmed on the native full cell (`--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules`), never on a 10×10 µm coarse patch. The paper's 10×10/30×10 domains are the dev-smoke geometry; the flat-patch scenarios above are for mechanism development, not for reporting.
- **Nucleation model differs.** Paper uses de novo random nucleation with unlimited tubulin. FF at physiological baseline should use a finite tubulin pool and (for animal cells) nucleation biased to MTOC/branch sites — the 10 µm⁻²·min⁻¹ de novo rate is plant-cortex-specific.
