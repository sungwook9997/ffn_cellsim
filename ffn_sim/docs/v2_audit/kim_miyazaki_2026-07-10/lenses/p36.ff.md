# FF lens — p36

**Paper:** Jung, Fillenwarth, Matsuda, Li, Inoue, Kim — "Collective and contractile filament motions in the myosin motility assay", *Soft Matter* 2020, 16, 1548–1559. DOI 10.1039/c9sm02082a.

**One-line relevance:** This IS the FF engine's exact physics family — the canonical **Kim-lab agent-based Brownian-dynamics actomyosin code** (cylindrical-segment F-actin with harmonic stretch+bend, explicit myosin motors on the **Parallel Cluster Model** mechanochemical cycle, ACP cross-linkers unbinding via **Bell's law**, harmonic volume-exclusion). Cytosim/FF and this code are sibling implementations of the same fine-grained mechanistic contract. It hands FF a validated motor kinetic scheme, a cross-linker off-rate constant, a volume-exclusion form, and a rich menu of emergent validation targets (biphasic gliding, bundle↔ring morphology, percolation-gated contractility). The one hard limitation: several force-law constants (k_{s,A}, k_{b,A}, drag γ, dt, r_{c,A}) live in ESI Tables S1/S2 which were NOT in the supplied PDF — flag for ESI harvest before quoting exact stiffness/timestep numbers.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Myosin: replace/cross-check the Hill(1938) force–velocity front-end with the **Parallel Cluster Model (PCM, Erdmann–Schwarz 2012/2013)** load-dependent walk+unbind (FF module: `myosin`)
FF currently drives the Stam–Hocky bipolar minifilament with a Hill (1938) force–velocity law and a separate detachment rule. This paper's motor uses the PCM: a **single "one-arm motor" representing N_h cooperative heads** with **3 mechanochemical states and 5 rates**, from which the *emergent* walking rate k_{w,M} and unbinding rate k_{u,M} come out **coupled** — both ∝ the ATP-dependent head-unbinding rate k₂₀ and **both decrease under load** ("catch-bond-like"), with **stall force ∝ 1/k₂₀**. Why it beats Hill-as-front-end: Hill gives f–v but says nothing self-consistent about *when the head lets go under load*; PCM makes walk-rate, detach-rate, duty ratio and stall **one coupled cross-bridge cycle** — exactly the "no lumped proxy" principle. The single control knob k₂₀ then reproduces the whole **biphasic speed curve** (walk-limited → detach-limited) that a Hill law alone cannot. Recommend: keep Stam–Hocky *geometry* (bipolar minifilament, N heads) but adopt PCM as the per-head kinetic core, or at minimum add PCM as a parity oracle for the current motor.

### M2 — Cross-linker: keep Bell's-law off-rate but adopt this paper's **percolation-gated contractility law** as the design principle (FF module: `crosslinker` α-actinin/filamin)
The paper's central emergent result is that network contractility/heterogeneity is **maximal at INTERMEDIATE cross-linker density AND intermediate off-rate** — you need enough connectivity to transmit stress (a **percolation threshold ≈ 3 % of actins** at C_A=60 µM fully cross-links the network) but enough deformability (unbinding) to let motors rearrange and buckle filaments. Too many/too-stable crosslinks → frozen network, motors can't buckle, tension collapses. Too few/too-transient → free gliding, no stress. This is directly the FF cortical-tension γ / network-contraction problem: it says γ-generation is **non-monotonic in crosslink density and off-rate**, so FF should *sweep both* rather than crank crosslink density up. Mechanistically it also ties tension to **filament buckling** (below), not to stretch.

### M3 — Contraction driver = **filament buckling asymmetry**, an explicit mechanistic channel FF already can express (FF module: `filament` bending + `myosin`)
The paper states net tensile (contractile) stress arises because **compressive loads are relaxed by filament buckling while tensile loads are carried** — a stretch/compression rectification. FF actin filaments are explicit bending beams (κ, ℓ_p=17 µm), so FF *already has the ingredient* but may not be measuring it. Adopt their diagnostic: monitor per-chain spring forces and confirm **tension dominates because compressed segments buckle**. Crucially the paper shows that at *very high* crosslink density buckling is **suppressed** (segments between crosslinks too short to buckle) → contraction falls — a concrete mechanism for a γ ceiling. This is a candidate lever for FF's open **cortical-tension γ-floor/ceiling** problem: γ is buckling-rectification-limited, not just motor-force-limited.

### M4 — Volume-exclusion as an active-matter organizer (FF module: `filament` excluded-volume / LJ)
FF runs LJ-repulsive excluded volume already. The paper shows that **steric repulsion ALONE (no crosslinkers, no motors beyond gliding) self-organizes gliding filaments into thick bundles and rings**, with morphology set by repulsion strength κ_{r,A} and actin concentration C_A. Take-home for FF: excluded-volume strength is a *first-class morphogenetic parameter*, not a numerical nicety — its magnitude changes emergent architecture (bundle vs ring). When FF studies cortex/lamellipodium self-organization, the EV strength must be at a physical value, and its variation is a legitimate mechanistic axis.

### M5 — "One-arm motor = N_h cooperative heads" coarse-graining that stays mechanistic (FF module: `myosin`)
Each motor arm lumps **N_h = 8 heads** but keeps them mechanistically coupled through the PCM (binding rate scales 40·N_h, more processive + higher stall). This is a sanctioned coarse-graining that FF's bipolar-minifilament head-count can mirror: it lets FF tune processivity/duty-ratio/stall by a single N_h without abandoning the cross-bridge cycle. Useful for the crawl-speed / motor-driven-flow work where per-head resolution is expensive.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### L1 — Volume-exclusion repulsive harmonic potential (Eq. 6) — port directly as an FF excluded-volume option
```
U_r = ½ · κ_{r,A} · (r₁₂ − r_{c,A})²    if r₁₂ < r_{c,A}
U_r = 0                                  if r₁₂ ≥ r_{c,A}
```
Symbol map to FF state: `r₁₂` = minimum distance between two actin cylinder segments (FF: segment–segment closest approach); `r_{c,A}` = actin-segment diameter (FF: filament excluded-volume cutoff, native ~7–8 nm); `κ_{r,A}` = repulsion stiffness (N·m⁻¹, ESI value). This is a **soft harmonic** alternative to FF's LJ-repulsive — softer, tunable, and the paper varies κ_{r,A}/κ* over 0.1–3 as a morphogenetic axis. Use as an on-demand parity form; the exact κ_{r,A}* is in ESI.

### L2 — Bell's law ACP off-rate (unnumbered, ref 23 Bell 1978) — matches FF's existing crosslinker off-rate
```
k_{u,ACP}(F) = k⁰_{u,ACP} · exp( F · λ / k_B T )
```
Symbol map: `k⁰_{u,ACP}` = zero-force off-rate = **0.115 s⁻¹** (reference, see P1); `F` = tensile load on the cross-link (FF: crosslink bond force); `λ` = force scale / bond compliance length (in ESI; FF: the F_b in k_off=k_off0·exp(F/F_b), so λ/k_BT ↔ 1/F_b); `k_BT` = thermal energy. **This is identical in form to FF's current α-actinin/filamin Bell-Evans off-rate** — so it's a direct cross-check of FF's k_off0 and F_b, not a new mechanism. Note FF already anchors α-actinin k_off0=0.066 s⁻¹ (Ferrer 2008, per CLAUDE.md); this paper's generic ACP 0.115 s⁻¹ is a *different, generic* crosslinker reference — do NOT overwrite Ferrer; use 0.115 s⁻¹ only as a sweep-center for a generic-ACP scenario.

### L3 — PCM motor kinetics (unnumbered, refs 24/25 Erdmann–Schwarz) — port as motor kinetic core / oracle
Scheme: **3 mechanochemical states, 5 rates**; one control rate = **k₂₀ = ATP-dependent head unbinding rate**. Emergent laws:
- k_{w,M} ∝ k₂₀ and **decreases with load** (catch-bond-like).
- k_{u,M} ∝ k₂₀ and **decreases with load**.
- Stall force ∝ **1/k₂₀**.
- Binding on-rate = **40·N_h s⁻¹** per motor arm to one actin site.
- Unloaded walk velocity at reference k₂₀=20 s⁻¹ = **140 nm s⁻¹**.
Symbol map to FF: k₂₀ ↔ an ATP-set base detachment rate driving the Stam–Hocky head cycle; k_{w,M} ↔ FF head step rate; stall force ↔ FF motor stall (currently Hill). The full 5-rate table is in ESI Table S2 — **harvest ESI before implementing**; without it, port the *emergent* laws above as an acceptance oracle for FF's motor (walk∝k₂₀, stall∝1/k₂₀, unloaded v=140 nm/s at k₂₀=20).

### L4 — Persistence length from bending stiffness (Eq. 3) — parity check for FF's κ↔ℓ_p mapping
```
ℓ_p = k_{b,A} · r_{0,A} / (k_B T)
```
Symbol map: `k_{b,A}` = FF angular bending stiffness, `r_{0,A}` = equilibrium segment length. FF sets ℓ_p≈17 µm for actin; use this identity to verify FF's chosen k_{b,A}·r_{0,A}/k_BT actually lands at 17 µm at native segment length. Eq. 4 (tangent–tangent correlation ⟨u(s)·u(s+Δs)⟩) is the **measurement protocol** to recover the *effective* ℓ_p once motors bend the filament — adopt as an FF diagnostic (motor-loaded filaments have lower effective ℓ_p than the nominal Eq. 3 value).

### L5 — Diagnostics to port (measurement protocols, not forces)
- **Velocity autocorrelation** ⟨cos φ(τ)⟩ (Eq. 1): endpoint heading persistence vs lag τ; exponential decay for pure motor gliding. FF crawl/flow observable.
- **Pairwise velocity-direction correlation** ⟨cos α(r)⟩ (Eq. 2): collective/parallel motion vs separation r (bin Δr=0.2 µm, cutoff 1 µm). FF collective-flow observable.
- **Morphological heterogeneity Q_A** (Eq. 5): N_G×N_G grid (N_G=20), mean over rows/cols of the std of per-grid segment count → bundling/clustering metric. FF cortex-uniformity / condensation observable.
- **Spring energy density map**: 30×30 grid, Σ(segment spring energy)/grid volume [J m⁻³] → local-stress heat map. FF stress-localization observable.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenarios)

**Scenario FF-MA (motility assay, quasi-2D):**
- Domain **10 × 10 × 0.1 µm**, periodic in x,y. (This is a *quasi-2D slab* — a legitimate reduced FF test geometry, NOT a substitute for the native 3D cortex conclusion.)
- Motors immobilized at random (x,y) at one z; F-actin plane offset by the **13.5 nm motor-arm length** so a motor binds only when (x,y) coincide — reproduces surface-tethered geometry. FF clutch anchors pinned in space.
- F-actin self-assembles to mean length ⟨L_f⟩; distributed lengths (FF already uses exponential 1–10 µm).
- Protocol: during assembly a fraction of motors bind without walking; after assembly all motors walk toward barbed ends. Sample endpoint velocities every **2 s**; run **~100 s**; time-average morphology/force over the **last 50 s**.

**Three campaigns to replicate as FF acceptance runs:**
1. **Base (no EV, no ACP):** sweep ⟨L_f⟩ (0.62–5.06 µm), R_M (motor density; spacing 0.11–1.1 µm), k₂₀ (5–640 s⁻¹). Target: length-threshold-for-fast-gliding and biphasic-k₂₀.
2. **EV on, no ACP:** sweep C_A (15–240 µM), κ_{r,A} (0.1–3×). Target: bundle↔ring morphology map.
3. **ACP on, no EV:** sweep R_ACP (0.001–0.1), k⁰_{u,ACP} (0–10×). Target: percolation-gated contractility optimum.

**Note on scale rule:** these are *in-vitro reconstitution* scenarios at OUR fine-grained filament resolution — valid as FF validation because the paper's model is the same explicit-agent physics. They are **not** the native MCF7 cortex; findings from them must be re-expressed as *mechanism* (PCM law, buckling rectification, percolation window) and then confirmed on the native `--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules` cell before any cortex-tension conclusion.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Purified in-vitro assay ≠ living cortex.** Quasi-2D slab, immobilized motors, tunable-but-not-always-physiological parameter ranges (the authors explicitly say ranges are chosen to reveal effects, not to be physiological). Adopt the **laws and the emergent design principles** (PCM coupling, buckling rectification, percolation window), NOT the sweep endpoints, as physiological FF settings. Physiological-baseline rule stands: FF native runs use real MCF7 densities, not R_M/C_A assay knobs.
- **Immobile motors** (surface-tethered) — the paper's motors don't diffuse/relocate. FF cortex motors are minifilaments embedded in the network (mobile). So the *quantitative* contractility numbers won't transfer 1:1; use the assay scenario only as a controlled mechanism testbed, then confirm on the native mobile-motor cortex. The paper itself notes ring formation at intermediate ACP density is absent *because* motors are immobile — a known artifact of the geometry.
- **k⁰_{u,ACP}=0.115 s⁻¹ is a GENERIC ACP**, not α-actinin. FF already anchors α-actinin k_off0=0.066 s⁻¹ (Ferrer 2008). **Do NOT supersede Ferrer.** Use 0.115 s⁻¹ only as a generic-crosslinker sweep center for the reconstitution scenario. No gate-loosening, no re-anchoring off this paper.
- **ESI dependency (hard).** k_{s,A}, k_{b,A}, drag γ, dt, r_{c,A}, κ_{r,A}*, and the full 5-rate PCM table (Tables S1/S2, Fig S1) are in the ESI, which was NOT in the supplied PDF. Do NOT quote exact stiffness/timestep/rate magnitudes as FF config values until ESI is harvested — port only the *forms* and the *emergent laws* now, flag the ESI harvest as a prerequisite for numeric implementation.
- **Small domain / short filaments** — the authors note their 10 µm box + short F-actin cannot reproduce the ~30 µm experimental rings (ref 14) or large-scale flows; a magic-number-free FF replication must not tune box/length to force ring size. Ring size is emergent, not a target to fit.
- **Species/isoform:** motor datum is non-muscle myosin II (140 nm/s) — appropriate for the FF cortex myosin, good. But k₂₀ up to 640 s⁻¹ probes smooth/skeletal regimes irrelevant to interphase cortex; keep production k₂₀ at the non-muscle-II operating point.
