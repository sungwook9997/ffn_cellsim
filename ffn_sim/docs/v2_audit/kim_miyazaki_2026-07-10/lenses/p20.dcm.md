# DCM lens — p20

**Paper:** Miyazaki, Yamazaki, Hasegawa (2022), "Analysis of Brownian Motion by Elementary School Students," *The Physics Teacher* 60, 478–483. DOI 10.1119/5.0059810.

**Verdict up front:** This is a **physics-education / outreach** article, not a mechanobiology or simulation paper. It reports a 3-hour distance-learning workshop for 27 Japanese elementary-school students who (1) hand-tracked 1-µm plastic beads in water with the open-source Tracker software and (2) played a 6-sided-die 2D random-walk board game, to discover the diffusive √t scaling of Brownian motion. **There is essentially nothing to port into the DCM engine.** No membrane/cortex/turgor/nucleus continuum physics, no cell–cell junction, no aggregate, no cortical-tension generation, no division, no confinement — none of DCM's open problems are touched. What follows is an honest, mostly-empty extraction so the negative result is on record.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

**None applicable.** The paper contains no mechanical mechanism relevant to a deformable-cell surface-mesh model:
- No cortex/membrane elasticity, no surface tension γ, no osmotic/turgor Π, no viscosity constitutive law, no bending κ.
- The only "mechanism" invoked is qualitative: water-molecule collisions push a bead at random (Fig. 5, Fig. 7). This is the textbook thermal-noise picture already implicit in DCM's stochastic integrator; the paper adds no functional form, no collision cross-section, no drag coefficient.
- Molecular motors (kinesin/myosin) are mentioned only pedagogically in the closing lecture — no force–velocity law, no duty ratio, no stall force. Nothing for cortex myosin γ generation.

No DCM module (`membrane` K_A/κ_m, `cortex` γ, `cytoplasm` η/Π, `nucleus` E_nuc, `contact` node-face/IPC/cadherin) can be advanced by this paper.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port

The paper states exactly two equations, the standard isotropic 2D Einstein diffusion relations:

- **Eq. 1:** ⟨[x(t) − x(0)]² + [y(t) − y(0)]²⟩ = 4·D·t
- **Eq. 2:** ⟨[x(t) − x(0)]² + [y(t) − y(0)]²⟩^(1/2) ∝ t^(1/2)

Symbol map to DCM: x(t),y(t) = a tracer's in-plane position; ⟨·⟩ = ensemble average; D = diffusion coefficient (never evaluated numerically in the paper); t = time. The generic 3D form is MSD = 6Dt.

**Porting relevance: nil-to-trivial.** This is the fluctuation baseline any BAOAB/Langevin thermostat already satisfies via the fluctuation–dissipation theorem (D = k_BT/ζ, ζ = 6πηa). DCM's per-node drag is derived from η = 65.9 Pa·s exactly this way. The paper supplies **no D, no η, no k_BT**, so there is nothing to calibrate against — Eq. 1 is at most a citation-of-convenience for "our thermostat produces MSD ∝ t," which the project already grounds in canonical Einstein–Stokes references (Berg 1983, cited here as ref. 1), not this outreach note.

No config field (`gamma`, `eta`, `Pi0`, `K_A`, `kappa_m`, `E_nuc`, drag ζ) receives a value here, and nothing supersedes a current default.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

**None applicable.** The two protocols are (a) 2D single-particle tracking of a free bead in bulk water and (b) a paper-and-dice 2D lattice random walk. Neither is a DCM scenario — there is no confinement geometry, no aspiration, no AFM indentation, no division, no aggregate, no substrate/durotaxis. The only conceivable (very weak) use is a **thermostat sanity check**: run a single passive DCM tracer node / free particle at η = 65.9 Pa·s and confirm MSD grows linearly with the analytic slope 6·k_BT/(6πηa) — but this is a generic FDT unit test the project can (and should) anchor to primary Langevin references, not to a children's workshop.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS

**None.** The quantitative outputs are low-precision hand-clicked bead √MSD (≈6–7 µm at 20 s, Fig. 4a), dice-game √MSD (≈4.5 cm at N≈50, Fig. 8), and Likert survey statistics (interesting 4.69±0.64, difficult 3.27±1.09). These are pedagogical/education outcomes, not mechanistic cell observables. No tension, shape index, division force, ring-closure rate, compaction fraction, contact angle, or cortical-flow speed is measured. **Do NOT KB-register any value here as a DCM validation target.**

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Regime mismatch is total:** in-vitro inert 1-µm plastic beads in pure water, 2D projection, hand-tracked by children — vs. a living MCF7 3D deformable cell with active cortex, turgor, nucleus. The paper's √t "result" is the passive-diffusion baseline that active cell mechanics deliberately depart from; treating it as a DCM datum would violate the physiological-baseline rule (water, not 65 Pa·s cytoplasm; no turgor; no active stress).
- **Provenance-only value:** the paper is a genuine Miyazaki-lab (Kyoto) outreach item; its worth to the corpus is completeness/provenance, nothing more.
- **Citation hygiene:** if ever cited, cite it only as an education/outreach reference for the *pedagogy* of Brownian motion, never as a mechanistic source for D, η, or motor physics. The real primary sources for those (Berg 1983; Bustamante 2001; Kodera 2010) are in its reference list and should be used directly instead.

**Bottom line:** relevance to the DCM engine ≈ 0. Logged as a negative-result lens; no mechanism, equation, parameter, protocol, or validation target is actionable.
