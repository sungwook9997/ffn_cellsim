# DCM lens — p37

**Paper:** Cifuentes, Athamneh, Efremov, Raman, **T. Kim**, Suter (2024) *MBoC* 35:ar47 — "A modified motor-clutch model reveals that neuronal growth cones respond faster to soft substrates." DOI 10.1091/mbc.E23-09-0364.

**Verdict up front (honesty):** This is a **1-D lumped stochastic motor-clutch** model of a single *Aplysia* filopodium on an elastic substrate. It has **no surface mesh, no membrane/cortex/nucleus/turgor, no volume constraint** — i.e. none of the DCM continuum machinery. Most of its content lands on the **FF engine** (adhesion clutch off-rate, motor force-velocity, traction). For DCM the payoff is **narrow but real and at exactly one lever we have open**: a *force-gated, pool-saturating adhesion-reinforcement law* that can replace/augment DCM's current age-based cadherin-junction maturation, plus a clean **substrate-stiffness (durotaxis) boundary condition + validation curve**. Everything here is at the interface/BC level of a DCM cell, not inside the bulk — so it is portable without breaking the surface-mesh scale. Score reflects the narrowness: this is a supporting cross-check, not an engine rebuild.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Force-gated, pool-saturating reinforcement for the cadherin junction (BEST lever)
**DCM module:** cell-cell contact = the ONE cadherin catch-bond junction interface (Rakshit KU-4.2), with load-sharing clusters + contact-age maturation (currently the "maturation" is contact-age driven; the S1–S4 cadherin redesign showed maturation is real but the aggregate-σ compaction *bypasses* it).

**Change:** adopt the paper's **reinforcement rate law (Eq 6)** as the junction's *cluster-growth* law, replacing pure age-based maturation with a **force-threshold + available-pool** term:
> add a new bond to the junction cluster at rate `k_add = k_add0 · (# engaged bonds carrying F ≥ F_t) · (n_max − n)/n_max`.

**Why it beats what DCM does now:**
- DCM's maturation is currently **time/age-gated** (contact age → maturation). The S4 finding was precisely that age-gated maturation is *not* the compaction rate-limiter because aggregate-σ pulls straight past it. The paper gives the mechanistically correct alternative: maturation is **force-gated** — a junction only recruits more cadherins where existing bonds are actually loaded (F ≥ F_t). This is the fine-grained option (per the hard rule) over the lumped "age → strength" proxy.
- The `f_available = (n_max − n)/n_max` factor is a **derivable saturation** (finite cadherin pool), not a tuned cap — it gives the junction a natural equilibrium size without a magic-number ceiling.
- It converts an **unstable oscillating** coupling state into a **stable steady state** (their Fig 6: reinforcement ON → constant deformation; OFF → permanent load-and-fail oscillation). DCM's loose→compact aggregate and its cell-cell interfaces currently reach steady contact only through drag; a force-gated recruitment term is a *mechanistic* stabilizer for the junction interface.

**Caveat baked in:** in the paper reinforcement is between actin↔substrate (integrin/apCAM-like). In DCM it maps to **cadherin bond recruitment into the trans junction cluster** — same math (threshold-gated positive feedback on a shared-load spring bundle), different molecule. Keep the catch-bond off-rate (Rakshit) as the *removal* half; use Eq 6 only for the *addition* half. Do NOT import their Bell **slip**-bond off-rate over the cadherin catch-bond (see Risks).

### M2 — Substrate-stiffness boundary condition (durotaxis scenario)
**DCM module:** substrate/ECM contact BC (node–face penalty against a rigid plane today).

**Change:** replace the rigid substrate with an **elastic substrate spring `K_sub`** (Eq 9 force balance) under the adhered basal nodes, and sweep it. This turns the DCM "cell on a plane" into a **durotaxis-capable** scenario: the cell's basal adhesion cluster loads a compliant substrate, and cluster growth (M1) becomes stiffness-dependent.

**Why:** durotaxis / confinement response is on DCM's open list and DCM currently has **no compliant-substrate BC**. This paper supplies the minimal mechanistic one (a linear substrate spring per adhesion patch) plus the *emergent* stiffness dependence — no explicit "durotaxis force" is coded; the directional bias emerges from stiffness-dependent cluster reinforcement. That is the mechanistic-over-lumped choice.

### M3 — Coupling-strength observable: retrograde-flow / deformation threshold as a maturation clock
**DCM module:** contact-age maturation timer.
**Change:** define junction "strong-coupling" not by elapsed age but by a **kinematic threshold** — the interface reaches maturity when relative sliding (analogue of retrograde flow) drops below a fraction of its unloaded value. In the paper: strong coupling ≙ actin flow decays to **20 nm/s = 80% reduction** of the 100 nm/s unloaded rate. For DCM the analogue is *cell-cell interface slip velocity* falling to 20% of its free value → declare the junction mature.
**Why:** replaces an arbitrary maturation-time constant with a **grid-invariant kinematic criterion** (a ratio, not an absolute clock) — cleaner against the no-magic-number rule.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (to the junction/BC level)

Transcribed from Materials & Methods (Eqs 4–9, p10–11). Symbols remapped to DCM state.

**(Eq 6) Reinforcement / cluster-growth rate — the port target:**
```
k_add(t) = k_add0 · ( Σ_{i=1}^{n_eng} a_i(t) ) · f_available(t)
a_i(t)   = 1 if F_bond,i(t) ≥ F_t , else 0
f_available(t) = (n_max − n(t)) / n_max
```
DCM mapping: `F_bond,i` = tension on cadherin bond *i* within a face-pair junction cluster (from the trans-bond spring, below); `n_eng` = currently engaged bonds on that face-pair; `n(t)` = bonds in the cluster; `n_max` = cadherin pool per contact face (derive from surface density × contact-face area, NOT a fitted integer). `k_add0` feeds a new junction config field `junction.k_add0` (s⁻¹). Fire it in the same Gillespie/event loop that already handles cadherin bind/unbind.

**(Eq 4) Bond force (shared-load Hookean spring):**
```
F_bond,i = K_bond · ( x_i − x_partner )
```
DCM mapping: `K_bond` = cadherin trans-bond stiffness (Rakshit); `x_i − x_partner` = extension of the trans-bond across the two apposed cell faces (node-face gap along the bond). Total junction load is shared **equally** across engaged bonds (paper's explicit assumption, Discussion) — so adding a bond lowers per-bond load and lengthens catch-bond lifetime. This is the load-sharing DCM already intends; Eq 4 is the concrete per-bond form.

**(Eq 9) Elastic-substrate force balance (for M2 substrate BC):**
```
x_sub = K_bond · ( Σ_{i=1}^{n_eng} x_i ) / ( K_sub + n_eng · K_bond )
```
DCM mapping: `x_sub` = displacement of the compliant substrate node under a basal adhesion patch; `K_sub` = substrate spring (config field `substrate.K_sub`, pN/nm → convert to SI N/m for DCM's meter units). Use this as the reaction of a Winkler-type elastic foundation under adhered basal DCM nodes.

**(Eq 5) Bell slip off-rate — port with CAUTION, as CROSS-CHECK only:**
```
k_off,i = k_off0 · exp( F_bond,i / F_b )
```
DCM already uses the **Rakshit catch-bond**, which is mechanistically superior for cadherin. Do NOT overwrite it. Keep Eq 5 as an *oracle* form for the integrin/FA-like limit and for parity tests, never as the cadherin runtime off-rate.

**(Eq 7) Linear (Hill-limit) force–velocity — NOT ported to DCM.**
`v_f = v_u·(1 − K_sub·x_sub/(n_m·F_s))`. DCM has no retrograde actin flow; this belongs to FF. Listed only for completeness / M3's threshold ratio.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce (as DCM scenarios)

- **P1 — Compliant-substrate durotaxis run:** one DCM cell adhered to an **elastic substrate** (Winkler springs `K_sub` under basal nodes, Eq 9) instead of a rigid plane. Sweep `K_sub` over {2.5, 4, 6.8, 14, 53, 106} pN/nm (→ SI). Turn M1 reinforcement ON. Measure: (a) time for basal junction/adhesion slip to fall to 20% of free (M3), (b) substrate deformation at that time. This is the DCM analogue of the paper's Fig 7 latency + deformation curves.
- **P2 — Reinforcement ON/OFF ablation:** same cell, junction/basal cluster with vs without Eq-6 recruitment (paper Fig 6). Expect: OFF → persistent load-and-fail oscillation of contact deformation; ON → stable plateau. This is a **stability regression test** for the junction interface, directly reproducing their Fig 6 qualitative result.
- **P3 — Microneedle / local traction analogue:** a stiffness-calibrated point probe (`K_sub` spring) pressed onto one region of the cell surface; track local deformation build-up (their Fig 2). Usable as a **local-adhesion AFM-like scenario** to test junction/adhesion reinforcement in isolation from bulk turgor.
- **P4 — Aggregate compaction cross-check:** in the N≈400 aggregate, swap age-gated maturation for force-gated Eq-6 recruitment on the cadherin junctions and re-run the loose→compact test. Question to answer: does force-gated recruitment change the compaction *rate* where age-gating did not? (S4 said aggregate-σ bypassed age maturation; force-gating ties recruitment to actual load and may couple differently — worth an explicit test, not a foregone conclusion.)

## 6. RISKS / caveats (and how to adapt without breaking physiological-baseline)

- **Different organism, different molecule, in-vitro:** *Aplysia* neuronal growth cone, apCAM–ConA adhesion, 14 °C sea water — not living MCF7. **Adapt:** port the *mechanism form* (force-gated recruitment, saturating pool, kinematic maturation threshold) and re-anchor every constant to MCF7/cadherin values from our KB; use their numbers only as oracle shapes. Never KB-register the *Aplysia* parameter values as DCM defaults.
- **Slip-bond vs our catch-bond:** their off-rate is pure Bell **slip** (Eq 5); they explicitly state no evidence for catch bonds in apCAM–ConA. DCM's cadherin is a **catch-bond (Rakshit)** — mechanistically richer. **Do NOT** replace it. Use Eq 6 for the *addition* half only; keep Rakshit for removal. (This is the fine-grained-over-lumped rule.)
- **1-D lumped vs 3-D surface mesh:** every quantity is scalar 1-D. **Adapt:** apply Eq 4/Eq 9 **per contact face-pair / per basal node patch** on the mesh (a bundle of trans-bonds per face), so load-sharing is local to the interface, not a single global spring. Do not collapse the whole cell-cell interface to one scalar clutch — that would be the lumped shortcut we reject.
- **`k_add0` is fit per-stiffness (bimodal) in the paper — a fitting red flag.** **Do NOT** import the bimodal `k_add0(K_sub)` curve; that is fitted to their data and would violate no-fitting. Set a single physiological `k_add0` and let stiffness dependence **emerge** from the F ≥ F_t gate. If it does not emerge, surface to PI rather than fitting.
- **No membrane/cortex/turgor coupling:** the model omits everything DCM does well. So the reinforcement law must be added **on top of** the fully physiological DCM baseline (turgor Π₀, γ, K_A, η all ON) — never run the durotaxis scenario on a stripped cell. Their "stable steady state" is a 1-D artifact-free result; on the native full DCM cell the junction reinforcement must be validated against the *pressurized* interface, not a floppy one.
- **`n_m` grows linearly with stiffness in their fit** (motor reinforcement). This is the FF/cortex side, not DCM structure. For DCM's γ-generation open problem it is only an *anchor* (γ scales with engaged-myosin number × F_s = 6 pN) — a supporting datum, not a mechanism to port here.
- **Relevance ceiling:** even fully adopted, M1–M3 touch the *contact/adhesion interface and substrate BC*, not the DCM bulk continuum. This paper cannot advance γ-generation, cytokinesis, blebs, or membrane–cortex coupling. Its one strong contribution is a better (force-gated, saturating) junction-maturation law and a compliant-substrate durotaxis scenario.
