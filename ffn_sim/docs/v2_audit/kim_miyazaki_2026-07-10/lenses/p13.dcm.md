# DCM lens — p13

Paper: Saraswathibhatla et al. 2025, "Swirling motion of breast cancer cells radially aligns collagen fibers to enable collective invasion" (bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.01.31.635980). Chaudhuri (Stanford, experiment) + Taeyoon Kim (Purdue, fiber-network BD sim). **NOT peer-reviewed.**

**Bottom line for DCM (relevance ≈ 2/5).** This paper's mechanistic engine is a fiber-ECM Brownian-dynamics model — that is FF territory, not DCM. But its *cell-level* observations are directly about a MCF10AT spheroid (~250 cells, ~100 µm) at an ECM interface, driven by exactly DCM's three ingredients: **E-cadherin/p120 cell-cell junction + actomyosin cortex contractility + β1-integrin/FAK cell-ECM adhesion.** The single most valuable DCM import is **"swirling"** — an *emergent tangential (shear) collective rotation* of interfacial cells that (i) is a clean validation target for whether DCM's cadherin-junction + cortex aggregate produces the right collective-motion mode, and (ii) reframes the open **compaction-RATE / T1-rearrangement** problem: the real active rearrangement mode at an aggregate surface is **tangential neighbor shear**, not radial pulling. There are **no DCM parameters or force-laws to import** (all quantitative model constants are ECM-fiber constants → FF). The contribution is scenario geometry, validation targets, and a mechanistic reframe.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

**M1 — Swirling as an emergent-collective-motion validation mode for the cadherin-junction aggregate (aggregate σ / junction module).**
Cells at the spheroid–ECM interface migrate *tangentially* to the interface in a non-coherent rotational ("swirling") manner — dominant tangential velocity, small radial velocity — on days 1/3/5 (Fig. 3c,f,i). This motion is **abolished by Ecad KD or p120 KD** (cells "stuck", no tangential motion, Fig. 3j–n) and by **blebbistatin/Y-27632** (myosin/ROCK), and confirmed in vivo (4T1 intravital: control swirls, Ecad KD does not, p=0.0274 vs ns 0.3456; Fig. 3q). **Why it beats DCM-now:** DCM currently treats the cadherin junction as a compaction/cohesion device measured only through porosity/contact and aggregate-σ. It has no *tangential collective-flow* observable. Swirling is the natural read-out that the junction + cortex are producing *shear at the interface, not just normal cohesion* — a discriminating test the current gates miss. Adopt as a new DCM aggregate observable: decompose surface-node velocities in spheroid-polar coordinates and check ⟨|v_tang|⟩ ≫ ⟨|v_rad|⟩ near the free surface, and that this vanishes when the junction is disabled.

**M2 — Reframe the compaction-RATE / T1 open problem as *shear-driven tangential neighbor exchange* (T1 rearrangement escalation, junction module).**
The paper's central surprise is that the *active* cell mode at the aggregate boundary is tangential shear, and that this shear — not radial contraction — is what drives long-range remodeling. DCM's open item is "loose→compact needs cadherin T1 rearrangement escalation, not aggregate-σ." This paper says the physiological rearrangement lever is **tangential (circumferential) relative sliding of neighbors under a load-sharing, actomyosin-powered junction** — i.e. the T1 topology-flip should be triggered by tangential shear stress across a junction interface, gated by cortex contractility, not by a scalar aggregate σ. **Why it beats DCM-now:** aggregate-σ compaction was shown (memory) to be junction-independent and drag-limited (~100 s vs real 24–48 h); a shear-driven, junction-mediated T1 mechanism is the fine-grained alternative the hard-rule demands, and this paper is direct evidence that the physiological rearrangement is shear-mode.

**M3 — Requirement triad as a mechanistic gate set (junction + cortex + substrate-adhesion).**
Collective motion needs ALL THREE, each independently knocked out with a distinct phenotype (Fig. 5, Ext.10):
- Ecad/p120 (junction): KD → no motion at all.
- Myosin/ROCK (cortex γ generation): blebb/Y27 → swirling ↓, deformation ↓, no remodeling.
- β1-integrin (cell-ECM): block → swirling persists but reduced, remodeling ↓.
- FAK (adhesion signaling/maturation): inhibit → no swirling, no remodeling.
**Why it beats DCM-now:** gives DCM a clean multi-perturbation validation matrix — turning off each DCM module (junction stiffness→0, cortex γ→0, substrate/FA adhesion→0) should reproduce the *ordering* of phenotype severity, not just a single pass/fail. This is a mechanistic cross-check that DCM's three compartments are wired with the right dependency structure.

**M4 — Basement-membrane as a mechanical insulator (membrane / stiff-shell scenario; membrane–cortex coupling analog).**
An intact laminin-332 BM around MCF10A acini *shields* the surrounding ECM: cells still move tangentially, but produce NO col1 deformation/alignment; once the BM is breached (day 3), radial deformation + remodeling + invasion follow (day 7) (Fig. 6). **Why it beats DCM-now:** DCM has a membrane compartment (K_A, κ_m) but no concept of a *stiff outer shell that decouples internal cortex forces from the outside environment*. This is a ready DCM scenario: wrap the aggregate (or a single cell) in a high-K_A / high-κ_m BM layer and show that cortex-generated tangential stress does NOT transmit to an external ECM until the shell is perforated. It directly informs the membrane–cortex coupling open problem: a stiff area-incompressible shell can screen cortical shear.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

**Honest caveat:** Eqs. 3–9 are the fiber-network BD engine (Langevin, fluctuation-dissipation, cylinder drag, harmonic stretch/bend, Bell slip-bond crosslinker). These belong to **FF**, not DCM — DCM is a surface-mesh continuum, not a bead-rod filament network. None port *as-is* to DCM nodes/faces. Two are worth transcribing for conceptual mapping only:

**Eq. 3 — Overdamped Langevin force balance (already DCM's integration form).**
```
F_i − ζ_i·(dr_i/dt) + F_iᵀ = 0
```
Maps onto DCM's own overdamped node update: F_i = sum of DCM nodal forces (membrane K_A + bending κ_m + cortex tension γ + turgor Π + contact penalty + junction), ζ_i = per-node drag derived from cytoplasm η (DCM already does this: per-node drag from η=65 Pa·s). This confirms DCM's integrator class is the correct overdamped limit; no change needed, just cross-check that DCM's ζ is dimensionally the drag on a node the way Kim's ζ is drag on a cylinder element (Eq. 5, `ζ = 3πμ r_c (3 + 2r_0/r_c)/5`) — different geometry, same role.

**Eq. 2 — Radial alignment index (observable, port as a DCM *measurement* on the aggregate boundary / any coupled ECM).**
```
radial alignment index = |cos(θ_f − θ_n)|
```
θ_f = local direction (here fiber; for DCM: local surface-deformation or a coupled ECM fiber direction), θ_n = spheroid-boundary outward normal. 1 = radial, 0 = tangential. **DCM use:** compute on the interfacial deformation field of a DCM aggregate to classify whether emergent boundary stress is radial or tangential — the diagnostic that would let a DCM+ECM run reproduce the paper's radial-vs-tangential distinction.

**Emergent, NOT an equation to code:** negative normal stress (shear → contractile normal stress of comparable magnitude, Janmey 2007 ref 24). This is a property of the *fibrous ECM*, not the cell surface. DCM should NOT hand-code it; it would appear only in a coupled ECM (FF) layer. Flag: do not lump it into a DCM cell force.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce as DCM scenarios

**G1 — Aggregate-at-ECM-interface, equatorial-plane polar decomposition (primary DCM scenario).**
DCM N≈400 aggregate (or the ~250-cell/100-µm spheroid), place at a free/ECM boundary. In the equatorial plane, decompose interfacial cell (surface-node) velocities into radial and tangential components in polar coordinates about the aggregate centroid, averaged within one cell length (~20 µm) of the boundary. **Pass criterion (from paper): ⟨|v_tang|⟩ ≫ ⟨|v_rad|⟩** (swirling), speeds ~0.05–0.2 µm/min. This is directly runnable on a DCM aggregate today (no ECM needed) as a junction+cortex collective-motion test.

**G2 — Junction / cortex / adhesion knockout matrix (M3 gate).**
Run G1 four times: baseline; cadherin-junction disabled; cortex γ → 0 (myosin off); substrate/FA adhesion off. Reproduce the phenotype ordering — junction-off → no motion; cortex-off → motion collapses; adhesion-off → motion persists but reduced.

**G3 — Annular shear boundary condition (dipole mode) — for the coupled DCM⊗ECM / FF cross-check.**
Concentric annulus: outer R=200 µm (fixed), inner R=50 µm (spheroid surrogate), slab height 1 µm. Apply **dipole** tangential shear at the inner boundary: rotation sense set by circumferential angle — CW if `(angle from +x, CCW) mod 60° < 30°`, else CCW; ramp 0.001°/step until fiber tension > 100 pN. Only the dipole mode (not stochastic/uniform) reproduces experiment. **For DCM:** this is the boundary condition a DCM aggregate's *surface tractions* should impose on a surrounding ECM — the inner boundary IS the DCM cell-surface. Use it to validate that DCM-generated interfacial shear, fed to an ECM layer, yields the radial-alignment response. (Primarily an FF/ECM protocol; DCM supplies the inner-boundary traction pattern.)

**G4 — BM-insulator scenario (M4).**
Single cell or small acinus wrapped in a stiff area-incompressible BM shell (laminin-332 analog, high K_A/κ_m). Show cortex tangential stress does NOT transmit to an external ECM/substrate while the shell is intact; introduce a perforation (breach) and show radial stress transmission resumes. Timing anchor: acini BM breached by day 3, collective invasion by day 7.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Regime mismatch: engine is ECM, not cell.** The paper's quantitative model is a collagen fiber-network BD sim (Kim lab). Its parameters, equations, and force-laws are FF, not DCM. Do not import any fiber constant into a DCM cell field. DCM's contribution here is scenario + emergent-observable validation, not new cell physics.
- **In-vitro reconstituted acIPN + MCF10AT / 4T1, not living-MCF7 mechanics.** Bulk tissue moduli (1.4/4.7 kPa) and plasticity (15/32%) are hydrogel/tissue = cells+ECM composite, NOT single-cell cortex/turgor setpoints. Using them as a DCM *cell* modulus would violate the physiological-baseline rule (wrong compartment). They are legitimate only as the *external environment* stiffness in a coupled scenario.
- **2D-equatorial / quasi-2D slab (1 µm height) sim vs DCM's full 3D surface mesh.** The annular sim is quasi-2D; the swirling is measured in the equatorial plane. DCM is 3D — reproduce swirling in the 3D aggregate but sample the equatorial band to compare. Don't collapse DCM to 2D.
- **Cell type: MCF10AT (pre-malignant) / 4T1 (mouse) / MDA-MB-231**, not MCF7. Swirling is a *collective* junction-dependent phenomenon; MCF7 (our target) is E-cadherin-positive and epithelial, so the junction-dependence should transfer, but absolute speeds/rates are cell-line specific — treat 0.05–0.2 µm/min as an order-of-magnitude target, not a fit datum (no fitting rule).
- **Negative normal stress must not be lumped into a DCM cell force.** It is an emergent ECM-network property. Coding it as a phenomenological cell-surface force would be a lumped-proxy violation of the mechanistic hard-rule. It appears only if/when DCM couples to a fine-grained ECM (FF collagen Mikado).
- **bioRxiv preprint, not peer-reviewed.** Flag verdict = CHECK before citing any number in a deliverable. Fiber-model params are *model* (chosen) values, not measured — model-contract reference only, never empirical constants.
- **Swirling as T1-rearrangement lever (M2) is a hypothesis-import, not a validated DCM result.** The paper shows tangential shear is the active interface mode; it does NOT show a cadherin T1 topology-flip rate. Adopting shear-driven tangential neighbor exchange is a mechanistically-motivated design direction for the open compaction-rate problem — surface to PI as a scoped mechanism change, do not silently wire it into a gate.
