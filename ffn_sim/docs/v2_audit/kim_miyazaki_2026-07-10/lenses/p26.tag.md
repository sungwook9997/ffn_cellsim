# TAG-validation lens — p26

**Paper:** Matsuda, Jung, Sato, Kobayashi, Yamagishi, Kim, Yajima (2024) — "Myosin-induced F-actin fragmentation facilitates contraction of actin networks." *Cytoskeleton* 81(8):339–355. DOI 10.1002/cm.21848.
**Lab:** Taeyoon Kim (Purdue, agent-based actomyosin Brownian dynamics) + Junichiro Yajima (UTokyo, in-vitro reconstitution). Open access.

---

## Paper kind

**Hybrid: experimental (in-vitro reconstitution) + simulation (agent-based Brownian dynamics).** Not analytic — there is no closed-form law offered as theory; the "500 pN" severing criterion and "5 pN/head" stall are *cited single-molecule/model constants*, not derived here.

Consequences for how the numbers may be used:
- The **in-vitro measured** items (fragment retraction speed-ratio ≥2×; qualitative biphasic contraction vs ATP; cluster-size trends vs anillin/myosin) → **overlay / cross-check targets only, never fit.**
- The **simulation-output** curves (contraction speed µm²/s, moving speed µm/s, severing events/s, # clusters, all vs R_M/R_ACP/k₂₀/⟨L_f⟩) are **model output on a 20×20×0.1 µm periodic slab of a *different* system (Taeyoon Kim's own framework, the same lineage we treat as an on-demand FF oracle)** → **comparison-only, never a truth source and never a gate band.** Reproducing them would be self-validating one agent-based model against another.
- The two **cited constants** (F_frag = 500 pN, f_stall = 5 pN/head) are the genuinely reusable, source-anchored quantities → **parameter candidates** (with the caveat that full provenance / Table S1 is in the un-ingested SI).

This is squarely **FF-regime** physics (fine-grained actin + Bell's-law ACP + bipolar myosin catch-bond + excluded volume + a *tension-induced severing rule FF does not yet implement*). DCM relevance is LOW/indirect.

## New gate candidates (all "candidate — PI-gate review", none authored)

1. **proposed_vg_id = VG-FF-actin-tension-sever** *(verification, FF)*
   - Observable: an FF actin segment under extensional tension is removed/severed when F_ext exceeds the threshold; below it, it is retained.
   - Band/value: **F_frag = 500 pN** (Tsuda 1996), hard threshold; verification is that the *rule fires correctly*, not a fitted band.
   - Engine: **FF** (new force law — FF does not yet have tension-induced severing).
   - Nature: this is really a **mechanism to add + a verification of it**, not an emergent-observable gate. Quote: "a filament segment is removed when its extensional tensile force exceeds 500 pN" (p.4; p.12 worked estimate: ~60 heads, ~300 pN avg, subset >500 pN).
   - Caveat: this replaces a would-be lumped "severing rate" with a mechanistic per-segment force criterion — aligned with the architectural principle. Adopt the *rule*; do not adopt the paper's severing-events/s curves as bands.

2. **proposed_vg_id = VG-FF-sever-retraction-jump** *(cross-check, FF)*
   - Observable: upon a tension-induced sever event during contraction, the freed fragment's retraction speed jumps relative to its pre-event speed.
   - Value: **S_after/S_before ≥ 2** (diagnostic of tension release, not buckling), observed in **8/10** in-vitro events (Fig 5c, p.10).
   - Engine: **FF** — measurable at our scale once severing is implemented (track a fragment centroid, compare pre/post speed).
   - Class: **experimental cross-check / overlay** — a *qualitative signature* (≥2× fold-change), not a tuned magnitude. Defensible as a "does the mechanism produce the right kinematic signature" check; NOT a fit target.
   - Caveat: only meaningful after VG-FF-actin-tension-sever exists; stage together.

**Explicitly NOT proposed as gates** (would violate the philosophy): the contraction-speed/moving-speed/severing-events/#clusters curves vs R_M/R_ACP/k₂₀/⟨L_f⟩ — these are simulation output of a sibling agent-based model on a bespoke 2D reconstitution geometry, arbitrary-unit-adjacent, and would be self-validation. The biphasic-vs-ATP optimum (~100 µM) is a real experimental phenomenon but at network scale with assay-specific geometry — at most a future qualitative overlay, no defensible single-cell band.

## Oracle candidates

**None.** The paper offers no closed-form law suitable as an analytic cross-check. The nearest thing to an equation is the worked order-of-magnitude estimate (⟨L_f⟩=2.6 µm ⇒ ~370 monomers, R_M=0.32 ⇒ ~60 heads, half bound × 5 pN ⇒ ~300 pN average, tail >500 pN, p.12) — this is a useful **sanity-gate consistency check** for FF ("does a 2.6 µm filament in a high-connectivity network accumulate O(100 pN)–O(500 pN) tension?"), but it is a back-of-envelope, not a validatable analytic ground truth. Note it in the FF severing sanity-gate docstring, do not elevate to an oracle. Bell's law (ACP) and PCM/Hill (myosin) are already our mechanistic runtime, not new oracles.

## Duplicates / notes

- **Bell's-law ACP unbinding** and **myosin catch-bond force-velocity** are already mechanistic in our stack (MC-U2-fa-motor-clutch Bell-Evans; CLAUDE.md mandates Stam-Hocky bipolar minifilament + Hill + Rakshit catch-bond). This paper *uses* those mechanisms but adds nothing to their bands — the paper itself states anillin cross-linker kinetics are unknown (p.15), so no ACP-lifetime band is offered.
- The **Taeyoon Kim agent-based framework** is the same lineage we treat as an on-demand FF cross-check oracle (per the "oracle is cross-check, not truth" rule and the DCM⊗ECM–Kim comparison in memory). Reproducing its curves would validate FF against another simulation — do it as an on-demand cross-check at most, never register its outputs as truth.
- **Genuinely new to our KB:** *tension-induced F-actin fragmentation/severing* as a distinct force-relaxation mechanism (separate from buckling and from ACP unbinding). FF currently has no severing at all. This is the paper's real contribution to our validation surface — one new mechanism (F_frag=500 pN rule) + its kinematic signature (≥2× retraction jump).
- **Provenance hole:** Table S1, Eqs S1–S3, Fig S4 (catch-bond curve) are in the un-ingested SI. Treat all simulation constants except the two cited literature values as incomplete-provenance.
