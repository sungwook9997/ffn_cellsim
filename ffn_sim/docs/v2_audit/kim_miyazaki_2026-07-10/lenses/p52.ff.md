# FF lens — p52

**Paper:** Kubota, Ogawa, Miyazaki, … Ishiwata, Suzuki (2021) *Microscopic Temperature Control Reveals Cooperative Regulation of Actin–Myosin Interaction by Drebrin E*, Nano Lett. 21, 9526–9533. DOI 10.1021/acs.nanolett.1c02955.

**One-line verdict for FF:** In-vitro HMM/actin motility assay (no simulation). It hands FF two genuinely usable things — (a) a clean **HMM sliding-velocity vs temperature dataset (~7–8 µm/s at ~28–30 °C → ~15 µm/s at 37 °C, unloaded)** as a myosin-motor sanity/validation anchor, and (b) a **mechanistic template for an allosteric actin-side-binding-protein regulator** (drebrin E) that inhibits actomyosin sliding **cooperatively (Hill n: ~1 → 4.0 at 37 °C, IC50 few nM → >30 nM, Kd 6→32 nM)** by changing the actin helical pitch (36→40 nm) rather than by competing off myosin. It is NOT a cortex/crosslinker/clutch parameter source and does not touch the native-scale open problems (γ-floor, contraction, grid-drag) directly. Relevance to FF: **moderate-low (2/5)** — a motor calibration datum plus an optional future ABP-regulator module; no equation FF must adopt today.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Myosin motor: unloaded sliding-velocity(T) as a validation anchor (adopt as test, not runtime change)
- **FF module:** `ff/` myosin II Stam–Hocky minifilament + Hill (1938) force–velocity.
- **What:** The assay directly measures the **unloaded actin sliding velocity of HMM** (skeletal, fast) = Hill's `v_max` (the F=0 intercept of the force–velocity curve) as a function of temperature. FF's Hill motor has a `v_max` (unloaded walking speed toward the barbed end) parameter that is currently set from other sources; this paper is an independent in-vitro cross-check for that intercept and its Arrhenius temperature scaling.
- **Why it helps:** FF has no direct experimental hook on its motor `v_max` calibration at physiological 37 °C for a fast skeletal myosin. This is a clean single-filament number. **Caveat (see §6): it is HMM/skeletal muscle myosin, not NMII** — use it as an order-of-magnitude/temperature-scaling sanity check, not as the NMII cortex `v_max`.

### M2 — New optional module: allosteric actin-conformation ABP regulator (cooperative motor gate)
- **FF module:** a **new side-binding regulator** in the crosslinker/ABP family (sits alongside α-actinin/filamin), coupling to the myosin duty via a **per-filament conformational state**, NOT via steric competition.
- **What the paper establishes mechanistically:**
  1. Drebrin E stays **bound** during sliding (Qdot F.I. unchanged, Friedman p=0.344) → regulation is **not** occupancy loss / competitive displacement.
  2. It changes the **actin helical half-pitch 36 → 40 nm** (ref 22, AFM) and the pitch change **propagates ≤2 helical repeats** past each bound molecule (ref 23) → an **allosteric, cooperative** modification of the track the myosin walks on.
  3. The net effect on the motor is **cooperative inhibition of sliding** (Hill n up to 4.0 at 37 °C).
- **Why it beats the current FF picture:** FF today models ABPs only as **crosslinks** (mechanical bonds with Bell–Evans off-rates). It has **no representation of an ABP that regulates the myosin duty by changing the filament's conformational/track state**. This is the same class of mechanism as **tropomyosin/troponin** thin-filament regulation (the paper draws the analogy explicitly). If FF ever needs thin-filament-style motor gating (e.g. Ca²⁺/regulatory control of cortical contractility, or spatial patterning of where myosin can engage), this paper is the fine-grained template: a **per-actin-subunit "on/off" conformational field** whose neighbor-coupling gives emergent cooperativity, feeding the local myosin attachment probability. **Do not adopt now** as a lumped multiplier on force — that would violate the mechanistic rule; adopt only as an explicit per-subunit state with nearest-neighbor coupling if the physics is needed.

### M3 — Do NOT adopt: temperature as a runtime driver
- The paper's headline lever (heat pulse) is an **experimental probe**, not a cortex mechanism. FF runs isothermal at 37 °C (physiological baseline). The temperature axis here is only useful to (i) pick the 37 °C values and (ii) sanity-check Arrhenius scaling of `v_max`. Introducing a temperature field into FF would be scope creep with no physiological cortex justification.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

The paper contains **no force law, no bond-kinetics rate law, no motor force–velocity law**. Everything is empirical/geometric. Two schemes are portable as *regulator kinetics*, not mechanics:

### E1 — Equilibrium occupancy of an actin-side-binding regulator (langmuir)
```
p_drebrin = [D] / (K_d + [D])
```
- Map to FF: `[D]` = free regulator concentration (µM-scale config), `K_d` = dissociation constant, `p_drebrin` = fractional occupancy of side-binding sites along a filament. In a fine-grained FF port this is the **steady-state fraction of actin subunits carrying the bound regulator**; the microscopic realization is per-site on/off with `k_on[D]` and `k_off = k_on·K_d`. FF would sample bound sites stochastically (like clutch/crosslink binding), not impose `p` directly.
- Stoichiometry: **1 regulator : 5 actin subunits** (saturating), N-globular domain → pointed end, C-loop → barbed end (orientation matters if the pitch field is directional).

### E2 — Cooperative dose–response of motor sliding (Hill, phenomenological — an ORACLE, not runtime)
```
v(C) = 1 − [ C^n (1 − b) ] / ( k^n + C^n )
```
- `v` = normalized sliding velocity (1 = uninhibited), `C` = [regulator], `k` = IC50, `n` = Hill coefficient, `b` = residual velocity floor at saturating regulator.
- **This is a lumped phenomenology and must live in `validation/oracles/`, never in the runtime** (hard rule: mechanistic over proxy). Its role: if FF implements M2 as an explicit per-subunit conformational field with neighbor coupling (E3-style), the **emergent** `v` vs `[D]` curve from the fine-grained run must reproduce this Hill form — n≈1 (non-cooperative) at low coupling, n≈4 at strong neighbor coupling. It is the acceptance oracle for the cooperativity, exactly the pattern the project uses for Chan-Odde/Bell-Evans.

### E3 — Cooperativity mechanism to reproduce (nearest-neighbor conformational coupling)
- The physical origin of n≈4 is: each bound regulator flips the local actin pitch and **that flip propagates ≤2 helical repeats to neighbors**, so binding is cooperative and the myosin-inhibiting conformation spreads. In an explicit FF port this is a **1-D Ising-like coupling along the filament backbone**: subunit state `s_i ∈ {native, drebrin-pitch}`, coupling `J` to neighbors within ±2 repeats, field from bound-regulator occupancy. Emergent Hill n is set by `J` (coupling range/strength), NOT tuned to 4 — 4 is the validation target.
- **Temperature enters here microscopically**, not as a runtime driver: raising T raises the actin–myosin affinity/engaged-head number (refs 19–21), which competes against the drebrin-pitch inhibition — this is why IC50 and apparent K_d rise with T. FF at fixed 37 °C just uses the 37 °C endpoints.

### E4 — Geometric estimators (methodology only, not FF physics)
- Temperature map: `T(x) = −3.1·ln(x/x0) + 42.9` (°C, x0=1 µm) — IR heater calibration, irrelevant to FF.
- Velocity: `v = |r₂ − r₁| / 0.5 s` over a 5-frame window — this is the **measurement protocol** FF should mirror when it computes a "motor sliding velocity" observable (displacement / fixed window), for apples-to-apples comparison to §5 targets.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce

**Scenario "FF-motility-assay" (a new dev-scale validation scenario, explicitly non-native/non-cortex):**
- Geometry: **gliding assay** — HMM (or FF's minifilament heads) surface-anchored on a plane; short **1–5 µm** actin filaments sliding over them in ATP. FF already has the pieces (surface-anchored motor heads + free filaments); this is a 2-D reduced test bed for the **motor `v_max` observable**.
- Density: calibration used long, dense filaments; velocity assay used short, sparse filaments (single-filament tracking). For FF, sparse (isolated filament) is the right config to read unloaded `v_max`.
- Regulator decoration: bind the M2 regulator at occupancy `p` (E1) from `[D] ∈ {0,10,20,50,250} nM` at K_d(37 °C)=32 nM → sweep to build the emergent `v([D])` Hill curve.
- Measurement window: displacement / **0.5 s** (mirror E4), immobile filaments = velocity 0, so the FF observable is defined identically to the paper.
- **This is a purified-in-vitro cross-check scenario, dev-scale, explicitly labelled non-authoritative** per the native-scale rule — any conclusion still has to be reconfirmed against native cortex behavior where the mechanism is used. Its only job is to calibrate/validate the motor and (optionally) the regulator module in isolation.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Isoform/species mismatch (biggest).** The motor is **HMM = fast skeletal-muscle myosin II**, and the assay is **fully purified in vitro at 25–37 °C**. FF's cortex motor is **non-muscle myosin II (NMII)**, which is ~10–100× slower (unloaded ~0.1–0.5 µm/s) and has a much higher duty ratio. **Do NOT set the NMII cortex `v_max` to 7–15 µm/s.** Use these numbers only as (a) a validation target for a *skeletal-myosin* gliding-assay scenario, and (b) an Arrhenius/temperature-scaling sanity check. Confusing HMM speed for NMII speed would be a category error like the earlier AFM-stiffness artifact.
- **Regulator is neuronal (drebrin E), not a generic cortex ABP.** Drebrin E is a growth-cone/dendritic-spine regulator; MCF7 cortex physiology may not use it. Adopt M2 only as a **template for the class** (allosteric conformation-changing ABP, tropomyosin-like), not as an MCF7 parameter. Registering K_d/Hill in the KB is fine as literature anchors, but they are **not** MCF7 cortex constants.
- **Hill equation is lumped phenomenology.** It must never enter the FF runtime (hard rule). Its only sanctioned home is `validation/oracles/` as the acceptance curve for an emergent, fine-grained neighbor-coupling model (E3). Setting a runtime multiplier `v·Hill([D])` would replace a mechanistic process with a proxy — forbidden.
- **2-D gliding assay vs 3-D cortex.** The motility-assay scenario is a reduced 2-D bench for *motor calibration only*; it is explicitly dev-scale and non-authoritative. Any use of the calibrated motor/regulator in a conclusion must be reconfirmed on the native 38000-filament cortex (`--from-resting --microtubules`).
- **Temperature is a probe, not a mechanism.** FF stays isothermal at the 37 °C physiological baseline; only the 37 °C endpoints are used. Do not build a temperature field into the runtime — no cortex justification, scope creep.
- **No mechanics transcribable.** There is no bending/stretch/off-rate/branch law here. Anyone mining this for a force law will come up empty — the value is the motor velocity number + the cooperative-regulation mechanism template + clean kinetic constants, nothing more.
