# DCM lens — p02

**Paper:** Kim, Hwang & Kamm (2011) "Dynamic Role of Cross-Linking Proteins in Actin Rheology", *Biophys. J.* 101(7):1597–1603, DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2011.08.033.

**One-line honesty statement.** This is a *fine-grained Brownian-dynamics network* paper, not a continuum-cell paper. Its direct home is the **FF engine** (crosslinker Bell kinetics). For **DCM** (surface-mesh continuum) it is **indirect** but NOT empty: its central mechanistic result — *at physiological strain rates, cross-linker **unbinding + rebinding-to-a-different-partner** (turnover), not unfolding, is what produces plastic network reorganization and stress relaxation* — is the exact conceptual engine DCM's biggest open problem needs: the **cadherin-junction T1 rearrangement** that must drive loose→compact aggregate compaction at the real 24–48 h rate (currently drag-limited aggregate-σ, which the project has explicitly ruled invalid for RATE). Everything below is filtered for "genuinely usable at OUR single-cell / N≈400 surface-mesh scale," and I flag lumped-vs-fine-grained tension where it exists.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cadherin-junction **T1 rearrangement** = "unbind-above-threshold → rebind to a DIFFERENT partner" (STRONGEST DCM lever)
- **DCM module:** cell–cell cadherin catch-bond junction interface (`dcm/` junction / Rakshit KU-4.2 cluster) + the aggregate compaction driver.
- **Change:** give the junction cluster a *turnover-to-new-partner* channel, not just unbind→rebind-same-face. The paper (Fig. 4A) shows that below a rupture-force threshold a crosslinker rebinds to the *same* filament (turnover=0, no topology change), but **above ~20 pN nearly all rebind to a *different* filament** (turnover=1) after a *diagonal displacement*. This partner-swap is what irreversibly relieves load and reconfigures the network → plastic strain → stress relaxation. In DCM terms: a node-face cadherin bond that is loaded past its threshold should **release and re-form against a neighbouring face** (the geometric analog of a T1 neighbour exchange), not just re-anchor to its original partner.
- **Why it beats DCM-now:** the project has established (memory: `project-dcm-compaction-turgor-blocked`, `project-dcm-cadherin-cluster-redesign`) that aggregate-σ compaction is a *mechanical end-state only* — its RATE is drag-limited (~100 s), whereas real MCF7 compaction is 24–48 h, gated by **cadherin-mediated T1 rearrangement**, and that "freezing the junction leaves 0.234 unchanged" (σ-compaction is cadherin-independent). This paper supplies the *missing mechanistic law* for that rearrangement: reorganization is **turnover-driven partner-swapping above a load threshold**, with an *emergent, force-dependent* displacement — exactly the fine-grained (non-lumped) escalation the PI scoped for "(b) T1 rearrangement escalation."
- **Fine-grained compliance:** this is the mechanistic option — the junction reconfigures via explicit force-triggered bond turnover, NOT a lumped "compaction rate" fit.

### M2 — Compaction / aggregate-σ relaxation is **cross-link-turnover-limited**, not drag-limited (constitutive closure)
- **DCM module:** aggregate surface tension σ compaction driver + junction cluster kinetics.
- **Change:** replace (or gate) the current drag-limited σ-pull with a relaxation law whose *clock is set by junction turnover rate*, per Eqs. 5–7. The stress a compacting interface can sustain **decays as intact junctions turn over** (`N_ACP` → junction count), and rebinding sets a **finite residual tension plateau** (b/a). This reframes the compaction rate as governed by `k⁰_ub`(cadherin) × contact-age maturation, not by cytoplasm drag — which is precisely the correction the memory notes demand.
- **Why it beats DCM-now:** gives a *physically-clocked* compaction timescale (junction off-rate) instead of a drag artifact, and predicts a **non-zero residual interfacial tension** (rebinding-damped) rather than full relaxation — matching the observed persistence of MCF7 aggregate cohesion.

### M3 — **Load-sharing "supportive framework"** → only a few junctions carry the load and rupture
- **DCM module:** load-sharing cadherin cluster (already have clusters; this refines the load distribution).
- **Change:** model the junction interface so that a **small fraction (~25%)** of bonds bears **~85%** of the load and preferentially ruptures/turns over (Fig. 4, text p.1600). Rupture is concentrated in the high-tension subset; that subset's partner-swap is what cascades into reconfiguration.
- **Why it beats DCM-now:** justifies why compaction can proceed even though most junctions never rupture — reorganization is carried by a stressed minority, so the aggregate stays cohesive (few bonds break at once) while still flowing. This is the load-sharing cluster refinement the DCM redesign wanted.

### M4 — Cortex **nonlinear surface rheology**: strain-stiffening + relaxation + plasticity constitutive closure (optional, altitude-appropriate)
- **DCM module:** actomyosin cortex surface tension γ (currently pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2, elastic).
- **Change:** if/when DCM needs a *rate-dependent* cortex (bleb, aspiration, AFM transients, confinement), replace the constant/elastic γ with the emergent constitutive law this paper measures: two-regime **strain-stiffening** (Eq. 4), **K~γ³** differential stiffening, **stress relaxation to a finite plateau** (Eq. 7), and **plastic strain accumulation** (Eq. 10). Because DCM's cortex is *already* a continuum surface field, a viscoelastic-plastic surface-tension law is at the correct altitude — this is **not** lumping a fine-grained cortex, it is closing an already-continuum field with a literature-anchored nonlinear law.
- **Why it beats DCM-now:** DCM's elastic γ cannot reproduce hysteresis, stress relaxation, or the ~10 Pa reorganization endpoint that real cortex shows under transient load; this supplies all three with published coefficients.
- **Caveat (honest):** this is the most "lumped" of the four; prefer M1–M3 (fine-grained junction turnover) and treat M4 as a closure only where DCM genuinely operates as a continuum surface (it does at cortex level).

### M5 — Negative result: **skip cadherin/crosslinker domain-unfolding** in the junction model
- **DCM module:** junction bond model.
- **Change:** do **not** add Ig-domain unfolding to the cadherin/junction description. The paper's whole point: at physiological strain rate (γ̇_eff ≈ 0.1 s⁻¹) **unfolding is negligible and cannot change topology or relax stress** — only unbinding does (Figs. 3A, 5, 6). This is a *simplification-justifying* finding: the DCM junction needs force-dependent unbinding+rebinding-turnover; the unfolding channel is safe to omit at our rates.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (to surface-mesh / junction-continuum level)

Symbols remapped to DCM state: N_j = number of intact cadherin bonds in a junction interface (paper's `N_ACP`); σ_j = interfacial/aggregate tension the junction sustains (paper's τ); F = per-bond tension (paper's |F_s|); k⁰ = zero-force cadherin off-rate; nodes/faces = DCM mesh.

**Bell slip off-rate for junction bonds (paper Eq. 3)** — cross-check / baseline for the cadherin unbinding channel:
```
k_ub = k⁰_ub · exp( λ_ub · |F| / (k_B T) )          for loaded bond (r ≥ r₀)
     = k⁰_ub                                          for slack bond
```
Map: `k⁰_ub` → DCM cadherin `k_off0`; `λ_ub` → bond mechanical compliance; `|F|` → per-bond junction tension from the node-face penalty + cluster load. **NOTE:** DCM's ratified cadherin is a **catch-bond** (Rakshit KU-4.2); this Bell *slip* form is a **cross-check / degenerate limit only**, not a replacement — do NOT overwrite the catch-bond with this.

**Junction-count decay under held strain (Eq. 5):**
```
N_j(t) = N_j⁰ · exp( -2 k⁰_ub t )
```
Factor 2 = either end can release. This is the clock for M2 — the number of intact cadherin bonds decays exponentially with the off-rate; the compaction/relaxation timescale is `~1/(2 k⁰_ub)`, NOT the drag time.

**Interfacial-stress relaxation ODE + closed form (Eqs. 6–7):**
```
dσ_j/dt = (a·σ_j − b) · dN_j/dt

σ_j(t) = b/a + (σ_j⁰ − b/a) · exp[ a·N_j⁰ ( exp(−2 k⁰_ub t) − 1 ) ]
```
Map: each junction turnover drops interfacial tension by ∝σ_j (the `a·σ_j` term); rebinding limits the drop by constant `b` → **finite residual plateau σ_j → b/a** (with rebinding) vs **σ_j → 0** (without). Port this as the aggregate-σ relaxation closure: a compacting DCM interface relaxes toward a non-zero cohesive tension set by the cadherin rebinding rate.

**Two-regime cortex strain-stiffening (Eq. 4)** — for M4 surface constitutive closure:
```
σ_cortex(γ) = c₁ γ^{x₁} + c₂ γ^{x₂}
```
with c₁=2.5 Pa, x₁=0.4 (bending-dominated, low γ), c₂=500 Pa, x₂=4 (stretch-dominated, high γ). Differential surface modulus `K = dσ/dγ ~ γ³` for γ > ~0.1. Map: γ → cortex surface strain; c₁/x₁ set by bending stiffness (κ_m analog), c₂/x₂ by extensional stiffness (K_A analog).

**Plastic-strain accumulation (Eqs. 8–10)** — irreversible reorganization rate:
```
dγ/dt = c · exp( −11.5 γ )          (restoring force F_r ~ exp(11.5 γ))
γ(t)  = ln{ exp(11.5 γ₀) + 11.5 c t } / 11.5
```
Map: γ → residual (plastic) interfacial/cortex strain retained after unloading; c set by strain rate (c = 0.11 / 0.015 / 0.0005 for γ̇_eff = 10 / 1 / 0.1 s⁻¹). Gives an irreversible-strain rate law the DCM compaction/relaxation can accumulate.

**ACP-arm WLC force-extension (Eq. 1)** — **do NOT port to DCM.** This is the fine-grained per-arm force law (persistence 0.33 nm, saw-tooth unfolding). It belongs to FF; at DCM's continuum altitude the junction is a bond spring, not a WLC-with-Ig-domains. Listed only for completeness.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce (as DCM scenarios)

These are *bulk-rheology* protocols on a 2.8 µm cube; DCM adapts them as **single-cell cortex / two-cell junction** transient tests at native+full-compartment scale.

- **P1 — Stress relaxation (junction/compaction test):** pre-load a two-cell junction interface (or an N≈400 aggregate) to an initial interfacial tension σ_j⁰ (paper τ₀ = 5 Pa), hold contact geometry fixed, watch σ_j relax. **Expect:** relaxation toward a *finite* plateau (rebinding on) vs toward zero (rebinding off) — validates M2/Eq. 7. Directly probes whether DCM's junction turnover gives the right compaction clock.
- **P2 — Plastic-deformation (T1 reorganization test):** shear/compress the aggregate to a set strain (paper γ = 1.0) at γ̇_eff = 0.1 s⁻¹, then **release** and measure retained (plastic) strain. **Expect:** with partner-swap turnover ON, a non-zero residual strain (plastic reorganization); with it OFF, full recovery — validates M1/Eq. 10.
- **P3 — Strain-stiffening (cortex constitutive test):** ramp cortex surface strain, measure σ_cortex(γ) and K=dσ/dγ. **Expect:** stiffening then collapse, K~γ³ — validates M4/Eq. 4. Run on a native full-compartment single cell (turgor+cortex+nucleus ON).
- **P4 — Hysteresis (rate-dependence sanity):** load→unload the junction at three rates (γ̇_eff = 10, 1, 0.1 s⁻¹). **Expect:** larger hysteresis at *lower* rate (more time for turnover), negligible at very high rate — confirms the turnover mechanism is rate-clocked, not artifactual.

## 6. RISKS / caveats (regime mismatch → how to adapt without breaking physiological-baseline)

1. **In-vitro purified filamin network ≠ living MCF7 cortex/junction.** All numbers are reconstituted F-actin + filamin at 12.1 µM, room-T, no motors, no membrane. **Adapt:** use the *mechanism* (turnover→partner-swap→plastic reorg) and the *scaling forms* (Eqs. 4–7, 10), but take absolute Pa values (1 Pa plateau, 10 Pa endpoint, c₁/c₂) as **cross-check oracles only**, not DCM setpoints. The physiological setpoints (γ interphase 0.15 / mitotic 1.5 mN/m, Π₀=40 Pa, η=65 Pa·s) stay from the DCM baseline; this paper does not override them.

2. **Crosslinker (filamin/α-actinin) ≠ cadherin.** The Bell slip params (`k⁰_ub=0.115`, `λ_ub=1.04e-10`) are ACP–actin rupture, not cadherin–cadherin. DCM's junction is a ratified **catch-bond** (Rakshit KU-4.2). **Adapt:** use this paper for the *architecture* of the turnover mechanism (threshold-gated partner-swap, load-sharing minority), keep the catch-bond force law. Do NOT replace the catch-bond with this slip law; the slip form is only a degenerate cross-check.

3. **Fine-grained network vs continuum surface.** Eqs. 1 (WLC arm), the 2.8 µm cube, and the per-bead BD are *below* DCM's altitude. **Adapt:** port only the emergent constitutive laws (Eqs. 4–7, 10) and the mechanism (M1–M3, M5); leave the per-arm mechanics to FF. This keeps DCM at its correct continuum-surface altitude and avoids fake fine-graining.

4. **Rate-rescaling ("k⁰·t_d invariance") is a modeling trick, not physiology.** The paper reaches second-scale behavior by scaling zero-force rates ×n and time ×1/n. **Adapt:** DCM must drive scenarios at the *true* physiological γ̇_eff ≈ 0.1 s⁻¹ with true `k⁰_ub`; do not import the ×100 rate scaling into a production run (it would be an unphysical baseline). Use scaling only as a wall-time sanity device with the result reported at true rates.

5. **No magic-number risk to watch:** the partner-swap threshold (~20 pN), the 11.5 plastic exponent, c₁/c₂ are *fitted in-vitro constants*. If any of these get wired into a DCM production run, they must be **derived/cross-checked**, not tuned to pass compaction gates — flag to PI if a compaction gate would only pass by adjusting them. The mechanism (turnover→partner-swap) is the deliverable; the constants are oracle overlays.

6. **No motors, no membrane (authors' own limitation, p.1602).** This is a passive-crosslinker rheology; DCM cortex has myosin-borne active tension (γ generation open problem). **Adapt:** treat this paper as informing the *passive reorganization / relaxation* channel of the cortex and junctions; it says nothing about active γ generation — that open problem is untouched here.
