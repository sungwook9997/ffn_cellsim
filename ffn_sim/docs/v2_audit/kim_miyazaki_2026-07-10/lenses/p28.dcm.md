# DCM lens — p28

**Paper:** Ishiwata et al. 2017, *Dynamic properties of bio-motile systems with a liquid-crystalline structure*, Mol. Cryst. Liq. Cryst. 647:1, 127–150. DOI 10.1080/15421406.2017.1289445. Review (Ishiwata / Miyazaki group).

**One-line verdict for DCM:** A review; ~two of its three legs are off-scale for us (muscle sarcomere SPOC, in-vivo cardiac SL). But the **Miyazaki artificial-cell contractile-ring** leg (§3) and the **mitotic-spindle continuum-mechanics** leg (§2) directly touch two *named* DCM open problems — **cell division / cytokinetic ring** and **confinement response** — and supply clean validation numbers. Everything usable here is validation-grade or one mechanistic closure law; nothing rewrites the cortex/compaction core.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cytokinetic furrow: constriction rate ∝ ring diameter (constant strain-rate closure) → DCM `division` / cytokinetic-ring module
The single cleanest DCM-scale mechanism in the paper. In cell-sized water-in-oil droplets, purified actin+bundler self-organizes ONE equatorial ring and, with high HMM, "the ring constriction rate was nearly proportional to the initial ring diameter" (p20, Fig. 3-4b), recapitulating the shared feature of contractile rings across animal cell types [refs 67,68 = Carvalho/Turlier line].

- **DCM change:** implement the cytokinetic furrow as an **equatorial band of elevated cortical/line tension** γ_ring on the belt of faces at the cell midplane, and drive it as a **constant-per-length strain rate** ε̇ (each unit arc of the ring shortens at the same fractional rate). This yields absolute closure velocity ∝ current diameter — an emergent, not prescribed, size scaling.
- **Why it beats what DCM does now:** DCM currently has **no cytokinetic ring** (division listed as an open problem; §5 aggregate compaction is the only active-tension surface machinery). A furrow implemented as a lumped "pinch force" would violate the mechanistic rule. A *line-tension band that closes at constant strain rate* is the fine-grained surface-mesh analogue of a myosin-loaded ring and reproduces the measured rate∝diameter law without a magic pinch constant.

### M2 — Cell-sized spherical confinement as a first-class DCM boundary condition → new `confinement` scenario
Rings form only when droplet diameter < ~20 µm ≈ L_p of actin (p20, Fig. 3-3c,d). The physics: when the confining radius drops to the filament persistence length, filaments act as rigid rods and localize to the equator to minimize bending energy.

- **DCM change:** add a **rigid spherical (or channel/well) confinement BC** of radius R_conf that pushes on the surface mesh via the same node-face penalty (Ericson) already used for cell-cell contact, just with a static wall. Run the cell/aggregate under decreasing R_conf.
- **Why:** confinement response is a named DCM open problem and DCM has all the machinery (penalty contact, turgor) to reproduce a wall; this paper gives the geometry and the ~20 µm/≈R=L_p threshold as the reference regime. (The *emergence* of the ring-at-equator from bending is FF-level; DCM reproduces the confinement geometry and the resulting shape/tension response.)

### M3 — Three-timescale active viscoelasticity for stiff inclusions (spindle → nucleus/mitotic-inclusion rheology reference)
Active microrheology of the Xenopus spindle (§2, Fig. 2-5): **elastic solid < 1 s → viscous fluid ~10 s → active elastic-restoring ~minutes**, with |G*| rising 0.1→1 nN/µm over ω = 0.001–10 rad/s and an anomalous **Poisson ratio ~2.0** (vs 0.5 for a passive continuum) and **~2× mechanical anisotropy**.

- **DCM change:** use this as the **rheological target for the DCM nucleus / any active-material inclusion during division**, and as justification that an active mesostructure legitimately shows Poisson > 0.5 and direction-dependent stiffness — i.e. DCM's stiff inclusion should NOT be forced to a passive isotropic E_nuc if it carries active stress. Concretely: a Kelvin–Voigt (short-time elastic) + Maxwell (10-s relaxation) + slow active-restoring term.
- **Why it beats now:** DCM's nucleus is a single passive stiffer inclusion (E_nuc 1–10 kPa, isotropic). This paper shows the mitotic active mesostructure is (i) frequency-dependent, (ii) anisotropic, (iii) super-elastic in Poisson — a checklist for when DCM eventually models the dividing/mitotic interior. **Caveat: MT-based spindle ≠ our actin cortex; use as a rheology *pattern*, not as cortex parameters.**

### M4 — Uni-lamellar giant-liposome shell as the DCM membrane-provenance reference
Inverted-emulsion transfer gives >90% uni-lamellar giant liposomes (§3.2, Fig. 3-5). This is the experimental realisation of DCM's "closed single-bilayer surface enclosing a volume" — a provenance anchor that our membrane compartment (single K_A/κ_m surface) is the right idealisation, and a future comparison substrate for a reconstituted deformable cell.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port

### E1 — Constant-strain-rate ring closure (the rate∝diameter law)
Verbal law (p20): `d(ring size)/dt ∝ initial ring diameter`. Mechanistic form (constant contractile strain rate ε̇):

```
(1/D) dD/dt = −ε̇  = const      ⇒   D(t) = D₀ · exp(−ε̇ · t)
⇒  dD/dt = −ε̇ · D            (absolute rate proportional to current diameter)
```

- **Map to DCM:** `D` = equatorial ring diameter = mean node-ring diameter across the furrow midplane; `D₀` = diameter at furrow onset; `ε̇` = contractile strain rate of the equatorial γ_ring band, **derived from myosin line density and per-motor force**, NOT fit. The band contributes an inward line force per unit arc; the constant-ε̇ closure emerges when contraction is disassembly-limited (ring stays ~constant tension as it shortens). Validation: log D(t) should be linear with slope −ε̇, and the intercept scaling should give dD/dt ∝ D₀.

### E2 — Furrow force balance vs turgor (must-overcome condition)
For the furrow to constrict against the enclosed-volume turgor Π₀ ≈ 40 Pa, the equatorial band's inward Laplace-like traction must exceed the pressure. Ring line tension λ_ring (force, N) across a furrow of radius r produces inward force per unit membrane area ~ λ_ring·κ_furrow (κ = geodesic curvature of the furrow). Constriction requires:

```
λ_ring / r  ≳  Π_internal  (≈ Π₀ + osmotic response as V drops)
```

- **Map to DCM:** `λ_ring` = line tension on the belt of furrow edges; `r` = current furrow radius (= D/2); `Π_internal` = DCM enclosed-volume/turgor term. **Physiological-baseline hook:** DCM must run the furrow ON TOP of the real resting turgor (Π₀≈40 Pa, γ≈0.15 mN/m interphase → myosin up-regulated toward mitotic γ≈1.5 mN/m), not on a floppy zero-pressure shell. As the cell divides, the enclosed-volume constraint should track two forming compartments.

### E3 — Confinement / ring-localisation threshold (bending-energy)
Worm-like-chain bending energy of a filament of bending rigidity κ = k_B T · L_p confined at radius R:

```
E_bend = (κ/2) ∫ (∂θ/∂s)² ds ,   κ = k_B·T·L_p
ring-at-equator favored when   R_conf ≲ L_p   (≈ 2.5–7.5 µm radius ⇔ ≤20 µm diameter)
```

- **Map to DCM:** sets the confinement scenario threshold R_conf ≈ L_p ≈ 5–15 µm. (Bending term itself is FF-level; DCM uses it only to pick the confinement radius sweep range and to predict equatorial tension concentration.)

### E4 — Anisotropic / anomalous-Poisson elastic response (spindle inclusion)
Reported: pole-to-pole Poisson ν ≈ 2.0, ~2× stiffness anisotropy, |G*|(ω) 0.1→1 nN/µm.

- **Map to DCM:** if/when the mitotic interior is modelled, the inclusion constitutive law must permit direction-dependent modulus E_∥ ≈ 2·E_⊥ and ν > 0.5 (active). Not a passive Hookean E_nuc. Flag as a *future* constitutive extension, not a current default change.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce (DCM scenarios)

1. **Cytokinesis scenario (`division`):** single MCF7-like cell at full physiological baseline (Π₀≈40 Pa, γ≈0.15→1.5 mN/m, nucleus, cytoplasm η, membrane), tag an equatorial belt of faces as the furrow, ramp γ_ring / apply the constant-ε̇ contraction, watch D(t). **Success = the cell pinches into two closed sub-volumes; D(t) exponential; dD/dt ∝ D₀ across a range of R_cell.**
2. **Confinement scenario (`confinement`):** enclose the cell in a rigid sphere/well of radius R_conf and sweep R_conf from ~15 µm down to ~5 µm (through L_p). Measure equatorial tension concentration and shape change. Reference threshold: response sharpens at R_conf ≈ L_p (≈ Ø 20 µm).
3. **Active-microrheology probe (validation harness):** apply sinusoidal compression to the DCM inclusion/cell over ω = 0.001–10 rad/s, extract |G*|(ω) and phase δ(ω), and check for the three-regime crossover (elastic <1 s / viscous ~10 s / active ~min). Reusable as a general DCM viscoelastic-signature test.
4. **Anisotropic force–shortening loop:** compress along two orthogonal axes and compare force–deformation hysteresis (long-axis vs short-axis) — reproduces the spindle's ~2× anisotropy protocol; usable to characterise any DCM anisotropic inclusion.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Purified in-vitro, not living MCF7.** The ring result is reconstituted actin+HMM (±bundler) in a **water-in-oil droplet with a lipid monolayer** — no cortex-membrane coupling, no turgor, no nucleus, no full compartment stack. **Adapt without violating physiological-baseline:** DCM must run the furrow ON the full physiological baseline (real Π₀, γ, viscosity, nucleus) — reproduce the *rate∝diameter closure law and the ~20 µm confinement threshold as qualitative/scaling targets*, not the absolute droplet numbers. The droplet lacks the turgor the furrow must overcome (E2), so DCM is the *harder, more physical* test.
- **Spindle is microtubule-based, Xenopus egg extract.** The 10–100 Pa modulus, 2× anisotropy, ν≈2.0 belong to an **MT/motor active nematic**, NOT our actin cortex or a somatic interphase nucleus. Do **not** import these as cortex or E_nuc values (E_nuc is 1–10 kPa, ~100× stiffer). Use only as (i) a rheology *pattern* for a future mitotic-interior model and (ii) justification that active inclusions can legitimately show ν>0.5 and anisotropy.
- **Ring emergence is FF-level, not DCM-native.** The self-organisation of filaments into a ring at the equator (bending-energy minimisation, L_p threshold) is fine-grained-filament physics. DCM can only *prescribe* the equatorial band and validate the *closure law + force balance*; it cannot emergently nucleate the ring from continuum surface state. Keep the mechanistic honesty: label the DCM furrow band as "imposed geometry, emergent closure dynamics."
- **No magic numbers.** The constriction strain rate ε̇ must be **derived** from myosin line density × per-head force / drag — never set to make D(t) match a target. HMM 5/25 µM are in-vitro concentrations, not DCM inputs; they only tell us "more motor → faster/complete constriction," a monotonic sanity check.
- **Muscle-SPOC and cardiac-SL legs are out of scope for DCM.** The sarcomere liquid-crystal, SPOC ODEs, and in-vivo SL-nanometry are muscle-tissue physics with no single-cell-surface analogue; ignore for DCM (they are FF/TAG material at most).
