# TAG-validation lens — p20

**Paper:** Miyazaki, Yamazaki & Hasegawa (2022), "Analysis of Brownian Motion by Elementary School Students," *The Physics Teacher* 60, 478–483. DOI 10.1119/5.0059810.

## Paper kind

**review / method (physics-education outreach)** — with one embedded *analytic* textbook relation and one low-precision *experimental* demonstration dataset.

This is a pedagogy/outreach article describing a 3-hour distance-learning workshop for elementary-school students (grades 4–6) built around Brownian motion. It is **not** a reconstitution assay, not a simulation, and not a mechanistic mechanobiology measurement. Its quantitative content is (a) the canonical 2D Einstein diffusion law MSD = 4Dt (analytic, textbook), (b) hand-clicked √MSD vs t for 1-µm beads in water (a diffusive-scaling *demonstration*, no D reported), (c) a dice random-walk √MSD vs N (physical Monte-Carlo demo), and (d) survey/pedagogy outcomes. None of this is a validation-grade mechanobiology datum for ffn_cellsim.

Usage rule under our philosophy: the diffusion law is at most an elementary reference for the canonical Brownian/thermostat baseline (which any BAOAB integrator must already satisfy, at higher rigor); the bead numbers are qualitative overlay-only and NOT fit targets; the dice/survey numbers are outside scope entirely.

## New gate candidates

The bead √MSD is qualitative (no D), the dice walk is a Monte-Carlo teaching demo, the survey data is pedagogy. ## Oracle candidates

**Marginal / already covered.** The single closed form MSD = 4Dt (2D) — equivalently ⟨r²⟩ = 2·d·D·t in d dimensions — is a legitimate analytic law and *could* in principle back a Brownian-diffusion cross-check oracle (Stokes–Einstein D = k_BT/(6πηr) closing it). But:
- Our oracle set already targets the mechanistic ground truths (Helfrich bending, Young–Laplace, Bell–Evans, Hill, Kelvin–Voigt), and the thermal baseline is enforced by the BAOAB integrator's fluctuation–dissipation validation.
- p20 is a downstream textbook restatement, not the primary source; it supplies no numerical D and no viscosity. It is not KB-registration-worthy as an oracle source.

If a diffusion oracle is ever wanted, cite the primary Einstein 1905 / Stokes–Einstein relation, not this paper.
