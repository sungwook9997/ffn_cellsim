# DCM lens — p55

**Paper:** Oyama et al. 2015, *Directional Bleb Formation in Spherical Cells under Temperature Gradient*, Biophys. J. 109(2):355–364, DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2015.06.016.

**One-line:** A rounded HeLa cell held spherical by cortical-actomyosin tension vs outward hydrostatic pressure blebs directionally when that balance is *spatially* broken (cortex density/tension ↑ one side, cortex–membrane detaches + ruptures the other side). This is an almost turnkey experimental target for the DCM bleb + membrane–cortex-coupling module.

**Bottom line for DCM:** This is NOT a source of new force constants or moduli (it is a live-cell IR-heating paper; the only equations are dye-thermometry calibrations and heat-conduction fits — irrelevant to us). Its value is (a) a **bleb-nucleation mechanism spelled out at exactly our compartment level** (membrane vs cortex vs turgor), (b) a clean set of **geometric validation targets** (bleb length, angle, onset latency, min-asymmetry threshold, cortex near/far ΔF asymmetry), and (c) a concrete recipe for how to **impose a spatially asymmetric cortical-tension field** as a DCM perturbation. The temperature gradient itself is out of DCM's parameter set — we port the *induced cortex asymmetry*, not the thermal driver.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Bleb module: nucleate from a local cortex-tension / membrane-cortex-adhesion imbalance (not a scripted balloon)
DCM currently lists "bleb" as an open problem. This paper gives the fine-grained mechanistic sequence to implement, staged (Fig. 4E):
1. **Local cortical-tension asymmetry** (warm side γ↑, or an equivalent local contractility spike). The steady spherical shape is the Laplace balance `2γ/R = ΔP` (turgor); a *local* γ excess overloads the membrane–cortex adhesion patch there.
2. **Membrane–cortex detachment**: the plasma membrane delaminates from the cortex where the adhesion (linker) stress exceeds a threshold. DCM must therefore represent the **membrane–cortex adhesion as an explicit, breakable coupling** (per-node linker springs with a Bell-Evans-style force-dependent detachment), *not* a rigid membrane==cortex identification.
3. **Cortex rupture** at the same (warm) side — the actomyosin network locally loses density and tears (myosin-II-driven disassembly, refs 41–44). In DCM-continuum terms this is a **local drop of γ and K_A of the cortex layer** (a damage/severing field), opening a low-tension window through which turgor pushes membrane out.
4. **Bleb extension**: unopposed turgor ΔP inflates the bare membrane through the rupture; cortex + membrane on the *cool* side flow toward the low-tension region (mass conservation of cortex).
5. Cortex reforms under the bleb and the bleb retracts (recovery) unless rupture escalates it.

**Why it beats DCM now:** DCM has no bleb at all, and its membrane and cortex are effectively co-located. This gives the *minimal* extra state needed — a breakable membrane–cortex linker layer + a local cortex-damage/tension field — to make blebs emerge mechanistically instead of scripted. It reuses machinery DCM already has (γ field on faces, ΔP turgor, node-face contact, K_A).

### M2 — Membrane–cortex coupling as an explicit breakable adhesion layer
The paper's central observable is that **cortex and membrane move IN PHASE until detachment, then separate** (Fig. 4C,D). DCM's single closed surface conflates the two. Adopt a **two-sheet representation** at least locally: membrane nodes and cortex nodes connected by linker springs `f_link = k_link·(d − d0)` that **break above a threshold force** `f_break` (Bell-Evans off-rate `k_off = k0·exp(f_link/f_β)`, consistent with the mechanistic rule — same clutch machinery as the FA/cadherin layers). Detachment is then emergent, not a flag. This is the prerequisite for M1 and is the physically correct picture of a bleb.

### M3 — Spatially heterogeneous cortical-tension field γ(face) with a damage/severing channel
DCM today carries γ as (effectively) a scalar surface tension. This paper *demands* γ be a **per-face field** that can (i) be locally elevated (contractility up) and (ii) locally collapse (rupture). Add:
- `gamma_face[i]` array (already implied by surface-mesh discretization; make it first-class + spatially settable).
- a **cortex-damage scalar** `D[i] ∈ [0,1]` that multiplies γ and K_A of the cortex layer, driven up where cortex strain-rate/tension exceeds a rupture threshold (myosin-severing analog). This is the same "network disassembly" lever FF would model with explicit crosslinker off-rates; at DCM continuum scale it is a damage field.

**Why:** it converts the qualitative "warm-side rupture, cool-side accumulation" into a mechanistic γ redistribution + local strength loss that any localized perturbation (not just heat) can trigger — confinement blebs, division-furrow blebs, aspiration blebs all reuse it.

### M4 — Cortex mass/density advection to the low-tension side (conserve cortex)
Fig. 3/4: F-actin & myosin **decrease at the warm side, accumulate at the cool side**, and this flow is what inhibitors (blebbistatin, Y-27632, CytoD) block. Model cortex as a **conserved surface density ρ_cortex(face)** advected by the tension-gradient-driven cortical flow: high-tension regions contract and pull cortex toward low-tension regions (a Marangoni-like surface flow, `v ∝ −∇γ / η_surf`). γ and K_A scale with local ρ_cortex. This is the mechanistic origin of the near↓/far↑ asymmetry and directly relevant to the OPEN **cortical-tension γ generation** problem — γ is not a global constant but a redistributable field tied to actomyosin density.

### M5 — Cytokinesis / division coupling (secondary)
Fig. S8: heating a dividing cell with ΔT>10 °C inhibits/mis-partitions cytokinesis (trisection 33%). Mechanistically this says the **furrow is a localized high-γ band** whose closure competes with ectopic contractility elsewhere; supports DCM's planned cytokinetic-ring line-tension law being a *localized γ band on faces*, and gives a perturbation test (asymmetric γ during furrowing → mis-division). Lower priority — no ring-tension number is measured here.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (to the surface-mesh / continuum level)

Note: the paper's *written* equations (Eqs. 1–6 in the dossier) are dye calibrations and heat-conduction fits — **do not port**; they are apparatus, not cell mechanics. The load-bearing physics is stated verbally (dossier §Governing Eq. 7, p.351 of text). Port these:

**E1 — Laplace force balance of the rounded cell (baseline, already in DCM; state explicitly as the bleb precondition):**
```
2·γ / R  =  ΔP            (spherical steady state)
```
- `γ` → DCM cortex surface tension (per-face `gamma_face`), interphase ≈0.15 mN/m.
- `R` → cell radius ≈7.5 µm.
- `ΔP` → turgor/osmotic pressure Π₀ (enclosed-volume constraint), ≈40 Pa.
Sanity: `2·0.15e-3 / 7.5e-6 = 40 Pa` — consistent with the DCM baseline; the paper's own force-balance is numerically the DCM baseline. A bleb nucleates where locally `2·γ_local/R_local < ΔP` (rupture drops γ_local → membrane bulges).

**E2 — Membrane–cortex linker (adhesion) stress and breakage (new, M2):**
```
f_link,i = k_link · (|x_mem,i − x_cortex,i| − d0)
detach when f_link,i ≥ f_break     (or Bell-Evans: k_off,i = k0·exp(f_link,i / f_β))
```
Map: `x_mem,i` = membrane node i, `x_cortex,i` = paired cortex node, `k_link` = linker stiffness (ERM/linker density), `f_break` derivable from linker areal density × single-linker rupture force — **surface to PI for the value; do not tune to pass a bleb gate.**

**E3 — Local cortex damage / tension collapse (new, M3):**
```
γ_eff,i = (1 − D_i) · γ0,i ,   K_A,eff,i = (1 − D_i) · K_A
dD_i/dt = α·⟨(σ_cortex,i − σ_rupture)_+⟩ − β·D_i        (damage up on overstress, heals with rate β)
```
Map: `σ_cortex,i` = local cortex tension (from γ_eff and strain), `σ_rupture` = rupture threshold, `β` = actin reformation rate (cortex recovers → bleb retracts, matches observed regrowth). α, β, σ_rupture are mechanistic (severing kinetics) — PI-scoped, NOT gate-fit.

**E4 — Cortex-density advection / Marangoni surface flow (new, M4):**
```
∂ρ_cortex/∂t + ∇_s·(ρ_cortex · v_s) = 0 ,   v_s = −(1/η_surf)·∇_s γ_eff
γ0,i = γ*·(ρ_cortex,i / ρ0)   (tension scales with local actomyosin density)
```
Map: `∇_s` = surface gradient on the mesh (per-edge), `η_surf` = membrane/cortex surface viscosity (already a DCM compartment), `ρ_cortex` = new per-face conserved density, `v_s` = tangential cortex flow. This is the mechanistic origin of the near↓/far↑ asymmetry.

**E5 — Imposed asymmetry as the DCM perturbation (how we drive it without the thermal model):**
Replace the temperature field with a **prescribed spatial γ modulation** at initialization/onset:
```
γ0,i(θ)  =  γ_base · [1 + A·cos(θ)]        θ = angle from perturbation axis, A = asymmetry amplitude
```
Choose `A` from the *measured* cortex asymmetry ΔF/F₀ (near↓/far↑, Fig. S12) rather than inventing it — that ties the perturbation to data, not a magic number. (See §3, §6.)

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce (DCM scenarios)

**P1 — Directional (polar) bleb scenario (primary).**
- Init: single rounded cell at full physiological baseline (γ=0.15 mN/m or mitotic 1.5, ΔP=40 Pa, η=65 Pa·s, nucleus ON, membrane–cortex linkers ON, `--from-resting` analog for DCM).
- Perturb: impose the E5 axial γ asymmetry `γ(θ)=γ_base[1+A cos θ]` at t=0 (proxy for "sharp gradient toward heat source"), with a co-located cortex-damage seed on the high-γ face patch.
- Expect: single directional bleb toward the low-γ (rupture) side, ~3 s latency, extension a few µm along the asymmetry axis, small angular spread.

**P2 — Multiple (non-directional) bleb scenario (control).**
- Perturb: *uniform* γ elevation / global mild cortex weakening (no spatial asymmetry, small A).
- Expect: multiple non-directional blebs (paper: 84% under uniform heating). This is the negative control that proves directionality comes from the *asymmetry*, not the magnitude — a discriminating test the DCM bleb module must pass.

**P3 — Threshold sweep.**
- Sweep the asymmetry amplitude A downward; find the minimum asymmetry that still yields a *single directional* bleb. Paper analog: min across-cell ΔT = 1.3 °C (R≈0.065 °C/µm) still gives a polar bleb. DCM reports its own A-threshold as an emergent number (not fit to 1.3 °C — different driver).

**P4 — "Motionless"/denaturation is OUT OF SCOPE** (thermal protein denaturation >50 °C, actomyosin-independent) — do not model; it is explicitly non-mechanical arrest.

**P5 — Cytokinesis perturbation (secondary).** During a division scenario, add an ectopic high-γ patch off the furrow axis; test for mis-partition/trisection (paper Fig. S8, 33%). Only when DCM's cytokinetic-ring line-tension law exists.

**P6 — Inhibitor analogs (mechanism validation).** Map drugs to DCM levers to check the mechanism is load-bearing:
- Blebbistatin / Y-27632 (myosin/ROCK off) → set active contractility/γ-generation to zero → blebs suppressed (paper: yes).
- Cytochalasin D / Latrunculin B (actin off) → collapse cortex K_A & γ → blebs suppressed.
- Jasplakinolide / nocodazole → longer blebs (cortex stabilized / MT removed). Nocodazole ≈ remove nucleus/MT confinement → longer extension.
These are qualitative pass/fail (sign of effect), not quantitative fits.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Driver mismatch (biggest).** The paper's stimulus is a *temperature gradient*; DCM has no temperature field and should not add one. We port the **induced cortex asymmetry (γ redistribution + local rupture)**, not the thermal cause. This is legitimate: the paper itself shows actomyosin is the effector and the thermal input just breaks its symmetry. Encode the asymmetry directly (E5), sourcing amplitude A from the measured ΔF/F₀ so it is data-anchored, not a magic number. **Do NOT invent a γ(T) law from this paper** — the paper explicitly leaves the *sign* of cortical-tension-vs-temperature open (contractility-up refs 20,33,34 vs stiffness-down refs 35–40). Adding a temperature-dependent γ would be an unsupported magic mechanism.
- **Cell type: HeLa, not MCF7.** Rounded HeLa (mitotic/trypsinized), R≈10 µm vs our MCF7 R≈7.5 µm. Same regime (rounded, cortex-tension-dominated), so lengths/angles transfer as *order-of-magnitude* targets; treat the 8.1 µm bleb length as a band, not a point target, when validating MCF7.
- **Metaphase/rounded, not adherent-interphase.** The strong blebs need a *rounded, pressurized* cell — the paper shows flat adherent cells barely bleb (0.5 µm). This matches DCM's physiological-baseline rule: run the bleb scenario from a properly turgor-pressurized rounded baseline (`--from-resting` analog), never a floppy zero-turgor shell — a floppy cell will not bleb and would misdiagnose the mechanism.
- **No moduli / rate constants.** This paper gives ZERO force constants, off-rates, or moduli. Every new-mechanism parameter (`k_link`, `f_break`, `σ_rupture`, α, β, η_surf) must come from elsewhere or be PI-scoped. Reproducing the 7.5–8.1 µm length must be **emergent**; back-fitting these constants to hit it violates the no-fit + no-magic-number + no-gate-tuning rules — surface to PI if the emergent length is far off rather than tuning.
- **Membrane phase-transition contribution (SI-3).** The authors considered and *rejected* a lipid-phase-transition driver in favor of actomyosin. So DCM is right to model the bleb via cortex/membrane mechanics, not a lipid-phase model — no action, just confirmation.
- **Ca²⁺ not essential** for the directional bleb (EGTA/BAPTA don't block it) — so DCM does NOT need a Ca²⁺ channel to reproduce polar blebbing. Ca²⁺ only modulates *multiple*-bleb probability; out of scope for the core mechanism.
- **Relevance to the listed OPEN problems:** directly advances **bleb** and **membrane–cortex coupling** (M1–M4 are the mechanism). Touches **cortical-tension γ generation** by reframing γ as a redistributable density-tied field (E4/M4) rather than a global scalar. Does **not** help the loose→compact aggregate-compaction RATE problem (single-cell, no cadherin/T1 content). Marginal for **division** (P5, no ring number).
