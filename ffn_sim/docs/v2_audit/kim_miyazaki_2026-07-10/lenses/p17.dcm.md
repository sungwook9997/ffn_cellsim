# DCM lens — p17

**Paper:** Prigent Garcia et al. 2026, *Kinetic Control of Out-Of-Equilibrium Dynamics in the RhoA Signaling Cascade Shapes Actomyosin Contractility* (bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.02.08.704688). Robin (Sorbonne) × Kim (Purdue). C. elegans 2-cell embryo, pulsed cortical contractions.

**Bottom line for DCM.** This is a cortex-contractility *timing* paper, not a surface-mesh/continuum cell paper. Nothing maps to node-face contact, membrane K_A, bending κ_m, or the cadherin junction. But it delivers ONE genuinely useful mechanistic upgrade for DCM: it shows cortical myosin (hence cortical tension γ) is **not a static set-point** — it is an out-of-equilibrium field obeying a first-order binding/unbinding turnover law, dN/dt = k_app − k_off·N, that behaves as a leaky-integrator / high-pass filter of upstream signaling. DCM currently hard-codes γ ≈ 0.15 mN/m (interphase) / 1.5 (mitotic) as a constant. This paper is the recipe to make **γ(t) a dynamic, turnover-governed field on the cortex faces** — which directly attacks the open "cortical-tension γ generation" and time-dependent-contractility problems, and gives DCM pulsed contractions for free. Everything else here is FF-scale (agent-based cylinder network) and should be routed to the FF lens.

Relevance is deliberately moderate: the mechanism is real and portable, but it is a *temporal* refinement of a compartment DCM already has, not a new compartment or force law. In-vivo C. elegans embryo cortex, not MCF7 — parameters are order-of-magnitude guidance, not physiological setpoints to hard-adopt.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

**M1 — Cortex tension as a first-order turnover field γ(t), not a constant (PRIMARY).**
Module: `dcm/` actomyosin cortex (the γ term / `cortical_tension.py`).
Change: replace the scalar set-point γ with a per-face (or per-node) active-myosin surface density m(x,t) that evolves by the turnover master equation (Eq. 5), and set γ = γ_ref · m / m_ref. Cortical tension then *charges* toward its instantaneous set-point with time constant τ = 1/k_off (~10–40 s) instead of snapping to it.
Why it beats current DCM: DCM's static γ cannot represent (a) the transient over/undershoot when a cell is perturbed (confinement, aspiration, division onset), (b) pulsed contractions, or (c) the fact that a *fast* upstream drive (mitotic entry, sudden confinement) is attenuated by turnover — the cortex physically cannot ramp tension faster than k_off allows. This is the fine-grained, mechanistic option (turnover kinetics) over the lumped constant. It reuses machinery DCM already needs for osmoregulation-borne γ = ΔP·R/2 — γ_ref just becomes the pressure-borne target and m(t) the kinetic gate on it.

**M2 — Out-of-equilibrium / high-pass response of cortex tension (Eq. 4).**
Module: same cortex tension term; a diagnostic + a hard speed limit.
Change: when DCM drives γ up (mitotic rounding, confinement stiffening) at angular rate ω, the achievable tension amplitude is attenuated by 1/√(1+ω²/k_off²) and phase-lagged. Adopt this as the physically-correct *rate ceiling* on how fast γ can change, rather than allowing an instantaneous jump. Why: prevents an unphysical step in cortical tension driving a mesh instability, and it is derivable (not tuned) — the ceiling is set by measured k_off.

**M3 — Assembly-before-contraction ordering (F-actin leads myosin by ~0.5 s; formin→myosin ~4.5 s; delay-tunes-force is non-monotonic, optimum ~5 s).**
Module: cortex tension activation sequencing; relevant if/when DCM couples a cortex-remodeling event to a contraction (division ring, bleb healing, confinement re-cortexing).
Change: gate myosin/tension onset behind a short actin-assembly delay d_M so tension is only applied to an already-cross-linked cortex. Why it beats current DCM: DCM applies γ to whatever the current mesh is; the paper's agent-sim shows applying motor force to an un-cross-linked network *collapses* it and yields LESS net force (Fig 5D non-monotonic, force max at ~5 s delay, min at 0 s). At DCM's surface-mesh scale this translates to: do not spike γ on a freshly-remeshed / freshly-nucleated cortex patch — ramp it in over ~τ_actin. This is a coarse-grained echo of an emergent FF result; keep it as a soft sequencing rule, not a hard law.

(Everything about the agent-based cylinder network — Bell's-law ACP off-rate, parallel-cluster-model motors, formin barbed-end elongation 1.2 µm/s — is FF territory, not DCM. Route to FF lens.)

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (to the surface-mesh / face level)

**Eq. 5 → DCM cortex turnover (the port that matters).**
Paper: dN/dt = k_app − k_off·N   (N = tracked cortical myosin molecule count in a region).
DCM face-level form, per cortex face f (area A_f):
  d m_f/dt = k_app,f − k_off · m_f
where m_f = active cortical myosin *areal density* on face f (units: molecules·µm⁻², or normalized 0–1). Map:
  • m_f  → drives the cortex tension of face f.
  • k_app,f (molecules·µm⁻²·s⁻¹) → the local recruitment set by upstream drive (mitotic state, ROCK proxy, or pressure demand). In DCM this is the *control input* — a scalar per face you raise to command more tension.
  • k_off (s⁻¹) → single global turnover rate ≈ 0.03–0.10 s⁻¹.

**Eq. 3 → instantaneous set-point ("signaled" density).**
Paper: [Myo⁰] = k_app / k_off.
DCM: m_f^set = k_app,f / k_off is the tension the face would reach if the drive froze. Then tension:
  **γ_f(t) = γ_ref · m_f(t) / m_f^ref**, with m_f relaxing toward m_f^set at rate k_off.
So DCM's existing pressure-borne target γ = ΔP·R/2 becomes γ_ref (the set-point), and m_f(t)/m_f^set is the kinetic *fill fraction* gating it. At steady state m_f = m_f^set and γ_f = γ_ref exactly — full backward compatibility; the kinetics only bite during transients.

**Eq. 7 → analytic transient (validation / cheap update).**
Paper: N(t) = N_eq² + (N_eq¹ − N_eq²)·e^(−k₂t), relaxation between two set-points with τ = 1/k_off.
DCM use: exact face-tension response to a step change in commanded drive — use for a unit test (does the cortex charge with the right τ?) and, if desired, as a closed-form per-step integrator for m_f (no stiffness worry).

**Eq. 4 → high-pass attenuation (rate ceiling / diagnostic).**
Paper: amplitude factor √(1+ω²/k_off²) for sinusoidal forcing at ω.
DCM: for any cyclic or ramped tension command at rate ω, realized γ amplitude = commanded / √(1+ω²/k_off²), phase-lagged by arctan(ω/k_off). Use to bound how fast a DCM protocol may ramp γ.

**Eq. 8 → out-of-equilibrium metric (observable).**
Paper: Δ = (max−min)_target / (max−min)_measured.
DCM: report Δ_γ = amplitude of commanded set-point vs amplitude of realized cortex tension over a protocol — a single number saying how far DCM's cortex is being driven out of equilibrium (should be ~1 for slow protocols, >1 for fast/mitotic).

**No magic numbers introduced.** k_off is a measured turnover rate (derivable τ = 1/k_off), grid-invariant, not tuned to a gate. d_M and τ_M are the paper's stated sim values, used as timing scaffolding only. All new fields default to the *equilibrium* limit (k_off→∞ ⇒ instantaneous ⇒ current behavior), so turning them on at physiological values is the physiological-baseline-compliant production setting, and off is exact back-compat.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

**P1 — Pulsed-contraction cortex scenario (single cell).** Drive k_app,f on a patch of the DCM cortex with a ~30 s-period modulation (or a step) and watch γ_f(t) charge/discharge with τ = 1/k_off. Observable: does realized tension lag the commanded set-point by ~arctan(ω/k_off) and blunt by √(1+ω²/k_off²)? This is a pure DCM cortex-timing test — no new geometry, uses the existing single MCF7-like R=7.5 µm mesh. (C. elegans embryo cortex is ~flat/large-radius; at DCM scale reproduce only the *local* patch dynamics, not the embryo geometry.)

**P2 — Step-response calibration.** From a relaxed cortex, step the commanded drive (k_app 1×→3×, as in mel-11(it26)) and fit the γ(t) rise to Eq. 7; confirm τ = 1/k_off. Unit test for M1.

**P3 — Assembly-before-contraction sequencing test.** After a remesh (or a bleb-heal / division-furrow re-cortexing event), apply γ (a) instantly vs (b) after d_M=5 s ramp, and measure net contractile deformation. Expect the delayed ramp to yield MORE stable contraction and less local mesh collapse (DCM-scale analog of Fig 5D non-monotonicity). Note: the paper's non-monotonic optimum is an *agent-network* emergent result; at DCM continuum scale treat P3 as a qualitative stability check, not a quantitative force-vs-delay curve.

(The paper's 20×20×0.1 µm periodic slab, 16-subdomain RhoA activation, TIRF single-molecule protocol, AFM/aspiration — none are DCM geometries. The slab agent-sim is an FF scenario.)

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Wrong system for magnitudes.** In-vivo C. elegans 2-cell embryo cortex, interphase, RhoA-*pulsatile*. MCF7 is a mammalian carcinoma cell and is not natively pulsatile at 30 s. Adopt the *kinetic form* (turnover ODE, high-pass ceiling) and the *order-of-magnitude* k_off, but do NOT import the 30 s pulse period, ~5% pulse contraction, or the 4.5 s cascade delay as MCF7 physiological setpoints. They are scenario stress-tests, not baseline values. Flag k_off for an MCF7-specific turnover datum before any production claim (single open KB parameter need).
- **Do not overwrite γ magnitude.** The paper gives timing only; it must NOT touch DCM's γ_ref = 0.15 / 1.5 mN/m or the pressure-borne γ = ΔP·R/2. The upgrade is multiplicative and equilibrium-preserving: at steady state γ_f = γ_ref exactly.
- **Physiological-baseline compliance.** The new kinetic gate must initialize at its *equilibrium* value (m_f = m_f^set, so γ_f = γ_ref) at run start — never at zero cortical myosin (that would be a floppy-cortex cold-start, exactly the un-physical baseline the HARD rule forbids). Perturbations then evolve m_f from the physiological set-point.
- **Continuum vs agent scale.** The delay-tunes-force non-monotonicity (Fig 5) is an emergent property of a *discrete cross-linked filament network* (premature motor engagement collapses an un-cross-linked mesh). DCM's cortex is a continuum surface tension, not an explicit network — it cannot mechanistically reproduce "network collapse from premature motors." Use M3/P3 only as a qualitative sequencing heuristic; the quantitative delay-force curve belongs to FF. Do not claim DCM reproduces the collapse mechanism.
- **No gate-loosening / no fitting.** k_off and d_M enter as measured/derived constants; Eq. 7 τ is a *prediction* to test, not a fit target. If a DCM cortex test wants a different τ to pass, surface to PI — do not retune k_off.
- **Preprint, not peer-reviewed.** Cite with that caveat in any KB RunResult/Parameter row.
