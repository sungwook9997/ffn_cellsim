# DCM lens — p71

**Paper:** Muresan, Sun, Yadav, Tabatabai, Lanier, Kim, Kim, Murrell — *F-actin architecture determines constraints on myosin thick filament motion*, Nat. Commun. 13:7008 (2022). DOI 10.1038/s41467-022-34715-6.

**One-line for DCM:** A fine-grained (FF-turf) in-vitro + agent-based study showing that cortex **architecture** (branched Arp2/3 vs bundled/linear formin) controls the *ab initio generation and transmission* of myosin active stress — bundled networks generate/transmit tension (contract to asters, C_rr∼1/r continuum-like, τ_v≈22–50 s), branched networks generate essentially **zero** tension (no ablation retraction, caged myosin). For DCM the payload is indirect but real: it is a mechanistic justification that **cortical surface tension γ is an architecture-/state-dependent generation term, not a constant**, plus a handful of continuum-scale validation numbers (τ_v, elasticity ~1–10 Pa, the 1/r transmission limit). It offers **no** surface-mesh equations and **no** MCF7 parameters — it is native filament-scale purified-protein physics.

> Honest scope note: this is primarily an **FF** paper (explicit filaments, Arp2/3 branches, 64-arm myosin thick filaments, volume-exclusion caging). Everything below is what survives coarse-graining to the DCM closed-surface-mesh level. Relevance to DCM: **moderate (2.5/5)** — one strong conceptual lever on the open γ-generation problem, a few validation targets, no direct port.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cortex tension γ as an **architecture-weighted generation term** (top lever)
**DCM module:** actomyosin-cortex surface tension γ (the module carrying the interphase γ≈0.15 / mitotic γ≈1.5 mN/m and the pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2).
**Change:** make the *active* (myosin-generated) part of γ scale with a **bundled/aligned cortex fraction** φ_bundle rather than assume any myosin density produces its full dipole stress. The paper's central result: at *constant total actin and constant myosin*, contractile strain ⟨ε⟩_max drops **>10×** (from ~0.8–1.3 in bundled mDia1 to ~1.5×10⁻¹→1×10⁻³ in branched Arp2/3, p=8.1×10⁻⁵), and ablation shows branched networks accumulate **no detectable tension at all**. So a myosin thick filament in a branched/high-density gel is sterically caged (sub-diffusive, α<1, suppressed rotation) and is **not** an ideal force dipole.
**Why it beats current DCM:** DCM today treats active γ as set by myosin density / activation only (the γ-floor work chased force-*magnitude*/density). This paper says architecture gates generation *independently* of density — a lumped "more myosin → more γ" is mechanistically wrong when the local network is branched/isotropic. A DCM cortex state variable φ_bundle (0=Arp2/3 gel, 1=aligned formin bundle) multiplying the active tension gives a fine-grained-justified reason the same NMII can yield low OR high γ — directly germane to the **open γ-generation gap**.

### M2 — "Virtual ablation" retraction as a cortex-tension **read-out** (not a driver)
**DCM module:** cortex/membrane surface-viscoelasticity + the enclosed-volume constraint.
**Change:** add a diagnostic scenario that severs a strip of cortex faces (zero their γ) and records the retraction strain ε(t) of neighbouring surface nodes, fit to Kelvin–Voigt (Eq 12). This is exactly how the paper *measures* whether tension exists.
**Why:** gives DCM a falsifiable, literature-anchored probe of the emergent cortical tension and surface viscosity (τ_v below), replacing indirect γ inference. A DCM cortex with real pre-tension must retract; one at floppy zero (the physiological-baseline failure mode CLAUDE.md warns about) will not.

### M3 — Membrane–cortex coupling **attenuates** active tension
**DCM module:** membrane–cortex coupling / tethering.
**Change:** when cortex nodes are tethered to the membrane (His–Ni bond analog = DCM membrane-cortex link), reduce the transmitted active contractile stress. The paper repeatedly finds membrane attachment attenuates network contractility (Fig 2e,f).
**Why:** DCM currently couples membrane and cortex as additive elastic layers; this says the coupling should *bleed* active tension (anchored network cannot flow into asters). Relevant to bleb / confinement / spreading scenarios where membrane detachment locally *increases* cortical flow.

### M4 — Continuum-surface-tension assumption is **only valid for well-connected cortex**
**DCM module:** the whole premise that cortex = a smooth continuum γ field on the mesh.
**Change/guardrail:** the paper shows stress propagates as C_rr∼1/r (continuum-like, α=0.8±0.2 and 1.15±0.3) **only** in bundled/linear networks; branched gels show **no assignable 1/r** — stress does not propagate. So DCM's continuum surface-tension is a faithful coarse-graining *for the interphase/aligned cortex* but would **over-transmit** stress if the cortex were branched-dominated. Treat this as a validity boundary, not a new force.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

### E1 — Kelvin–Voigt retraction (Eq 12) — cortex surface viscoelasticity
```
ε(t) = (σ / E_v) · (1 − e^{−t/τ_v}),   τ_v = η_v / E_v
```
Map to DCM: after a virtual-ablation cut, ε(t) = fractional retraction of the cut-edge surface nodes; σ = pre-cut cortical tension (≈ γ·curvature or the local face stress); **E_v ↦ cortex areal/shear elastic modulus (from K_A, γ)**; **η_v ↦ cortex surface viscosity** (the DCM membrane/cortex surface-viscosity field); **τ_v = η_v/E_v** is the emergent relaxation time the DCM run should reproduce. This is the one equation that lives natively at the surface-continuum level.

### E2 — Mean contractile strain (divergence of surface flow)
```
⟨ε⟩ = ⟨∇·x_c⟩ ,  contractile when ⟨ε⟩ < 0
```
Map to DCM: x_c = surface-node displacement field; ⟨ε⟩ = surface divergence over the cortex mesh; ⟨ε⟩_max and d⟨ε⟩/dt are the DCM compaction/contraction observables. This is the paper's primary contractility metric and maps 1:1 onto a DCM cortex-flow diagnostic (and, at the aggregate level, onto the loose→compact σ-driven surface flow).

### E3 — Two-point correlation decay (Eqs 10–11, fit C_rr∼1/r^α)
```
C_rr ~ 1/r^α ,  α≈1 ⇒ coarse-grainable continuum
```
Map to DCM: not a force but a **coarse-grainability test** — DCM assumes the cortex is a continuum on µm scales; α≈0.8–1.15 (bundled) validates that assumption for the interphase cortex, α unassignable (branched) marks where it fails. Use as an acceptance check, not a runtime term.

### Not portable (native filament-scale, keep in FF, not DCM)
- Nematic order Eq (1), translational/rotational MSD Eqs (2,3), van Hove Eqs (4,5), NGP Eq (6), GSER Eqs (8,9), autocorrelation Eq (7): these describe **individual thick-filament motion** and are FF/particle-tracking observables. They do not survive to the DCM surface level (DCM has no explicit thick filaments). Flag: if DCM ever exposes an embedded-probe diagnostic, MSD-α is the natural read-out, but that is an FF concern.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce as DCM scenarios

- **P1 — Virtual laser-ablation retraction.** Zero γ on a 20–30 µm strip of cortex faces (analog of the 337/435 nm line cut), then track surface-node retraction ε(t) vs. time (dt=3 s cadence, 50 post-cut frames), fit Kelvin–Voigt (E1) → extract τ_v; compare to 22±10 / 50±25 s. Direct DCM cortex-tension existence test. Runs on the native full-compartment cell only (per HARD rule).
- **P2 — Architecture sweep of active γ.** Hold myosin activation fixed, sweep the cortex bundled-fraction φ_bundle 0→1; measure ⟨ε⟩_max and d⟨ε⟩/dt. Reproduce the >10× drop from bundled to branched. Establishes the M1 gating law without tuning to a target.
- **P3 — Membrane-tether attenuation.** With/without cortex-membrane tethering, measure contractile ⟨ε⟩_max; expect attenuation when tethered (M3).
- **P4 — Contraction-to-aster (aggregate relevance).** Above a critical active-tension threshold, bundled cortex flows inward within 2–4 min. This is the *single-network* analog of DCM's loose→compact aggregate flow; useful as the mechanical-end-state timescale sanity (caveat: this is fast in-vitro purified contraction, NOT the 24–48 h cadherin-T1 aggregate compaction — see risks).

Not reproducible in DCM (FF-only): the Arp2/3 vs mDia1 nucleation-from-bilayer assembly, thick-filament MPT, the 4×4×1.5 µm agent-based VE box.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

1. **Cortical retraction time τ_v = 22 ± 10 s** (dense) / **50 ± 25 s** (sparse) after virtual ablation — DCM cortex with real pre-tension must retract on this scale; a floppy zero-tension shell will not retract → catches the physiological-baseline failure mode.
2. **Branched cortex → no retraction / no accumulated tension** — a DCM cortex at φ_bundle→0 should show ⟨ε⟩_max collapse to ~10⁻³ and negligible retraction.
3. **Contractile strain ⟨ε⟩_max ≈ 0.8–1.3, strain-rate ~10⁻³ s⁻¹** for the bundled/contractile reference — target band for active cortical-flow onset.
4. **Stress transmission continuum limit C_rr∼1/r (α=0.8–1.15)** — validates DCM's continuum surface-tension treatment for the aligned interphase cortex; branched → no 1/r (validity boundary).
5. **Branched-gel elasticity ~1–10 Pa** — soft-modulus datum for a branched cortex state (NOT the interphase MCF7 cortex).
6. **Contraction-to-aster within 2–4 min** — mechanical-end-state timescale for a *single* actively-contracting network (order-of-magnitude only).

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Purified in-vitro ≠ living MCF7.** Egg-PC supported bilayer, pH raised to 8.5 to suppress nucleation, skeletal-muscle myosin II (SKMMII, ~20 molecules/thick filament — the paper itself notes NMMII has ~300 and higher duty ratio → more processive). MCF7 cortex uses NMMII. So the *absolute* contractility numbers are not MCF7; use only the **architecture-dependence direction** and the τ_v/elasticity orders of magnitude. Do not KB-register these as MCF7 parameters.
- **Quasi-2D thin cortex-mimetic, not a closed 3D shell.** The reconstituted "cortex" is a 0.5–1.6 µm thin film on a flat bilayer; DCM is a closed volume-enclosing surface with turgor Π₀. The 1/r transmission and aster contraction are 2D-planar; adapt as *surface-manifold* divergence on the mesh, not planar.
- **This measures stress GENERATION at fixed density, not RATE of aggregate compaction.** The 2–4 min aster contraction is a fast purified-protein event; it must **not** be cited as the loose→compact aggregate compaction rate (real 24–48 h, cadherin-T1-rearrangement-limited per the standing PI scope). M1/M2 touch the *cortex tension generation* problem, which is a different open problem from aggregate compaction rate.
- **φ_bundle must be derivable, not tuned.** Introducing an architecture-weighted active-γ (M1) risks becoming a magic knob. To respect the no-magic-number rule, φ_bundle must be a *derived* cortex state (e.g. from a nematic-order / alignment field the FF engine or a cortex sub-model produces), initialized at the physiological interphase value — not fit to make a γ gate pass. If no principled φ_bundle source exists at DCM level, surface to PI rather than defaulting.
- **The dominant mechanism (volume-exclusion steric caging of thick filaments) is genuinely FF-scale.** It cannot be represented on a DCM surface mesh (no explicit filaments/motors). Its DCM-level *consequence* — architecture suppresses γ generation — is what transfers; the mechanism itself belongs in the FF lens.
- **Physiological-baseline compliance:** any adoption (φ_bundle, virtual-ablation) must start from the `--from-resting` native full-compartment cell at real turgor/γ, never a stripped or coarse build.
