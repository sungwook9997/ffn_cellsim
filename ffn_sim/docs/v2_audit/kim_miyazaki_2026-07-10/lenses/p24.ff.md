# FF lens — p24

**Paper:** Jung, Tabatabai, Thomas, Tabei, Murrell & Kim (2019), *Dynamic motions of molecular motors in the actin cytoskeleton*, Cytoskeleton 76(11–12):517–531. DOI 10.1002/cm.21582.
**Why it matters to FF:** This IS the FF target class — a fine-grained, filament-resolved, Brownian-dynamics actomyosin model (Taeyoon Kim lab) that is the direct sibling of Cytosim physics FF re-implements. Explicit F-actin as segmented bending beams (κ_s, κ_b), Bell's-law slip-bond cross-linkers (Eq 1, filamin-A anchor), PCM force-dependent myosin walk/unbind with 3.8 pN/head stall, F-actin treadmilling. Every mechanism here maps 1:1 onto an FF module, and the paper's central finding — **F-actin turnover is the stress-relaxation channel that keeps the cortex ergodic** — speaks directly to FF's open problems (cortical-tension γ floor, network contraction & stress relaxation, filament turnover, motor-driven flows, aster/condensation symmetry-breaking).

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### 1.1 F-actin treadmilling turnover as an explicit stress-relaxation channel (turnover module) — HIGH PRIORITY
The paper's headline result: without turnover, motors stall/confine three ways; **with treadmilling (k_-,A = k_+,A > 0) all three regimes vanish** because depolymerizing a pointed-end segment *permanently* drops every ACP and motor bound to it, relaxing the force it carried. Critically, they find treadmilling relaxes force **more efficiently than ACP unbinding** (Fig 6a–b vs Fig 4): a segment disappearing severs *all* attached links at once, whereas Bell-law unbinding only releases one over-stretched cross-link at a time.
- **FF change:** FF has barbed-end growth (Bois2011 ratchet, KB-3.6) but the current cortex has no matched pointed-end depolymerization → no true treadmilling → filament-length steady state and force relaxation are both incomplete. Add a **pointed-end segment-removal channel k_-,A tied to k_+,A** so ⟨L_f⟩ holds steady while the network continuously relaxes internal stress. When a segment is removed, ALL cross-linker/motor bonds anchored on it must be released (this is the mechanistic crux, not a lumped decay).
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF's open γ/contraction/stress-relaxation problems are all "network locks up under motor load." This paper shows the physiological fix is turnover, not softer bonds or more unbinding. It gives FF a *mechanistic* relaxation lever (real actin dynamics) instead of any lumped viscoelastic damper — consistent with the fine-grained-over-lumped hard rule.

### 1.2 One-arm vs two-arm motor connectivity distinction (myosin module)
Force generation is **entirely gated by how many antiparallel filaments a motor engages**: one-arm motors (bind 1 F-actin) bear only ~0.1 pN and never stall ("pulling a rope on a slippery surface"); two-arm motors (bind a pair of relatively antiparallel filaments) build ~4–5 pN and stall. FF's Stam–Hocky bipolar minifilament already has multiple heads on two half-filaments — the lesson is that **the emergent force is set by the local antiparallel geometry each half engages**, and FF should log/verify that a minifilament straddling parallel filaments produces near-zero net tension (a mechanistic self-check), rather than assuming every bound motor contributes stall-level force.
- **Why it beats current FF:** guards against over-counting motor tension in aligned/bundled regions (filopodium, stress-fiber), which is exactly where γ can be spuriously inflated.

### 1.3 Network-scale force transmission / "web-like taut-filament" tug-of-war (emergent, no code change — a diagnostic)
In low-connectivity networks with many motors, tension propagates motor→motor through taut filaments+ACPs, forming a web of >25 pN load-bearing paths that globally stalls motors (Fig 3d). This is an emergent prediction FF should reproduce, not a mechanism to add — it validates that FF's excluded-volume + bond network transmits long-range stress correctly.

### 1.4 Motor-driven aster formation & polarity sorting (emergent condensation — symmetry-breaking validation)
At low connectivity + high motor density + fast ACP unbinding, the network self-organizes into **aster-like polarity-sorted clusters** with barbed ends at the center and buckled filaments (Fig 4d, S11). Motors keep walking near the center but cannot escape. Directly relevant to FF's "buckling/condensation symmetry-breaking" and "confinement-driven self-organization" open problems — a ready-made emergent target for FF's motor+crosslinker+turnover stack.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### 2.1 Bell's-law ACP (cross-linker) slip-bond off-rate — Eq 1
```
k_u,ACP = k0_u,ACP · exp( λ_u,ACP · |F_s,ACP| / (k_B T) )   if r ≥ r_0,ACP
k_u,ACP = k0_u,ACP                                          if r <  r_0,ACP
```
- Symbol → FF state: `k0_u,ACP` → crosslinker zero-force off-rate (`k_off0`); `λ_u,ACP` → force-sensitivity length (Bell reactive length x_β); `|F_s,ACP|` → magnitude of the crosslinker arm spring force (FF crosslinker bond tension); `r` → current arm length, `r_0,ACP` → equilibrium arm length; `k_B T` → thermal energy.
- **Map to FF:** this is FF's existing Bell–Evans crosslinker off-rate `k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b)` with `F_b = k_BT/λ_u,ACP`. **Key refinement to adopt: gate the exponential on `r ≥ r_0` (only *stretched* arms feel force-accelerated unbinding; compressed arms unbind at baseline).** FF should confirm it does not accelerate off-rate under compression.

### 2.2 PCM force-dependent motor walking & unbinding (myosin module)
Both the walk rate `k_w,M` and unbind rate `k_u,M` per arm come from the **Parallel Cluster Model** (Erdmann & Schwarz 2012; Erdmann, Albert & Schwarz 2013), which resolves cooperative mechanochemistry of N_h heads. Both rates **decrease monotonically with arm load** (Fig S1); walking halts above stall = **3.8 pN per myosin head**. On reaching the barbed end the arm slides off; after slide-off/unbind an arm may **hop** to a different filament; if all arms detach the motor **free-diffuses** until rebinding.
- **Map to FF:** FF currently uses Hill (1938) f–v + Stam–Hocky. PCM is the more rigorous multi-head substitute for the *per-head* stall+duty and is what the Kim lab benchmarked (Kim 2015). At minimum, port the three explicit state transitions: (i) load-dependent walk `k_w,M(F)`, (ii) load-dependent unbind `k_u,M(F)`, (iii) barbed-end **slide-off**, plus (iv) **free-diffusion of the fully-detached motor** and (v) **hopping** rebind — FF should verify all five are present as explicit mechanistic states, not lumped into a single "walk-or-stall" toggle.

### 2.3 Treadmilling kinetic scheme (F-actin dynamics)
```
nucleation  (k_n,A): create 1 seed segment, random orientation ⟂ z
polymerize  (k_+,A): add segment at barbed end
depolymerize(k_-,A): remove segment at pointed end
turnover:   k_t,A ≡ k_-,A = k_+,A   ⇒ constant ⟨L_f⟩ at steady state
```
- **Map to FF:** FF has (k_n via nucleators, k_+ via formin/Arp2/3 ratchet). Add **k_- pointed-end removal locked to k_+**; on removal release all bound ACP/motor states on that segment. Treadmilling rate `k_t,A` becomes the FF cortex's tunable stress-relaxation knob.

### 2.4 MSD analysis operators (validation instrumentation — port to FF observables)
- TE-MSD Eq 2, E-MSD Eq 3, τ-MSD(t) Eq 4 (τ=10 s), local log-log slope α (α<1 sub / =1 diff / 1<α<2 super / =2 ballistic). E-MSD≈TE-MSD ⇒ ergodic.
- Radial distribution g(r) Eq 5 = ⟨P(r)/(2πrΔr ρ)⟩, Δr=20 nm, averaged 0.1<r<1 µm → ḡ(t); aggregation metric = g_max/g_init.
- **Map to FF:** add these as post-processing on FF motor/actin trajectories to compare motor mobility, ergodicity, and network aggregation head-to-head with this paper.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenario)

### 4.1 Cortex-like slab (simulation geometry)
- Domain **5 × 5 × 0.2 µm** thin rectangular slab; **PBC in x, y only** (z open) → a cortex patch.
- Nucleation random, **perpendicular to z, equal probability** → isotropically-oriented cortex-like network (no pre-alignment).
- **Preassembly protocol (import this):** assemble the network **without motors for 1,000 s** to reach steady-state morphology, THEN import frozen morphology + add motors. This isolates motor motion from ongoing bundling and mirrors the Linsmeier 2016 in-vitro protocol. FF should adopt the same "relax network first, then activate motors" staging for any motor-mobility scenario (analogous to FF's existing `--from-resting`).
- Connectivity swept purely via ⟨L_f⟩ and R_ACP (§3 table). Simulate to **T = 1,000 s**.

### 4.2 In-vitro reconstituted 2-D cortex (physical validation assay — optional FF-vs-experiment scenario)
- Supported lipid bilayer (egg PC : Oregon Green DHPE 100:1); F-actin prepolymerized at **2.64 µM G-actin**, phalloidin-stabilized (1.32 µM, blocks turnover), 75:25 dark:labeled; crowded onto bilayer with 0.25% 14 kDa methylcellulose; F-buffer (50 mM KCl, 1 mM MgCl₂, 10 mM imidazole, 200 µM EGTA, 500 µM ATP, pH 7.5).
- Crosslinker: α-actinin (high-xlink = 10:1 G-actin:α-actinin). Motor: skeletal-muscle myosin (SKMM), low density 0.5 nM (single-motor tracking) / high density ≥150 nM (image-correlation).
- **Note:** phalloidin blocks turnover → this assay is the *no-turnover* limit. It validates local force stalling + aggregation, NOT the turnover-rescue regime (which only exists in cells / sim).

## 6. RISKS / caveats

### 6.1 Purified reconstituted / coarse in-vitro-like vs living MCF7 cortex
- The sim is a **5×5×0.2 µm periodic patch**, not a whole pressurized cell. FF's hard rule = validate at **NATIVE 38000-filament cortex + full compartments (membrane, turgor, nucleus, gravity)**. Use this paper's numbers as **mechanism cross-checks and a small-patch sanity scenario ONLY**; any conclusion (e.g. "turnover sets γ relaxation") must be reconfirmed on the native full cell before it is reported. Do not draw a γ conclusion from a bare 5×5 patch.
- In-vitro assay uses **skeletal-muscle myosin** (4% duty, <5 µm/s) — a different isoform from the cortical NMII FF models; the paper itself only uses it for *qualitative* validation. Anchor FF to the **NMII** numbers (v<140 nm/s, PCM stall 3.8 pN/head), not SKMM.
- Phalloidin-stabilized in-vitro = **no turnover**; do not read the in-vitro aggregation/stalling as the physiological state — the physiological cortex is the turnover-ON regime where these vanish.

### 6.2 Coarse-graining / rate-scale mismatch (biggest porting risk)
- This model coarse-grains F-actin into **cylindrical segments**, so `k_t,A` and `k_+,A/k_-,A` are **per-segment, coarse-scale** rates (values 1–70 s⁻¹). They are NOT native monomer on/off rates and cannot be dropped into FF's native cortex verbatim. Convert by matching the **filament half-life / net treadmilling velocity** (segment length × k_t,A), not the raw rate. Surface the conversion to PI as a derived quantity — do NOT tune k_t,A to make a γ or contraction gate pass (magic-number / gate-loosening rules).
- Segment stiffnesses κ_s,·, κ_b,·, drag coefficients, thermal amplitude live in **SI Tables S1–S2** (NOT in the 15-page PDF). Fetch SI before attempting a numerical parity port of the bending/stretch constants; until then treat κ values as unavailable and use FF's own Ferrer link stiffness + actin ℓ_p≈17 µm.

### 6.3 Physiological-baseline compliance
- The paper's control cases (k0_u,ACP=0 permanent crosslinks, k_t,A=0 no turnover) are **explicitly labeled non-physiological** by the authors. FF must run production at the **physiological operating point from t=0**: transient Bell crosslinkers ON (k0_u,ACP at the correct per-isoform value) and turnover ON (k_t,A>0). Use the no-turnover / permanent-crosslink cases only as diagnostic controls, never as a production baseline.

### 6.4 Isoform bookkeeping for crosslinker off-rate
- k0_u,ACP=0.115 s⁻¹ is **filamin A**; FF's α-actinin anchor is **0.066 s⁻¹** (Ferrer 2008, per citation audit). Keep them separate per crosslinker species — importing 0.115 as a blanket ACP off-rate would silently overwrite the α-actinin anchor and drift the KB.
