# FF lens — p25

**Paper:** Slater, Jung & Kim (2024) "Emergence of diverse patterns driven by molecular motors in the motility assay." *Cytoskeleton* 81(12):902–912. DOI 10.1002/cm.21808. NIH 1R01GM126256.

**One-line relevance:** This is the *same Kim-lab agent-based Brownian-dynamics lineage FF emulates* (Bidone 2017, Jung 2015/2020, Kim 2015, Li 2017), applied to the myosin motility (gliding) assay with **EXPLICIT, kinetically-resolved, surface-fixed motors** governed by the **Parallel Cluster Model (PCM; Erdmann & Schwarz 2012, Erdmann et al. 2013)** instead of a lumped propulsion force. The single most valuable transferable asset is the PCM motor-kinetics scheme as a mechanistic alternative/cross-check to FF's current Hill (1938) force–velocity + duty-ratio myosin, plus a ready-made emergent-pattern validation scenario. **Caveat up front:** the load-bearing numerical constants (κ_b*, κ_r*, motor stall force, PCM on/off/walk rate constants, drag coefficients, r0,A, stretch stiffness) live in Supporting Information Table S1, which is NOT in the supplied text — several ports below are *scheme-level* and need the SI (or the original Erdmann–Schwarz papers) to instantiate numerically.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### 1a. Myosin kinetics: Parallel Cluster Model (PCM) as a mechanistic peer/cross-check to Hill
- **FF module:** `ff/` myosin (Stam–Hocky bipolar minifilament + Hill 1938 force–velocity + duty ratio).
- **Change:** Add a PCM-based motor-cluster kinetics option. In PCM a small ensemble of N myosin II heads is coarse-grained into "clusters" of heads in the *same* mechanochemical state; the number bound, the collective walking velocity, and the collective unbinding rate are computed from the ensemble occupancy rather than head-by-head. Here **one motor "arm" ≡ 8 myosin heads** for stall force and kinetics (that is why C_M = 12 µM motor → 96 µM effective myosin).
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF's minifilament is mechanistically explicit at the *head-spring* level but its force–velocity is Hill's phenomenological hyperbola and its detachment is Bell-Evans on a single bond. PCM gives a *statistically exact* small-ensemble force generation and load-dependent detachment for a non-processive cluster (myosin II is non-processive — duty ratio ~0.1 at zero load, Harris & Warshaw 1993), which is exactly the regime where Hill+single-Bell can misestimate stall behavior and the *broad* single-motor speed distribution. This paper's own finding — that explicit motors produce a **broader gliding-speed distribution and qualitatively new structures (rings) that implicit/lumped propulsion never reproduces** — is a direct, empirical argument for keeping FF's motors mechanistic and against any "constant propulsion" shortcut.
- **Concretely for FF:** treat the PCM as an *acceptance oracle* for the minifilament: at fixed load F, the Stam–Hocky cluster's mean walk velocity v(F) and ensemble off-rate should match PCM(N_heads=8). This is a validation contract, not a runtime swap (keeps the fine-grained head model as runtime).

### 1b. "Explicit motors, never implicit" — hard confirmation of FF's design axiom
- **FF module:** any myosin / traction / crawl driver.
- **Change:** none required — this paper is *external validation of the CLAUDE.md mechanistic rule*. Figures S6: implicit motors tuned to the *same average gliding speed* (a) form **NO rings**, (b) give a larger low-speed fraction, (c) misrepresent both magnitude AND direction of propulsion (Freedman et al. 2017 also showed motor spacing controls filament velocity/persistence). 
- **Why it matters:** cite this the next time anyone proposes a lumped "front-bias treadmill" or constant-propulsion crawl shortcut (memory: front-bias treadmill was already REJECTED for FF crawl). This is the paper to point to.

### 1c. Collision-induced alignment via volume exclusion is the pattern-forming mechanism — audit FF's repulsion
- **FF module:** excluded-volume LJ repulsive between filaments/segments.
- **Change:** ensure FF's inter-filament repulsion is strong/prompt enough to produce *collision-induced re-orientation*, not just anti-overlap. In this model the repulsive strength κ_r is THE primary knob turning networks→flocks→bands→rings; the timestep Δt = 1.15×10⁻⁵ s was chosen specifically "small enough to allow filaments to exert repulsive forces as soon as they start overlapping." 
- **Why:** FF's cortex/lamellipodium self-organization (buckling/condensation symmetry-breaking — a listed open problem) is governed by exactly this collision-alignment coupling. If FF's LJ is too soft or its dt too large to engage repulsion at overlap onset, FF will under-produce alignment and never enter the band/ring regime.

### 1d. F-actin fragmentation / severing as an explicitly-flagged future mechanism (from the *sibling* Kim paper cited here)
- **FF module:** filament turnover / fragmentation (open problem: filament turnover/fragmentation).
- **Change:** this paper explicitly notes it does NOT include severing and flags it as future work; it cites **Li, Biel, Lomada, Yu & Kim 2017, *Soft Matter* 13:3213 — "Buckling-induced F-actin fragmentation modulates the contraction of active cytoskeletal networks."** That is the mechanism FF should mine for a buckling→fragmentation→contraction channel (Matsuda-2024-style facilitation). Not in this paper's numbers, but this is the pointer.
- **Why:** FF lists "network contraction & stress relaxation" and "filament turnover/fragmentation" as open; buckling-induced fragmentation is a known route to *facilitated* contraction and stress relaxation. Follow the Li 2017 reference.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

> The functional forms in *this* PDF are qualitative; explicit expressions are in SI Table S1 / Erdmann–Schwarz. What IS transcribable:

**(i) Persistence length ↔ bending stiffness mapping (Eq 2) — directly usable to set/verify FF's κ:**
```
l_p = κ_b,A · r0,A / (k_B T)
```
- `κ_b,A` = per-segment bending stiffness (units **N·m** in this model — NOT N·m² curvature modulus; note the convention), `r0,A` = equilibrium segment length, `k_B T` = thermal energy.
- **Map to FF:** FF sets actin ℓ_p ≈ 17 µm. Use this to check FF's angular-bending κ per segment against its segment length: given FF's r0 (bead spacing) and target ℓ_p=17 µm, κ_b = ℓ_p·k_B T / r0. This is a units-consistency gate for FF's bending term.

**(ii) Tangent-correlation persistence-length estimator (Eq 3) — a measurement protocol for FF:**
```
⟨cos θ(Δs)⟩ = ⟨ u(s) · u(s+Δs) ⟩  ,  fit to  exp(−Δs / l_p)
```
- `u(s)` = unit tangent along contour coordinate s. Lower correlation ⇒ higher curvature. **Port as an FF observable**: measure the *effective* ℓ_p of FF filaments under load/motor interaction (it deviates from the free-filament value when motors and collisions bend filaments) — a clean diagnostic for FF's motor-driven buckling.

**(iii) PCM walking + force-dependent unbinding (scheme, values in SI):**
- Motor binds stochastically when a surface-fixed head is proximal to a filament binding site; **walks toward barbed end with a load-dependent velocity (force–velocity relationship)**; unbinds (a) processively when it walks past the barbed end, and (b) force-dependently from anywhere.
- **Map to FF state:** walk velocity → FF minifilament head stepping rate; force-dependent unbind (b) is FF's Bell-Evans channel k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b); processive-past-barbed unbind (a) is a *geometric* detachment FF should also implement (head reaching filament plus-end tip must release — otherwise heads accumulate at the tip). **Action:** confirm FF minifilament heads detach on reaching the barbed end, not only on force.

**(iv) N-head cluster scaling:** stall force and kinetics of one motile unit = **8 myosin heads**. If FF wants to reproduce this assay it must group heads into 8-head units (or run the native minifilament with the equivalent head count) — do NOT tune a single effective spring.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (an FF scenario)

**"FF gliding-assay" scenario** — a clean, non-cortex FF benchmark that exercises filament bending + excluded volume + explicit motors with zero confounding compartments:
1. Thin quasi-2D box 10×10×0.1 µm³, PBC in x,y; **z-displacement of filaments frozen** (all coplanar) — gives stronger, cleaner volume-exclusion than 3D crossover.
2. **Surface-fixed motors** at z=0, random x,y placement, C_M=12 µM (8-head units → 96 µM effective). Motors bind proximal filament sites and walk toward barbed ends.
3. Filaments assembled at t=0 by stochastic nucleation+polymerization → **normal length distribution**, no depol/severing (length fixed during run); tune ⟨L⟩ via nucleation rate at fixed polymerization. Reference ⟨L⟩=2.5 µm, C≈60 µM.
4. Run 100 s; sample every 2 s.
5. **Sweep the 4 knobs pairwise** (κ_r, κ_b, C, ⟨L⟩) to map the phase diagram.

**Value to FF:** this is the lowest-compartment scenario that still stresses FF's *bending + LJ-repulsion + explicit-motor* triad. It is a superb kernel-parity / self-organization regression test *before* the native full cortex — but note the CLAUDE.md rule: any *conclusion* must be reconfirmed at native scale + full compartments. Treat this as a mechanism-isolation dev benchmark, labelled non-authoritative, not a cortex result.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for FF observables

Trend/regime targets (this paper reports mostly heatmap ranges, not tight single-value bands):

- **Four emergent classes**: homogeneous network, flock, band, ring. FF must reproduce all four purely from collisions between motor-propelled semiflexible filaments (no lumped alignment term).
- **Transition ordering**: network → flock → band → ring as **κ_r ↑ or κ_b ↓** (rigid+weak-repulsion = network; soft+strong-repulsion = rings). This monotone ordering is the primary qualitative gate.
- **Rings always coexist with bands** (both from tight alignment of many filaments); **ring+ring → ring merging NOT observed**; band+band→band and band+ring→thicker ring ARE observed.
- **Ring size set by κ_b (rigidity) & κ_r — NOT by C or ⟨L⟩.** Strong falsifiable prediction for FF.
- **Critical concentration**: thick bands vanish for **C < 60 µM**.
- **Number of rings ∝ ⟨L⟩** (Fig S10b); rings decrease as C drops.
- **Curvature ordering**: ring > band > flock > homogeneous network (Fig S7f).
- **Velocity correlation** ⟨|cos α|⟩ (Eq 1) peak at **tight/thick bands**, magnitude ~0.7–0.95; ~0.5–0.9 across κ_b–κ_r map.
- **Curl (rotational metric)** ~0.018–0.033 (units 1/s), maximal when many rings form.
- **Structure lifetime**: rings & bands longest-lived / most stable.
- **Explicit-vs-implicit control**: implicit motors → **no rings + larger low-speed fraction** at matched mean speed (Fig S6). FF, being explicit, must show rings — a pass/fail on FF's mechanistic fidelity.
- **Average gliding speed**: the single scalar to match between FF's minifilament and the assay (used to calibrate the implicit control); exact value in SI.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Purified in-vitro assay, NOT living cortex.** Surface-fixed motors, no membrane, no turgor, no crosslinkers, no nucleus, 2D coplanar. This is a *mechanism-isolation* benchmark only. Per CLAUDE.md HARD rule, any finding here must be reconfirmed on the native `--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules` full cell before it is reported or acted on. Use it as a Warp kernel regression, not a cortex conclusion.
- **Motors are IMMOBILE (surface-anchored).** The paper itself flags this as its main limitation (future work: mobile motors). FF's cortical myosin is mobile/embedded — so the *pattern phase diagram* is assay-specific and will NOT map 1:1 onto cortex organization. Port the *kinetics scheme and the mechanistic-vs-lumped lesson*, not the ring/band phase boundaries, into the cortex.
- **z frozen → over-strong volume exclusion.** The authors note collisions are more exclusive than real (filaments can't cross over). FF's native 3D cortex allows crossover; do not import the 2D repulsion calibration into 3D.
- **Length distribution mismatch.** This model uses a *normal* length distribution with no severing; FF cortex uses *exponential* 1–10 µm with barbed-end growth (KB-3.18). Keep FF's physiological exponential dist for cortex work; only adopt the normal ⟨L⟩=2.5 µm inside the isolated assay scenario.
- **Load-bearing constants are in SI (unavailable here).** κ_b*, κ_r*, F_stall, PCM rates, drag, r0,A, stretch stiffness are all in Table S1 not in the supplied text — do NOT back-fill with guesses (no-magic-number rule). Retrieve SI or go to Erdmann–Schwarz for PCM rates.
- **One run per condition** (cost). Their stochastic-variation claim is asserted, not shown per-condition; when FF reproduces this, run a seed ensemble (memory: seed-robustness BEFORE any "spontaneous" claim) rather than a single trajectory.
- **κ_b units are N·m here** (a per-segment convention), not the N·m² flexural rigidity — unit-slip hazard identical to the DCM=meters/FF=µm pitfall. Convert via Eq 2 with FF's own r0 before comparing.
- **PCM is a coarse-graining of head ensembles** — adopting it as *runtime* would technically be a lumping of heads and thus in tension with the fine-grained rule. Correct use = keep Stam–Hocky heads as runtime, use PCM as an acceptance oracle / validation cross-check only.
