# DCM lens — p64

**Paper:** Bidone, Kim, Deriu, Morbiducci, Kamm (2015), *Multiscale impact of nucleotides and cations on the conformational equilibrium, elasticity and rheology of actin filaments and crosslinked networks*, Biomech Model Mechanobiol 14:1143–1155, DOI 10.1007/s10237-015-0660-6.

**Bottom line for DCM:** This is a molecular→filament→network paper (all-atom MD → ENM/RTB normal-mode analysis → Kim-lab agent-based Brownian-dynamics crosslinked actin sheared in a 3 µm periodic box). It has **no membrane, no cortex surface tension, no turgor/osmotic pressure, no cell-cell junction, no closed-surface mesh** — i.e. none of DCM's state variables appear. It is a natural **FF-engine** paper (fine-grained crosslinked filaments), not a DCM one. The *only* thing DCM can legitimately borrow is a **network strain-stiffening constitutive relation σ(γ)** as an optional emergent-consistency reference for a cortex/cytoplasm effective law — and even that is second-hand (it belongs one level below the surface-mesh continuum). **DCM relevance is low (score 1).** Everything below is scoped to that single tangential lever; I flag explicitly where it must NOT be ported to avoid a lumped-mechanism or non-physiological-baseline violation.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

Honest triage — there is essentially **one** partially-adoptable mechanism and several **explicit non-adoptions**.

### 1a. (Weak, optional) Cortex/cytoplasm effective constitutive law: nonlinear strain-stiffening with a critical strain
- **DCM module touched:** cortex surface-tension law γ and/or the cytoplasm effective-stiffness response — *not* a new force, a *shape* for the existing response curve.
- **Change:** the paper's central emergent result is that a crosslinked actin network shears **linearly below a critical strain γ_c ≈ 0.5, then σ(γ) diverges super-linearly (strain-stiffening)** — Fig. 5a–d, §3.4. DCM currently treats the cortex as a (near-)linear surface tension γ with pressure-borne γ = ΔP·R/2 and constant K_A area elasticity; it has **no strain-stiffening onset**. If DCM ever needs an effective cortex/network constitutive curve (e.g. for AFM indentation large-strain response, or bleb/confinement where the cortex is locally stretched past ~50%), this paper says the *shape* is linear-then-stiffening with onset near γ≈0.5, and that the **stiffening magnitude at high strain is dominated by whichever element is softer** (crosslinker vs filament).
- **Why it (mildly) beats what DCM does now:** DCM's linear γ/K_A cannot reproduce the sharp high-strain upturn a real cortex shows; a piecewise or W-shaped strain-stiffening K_A(γ) with onset γ_c≈0.5 would be closer to physiology *if* a scenario ever probes >50% cortical strain. **But this is a "keep in back pocket" item, not a build item** — for the resting/interphase MCF7 operating point DCM lives well below γ_c and the linear law is fine.
- **HARD-RULE caveat:** do NOT hand-tune a stiffening exponent to pass a gate. If DCM adopts a strain-stiffening K_A, the onset and exponent must be *derived* from the FF-engine (which is exactly the class of network this paper models) or from an analytic semiflexible-network theory — never fitted. This is really an **FF→DCM upscaling handoff**, not a standalone DCM change.

### 1b. NON-ADOPTIONS (record so we don't chase them)
- **Filament rigidity from nucleotide/cation state (lₚ, kₛ, k_f):** belongs to FF's per-filament stretch/bend constants, invisible at DCM's surface-mesh scale. Do not surface into DCM.
- **ENM/RTB normal-mode machinery:** filament-internal elasticity extraction; no DCM analog.
- **Crosslinker (ACP) stretch/bend stiffness, R_ACP, 7 nm binding spacing:** these are FF crosslinker parameters (α-actinin / filamin / scruin). DCM's cortex crosslinking is already lumped into γ and K_A by design; re-introducing explicit ACP stiffness at DCM scale would *violate* the surface-mesh abstraction (it is the FF engine's job).
- **Cation/nucleotide chemistry (Mg²⁺/Ca²⁺, ATP/ADP/ADP-Pi):** no DCM chemical-state variable; out of scope for both engines at present.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh/continuum level

Only one relation is even conceptually portable, and only as an *effective law*, not a per-node force.

**Macroscopic shear stress / strain-stiffening curve (paper Eq. form, §2.3, Fig. 5):**

    σ = ( Σ F_clamped ) / A_z ,   applied strain rate  dγ/dt = 0.1 s⁻¹

- Symbol map to DCM: `σ` → an effective cortex/network shear stress one could compute on the DCM mesh as (tangential traction summed over a patch)/(patch area); `A_z` → area of the mesh patch or z-boundary; `γ` → local surface shear strain of the cortex triangulation; `F_clamped` → summed tangential node forces on a clamped boundary strip. **Grid-invariance risk:** σ defined this way is mesh-resolution-dependent unless normalized per unit reference area — must be validated grid-invariant before any use (DCM hard rule).
- Emergent shape to reproduce (not an equation the paper closes, but the target): `σ(γ)` ≈ linear (modulus G·γ) for γ ≲ γ_c=0.5, then super-linear divergence above γ_c. If DCM wants an effective law, encode as `K_A_eff(γ) = K_A0 · [1 + (γ/γ_c)^n · H(γ−γ_c)]` with **γ_c and n imported from FF, never fitted**.

**Persistence-length ↔ bending-rigidity bridge (used by the paper to convert lₚ→kₛ,k_f):**

    lₚ = κ / (k_B T),   κ = EI  (flexural rigidity)

- This is the *FF*-relevant bridge (filament EI). At DCM scale the only analog is the **membrane bending modulus κ_m**, which DCM already carries as an independent continuum parameter and does NOT derive from filament lₚ. So this equation is **informational only for DCM** — do not wire lₚ into κ_m.

**No equation for γ (cortex surface tension), Π (turgor), η (cytoplasm viscosity), K_A (area elasticity) appears in this paper.** There is nothing to port into those DCM fields.

## 3. PARAMETERS to set

**None supersede a DCM default.** The paper's quantitative parameters are all FF-scale (filament/crosslinker/box) or MD-scale. For completeness, the only DCM-adjacent number:

| symbol | value | units | DCM meaning | action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| γ_c (critical shear strain, stiffening onset) | ≈ 0.5 | dimensionless strain | onset of an *optional* strain-stiffening cortex law (§1a) | **do NOT set as a live config yet** — only if a >50%-strain scenario is built, and then import/verify via FF, not from this paper as a magic number |
| σ (network shear stress at 60% strain) | ~200–230 (soft ACP) → ~800–2500 (rigid ACP) | Pa | order-of-magnitude sanity band for an effective cortex/network stress at large strain | validation *reference* only (§5), not a config field |

Everything else (kₛ,ACP=0.002/0.2 N/m, k_f,ACP1=1.45×10⁻²⁵, k_f,ACP2=5.8×10⁻²⁵ N·m², R_ACP=0.01, 20 µM actin, 1.2 µm mean length, lₚ 3.5–12.7 µm, kₛ,actin 0.055–0.075 N/m, 140 nm × 7 nm segments) → **FF engine, not DCM.** Primary source for the segment/ACP constants is Kim et al. 2009a, not this paper.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

The paper's protocol is a **simple-shear rheometry test of a periodic network cube** — this is a **bulk-material** protocol, not a single-cell protocol. It is **not directly a DCM scenario** (DCM is a closed cell surface, not a periodic bulk box). Adaptation options:

- **(Legit, indirect) Cortex simple-shear calibration scenario:** if we ever want to *calibrate* a DCM effective cortex K_A_eff(γ), the natural place is to run the **FF** engine in exactly this paper's geometry (3 µm periodic box, shear at fixed rate, clamp boundary filaments, σ = ΣF/A_z) to extract σ(γ), then upscale into DCM. That makes this paper a **protocol template for an FF→DCM handoff**, not a DCM scenario itself.
- **Do NOT** reproduce the periodic-box shear as a DCM run — DCM has no periodic bulk mode and forcing one would strip the closed-surface/turgor baseline (physiological-baseline violation).
- Strain rate 0.1 s⁻¹ and critical strain 0.5 are the only numbers to carry into that FF calibration.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

DCM observables this paper can (weakly) constrain — all as **effective-law / order-of-magnitude references**, because the paper measures a bulk network, not a cell:

- **Critical strain for stiffening onset:** γ_c ≈ 0.5 (Fig. 5a–d). If a DCM AFM/confinement/bleb scenario drives cortical strain past ~50%, the effective response should begin to stiffen near this strain — a qualitative check, not a pass/fail gate.
- **High-strain network stress magnitude:** σ(60% strain) ≈ 200–230 Pa for a compliant-crosslinker network; up to ~800–2500 Pa if crosslinkers are as stiff as filaments (Fig. 5a–f). This is a sanity band for any effective cortex shear stress DCM (or the FF cortex under DCM) reports at large strain — it should land in hundreds of Pa, not kPa or single Pa.
- **Crosslinker-dominance principle (qualitative validation):** overall network stiffness is set by the *softer* element (compliant ACPs dominate → filament-rigidity changes barely register, p=0.88; rigid ACPs → filament changes register, p=0.03). For DCM this is a consistency check on the modeling choice to *lump* cortex crosslinking into γ/K_A: it is defensible precisely because physiological cortical crosslinkers (α-actinin, filamin) are compliant, so the cortex's aggregate response is crosslinker-dominated and a lumped γ is a reasonable continuum surrogate. **This paper thus retro-justifies DCM's lumped-cortex abstraction** for the compliant-crosslinker regime — a useful defensive citation.
- **Filament persistence length band:** lₚ 3.5–12.7 µm — an FF validation target, listed here only to note it does *not* map to a DCM observable.

No target here touches γ (cortical tension 0.15/1.5 mN/m), Π₀ (40 Pa), η (65 Pa·s), division force, ring closure, contact angle, or compaction — the paper measures none of these.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Wrong engine, wrong scale.** This is fine-grained crosslinked-filament rheology (FF territory) and molecular MD — DCM is a surface-mesh continuum. Forcing its parameters into DCM would *reverse* the ratified coarse-graining and re-introduce lumped-vs-fine confusion. The correct home for essentially everything here is the **FF lens**.
- **In-vitro purified F-actin, not living MCF7.** The network is reconstituted actin + purified crosslinkers (α-actinin/filamin/scruin) at 20 µM in a box, sheared as a passive material — **no myosin activity, no membrane, no turgor, no adhesion, no active tension**. Its σ(γ) is a *passive* elastic response. DCM's cortex is *active* (myosin-driven, pressure-borne γ). Adopting the passive stiffening curve wholesale would violate the physiological-baseline rule (it omits the resting active tension). Any use must be as a *passive stiffening correction added on top of* the active baseline, never as a replacement for it.
- **Bulk periodic geometry ≠ closed cell.** The 3 µm periodic shear box has no free surface, no enclosed volume, no ΔP — the exact opposite of DCM's baseline. The protocol cannot run natively in DCM.
- **Critical-strain and stress numbers are crosslinker- and concentration-specific.** γ_c≈0.5 and σ magnitudes shift with actin concentration, filament length, and crosslinker stiffness/density (paper says so explicitly). They are order-of-magnitude anchors, not universal constants — do not hard-code them as gate thresholds (magic-number risk).
- **Adaptation without violating baseline:** treat this paper strictly as (i) a *retro-justification* that lumping compliant cortical crosslinkers into γ/K_A is physically defensible, and (ii) an *FF-side* calibration template whose σ(γ) could one day be upscaled into a DCM effective K_A_eff(γ) — with γ_c and exponent imported from FF, layered on top of (never replacing) the active resting tension. Nothing here should be set as a live DCM config in the current work.
