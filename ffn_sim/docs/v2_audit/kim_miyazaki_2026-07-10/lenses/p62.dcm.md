# DCM lens — p62

**Paper:** Kubota, Miyazaki, Ogawa, Shimozawa, Kinosita, Ishiwata (2018), *Processive Nanostepping of Formin mDia1 Loosely Coupled with Actin Polymerization*, Nano Lett. 18, 6617–6624. DOI 10.1021/acs.nanolett.8b03277.

**Bottom line for DCM: LOW relevance (score 1/5).** This is an in-vitro single-molecule reconstitution assay of *one formin dimer* stepping on *one purified actin filament* under 4–6 pN constant load. Its entire content lives at the sub-filament (2.7 nm subunit) scale — barbed-end association/dissociation kinetics and FH2-half translocation. DCM is a closed triangulated surface mesh with lumped surface tension γ, turgor Π, membrane K_A/κ_m; it has **no barbed-end, no filament, no formin degree of freedom**. Nothing here ports to a node/face force law. This paper is squarely an **FF-engine** input (barbed-end growth kernel / KB-3.6 ratchet), not a DCM input. The only honest DCM touchpoint is *provenance for a cortical-actin turnover timescale* if — and only if — DCM ever replaces its phenomenological γ with a mechanistic turnover-driven cortex, which is not the current design. Sections below are written to that honest ceiling; most are "nothing applicable."

---

## 1. Mechanisms to adopt

**None that beat what DCM does now.** Enumerating the candidates and why each fails at our scale:

- **Cortical-tension γ generation (open DCM problem).** DCM's open γ-floor gap is a *force-magnitude* deficit (see `project-gamma-floor-likely-deficit.md`) — the cortex can't generate the interphase γ≈0.15 mN/m from myosin at native density. Formin mDia1 is a real cortical actin nucleator/elongator, so one might hope this paper feeds γ generation. It does **not**: the paper holds tension *constant* (feedback at 4–6 pN) and measures step kinetics, so it gives **no force-generation law, no force–velocity curve, and no tension output**. It constrains only the *zero/low-force elongation kinetics of a single formin*. γ in DCM is a surface-scalar; a single-formin step rate cannot be lifted to it without a full fine-grained cortex model (that is the FF engine's job, then coarse-grained back). Adopting nothing here is correct.

- **Loose coupling / multi-sized steps (2×, 3× subunit).** A refinement of the barbed-end ratchet mechanism (formin can skip one monomer then translocate two). This is a *fine-grained FF* mechanism with no surface-mesh analogue. Not for DCM.

- **Division / cytokinetic ring.** mDia1 does build the cytokinetic contractile ring in vivo, and DCM's division/ring is an open problem — but this paper contains **zero** ring geometry, ring line-tension, or closure-rate data. No adoptable content.

- **Confinement / aspiration / durotaxis / bleb / membrane–cortex coupling.** Absent from the paper entirely.

Verdict: **no DCM module change is warranted.** Route all extractable mechanism to the FF lens.

## 2. Equations to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

**None portable to DCM.** All five equations are 0-D data-analysis models over scalar dwell time `t`, not spatial PDEs/force laws over a mesh. For completeness (and to show they were evaluated against DCM state):

- **eq 1** `f(t) = aA e^{−kt} + aB kt e^{−kt} + aC k²t² e^{−kt}` — dwell-time histogram = sum of 1/2/3-sequential first-order reactions. No mapping to nodes/faces/γ/Π/η/K_A/κ_m. It is a stochastic *kinetic* model of one barbed end, orthogonal to DCM's deterministic continuum-mechanics surface solve.
- **eq 2** `P(t) = e^{−kt}` — survival (no-step) probability. Kinetic, not mechanical.
- **eqs 3–5** `p_single = e^{−kT}`, `p_double = kT e^{−kT}`, `p_triple = 1 − e^{−kT} − kT e^{−kT}` — probability that N sub-steps merge under temporal resolution T. Pure measurement-artifact model; irrelevant to any DCM observable.
- **Calibration** `F = 6πηrv` (Stokes drag on a 1 µm bead). This is generic Stokes drag; DCM already uses per-node drag *derived* from cytoplasm η=65.9 Pa·s (`project-dcm-physio-nodeface-contact.md`), not from a bead. Nothing new.

The only equation-shaped thing DCM shares in spirit is per-node drag, and DCM's is already correctly derived from bulk η — this paper adds nothing.

## 3. Parameters to set

**None feed a DCM config field.** For the record, the paper's quantitative parameters and where they belong (all → FF, not DCM):

| symbol | value | units | belongs to |
|---|---|---|---|
| forward unitary step | +2.8 (≈2.7 subunit) | nm | FF barbed-end kernel |
| backward unitary step | −2.4 | nm | FF barbed-end kernel |
| forward step rate k | 0.92 (40 nM) / 1.4 (75 nM) | s⁻¹ | FF formin on-stepping rate |
| backward step rate k | 0.56 (40 nM) / 0.62 (75 nM) | s⁻¹ | FF formin off-stepping rate |
| [G-actin] | 40, 75 | nM | FF solution BC |
| critical conc (both ends) | 64 | nM | FF |
| applied tension | 4–6 | pN | FF load regime |
| T | 26 ± 1 | °C | assay condition (below physiological 37 °C — see risks) |

None of these supersede a DCM default. DCM has **no** formin/actin-turnover config field to feed. The single conceivable future hook: if DCM ever adds a mechanistic turnover-driven cortex, the formin barbed-end renewal rate (~2.5–3.8 nm/s = 0.92–1.4 steps/s × 2.7 nm at 40–75 nM G-actin) could set the *cortical actin renewal timescale* τ_cortex — but that field does not exist today and would be built in FF and coarse-grained, not set from this paper into DCM.

## 4. Geometry / protocol to reproduce

**None usable as a DCM scenario.** The assay is an "actin dumbbell": two 1-µm beads in a dual optical trap linked by a single stretched filament, mDia1 immobilized on one bead via GST/glutathione. There is no cell, no closed surface, no confinement chamber, no aspiration pipette, no aggregate, no substrate. Nothing maps to a DCM single-cell or N≈400 aggregate scenario. (The trap-feedback constant-tension protocol is an FF-scale single-filament load protocol at most.)

## 5. Validation targets for DCM observables

**None.** DCM observables are surface/aggregate-scale: cortical tension γ, shape index/asphericity, contact angle, compaction/porosity, division force, ring-closure rate, cortical flow speed. This paper reports **none** of these. Its measured numbers (step sizes 2.8/−2.4/4.9/8.7 nm; rates 0.92/1.4/0.56/0.62 s⁻¹; area fractions; c_c=64 nM) are single-formin observables and are validation targets for **FF**, not DCM. A DCM run cannot and should not be asked to reproduce any of them.

## 6. Risks / caveats

- **Scale mismatch (fatal for DCM).** 2.7 nm subunit / single-filament / single-formin vs. DCM's ~7.5 µm cell surface mesh — ~3–4 orders of magnitude and a category change (discrete kinetic stepping vs. continuum surface mechanics). Forcing any of this into a DCM force law would violate the "keep proposals at our single-cell fine-grained scale, not lump the wrong scale" spirit — here the *paper's* scale is finer than DCM's, so it belongs in FF.
- **In-vitro purified, not living MCF7.** GST-tagged truncated FH1FH2 + rabbit skeletal G-actin in a defined buffer, 26 °C, no profilin, no crowding, no myosin, no membrane. Living cortex operates at 37 °C with profilin (>10× faster elongation), competing cappers, and myosin prestress. Any parameter lifted from here would be an *un-physiological baseline* for a cell (violates the physiological-operating-point HARD rule) unless corrected — another reason not to route it to DCM directly.
- **Constant-load assay ≠ tension generator.** The feedback holds 4–6 pN, so the data constrain kinetics *at* a load, not the load a cortex *produces*. It cannot inform the γ-generation deficit even in principle. Do not let the "formin builds cortex" intuition smuggle a single-molecule step rate into γ.
- **Correct routing avoids the trap.** The safe, rule-respecting action is: register the numbers as an **FF** barbed-end-growth provenance anchor (and a TAG SourceEvidence row), and record DCM relevance as marginal. No DCM config edit, no gate, no scenario.
