# DCM lens — p57

**Paper:** Kubota et al. 2017, *Biophysical Journal* 113:461–471 — "Biphasic Effect of Profilin Impacts the Formin mDia1 Force-Sensing Mechanism in Actin Polymerization." DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2017.06.012.

**Bottom line for DCM:** LOW relevance (score ~1/5). This is a purified single-molecule optical-tweezers study of one actin filament's barbed-end elongation under pN tension, capped by the mDia1 FH1-FH2 formin fragment, at 26 °C in vitro. It resolves molecular-scale (5.5 nm) formin translocation kinetics. DCM is a surface-mesh continuum engine (nodes/faces, γ, Π, η, K_A, κ_m) that does NOT resolve individual filaments — that is the **FF engine's** domain, where this paper is a strong, direct source (force-dependent `barbed_end_growth_kernel`, KB-3.6). There is exactly ONE thin conceptual thread that touches DCM: the mechanistic *origin* of cortical tension γ includes tension-accelerated actin assembly, which motivates a mechanosensitive (tension-dependent) γ-generation law rather than a static γ. Everything else (membrane, nucleus, junctions, aggregate σ, contact, division, confinement) is untouched by this paper. Below I record honestly what does and does not port.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

**(a) [WEAK/CONCEPTUAL — cortex module] Make cortical γ generation tension-sensitized, not static.**
The paper's central finding is that *stretching tension accelerates* mDia1-mediated barbed-end elongation (open-state probability p_open → 1 above ~3 pN; ADP-actin 3.4 → 10.9 subunits/s low→high tension), the OPPOSITE of the previously-assumed inhibition. Cortical actin at a tensed cell surface is under exactly such membrane/cortex tension. DCM currently carries γ as a pressure-borne quantity (γ = ΔP·R/2) with interphase ≈0.15 and mitotic ≈1.5 mN/m set as setpoints. This paper says the actin-*generation* side of the cortex's steady state is itself mechanosensitive: higher cortical tension → faster formin-driven filament elongation → denser/longer cortical actin → feeds back on γ. WHY it could beat current DCM: today γ is an imposed number; a tension-dependent assembly term would let the cortex partially *generate* its own tension setpoint mechanistically (relevant to the open "cortical-tension γ generation" problem). CAVEAT: at DCM's continuum scale this can only enter as a **lumped mesoscopic law**, which is in tension with the fine-grained-preferred hard rule — so this belongs in FF (fine-grained) and should reach DCM only as a *calibrated boundary condition* handed up from FF, never as a hand-tuned DCM term. Recommend: do NOT implement in DCM directly; log as an FF→DCM coupling candidate.

**(b) [NOT ADOPTED] Formin as an explicit force-sensing barbed-end capper.** Genuinely valuable but it is a filament-resolved mechanism → FF engine, not DCM. Flagged here only to record that it was considered and correctly routed away from DCM.

No other DCM module (contact/IPC, cadherin junction, T1 rearrangement, remesh, nucleus, membrane bending, osmoregulation) is advanced by this paper.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

**None port cleanly to DCM.** The paper's equations are per-filament molecular rate laws with a molecular working distance d = 5.5 nm; they have no surface-mesh (node/face) analogue. Transcribed here for completeness and to show the one factor that *could* be lifted as a phenomenological modulator:

**Eq. 1 (stair-stepping elongation rate):**
```
v_elong = kon·C_A·[1 − p_c⁰·exp(−f·d/k_B·T)] − koff·p_c⁰·exp(−f·d/k_B·T)
```
Symbols: `kon,koff` = G-actin on/off rates at a single formin-capped barbed end; `C_A` = local G-actin conc.; `f` = tension on the FH2 translocating half; `d = 5.5 nm`; `p_c⁰` = zero-force closed-state probability. There is NO mapping of `f` to a DCM node force, of `C_A` to any face/volume field, or of `v_elong` (subunits/s on one filament) to a mesh observable. DCM has no per-filament state to receive this.

**Force factor (the only transferable idea):**
```
p_c = p_c⁰ · exp(−f·d / k_B·T)     →     p_open = 1 − p_c
```
The Bell/transition-state factor `exp(−f·d/k_BT)`. IF (and only if) FF hands DCM a mesoscopic "cortex assembly gain," a DCM-level phenomenological form could be `γ_gen(σ_cortex) ∝ [1 − p_c⁰·exp(−σ·A_eff·d/k_BT)]`, mapping cortical surface stress σ (from face tensions) through an effective per-formin force. This is explicitly **NOT recommended for DCM** because A_eff would be an un-derivable lumping factor (magic-number risk) — it is documented only so the coupling is traceable. The clean home is FF, where `f` is a real per-filament load.

**F = 6πηrv** (Stokes drag, trap calibration) — irrelevant to DCM physics; it is a measurement-apparatus calibration.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

**No DCM-usable scenario.** The geometry is a two-bead "actin dumbbell": one 1-µm bead carries GST-mDia1 at a filament barbed end, the other anchors a phalloidin-stabilized pointed end; single-trap (~0.13 pN) vs double-trap (1–5.5 pN constant-force) tension. This is a single-filament mechanical assay, not a cell/aggregate/confinement/aspiration/AFM-on-a-cell geometry. There is nothing to reproduce as a DCM (membrane-enclosed volume) scenario. The dumbbell flexural-rigidity correction (EI = 33,900 pN·nm²) is a bead-rotation artifact model, not a cell-mechanics setup.

If anything, the FF engine could reproduce the dumbbell as a single-filament-under-constant-force elongation test — but that is an FF validation harness, out of DCM scope.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Scale mismatch is fundamental, not adaptable.** DCM operates on a triangulated surface continuum (7.5 µm cell, ~hundreds–thousands of faces); this paper resolves a 5.5 nm formin conformational step on one filament. There is no non-lumping way to inject per-filament kinetics into a surface mesh. Attempting to (e.g. an A_eff force-conversion factor) would introduce an un-derivable magic number and violate the no-magic-number hard rule. Correct disposition: route to FF.
- **In-vitro purified, not living MCF7.** Rate constants are for the isolated mDia1 FH1-FH2 fragment with purified rabbit-muscle actin, no cortex crosslinkers, no myosin, no membrane. They cannot be a DCM cortex parameter without violating the physiological-baseline rule (they are not an in-vivo cortical operating point).
- **Temperature 26 °C vs physiological 37 °C.** Rates are temperature-shifted; another reason not to import directly.
- **The one legitimate DCM-adjacent insight** — that cortical tension can *accelerate* (not inhibit) actin assembly, so γ-generation is mechanosensitive with positive feedback — should be pursued via **FF (fine-grained cortex) producing a calibrated γ that DCM consumes as a boundary condition**, exactly the sanctioned FF→DCM handoff. Do NOT implement a tension-dependent γ term inside DCM from this paper; that would be a lumped proxy the hard rules forbid.
- **Net recommendation:** register this paper's data under the FF and TAG-validation lenses. For DCM, the actionable output is a single note in the "cortical-tension γ generation" open-problem file: *γ generation is mechanosensitive (tension-accelerated formin assembly, Kubota 2017); model at FF scale and pass γ up to DCM — do not lump into DCM directly.*
