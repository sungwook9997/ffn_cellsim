# FF lens — p55

**Paper:** Oyama et al. 2015, *Directional Bleb Formation in Spherical Cells under Temperature Gradient*, Biophys. J. 109(2):355–364. DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2015.06.016.

**One-line for FF:** An in-vivo single-cell paper (HeLa, rounded metaphase / trypsin-rounded interphase, R≈10 µm) that resolves — by live actin/myosin/membrane imaging — the *mechanistic sequence* of bleb nucleation from a **cortical actomyosin symmetry break**: local myosin-driven contractility rise → **cortex–membrane detachment** → **myosin-driven cortex rupture (network disassembly)** → cortex recoil to the opposite pole → membrane bleb extension. It carries **no simulation constants, no force laws, no rate constants**. Its FF value is (a) a clean bleb-nucleation *scenario + mechanism sequence* our filament cortex must reproduce emergently, (b) a set of geometric/kinetic *validation targets*, and (c) a physical hook (temperature-set crossbridge number) into the Hill/duty-ratio motor layer. Everything here must be adapted so the trigger is an **explicit local myosin-density / crosslink-off-rate perturbation on the native 38000-filament cortex**, NOT a temperature field (which FF does not model).

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Bleb nucleation as an EMERGENT cortex→membrane sequence (not a lumped "bleb force")
The paper's Fig. 4E is a fully-resolved mechanistic chain that maps one-to-one onto FF's explicit compartments:

| Stage (Fig. 4E) | FF-native realization |
|---|---|
| (i) baseline sphericity | cortex filaments + Bell-Evans crosslinks + myosin minifilaments generate γ; membrane containment + turgor ΔP balance it (γ·2/R vs ΔP). Physiological baseline from t=0. |
| (ii) local contractility ↑ at warm pole | raise *local* myosin minifilament engagement/duty in a cortical patch → higher local contractile stress. |
| (iii) cortex–PM **detachment** | local cortex tension exceeds cortex↔membrane linker (ERM-type) rupture force → membrane peels from cortex. **FF needs an explicit cortex-membrane linker bond with a Bell-Evans off-rate.** |
| (ii'/iv) cortex **rupture** = network disassembly | myosin-driven filament fragmentation + crosslink unbinding cascade locally clears the network (density ↓ at warm pole). |
| (v) cortex recoil + bleb extension | remaining tensed cortex contracts toward the cool pole (density ↑ far side); membrane, no longer restrained, balloons out under ΔP at the cleared pole. |

**Why it beats current FF:** FF has cortex, myosin, crosslinks, membrane, turgor as separate explicit compartments but (per memory) has **no cortex↔membrane detachment channel and no local cortex-rupture channel** — blebbing cannot emerge. This paper specifies the exact missing couplings (a rupturable cortex-membrane linker + a myosin-driven local disassembly) and proves they are the physiological nucleation route. This is the mechanistic bleb pathway to build.

### M2 — Myosin-II as an active network-DISASSEMBLY / severing agent (cortex rupture)
The paper explicitly attributes the warm-side cortex-density *drop* to "actin network disassembly by the temperature-enhanced contractile force of [myosin]" (refs 41–44: Murrell & Gardel 2012 *PNAS* biomimetic actomyosin severing; Wilson et al. 2010 *Nature* myosin-II regulates actin disassembly). Critically, **the MRLC/actin density loss at the warm side persists even under blebbistatin, Y-27632, and cytochalasin D** (Fig. 3B) — i.e. the disassembly channel is partly independent of active contraction.

**FF change:** add a **contraction-facilitated F-actin fragmentation channel** to the myosin module — a myosin minifilament under high local tension increases the bond-scission probability of the actin segments it spans (buckling-induced severing). This is the Murrell–Gardel mechanism and is exactly the class flagged in the FF brief ("myosin: add F-actin fragmentation channel that facilitates contraction"). **Why it beats current FF:** FF myosin currently only walks/pulls; it cannot locally clear the network, so a cortex can never *rupture* — it can only densify or thin uniformly. Fragmentation gives FF the emergent symmetry-break + rupture this paper measures.

### M3 — Cortex–membrane linker as an explicit, force-rupturable bond
"Spontaneous bleb formation is initiated by detachment of the PM from the cell cortex … induced either by contractile force from actomyosin and/or by local rupture of the cortex" (refs 31,32 = Charras & Paluska 2008, Tinevez et al. 2009). FF's membrane containment is currently a soft geometric constraint. **Change:** represent cortex↔membrane attachment as explicit linker bonds (ERM/moesin-class) with a Bell-Evans off-rate `k_det = k0·exp(F/F_b)`; detachment (bleb seed) emerges when local cortical tension pulls these past their rupture force. Fine-grained, mechanistic — replaces the lumped "membrane always follows cortex" assumption.

### M4 — Temperature dependence of myosin force = a crossbridge-number knob (Hill/duty layer)
"[Tension by] muscle myosins increases at higher temperature (20) due to the **increased number of crossbridges** (33,34)" (Ishiwata-lab: Kaya & Higuchi 2010; refs 33/34). This is a mechanistic statement about the **duty ratio / number of attached heads** in the Stam–Hocky minifilament, not a lumped force scaling. **FF hook:** the paper does NOT give FF a temperature law (FF has no T field, and the paper *leaves the sign of cortical actomyosin T-dependence open* — refs 35–40 report the opposite trend for cells). So do NOT port a temperature scaling. Instead, note that "raise local contractility" in FF must be implemented at the *mechanistically correct locus* = increase the **number of engaged heads / duty ratio** of the minifilament, not a scalar force multiplier. This is the physiological knob (M1 stage ii).

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

The paper contains **no dynamical force laws or rate constants** — only fluorescence-thermometry calibrations and heat-conduction fits, which are apparatus physics, irrelevant to FF. Nothing to transcribe from the paper directly. The schemes FF should implement are the *named mechanisms* above, whose quantitative forms come from the cited primary sources (to be pulled separately if adopted):

- **Cortex-membrane detachment (M3):** Bell-Evans off-rate on the ERM linker bond
  `k_det(F) = k_det0 · exp(F / F_b)` — F = tension carried by the linker bond (FF bond force), F_b = linker rupture scale. Values NOT in this paper; source from Tinevez 2009 / ERM literature.
- **Contraction-facilitated fragmentation (M2):** per-segment scission hazard rising with the compressive/buckling load a spanning myosin imposes, `k_sever ∝ exp(-ΔG‡ + a·F_buckle)`. Values from Murrell & Gardel 2012 (ref 41), Vogel 2013 — not this paper.
- **Force balance the bleb violates (the only "law" stated):** rounded-cell sphericity = Laplace balance `2γ/R = ΔP`; a **local** imbalance nucleates a bleb. FF already encodes γ (emergent from filaments+myosin+crosslinks) and ΔP (turgor) explicitly — this paper just confirms the balance and that *local* (not global) perturbation is what blebs. Use it as the acceptance relation, not a new force.

**Net:** this is a validation/mechanism paper. Port mechanisms M1–M4; source the constants elsewhere.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenario)

**Scenario "polar-bleb":** an FF native-scale bleb-nucleation test that our cortex should pass.

- **Geometry:** single rounded cell, R≈7.5–10 µm (use the MCF7 native R=7.5 µm cortex, Nc≈266k), full compartments ON: membrane + cortex (38000 filaments, `--from-resting`) + cytoplasm/turgor ΔP≈40 Pa + nucleus + microtubules. Physiological baseline from t=0 (γ interphase ≈0.15 mN/m emergent).
- **Perturbation (the FF-legal substitute for the temperature gradient):** impose a **spatially localized cortical patch** (one pole, angular half-width ~30–45°, matching the sharp 1.3 °C / ~0.065 °C·µm⁻¹ across-cell gradient which is *sharp and one-sided*) where you either (i) raise local myosin minifilament duty/engagement (M4, "increased-contractility route"), or (ii) raise the local crosslink off-rate + enable M2 fragmentation (the "rupture route"). Both routes are predicted to nucleate a bleb — run both, they are the paper's two "cases."
- **Gradient sharpness matters:** a *sharp, one-sided* perturbation → **single directional (polar) bleb**; a *shallow/uniform* perturbation → **multiple non-directional blebs**. FF should reproduce this bifurcation by varying the angular sharpness of the patch. This is a strong emergent-behavior discriminator, not a fit.
- **Controls the paper provides (build as negative controls):**
  - Actin depolymerization (LatB/CytoD) or myosin-off (blebbistatin) or ROCK-off (Y-27632) → **no polar bleb**. FF: with myosin engagement / crosslinking off, the patch should NOT bleb → confirms the mechanism is actomyosin-driven, not an artifact.
  - Cortex stabilization (jasplakinolide) → bleb still forms and is *longer* → FF: stiffer/slower-turnover crosslinks should still permit the contractility-route bleb.
  - Microtubule depolymerization (nocodazole) → *longer* blebs; MT-intact spindle restrains extension → FF: microtubule compartment should act as a passive restraint on bleb reach.
  - Convection/flow-only control → **no bleb** (rules out mechanical flow). FF: a pure external drag field with no cortex perturbation must not bleb.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **No constants — do not fabricate parameters.** This is an experimental live-cell paper; it yields mechanism + validation targets, not force laws or rate constants. Any k_off, F_b, sever rate, or duty value for M2/M3/M4 must be sourced from the *cited* primary literature (Murrell–Gardel 2012, Tinevez 2009, Kaya–Higuchi 2010) and separately magic-number-audited — never back-fit to the bleb lengths here (no fitting).
- **The trigger is a temperature gradient, which FF does not and should not model.** Import only the *downstream mechanical asymmetry* (local myosin ↑ / local disassembly), imposed as an explicit cortical patch. Do NOT add a temperature field or a temperature-scaling of myosin force — the paper itself leaves the sign of cortical actomyosin T-dependence **unresolved** (refs 20/33/34 say force ↑ with T via crossbridge number; refs 35–40 say cortical tension ↓ with T). Porting a T-law would be adopting a contested, non-mechanistic scalar.
- **HeLa mitotic/rounded vs MCF7 interphase cortex.** Species/state differ; but the scenario is genuinely at our single-cell rounded-cortex scale (R≈10 µm ~ MCF7 7.5 µm), and the mechanism (cortex tension vs turgor, myosin disassembly, cortex-membrane detachment) is cell-type-general. Keep the FF run at the physiological MCF7 baseline (γ≈0.15 mN/m, ΔP≈40 Pa); use the paper's *ratios and phenomenology* (near↓/far↑, angle, incidence, length~R), not its absolute HeLa temperatures.
- **"Increased contractility" must be implemented at the mechanistic locus.** Increase engaged head number / duty ratio of the Stam–Hocky minifilament in the patch (M4) — do NOT multiply a lumped cortical-tension scalar. A scalar γ-multiplier would violate the fine-grained rule and mask whether FF can *emergently* rupture.
- **Native-scale requirement (HARD).** Per project rule, run and conclude ONLY on the native `--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules` cortex — a coarse cortex would collapse/thin on its own and fake a "rupture," confounding M2. The symmetry-break gate must be demonstrated native + full-compartment before it is reported.
- **Cortex-membrane linker (M3) is currently absent in FF.** Adding it is the largest build item and touches the membrane compartment; scope it as a new explicit bond type with its own Bell-Evans off-rate, gated + sanity-checked, before the bleb scenario can pass. Until then, only M2 (fragmentation → density symmetry break) is testable.
