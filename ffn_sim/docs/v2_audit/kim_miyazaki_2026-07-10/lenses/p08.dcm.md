# DCM lens — p08

**Paper:** Sasmal, Miyazaki, Carlier-Grynkorn & Tran (2026), *Chimeras of kinesin-6 and kinesin-14 reveal head-neck-tail domain functions and dysfunctions that lead to aneuploidy in fission yeast.* Curr Biol 36, 415–425. DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2025.11.073.

**Bottom line for DCM: LOW relevance (score 1/5).** This is a purely *in-vivo* molecular-genetics paper about the mitotic **spindle** — microtubules + directional kinesin motors inside a closed-mitosis yeast nucleus. It contains **no governing equations, no force laws, no bond kinetics, no moduli, no tensions** ("No in-house code was used in this study"). DCM is a triangulated closed-surface continuum cell (membrane / actomyosin cortex / cytoplasm / nucleus / cadherin junctions) — it does **not** model the MT/kinesin spindle at all. The only physical touchpoint is a *qualitatively invoked* nuclear-envelope resistive tension. Everything below is either an honest "not applicable" or a single speculative, forward-looking hook. Nothing here should be adopted into a production DCM run now; it is logged so the extraction is complete.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

**None adoptable now.** The paper's mechanisms live one level below DCM's abstraction (single-motor head/neck/tail domain function, MT sliding directionality) and in a compartment (mitotic spindle) DCM does not represent. Concretely:

- **Spindle sliding / elongation force** (Klp9 midzone antiparallel-MT sliding; Pkl1 pole minus-end focusing) — DCM has no microtubule or spindle module; there is no force to redistribute onto the surface mesh. Not adoptable.
- **Motor head/neck/tail directionality logic** — belongs to the FF fine-grained filament engine's motor layer, not the DCM continuum surface. Not a DCM lever.
- **ONE weak, forward-looking hook — nuclear-envelope resistive tension during shape change (future `nucleus` module, NOT now):** The Discussion (p10, citing refs 38–41) invokes, *qualitatively only*, that in **closed mitosis the nuclear membrane exerts a tensile resistive force** opposing fast spindle elongation, and that when the motor over-drives elongation the spindle **buckles into an S-shape and breaks**. The transferable *idea* — a deforming internal structure loads an enclosing membrane whose **area-elasticity K_A + bending κ_m** resist, and beyond a resistance threshold the enclosed structure buckles rather than the envelope yielding — is exactly the constitutive picture DCM already uses for its outer membrane (K_A area-elasticity, κ_m bending, enclosed-volume/turgor constraint). If DCM ever grows a **nuclear-envelope sub-mesh** (the nucleus is currently a stiffer inclusion with E_nuc ~1–10 kPa, not a load-bearing lipid-bilayer shell), this paper is a *biological motivation* to give that envelope its own K_A/κ_m/enclosed-volume machinery rather than a lumped elastic ball. **Why it would beat current DCM:** today the nucleus is a bulk-elastic inclusion with no surface-tension/bending resistance and no buckling channel; a bilayer-shell envelope with area/bending elasticity would let load-induced envelope tension and buckling emerge mechanistically. **But the paper gives zero magnitudes**, so this is a design-direction note, not an implementable change.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

**No DCM-usable scenario.** The assays are (i) live spinning-disk confocal of yeast spindle dynamics, (ii) FRAP of motor-eGFP, (iii) nmt1+/pREP81 over-expression titration, (iv) Ch16-ADE6 mini-chromosome-loss genetics. None maps to a DCM continuum scenario (no confinement BC, no aspiration, no AFM indentation, no aggregate/compaction, no durotaxis). The over-expression "titrate motor number → phenotype threshold" design is conceptually the kind of dose–response sweep DCM uses, but the dosed quantity (kinesin copies at a pole) has no DCM surface-mesh counterpart. Not reproducible as a DCM run.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

**None.** DCM validation gates target single-cell mechanobiology observables — cortical tension γ, area strain, contact angle, shape index, aspiration length, indentation force, aggregate compaction/porosity, division-ring closure. This paper reports **spindle** velocities (0.35–2.39 µm/min), spindle lengths (8.4–13.8 µm), FRAP times (29–47 s), and aneuploidy fractions (2–11%). A DCM run cannot produce any of these (no spindle, no MTs, no chromosome segregation). Registering them as DCM validation targets would be a category error. (They *could* seed a future FF/spindle KnowledgeClaim as SourceEvidence — provenance is clean, peer-reviewed Curr Biol, verified DOI — but that is out of DCM scope.)

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Wrong organism, wrong compartment, wrong physics class.** *S. pombe* closed mitosis vs. our single MCF7-like mammalian cell; the object of study is the intranuclear MT/kinesin spindle, not the actomyosin-cortex surface DCM simulates. Any "adoption" would be importing a mechanism DCM has no state variables for.
- **No magnitudes → magic-number / no-fitting hazard.** The one conceptual hook (nuclear-envelope resistive tension, buckling threshold) is given only qualitatively. Attempting to turn it into a DCM force would require inventing K_A/κ_m/threshold values from nowhere — a direct violation of the No-magic-number and No-fitting-to-data rules. **Do not** parameterize a DCM nuclear envelope from this paper; source such values from a dedicated nuclear-mechanics measurement paper instead.
- **Physiological-baseline rule.** Even the forward-looking nuclear-envelope idea must, if ever built, be initialized at real in-vivo nuclear-envelope tension/pressure from a mammalian source — not seeded from yeast spindle data. This paper cannot supply that baseline.
- **Scale-appropriateness:** the paper's mechanisms are sub-DCM (single-molecule domain function) and would be tissue-irrelevant shortcuts if lumped onto the surface mesh. Keeping them out of DCM respects the fine-grained-mechanistic-vs-lumped rule (their proper home is FF's motor layer, if the project ever adds an MT/spindle engine).

**Verdict:** Extraction complete; no DCM equations, parameters, protocols, or validation targets are applicable. One weak, magnitude-free design note about a future load-bearing nuclear-envelope (K_A/κ_m/enclosed-volume shell) is the sole conceptual carry-over, and it is explicitly not implementable from this paper.
