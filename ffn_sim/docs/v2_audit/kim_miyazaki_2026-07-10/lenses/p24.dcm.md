# DCM lens — p24

**Paper:** Jung, Tabatabai, Thomas, Tabei, Murrell & Kim (2019), "Dynamic motions of molecular motors in the actin cytoskeleton," *Cytoskeleton* 76(11–12):517–531, DOI 10.1002/cm.21582. Taeyoon Kim lab agent-based Brownian-dynamics actomyosin + Murrell-lab in-vitro cortex reconstitution.

**Bottom line for DCM (honest framing).** This is a *filament-resolved* cortex-network paper with no membrane, turgor, nucleus, or closed surface — its native home is the **FF engine**, not DCM. DCM is a surface-mesh continuum cell. So there are **no directly portable DCM equations or config numbers**. What *does* transfer is one genuinely load-bearing **conceptual mechanism**: **F-actin turnover is the plastic force-relaxation channel of the cortex**, and its rate sets whether accumulated cortical/aggregate stress *dissipates and remodels* or *jams and locks up*. That maps cleanly onto two of DCM's named open problems — (i) cortical-tension γ generation/relaxation and (ii) the loose→compact aggregate compaction RATE being "drag-limited, needs a rearrangement channel." The paper also explicitly identifies **cortex aggregation instability = cytokinetic-ring formation**, which is a direct hook for DCM cell division. Everything else is FF material. Adopt the *mechanism/timescale reasoning*, not the *filament parameters* (importing 3.8 pN/head or k⁰_u,ACP into a continuum γ would be a magic-number violation).

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cortex effective viscosity from turnover-driven stress relaxation (γ is NOT purely elastic)
**DCM module:** cortex (`gamma`, surface-viscosity term) + membrane surface viscosity.
**Change:** Give the actomyosin cortex a **Maxwell-type viscoelastic relaxation** with a relaxation time τ_relax set by F-actin turnover, rather than treating cortical tension γ as a purely elastic/instantaneous surface stress. The paper's central result (§3.6, Fig 6): with turnover off, motor-generated forces accumulate irreversibly (stalling, taut force-webs, aster jamming); turning turnover on **continuously relaxes those forces** — "if actin segments disappear due to depolymerization, all ACPs and motors bound to them lose connection permanently," so **turnover relaxes force more efficiently than crosslinker unbinding.** In continuum terms, cortical stress obeys σ̇ + σ/τ_relax = (elastic build-up), with τ_relax ≈ 1/k_t,A (whole-filament turnover ≈ tens of s in cells; Chugh & Paluch 2018).
**Why it beats current DCM:** DCM's γ is currently *pressure-borne and elastic* (γ = ΔP·R/2). That has no dissipative/plastic channel, so any DCM contractile perturbation is stored, not relaxed — exactly the failure mode the paper shows for the *no-turnover* case. A turnover-set relaxation time is the mechanistic reason a live cortex does **not** lock into force-webs and asters, and it is derivable (from a measured cortical turnover half-life), not tuned.

### M2 — Turnover as the missing plastic-relaxation channel that explains "aggregate-σ compaction is drag-limited"
**DCM module:** aggregate surface tension σ for compaction + remesh + T1 rearrangement (open).
**Change:** Adopt the paper's diagnosis by analogy: a contractile network **with no relaxation channel jams** (motors stall / asters trap / force-webs freeze — Fig 5, all three confinement regimes are the *k_t,A = 0* column). Compaction/remodeling only proceeds continuously **once a relaxation rate is present** (Fig 6: even a *slight* k_t,A restores superdiffusive, ergodic remodeling). This is the network-scale twin of DCM's finding that aggregate-σ compaction stalls at a drag-limited end-state and needs cadherin **T1 rearrangement escalation**. The lever: T1 rearrangement in the DCM junction layer plays the role that F-actin turnover plays here — the *plastic* channel that lets stored contractile stress convert into irreversible shape change instead of jamming.
**Why it beats current DCM:** It reframes the open compaction-rate problem correctly — the missing ingredient is a **rate-controlled plastic/relaxation channel with a physiological timescale**, not more drag or more σ. It supplies a first-principles argument (from an independent mechanistic model) for *why* aggregate-σ alone saturates, and points the redesign at a turnover/rearrangement escalation with a derivable rate.

### M3 — Cytokinetic-ring = localized cortex-aggregation instability (division hook)
**DCM module:** cell division / cytokinetic ring (open).
**Change:** The Discussion states explicitly: "when the cortex shows aggregating behaviors, such as **formation of cytokinetic rings**, it is expected that motor motions are confined." The paper's aggregation regime (low connectivity + high motor density + fast crosslinker unbinding → aster/cluster with polarity-sorted barbed ends at center) is the same contractile-clustering instability that, localized to the equator, forms the ring. For DCM: model the cytokinetic ring as a **circumferential band of elevated cortical tension γ (locally raised motor density / locally suppressed turnover)**, i.e. a localized relaxation-time reduction that lets contractile stress accumulate on the equatorial band while the poles stay turnover-relaxed.
**Why it beats current DCM:** DCM has no division ring mechanism; this gives a mechanistically-grounded, fine-grained-consistent way to *generate* the ring (spatial modulation of the same γ/turnover fields already in the cortex module) instead of a lumped imposed line tension.

### M4 — Connectivity → cortex stiffness ordering (calibration sanity, weak)
**DCM module:** cortex/membrane moduli (K_A, κ_m) — informational only.
**Change:** Network connectivity (set by mean filament length ⟨L_f⟩ and crosslink ratio R_ACP) monotonically controls network elasticity (higher connectivity → lower actin TE-MSD → stiffer; Fig 2a, Kim 2009). This is the *ordering* DCM's lumped cortex moduli should respect if ever re-derived from cortex composition; not a config change on its own.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

**None transcribe verbatim** — the paper's equations (Eq 1 Bell's-law crosslinker off-rate; Eqs 2–4 MSD; Eq 5 g(r)) are filament/tracer-level and belong to FF/analysis, not to a surface-mesh continuum. The **derived continuum relation** to add to DCM (motivated by M1/M2, NOT taken as a printed equation from the paper) is a Maxwell relaxation of cortical surface stress:

    dσ_c/dt  +  σ_c / τ_relax  =  K_eff · (dε_c/dt)          [DCM cortex, per face]

Symbol map to DCM state:
- **σ_c** → cortical surface tension carried per triangular face (the DCM `gamma` field, N/m).
- **τ_relax** → cortex stress-relaxation time ≈ **1 / k_t,A**, i.e. inverse F-actin turnover rate (s). Physiological cortex turnover half-life → τ_relax ~ 10–30 s.
- **K_eff** → effective cortex areal/shear stiffness (maps to DCM `K_A` for area strain, `kappa_m`-adjacent for the elastic build-up rate).
- **ε_c** → face areal strain (already computed by DCM's area-elasticity term); dε_c/dt from node velocities.
- Node force assembly: as now — σ_c on a face contributes the usual tension gradient force to its 3 nodes; only the *time evolution* of σ_c gains the −σ_c/τ_relax dissipative term.

Boundary/limit checks: τ_relax → ∞ recovers current elastic DCM cortex (no-turnover, jamming regime of Fig 5); τ_relax → 0 gives a fully fluid cortex (γ cannot sustain prestress). Physiological cortex sits in between — the paper's whole point.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

- **P1 — Flat cortex-patch calibration scenario (5×5×0.2 µm slab, PBC in x,y).** Reproduce as a DCM *flat membrane-cortex test region* to calibrate the cortex Maxwell relaxation (M1) and the aggregation instability (M3) *before* wrapping onto the closed sphere. A flat periodic patch isolates cortex rheology from curvature/turgor — a clean unit test for τ_relax. Observable: does an imposed contractile stress relax (turnover ON) vs. lock into a taut band (turnover OFF)?
- **P2 — Preassemble-then-activate protocol.** The paper assembles the network to steady-state morphology for 1000 s *without motors*, then imports and activates. This is methodologically identical to DCM's `--from-resting` checkpoint discipline (relax the passive structure to its physiological resting state, *then* switch on active perturbations). Reinforces (does not change) the DCM rule; do not draw conclusions from a fresh un-relaxed cortex.
- **P3 — Contractile-aggregation → ring scenario.** For the division hook (M3): initialize a physiological turnover-ON cortex on the closed cell, then locally lower τ_relax (or raise motor density) in an equatorial band and check that a contractile ring emerges as a localized-aggregation instability, poles staying relaxed.
- **P4 — Turnover sweep as a stability knob.** Reproduce Fig 6's qualitative claim at the DCM level: sweep τ_relax and confirm that long τ_relax → cortex jams/heterogeneous (asters), short τ_relax → homogeneous, uniformly-tensioned cortex. Gate: uniformity of γ across faces vs. τ_relax.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

The paper offers **few** continuum-level targets (it is a tracer-MSD paper). The DCM-usable ones:

- **Cortical-flow / γ-redistribution speed ≤ 140 nm/s** — any advective cortex flow in DCM must not exceed unloaded NMII speed (Cuda 1997). A DCM cortical flow faster than this is unphysical.
- **Contractile-clustering / ring timescale ~ 400 s** — aggregation completes by t < 400 s in *both* sim and in-vitro (Fig 4f). A DCM localized-aggregation (ring, or cortex heterogeneity) event should form on a comparable few-hundred-second scale, not instantly and not over hours.
- **Turnover→homogeneity monotonicity** — as τ_relax decreases, cortex heterogeneity (g_max/g_init analog; DCM: face-γ variance / node-density RDF) must decrease monotonically toward uniform (g ≈ 1). This is a *qualitative* acceptance gate for M1, directly from Fig 6e,f.
- **Prestressed-cortex velocity deficit** — Discussion: even with fast turnover, motors "walk slower than unloaded velocity since they still bear prestress from the cortex." DCM analog: a turgor-prestressed cortex should show a measurable reduction in cortical-flow speed vs. an unpressurized shell — consistent with DCM's physiological-baseline insistence on starting from a pre-tensioned cortex.

(Deliberately **not** listed as DCM targets: the 3.8 pN/head stall force, k⁰_u,ACP = 0.115 s⁻¹, TE-MSD magnitudes, α exponents, fraction-of-stalling — these validate FF, not a surface-mesh cell.)

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Wrong engine, mostly.** ~90% of this paper's transferable content is FF (explicit filaments, Bell's-law crosslinker unbinding, PCM motor kinetics, treadmilling). For DCM only the *turnover-as-relaxation-channel* concept and the *ring-as-aggregation* hook survive. Do not over-mine it for DCM; the dossier's "weak/indirect" DCM flag is correct.
- **Purified in-vitro / phalloidin vs living MCF7.** Headline confinement regimes are the *stabilized, no-turnover* control — explicitly non-physiological. Living cortex is the *turnover-ON* Fig 6 regime. Adapting without violating the physiological-baseline rule means: import only the turnover-ON behavior, set τ_relax from a *measured cortex turnover half-life*, never from the phalloidin/no-turnover control.
- **2D flat slab vs 3D closed surface + turgor.** The 5×5 µm PBC patch has no curvature, no enclosed volume, no ΔP. DCM's cortex is a closed pre-tensioned shell where γ = ΔP·R/2. Use the flat patch (P1) only as a *rheology calibration*, then re-tension on the closed sphere; do not port slab dynamics directly to the sphere.
- **Skeletal-muscle myosin (experiment) ≠ NMII (cortex).** SKMM (<5 µm/s, 4% duty) was an imaging convenience; the cortex-relevant motor is NMII (<140 nm/s). Use the NMII number for any DCM speed scale.
- **Magic-number trap.** k_t,A in the sim is a *per-segment* rate swept up to 70 s⁻¹ for numerical demonstration, **not** a physiological whole-filament turnover rate. Deriving τ_relax from the sim sweep would import a tuned number. τ_relax MUST come from an independently-measured cortical actin turnover time (surface to KB/PI for the datum), keeping the Magic-Number Block satisfied.
- **Aggregation is contraction-driven, not adhesion-driven.** The paper's aggregation is *intra-cortex* motor clustering, distinct from *inter-cell* cadherin-driven aggregate compaction. The M2 analogy is at the level of "a jammed contractile network needs a plastic relaxation channel" — do NOT conflate F-actin turnover (intracellular) with cadherin T1 rearrangement (intercellular) as the same rate; they are analogous *roles*, different mechanisms and timescales.

### One-line takeaway
Weak-but-real DCM value: F-actin turnover is the cortex's plastic stress-relaxation channel — give DCM's cortical tension a turnover-set Maxwell relaxation time (τ_relax ≈ 1/k_t,A), which both fixes the elastic-only γ and explains why aggregate-σ compaction jams without a rearrangement channel; the same aggregation instability is the cytokinetic-ring hook.
