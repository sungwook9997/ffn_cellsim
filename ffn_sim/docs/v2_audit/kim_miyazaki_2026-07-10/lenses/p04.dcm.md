# DCM lens — p04

**Paper:** Yu, Li, Murrell, Kim (2018) *Balance between Force Generation and Relaxation Leads to Pulsed Contraction of Actomyosin Networks*, Biophys. J. 115:2003–2013. DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2018.10.008.

**One-line for DCM:** This is a filament-resolved (FF-class) paper, so it does **not** port kernel-for-kernel into DCM's surface-mesh cortex — but it delivers the one design principle DCM's lumped cortex currently ignores: **cortical tension is a dynamic balance between myosin generation and turnover/severing RELAXATION, and without an explicit relaxation channel a contractile surface aggregates IRREVERSIBLY rather than reversibly (pulsed).** That is the same failure mode DCM's aggregate-σ compaction shows (drag-limited, irreversible end-state, wrong 24–48 h rate). The paper is a mechanistic argument for adding a turnover/severing-style relaxation term to the DCM cortical-tension field, plus a set of in-vivo pulse numbers usable as validation targets.

Honest scope: DCM relevance is **moderate-low but real**. Most content (PCM motors, Bell-law ACPs, 140 nm segments, treadmilling kernels) is FF territory; only the *coarse-grained phenomenology* and a few numbers cross over. Nothing here should be over-claimed as a native DCM equation to port verbatim — the maps below are continuum reductions, flagged as such.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Make cortical tension γ a per-face FIELD with an explicit RELAXATION channel (generation vs relaxation balance)
- **DCM module:** `dcm/` cortex surface-tension term (currently a single scalar γ, interphase ≈0.15 mN/m, pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2).
- **Change:** promote γ to a per-face state variable γ_f(t) governed by a generation term (myosin/RhoA setpoint) minus a **turnover-driven relaxation term** that pulls γ_f back toward the mean/baseline at rate k_relax. This is the coarse-grained image of the paper's core result (Figs. 1–4): slow relaxation → local γ concentrates → irreversible cluster; fast relaxation → homogeneous cortex; *balanced* relaxation → reversible (pulsed) concentration that grows then disassembles.
- **Why it beats current DCM:** DCM's static uniform γ cannot represent cortical flows, apical-constriction/furrow pulses, or the reversible-vs-irreversible distinction at all. More importantly it explains DCM's **open compaction-rate bug**: aggregate-σ contraction with no relaxation channel is exactly the paper's "irreversible aggregation" regime — a monotone freeze, not a remodeling process. Adding a relaxation/turnover term is the mechanistic justification for why physiological rate needs a remodeling channel (T1 / remesh / turnover), not a bigger σ.

### M2 — Curvature-gated LOCAL tension relaxation (continuum image of buckling-induced severing, Eq. 1)
- **DCM module:** couple the cortex γ_f relaxation to the membrane bending term κ_m / local mesh curvature.
- **Change:** where the cortex is sharply bent (high local curvature H_f), relax γ_f faster — the surface analog of the paper's finding that buckled (small-radius-of-curvature) filaments sever preferentially *inside* contracting clusters (>50% of severing events, Fig. 5c), providing SELECTIVE local force relaxation exactly where tension has concentrated. This is what converts an irreversibly-growing cluster into a reversible pulse.
- **Why it beats current DCM:** gives DCM a physically-grounded, self-limiting cap on runaway local contraction (a contracting patch bends → curvature rises → tension relaxes → patch disassembles), instead of a hard-coded σ ceiling. It is grid-derivable (curvature is already computed for κ_m) and NOT tuned to a gate.

### M3 — Turnover as the homogeneity-preserving stabilizer of the resting cortex
- **DCM module:** the `--from-resting` baseline cortex.
- **Change:** the paper (Discussion, and refs 17–19) establishes that F-actin turnover is what keeps a highly-contractile cortex HOMOGENEOUS and prevents spurious self-aggregation. DCM should carry a baseline γ_f relaxation-to-mean even in the resting cell, so a physiological-baseline cortex does not spuriously cluster under contact/contraction forces.
- **Why:** this is the physiological-baseline-correct default — a real interphase MCF7 cortex is smooth *because* it turns over, not because it is force-free. Matches the HARD physiological-baseline rule.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (as continuum reductions)

**(Continuum image of Eq. 1 — curvature-gated cortical relaxation).** Paper's segment severing:
$$k_{s,A} = k^0_{s,A}\exp(\theta_{s,A}/\lambda_{s,A})$$
DCM surface reduction — per-face tension relaxation rate:
$$k_{\text{relax},f} = k^0_{\text{relax}}\,\exp\!\big(H_f/\lambda_H\big)$$
Symbol map: θ_{s,A} (sum of bending angles at a segment's two ends) → **H_f, the local mean curvature of face f** (already available from the κ_m bending computation on the mesh); λ_{s,A} (angle insensitivity) → **λ_H, a curvature-insensitivity scale**; k⁰_{s,A} → **k⁰_relax, a baseline turnover relaxation rate**. Governs how fast γ_f is pulled back toward the local/global mean.

**Tension-field evolution (new DCM ODE per face).** Not in the paper explicitly, but the minimal coarse-graining of its generation-vs-relaxation narrative:
$$\dot\gamma_f = \underbrace{k_{\text{gen}}\,(\gamma^{\text{myo}}_f-\gamma_f)}_{\text{motor/RhoA generation}} \;-\; \underbrace{k_{\text{relax},f}\,(\gamma_f-\bar\gamma)}_{\text{turnover/severing relaxation}}$$
Symbol map: γ_f → per-face cortical tension (state); γ^{myo}_f → myosin setpoint (interphase 0.15 / mitotic 1.5 mN/m, or a RhoA-driven field for pulsing scenarios); \bar\gamma → neighborhood/global mean tension (the redistribution sink, = advection/diffusion of surface actin); k_gen, k_relax,f → generation and (curvature-gated) relaxation rates. The paper's three regimes (Figs. 1–4) are: k_relax ≪ k_gen → irreversible cluster; k_relax ≫ k_gen → homogeneous; k_relax ≈ k_gen → **pulsed**.

**Motor speed (Eq. 2/3) → cortical flow speed diagnostic.** ⟨v_M⟩ = mean node/surface-actin translocation over a 1 s window maps directly to a DCM observable: the mean tangential drift speed of cortex mesh nodes (cortical flow). Use the 1 s sampling interval convention to average thermal noise.

*(Eqs. 4 g(r) and 5 persistency are network-cluster diagnostics; their DCM analog is the SD of the per-face γ_f map — a heterogeneity index Q_γ — and the temporal autocorrelation of cortical-flow direction. Useful as post-hoc metrics, not forces.)*

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

- **P1 — Cortical-patch pulsed-contraction scenario.** On a DCM cell (or a cortex patch), impose a localized myosin/RhoA setpoint bump γ^{myo}_f on a ~2–4 µm region and watch whether γ_f + surface actin (a) grows irreversibly, (b) stays flat, or (c) grows-then-disassembles (pulse). Sweep k_relax to reproduce the paper's three regimes. Success = a reversible pulse of 2–4 µm / 40–100 s.
- **P2 — Turnover-suppresses-aggregation control (Fig. 1 analog).** Drive uniform contraction (raise mean γ) with relaxation OFF vs ON; confirm OFF → irreversible mesh clustering/faceting, ON → homogeneous cortex. This directly probes DCM's spurious-aggregation / faceting failure mode with a physically-motivated fix.
- **P3 — Connectivity three-regime (Figs. 2–3 analog).** Not a native DCM geometry (it is filament connectivity), but the qualitative "intermediate connectivity → max heterogeneity" maps to remesh resolution / cortex crosslink stiffness sweeps in FF; for DCM it is a caution, not a scenario.
- **Note:** the paper's domain is a quasi-2D 10×10×0.1 µm periodic patch — NOT a closed 3D cell. Any DCM reproduction must lift the phenomenology onto the closed triangulated surface, not copy the flat periodic box.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS (DCM observables)

1. **Pulse size:** a reversible cortical-tension pulse should be **2–4 µm** in diameter (ref 36).
2. **Pulse duration:** **40–100 s** (≈1 min) lifetime; a nascent/weak pulse is **<50 s** (Fig. 4f).
3. **Cortical flow / translocation speed:** **0.6–1.8 µm/s** (Figs 1c/2c/3c), with the signature that speed is high early (contraction) then drops after the cluster forms and material stalls at the focus (Fig. 1c) — a DCM cortical-flow run should show the same early-fast / late-slow profile.
4. **Regime boundary:** reversibility requires generation ≈ relaxation; too-slow relaxation → irreversible growth (>100 s, no disassembly), too-fast → no contraction. A DCM k_relax sweep should reproduce this bracket qualitatively.
5. **Homogeneity under turnover:** a turnover-ON resting cortex stays homogeneous (low Q_γ); turnover-OFF aggregates (drug-perturbation analog, Discussion) — a stability check for the `--from-resting` baseline.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Filament-resolved vs surface-continuum.** The paper's mechanisms live at the 140 nm segment scale; DCM's cortex is a lumped tension field. The maps in §1–2 are *coarse-grained reductions*, explicitly not native equations. Do not import k_{t,A}, k⁰_{s,A}, λ_{s,A} as DCM numbers — they are segment-scale rates; only Eq. 1's *functional form* (exp-in-curvature relaxation) transfers, with its scale re-derived at the mesh-patch level. Flag any use as derivation-required to avoid a magic-number violation.
- **2D quasi-planar periodic box vs closed 3D MCF7 cell.** Reproduce phenomenology on the closed surface, not the flat periodic patch. Curvature (H_f) is genuinely defined on our mesh (it is not on their thin slab), so the M2 curvature-gate is arguably *more* natural for DCM than for the source model — but validate that H_f is well-behaved under remesh before coupling it into relaxation.
- **Regime mismatch: pulsed contraction is a MORPHOGENESIS phenomenon (Drosophila apical constriction, C. elegans polarization), not interphase MCF7.** MCF7's physiological baseline cortex is *homogeneous*. So the primary DCM payoff is M3 (turnover keeps the resting cortex smooth and non-aggregating) and the M1/M2 relaxation channel to fix irreversible compaction — NOT literal pulsing as the MCF7 default. Pulsing is a mitotic/furrow scenario, applied as a perturbation FROM the homogeneous baseline (respects the physiological-baseline rule).
- **Purified/in-silico network, no membrane/turgor/nucleus.** The paper omits enclosed-volume pressure, membrane, and nucleus that DCM initializes ON. Adding the relaxation term must not disturb the pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2 balance — implement k_relax as pulling γ_f toward the *pressure-set* mean \bar\gamma, not toward zero, so the osmoregulated resting tension is preserved.
- **RhoA is deliberately absent here (the paper's whole point is mechanics alone suffice).** If DCM later wants biochemically-timed pulses, RhoA enters as the γ^{myo}_f pacemaker in the §2 ODE — but that is a separate future module, not this paper's contribution.
