# DCM lens — p69

**Paper:** Jung, Murrell, Kim (2015) "F-actin cross-linking enhances the stability of force generation in disordered actomyosin networks," Comp. Part. Mech. 2:317–327, DOI 10.1007/s40571-015-0052-9.

**One-line verdict for DCM:** This is a filament-resolved (FF-native) reconstituted-cortex paper — its *runtime* mechanism (explicit F-actins, ACPs, myosin TFs, Bell/catch bonds) sits an entire abstraction level below DCM's lumped surface tension γ. Its value to DCM is **not a new force to port**, but a **mechanistic coarse-graining ANCHOR**: it supplies (a) an emergent cortical *stress* σ_max whose thin-film conversion lands squarely on DCM's interphase γ, (b) a *density-scaling law* γ ∝ R_M^0.6 for how myosin sets tension, and (c) a *generation-vs-sustainment* (σ_max, S) split that motivates making DCM's γ a slowly-relaxing state rather than a frozen constant. Everything below is filtered to what is usable at OUR single-cell surface-mesh scale; the filament mechanics themselves belong to the FF lens.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cortical-tension γ: anchor the interphase value to an emergent network stress (STRONGEST lever)
- **DCM module:** `cortex` surface tension γ (currently a set constant: interphase ≈0.15 mN/m, mitotic ≈1.5 mN/m; pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2).
- **Change:** treat the cortex as a thin film of thickness h and convert the paper's *areal* contractile stress to a *line* tension: **γ = σ_max · h_cortex**. With the paper's plateau σ_max ≈ 600 Pa (reference condition R_M=0.014, R_ACP=0.1) and the slab thickness h = 0.5 µm, **γ ≈ 600 Pa × 0.5 µm = 3.0×10⁻⁴ N/m = 0.30 mN/m** — within a factor ~2 of DCM's interphase 0.15 mN/m.
- **Why it beats current DCM:** DCM's 0.15 mN/m is a lit-anchored *number*; this gives it a *bottom-up derivation* (myosin count × stall force, crosslinker-stabilized) and — critically — a **ceiling**. The paper shows σ_max saturates (~600 Pa plateau at R_ACP≳0.05) because it is *limited by the finite number of motors and their stall force*, not by crosslinker density. That is the same "γ-floor / γ-ceiling is a force-magnitude/generation limit" conclusion the project already reached independently (`project-gamma-floor-likely-deficit`, `project-gamma-floor-layered-resolution`) — this paper is an *external corroboration* of that root cause at the network level.

### M2 — γ generation vs. sustainment: make γ a relaxing state, not a frozen constant
- **DCM module:** `cortex` γ time-evolution.
- **Change:** adopt the paper's two-number decomposition — a fast **σ_max** (motors as *temporary* crosslinkers build stress in a few seconds) and a **sustainability S = ⟨σ⟩/σ_max** (fraction retained over 100 s; S→1 only when passive crosslinkers stabilize the mesh). Port this as a coarse γ(t) that *rises fast then decays toward S·γ_max* unless a stabilization term (crosslinker density surrogate) holds it up.
- **Why it beats current DCM:** DCM currently holds γ fixed for the whole run. Real cortex tension is dissipative — motors dump stress into network remodeling (mesh coarsening, motor aggregation) when crosslinkers can't hold. A relaxing γ is the mechanistically-honest version and would let DCM express *cortex softening/turnover* without inventing an ad-hoc decay.

### M3 — Crosslinker-as-molecular-clutch → mechanistic origin of cortex surface viscosity
- **DCM module:** `membrane`/`cortex` surface viscosity (currently a lumped parameter).
- **Change:** the paper (§3.4) shows ACPs act as a **molecular clutch that transmits force between F-actins via effective friction**, governed by k⁰_u,ACP and the load per crosslink. That *is* the microscopic origin of the cortex's in-plane viscous/plastic response. For DCM this justifies a **load-dependent (shear-thinning) surface viscosity**: friction drops when the crosslinker off-rate is accelerated by load (Bell, Eq. 1).
- **Why it beats current DCM:** replaces a constant surface-viscosity number with a load-sensitive one derived from the same Bell kinetics DCM already uses for its cadherin catch-bond junctions — internally consistent, no new magic number.

### M4 — Myosin-density knob for the interphase→mitotic γ switch
- **DCM module:** `cortex` γ modulation between cell-cycle states.
- **Change:** use the measured scaling **σ_max ∝ R_M^0.6** (motor molar ratio, valid below R_M≈0.06) as the functional form linking active-myosin density to cortical tension. DCM's mitotic/interphase ratio (1.5/0.15 = 10×) then predicts a required motor-density ratio of 10^(1/0.6) ≈ **46×**, OR — since the paper's σ_max also plateaus with crosslinker and stall-force limits — a combination of higher R_M *and* moving off the plateau. See Risk R4: a naive 10× R_M gives only 10^0.6 ≈ 4× tension, so the 10× interphase→mitotic jump cannot come from myosin density alone in this model's regime.
- **Why it beats current DCM:** turns the two hard-coded γ values into two points on a mechanistic curve, and flags that the mitotic switch needs more than "more myosin."

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

### E1 — Thin-film tension conversion (the porting equation)
```
γ_cortex = σ_max · h_cortex
```
- Map: `σ_max` = emergent areal contractile stress from the actomyosin network [Pa]; `h_cortex` = cortex thickness (paper slab = 0.5 µm; MCF7 cortex ~0.1–0.5 µm). Output `γ_cortex` → DCM `cortex.gamma` [N/m]. This is the single equation that lets the paper's number touch DCM state.

### E2 — Bell / slip-bond off-rate (already in DCM's vocabulary; here anchors surface viscosity M3)
```
k_u,ACP = k⁰_u,ACP · exp( λ_u,ACP · |F_s,ACP| / (k_B T) )      if r ≥ r_0
        = k⁰_u,ACP                                              if r < r_0
```
- Map: identical Bell form to DCM's cadherin junction kinetics (Rakshit KU-4.2). Symbols → `k⁰_u,ACP` reference off-rate, `λ_u,ACP` force sensitivity [m], `F_s,ACP` load on the crosslink [N]. In DCM this is *not* a per-filament bond but the constitutive law behind the load-dependent surface viscosity (M3): higher surface shear load → faster crosslink turnover → lower effective in-plane friction.

### E3 — Sustainability (drives the γ(t) relaxation of M2)
```
S = ⟨σ⟩ / σ_max ,   ⟨σ⟩ = time-avg of σ(t) from t(σ_max) to t = 100 s
```
- Map: coarse-grain to DCM as the retained fraction of peak cortical tension: `γ(t→) → S · γ_max`. S is the observable a DCM cortex-relaxation run should reproduce (S≈1 stable, S≪1 dissipative/remodeling).

### E4 — Areal stress sensor (validation-protocol equation only)
```
σ(t) = E · ε(t)
```
- Map: substrate readout for the reconstituted network; for DCM it defines the *measurement protocol* to reproduce (clamp a cortex patch, read stress vs. strain). Not a runtime force.

### E5 — Density scaling of tension (functional form for M4)
```
σ_max ∝ R_M^0.6          (R_M < ~0.06, at fixed R_ACP=0.1)
σ_max → plateau ~600 Pa  (R_ACP ≳ 0.05, at fixed R_M=0.014)
```
- Map: `R_M` = myosin molar ratio surrogate → DCM active-myosin density knob; the plateau encodes the motor-count/stall-force ceiling on γ.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce (as DCM scenarios)

### P1 — Cortex-patch clamp-and-read tension assay (validation rig)
- Reproduce Fig. 1b at DCM scale: take a **flat cortex patch** (a bounded region of the surface mesh), clamp two opposite edges, hold the others periodic/free, let cortical γ build, and read the reaction tension at the clamped edges. Target: steady γ consistent with 0.15–0.30 mN/m. This is the DCM analogue of the σ = Eε sensor and gives a *direct* γ measurement independent of the ΔP·R/2 route.

### P2 — Oscillatory surface rheology (surface moduli)
- Apply the paper's 5% sinusoidal strain to one clamped edge of the cortex patch over 0.1–10³ Hz; extract surface storage/loss moduli and phase δ = tan⁻¹(E″/E′). DCM analogue: measure the surface-mesh in-plane E′, E″ to check the cortex's viscoelastic response is in the emergent 10²–10⁵ Pa band and shows a critical (most-elastic) frequency.

### P3 — Generation→relaxation transient (for M2)
- Turn myosin on from a relaxed cortex, record γ(t) for ~100 s: expect fast rise to γ_max then relaxation to S·γ_max. Sweep the crosslinker-density surrogate to move S from ≪1 (dissipative, mesh coarsens) to ≈1 (stable). This is the DCM-scale reproduction of Fig. 2.

### P4 — Myosin-density sweep (for M4)
- Vary the active-myosin density knob and confirm γ ∝ (density)^0.6 in the low-density regime, with a saturating plateau — reproduces Fig. 5a/6a at the coarse level.

*(Note: no aggregate/durotaxis/AFM/division protocol is offered by this paper — it is a single reconstituted network, not a whole cell. The confinement here is a rigid-substrate clamp, not the cell-sized spherical confinement DCM needs.)*

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **R1 — Wrong abstraction level (primary).** This is an FF-native model (explicit filaments/motors/crosslinkers). For DCM every filament mechanism is *coarse-grained away* into the single scalar γ. Only the *emergent* outputs (σ_max, S, moduli, scaling law) are DCM-usable. Do not attempt to port filament forces into DCM — that belongs to the FF lens. Treat this paper as a **coarse-graining oracle**, exactly the sanctioned role for below-DCM-scale physics.

- **R2 — In-vitro purified reconstitution, not living MCF7.** The network is purified actin + filamin-A + non-muscle-myosin TFs self-assembled in a slab — no membrane, no turgor/osmotic pressure (Π₀), no nucleus, no volume constraint, no cadherin junctions. The physiological-baseline rule forbids importing this floppy, unpressurised, filamin-A-crosslinker regime as a DCM operating point. Use the numbers only as *cross-check targets on an already-physiological DCM cell* (turgor on, γ pressure-borne), never as an initial condition.

- **R3 — Rigid-substrate clamp ≠ closed curved surface.** σ = Eε reads stress against a 3×10⁴ Pa rigid substrate on a quasi-2D 0.5-µm slab. DCM's cortex is a *closed, curved, pressurised* surface where tension couples to Laplace pressure (γ=ΔP·R/2). The thin-film conversion E1 is geometry-agnostic (stress×thickness = tension) so it survives, but the *stiffness/moduli* numbers (E′,E″) were measured against a stiff substrate and should be treated as order-of-magnitude bands, not exact DCM targets.

- **R4 — Density scaling does not close the interphase→mitotic gap alone.** σ_max ∝ R_M^0.6 means a 10× myosin increase gives only ~4× tension, but DCM's interphase→mitotic jump is 10× (0.15→1.5 mN/m). Adapt: the extra factor must come from moving off the crosslinker plateau and/or raising the stall-force-limited ceiling (more heads engaged) — i.e. the mitotic switch is *multi-factor*, consistent with the project's stance that γ generation is a magnitude/density-bound problem with no single MCF7 datum. Do not tune R_M to force the 10× (magic-number violation); flag the gap.

- **R5 — Crosslinker identity mismatch.** Filamin-A (k⁰=0.115 s⁻¹, λ=1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m) is the crosslinker here; DCM/FF already re-anchored to α-actinin (Ferrer 2008, 0.066 s⁻¹). Keep the two distinct — do not overwrite the α-actinin off-rate with the filamin one. For the M3 surface-viscosity Bell law, use whichever crosslinker DCM declares as dominant, cited explicitly.

- **R6 — 100 s horizon.** The paper's dynamics run 100 s — relevant to fast cortical mechanics, NOT to the 24–48 h aggregate-compaction / T1-rearrangement timescale that is DCM's open problem. Nothing here addresses compaction RATE; do not cite it toward that.
