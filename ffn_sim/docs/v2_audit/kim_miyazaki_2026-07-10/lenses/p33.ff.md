# FF lens — p33

**Paper:** Miyazaki, Kinosita & Shiroguchi (2013), "Accurate polarity control and parallel alignment of actin filaments for myosin-powered transport systems," *RSC Adv.* **3**, 8728–8733. DOI 10.1039/c3ra41112e.

**One-line verdict for FF:** This is a purely EXPERIMENTAL in-vitro nanofabrication/motility-assay paper — no filament dynamics model, no Langevin/Brownian equations, no force potentials, no motor force–velocity law, no bond-kinetics rate scheme. It contributes to FF ONLY as (a) a citation-backed **parameter cross-check** (actin persistence length, diameter; myosin-V unloaded speed) and (b) a couple of **mechanism-flavor ideas** (gelsolin plus-end capping/nucleation; α-actinin as an emergent parallel-bundling crosslinker under flow). It is NOT a source for the Hill relation, stall force, duty ratio, minifilament architecture, or any off-rate. Relevance to FF is LOW.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

Honest triage — most of the paper is method engineering (biotin–streptavidin surface immobilization, flowchamber fabrication, µTAS transport) that has NO FF analogue. Only three items touch FF mechanism, and all are weak/optional:

1. **Filament module — plus-end capping + two-monomer nucleation (gelsolin, Ca²⁺-insensitive mutant).**
   - Change: FF currently grows filaments by barbed-end (plus-end) ratchet/formin elongation with distributed exponential lengths. This paper documents the complementary **capping** channel: gelsolin caps the plus end and, *in the presence of monomers, nucleates by recruiting two actin monomers* (Janmey 1985, ref 31), then the filament is capped so it stops elongating. If/when FF adds a turnover/severing-capping channel to cap the barbed-end growth ratchet (to set a steady-state length distribution rather than unbounded growth), gelsolin capping is the canonical mechanistic actor.
   - Why it *might* beat current FF: FF's length distribution is currently imposed as an initial exponential (1–10 µm) draw. A capping-rate channel would make the steady-state length distribution EMERGENT from (nucleation rate × elongation rate ÷ capping rate) instead of prescribed — more mechanistic. **Caveat:** this paper gives NO rate constants for capping/nucleation (it uses a non-physiological engineered Ca²⁺-insensitive mutant), so it cannot supply the numbers; it is only a pointer to the mechanism + ref 31. Do NOT port on p33's authority alone.

2. **Crosslinker module — α-actinin drives parallel isopolar bundling under external orienting field.** The paper's Fig 5a observation: with NO surface biotin, α-actinin alone crosslinks flow-aligned filaments trailing downstream into a dense (14.5 µm⁻¹) parallel array — "the dense isopolar array might be assembled by filament crosslinking alone in the presence of flow." Mechanistic reading for FF: **α-actinin crosslinks, in the presence of an aligning field (flow / shear / contractile stress), convert oriented-but-loose filaments into a stable parallel bundle** — i.e. crosslinker + alignment field → bundle condensation. This is exactly the FF filopodium/stress-fiber bundling motif and supports FF's use of α-actinin as the bundling crosslinker. It's a qualitative confirmation, not a new mechanism.

3. **Myosin-V processivity stepping over obstacles (context only).** Myosin V walks hand-over-hand and steps over surface-bound streptavidin "obstacles" without slowing. Weakly relevant to FF's multi-head walk logic (a bound crosslinker/obstacle on a filament should not stall a processive motor head) — but FF uses myosin-II minifilaments (non-processive, low duty ratio), not myosin-V, so this does not transfer to the FF motor model.

**Net:** nothing here forces an FF mechanism change. Items 1–2 are optional future hooks; item 3 is a wrong-isoform aside.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

**None.** The paper explicitly contains no force law, no potential, no rate equation, no force–velocity relation, no off-rate. The only quantitative relation is a trivial geometric assumption:

- (G1) **line density ∝ area density** — used to convert the 0.2 µM measured area density into a line density and to estimate the 1 µM array's 2.9 µm⁻¹ line density. Not portable as physics; it is a counting convention.

There is nothing to transcribe into FF state variables.

## 3. PARAMETERS / RATE CONSTANTS to set

Only three literature-grade single-filament/motor constants are usable, and all are *cited by* this paper rather than measured in it (so cite the primary source, use p33 as corroboration):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| actin persistence length ℓ_p | **15–20** | µm | filament bending: κ_bend = k_B T · ℓ_p (bead-rod angular stiffness), cortex + filopodium | **cross-check only** — FF uses ℓ_p ≈ 17 µm, sits inside this band → CONFIRMS, do not change | cited refs 16–19 (Yanagida 1984; Ott 1993; Yasuda 1996; Arai 1999) |

**Hard-rule note (no magic numbers / no fitting):** none of these is a tuning knob; each is a derivable physical constant used as an oracle. ℓ_p = 15–20 µm CONFIRMS the FF actin bending κ already in use → no change, log as corroborating citation. The MT band (5000–6000 µm) is a useful independent cross-check for the microtubule compartment bending modulus in the native full-cell build.

Values that look like parameters but are NOT physiological (do not ingest): the array densities (0.06–3.75 µm⁻² areal, 0.62–14.5 µm⁻¹ line), 5.4 ± 3.8 µm immobilized length, σ = 10.8° alignment — these are properties of an engineered 2D nanofab array, not of a living cortex, and violate the physiological-baseline rule if imported as cell parameters. The cortex areal density is set by KB-3.18 (~100/µm²), not by this assay.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce

The assay is a **surface-anchored, flow-aligned, one-end-tethered isopolar 2D array** — a nanofabrication geometry with no cell, no turgor, no membrane, no cortex. It is NOT a runnable FF single-cell scenario and should not be reproduced as production physics (it would be a stripped, non-physiological, non-native configuration — forbidden by the HARD rule).

The one *conceptually* reusable protocol element, as an OPTIONAL in-vitro-style unit test only (clearly labelled non-authoritative, never a source of a cell-scale conclusion):

- **Shear-flow alignment of a semiflexible-filament ensemble → measure emergent order parameter.** Seed a dilute population of FF actin filaments (distributed exponential lengths, ℓ_p = 17 µm) tethered at one end, impose a uniform drag/shear field, and measure the steady-state angular distribution. Target: a Gaussian orientation spread whose width is set by ℓ_p, filament length, and field strength (paper reports σ = 10.8° for 5.4 µm filaments at their flow). This would validate FF's semiflexible bending + hydrodynamic-drag response against a clean, analytically-tractable alignment observable. Strictly a mechanism unit test, decoupled from the native-cortex production line.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for FF observables

Only two numbers are defensible FF cross-checks, and both are single-element (not network) validations:

1. **Actin bending stiffness via persistence length:** FF's actin filaments must, in a thermal-only (no motor, no crosslink) equilibrium, exhibit tangent-tangent correlation decaying with ℓ_p = **15–20 µm** (target 17 µm). This is a standard FF sanity gate; p33 corroborates the band.
2. **Myosin-V unloaded gliding speed 0.62 µm/s** — usable ONLY if FF ever models a myosin-V processive motor; it is NOT a validation target for the myosin-II minifilament FF uses (different isoform, different duty ratio, different force–velocity). Log as an out-of-model reference.

**Not usable as FF validation targets:** polarity accuracy (99.6%), alignment σ (10.8°), array line/area densities, filament immobilized length — all are nanofab-quality metrics of an engineered device, not cell-mechanics observables (γ, contraction rate, G'(ω), buckling threshold, crawl speed). This paper provides no rheology, no stress relaxation, no contraction, no tension, no force–velocity curve, no branching-angle distribution.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Regime mismatch (severe):** purified in-vitro reconstitution on glass, quasi-2D, phalloidin-stabilized (turnover suppressed), engineered Ca²⁺-insensitive gelsolin mutant, external buffer flow as the aligning field — NONE of this maps onto a living 3D MCF7 cortex at physiological baseline. Any parameter/idea must be re-anchored to a cell context; treat p33 as corroboration of primary sources, not as a primary source itself.
- **Wrong motor isoform:** the paper's motor is myosin V (two-head, high-duty processive, plus-end-directed cargo transporter). FF's motor is the Stam–Hocky myosin-II bipolar minifilament (low duty, non-processive, contractile). The 0.62 µm/s speed and hand-over-hand stepping DO NOT transfer to the FF myosin model. Do not let this speed contaminate the Hill force–velocity / stall-force settings (which p33 does not provide anyway).
- **Densities are device metrics, not physiology:** importing 0.06–14.5 µm⁻¹/µm⁻² array densities as cortex densities would violate the physiological-baseline HARD rule; cortex density stays KB-3.18-anchored (~100/µm²). Flag and drop.
- **Phalloidin freezing:** filaments are phalloidin-stabilized (no depolymerization), so this paper cannot inform FF turnover/fragmentation kinetics despite touching capping — the length distribution here is fixed by fabrication, not by a dynamic cap/nucleate/elongate balance.
- **No fitting risk:** since there is nothing quantitative to port beyond already-known constants, there is no gate-loosening or magic-number exposure — the safe action is to log ℓ_p (15–20 µm) and MT ℓ_p (5000–6000 µm) as corroborating citations in the existing FF filament/MT bending config and take nothing else.

---

### Bottom line
p33 advances FF **marginally**: it corroborates actin ℓ_p = 15–20 µm and d = 6 nm (already in FF) and MT ℓ_p = 5000–6000 µm as bending-stiffness cross-checks, hands over a myosin-V-only reference speed (0.62 µm/s, out-of-model), and offers two soft mechanism pointers (gelsolin plus-end capping/nucleation as a future turnover hook; α-actinin bundling under an aligning field, confirming FF's bundling motif). It supplies zero force laws, zero rate schemes, and zero cell-mechanics validation targets. No FF code change is warranted beyond adding corroborating citations.
