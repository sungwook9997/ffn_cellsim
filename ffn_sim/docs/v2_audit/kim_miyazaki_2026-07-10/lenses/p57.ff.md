# FF lens — p57

**Paper:** Kubota, Miyazaki, Ogawa, Shimozawa, Kinosita, Ishiwata (2017) — *Biphasic Effect of Profilin Impacts the Formin mDia1 Force-Sensing Mechanism in Actin Polymerization.* Biophys. J. 113:461–471. DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2017.06.012.

**One-line for FF:** A gold-standard single-molecule (dual-trap optical-tweezers) quantitative law for **force-dependent, formin(mDia1)-mediated barbed-end elongation** — the Bell factor `exp(−fd/kBT)` with working distance d = 5.5 nm, kon/koff for ATP- *and* ADP-G-actin, and a profilin biphasic (accelerate/depolymerize) gate that becomes **mechanically switchable** under a few pN of stretching tension. This is the exact physics FF's `barbed_end_growth_kernel` (KB-3.6 ratchet) currently lacks: our formin elongation is force-*independent* (constant v₀≈0.623 µm/s), whereas the real mDia1 barbed end is a mechanosensor whose rate rises with tension and whose critical concentration drops with tension.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Formin (mDia1): make barbed-end elongation FORCE-DEPENDENT (mechanosensitive ratchet)
- **FF module:** `ff/` formin / `barbed_end_growth_kernel` (KB-3.6).
- **Current FF:** formin adds subunits at a fixed elongation velocity (v₀≈0.623 µm/s, force-blind). This is a lumped constant — it does not sense the tension on the filament it caps.
- **Change:** replace the constant add-rate with the transition-state elongation law (Eq. 1 below). Elongation = association (open state only) − dissociation (closed state only), where the closed-state probability shrinks under tension pulling on the translocating FH2-dimer-half. Net effect: **tension accelerates elongation and lowers the apparent critical concentration** — a genuine mechanistic force-sensor, not a proxy.
- **Why it beats current FF:** (a) it is the fine-grained mechanism (FH2 open/closed conformational equilibrium + Bell translocation) rather than a lumped velocity; (b) it directly couples FF's clutch/traction stress to actin *production*, which is exactly the missing feedback for crawl-speed, protrusion under load, and cortical-tension generation problems; (c) it is nucleotide-resolved (ATP vs ADP subunits behave very differently), matching FF's need to treat filament aging/turnover mechanistically.

### M2 — Profilin as an explicit, force-coupled biphasic modulator of the formin gate
- **FF module:** formin FH1 "capture-and-delivery" channel (new sub-state) + optional profilin field.
- **Mechanism:** profilin is biphasic — an optimal concentration accelerates elongation (capture-and-delivery of profilin–actin via the flexible FH1 loop), excess profilin *represses/depolymerizes*. Crucially the paper shows tension **coordinates** the two antagonistic sub-effects: force-enhanced FH2 translocation simultaneously accelerates profilin-dependent polymerization (steps 1,3) and represses profilin-dependent depolymerization (steps 2,4). A depolymerizing filament (net −1.8 subs/s) is switched to fast elongation (+40.2 subs/s) by ~4 pN — an all-or-none response, far exceeding the previously reported ~2×.
- **Why adopt:** gives FF a mechanistic "mechanical switch" for local actin assembly — a filament under load keeps growing even in a profilin regime that would otherwise dissolve it. This is directly relevant to load-bearing protrusions and cortex-under-tension. Adopt as an *optional* channel (profilin off by default) so the profilin-free force law (§1 M1) remains the baseline.

### M3 — Formin FH2 dimer stays attached (processive capping ≈ no detachment)
- **FF module:** formin bond lifetime.
- **Datum:** FH2 dissociation from the barbed end is ~1 per 20,000 subunit additions (Bni1p, ref. 20); over sub-micron→few-micron growth (hundreds–thousands of subunits) detachment is negligible below ~6 pN. Above ~6 pN the dumbbell fails (formin detach / filament sever / bead detach — indistinguishable).
- **FF use:** justify treating the formin cap as **effectively permanent** during a growth episode below ~6 pN; only introduce a formin-detachment channel above that force. Do NOT model formin off-rate as a fast Bell bond — that would be over-lumping. (Contrast: crosslinkers/clutches DO get Bell off-rates; the formin cap does not, per this datum.)

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### Eq. 1 — Stair-stepping force-dependent elongation (PRIMARY, port this)
```
v_elong(f, C_A) = v_poly − v_depoly
                = k_on · C_A · [1 − p_c⁰ · exp(−f·d / (k_B·T))]   ← association, open state only
                  − k_off · p_c⁰ · exp(−f·d / (k_B·T))            ← dissociation, closed state only
```
with the force-dependent closed-state probability
```
p_c(f) = p_c⁰ · exp(−f·d / (k_B·T))          d = 5.5 nm
p_open(f) = 1 − p_c(f)  → ~1 above ~3 pN
```

**Symbol → FF state map:**
| paper | meaning | FF state |
|---|---|---|
| `v_elong` | net barbed-end rate (subunits/s) | per-formin add/remove rate; convert subs→length via 2.7 nm/subunit |
| `C_A` | local G-actin monomer conc. (µM) | FF cytosolic monomer field at the barbed end (physiological pool) |
| `k_on` | association rate | formin add-rate constant (nucleotide-specific) |
| `k_off` | dissociation rate | formin remove-rate constant (nucleotide-specific) |
| `p_c⁰` | zero-force closed-state prob. | formin conformational parameter (nucleotide-specific) |
| `f` | tension on the FH2-half | **magnitude of the axial tension FF already computes on the barbed-end bond** (project bond force onto filament axis) |
| `d = 5.5 nm` | FH2-half working distance | fixed geometric constant (from FH2 crystal structure, Otomo/ref. 21) |
| `k_B·T` | at 26 °C ≈ 4.13 pN·nm | thermal energy; use FF's runtime temperature (physiological 37 °C → 4.28 pN·nm) |

Note `f·d/kBT` at f=4 pN, d=5.5 nm, kBT=4.13 → exponent −5.3 → exp≈0.005, i.e. p_c→~0.003·p_c⁰: essentially fully open above ~3 pN. Sign convention: **tension (stretching the barbed end away) is positive f and ACCELERATES** — opposite to a slip-bond. This is the whole point (mDia1 ≠ Bni1p, which is inhibited).

### Eq. 2 / 2′ — Stepping-second variant (NOT needed for mDia1)
```
v_elong = p_o·k_on·C_A·[1 − p_depoly⁰·exp(−fd/kBT)] − p_o·k_off·p_depoly⁰·exp(−fd/kBT)
```
For mDia1 the gating factor `p_o = 1` and Eq. 2 collapses to Eq. 1 with `p_c⁰ ↔ p_depoly⁰`. **The two models are experimentally indistinguishable for mDia1 — port only Eq. 1.** (Keep `p_o` as a hook if we ever add a different formin isoform.)

### Critical-concentration consequence (validation identity, not a separate law)
Set v_elong = 0 → apparent critical concentration
```
C_A,crit(f) = (k_off / k_on) · [ p_c⁰·exp(−fd/kBT) / (1 − p_c⁰·exp(−fd/kBT)) ]
```
→ decreases with tension (strongly for ADP, weakly for ATP). FF should reproduce this drop rather than hard-coding a fixed C_crit.

### Profilin channel (Eq.-level scheme, qualitative — no closed form given)
Fig. 7: four numbered steps. 1/2 = profilin-independent add/dissociate (= Eq. 1). 3/4 = profilin-complexed add/dissociate via FH1 capture-and-delivery. Under tension: steps **1,3 accelerated**, steps **2,4 repressed** — both governed by the same `exp(−fd/kBT)` FH2-translocation factor. Port profilin as two extra additive terms on the same Bell factor (accelerated poly + repressed depoly), gated by a profilin concentration with a biphasic weight (peak then repression). Keep off by default.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenario)

**Single-filament, formin-capped, tension-clamp "dumbbell" scenario** — a clean unit-test geometry for the FF formin kernel, before touching the native cortex:
- One actin filament (bead-rod segments), barbed end capped by a formin bond, pointed end anchored (phalloidin-stabilized in the assay → treat as fixed, non-depolymerizing).
- Apply a **constant axial tension** f to the barbed-end anchor (clamp): sweep f ∈ {0.13 (≈0), 1.5, 3.0, 4.2} pN (the paper's weak/moderate/strong windows).
- Immerse in a G-actin monomer pool at fixed C_A; sweep C_A: ATP 0.1–0.6 µM, ADP 2–6 µM.
- Measure barbed-end velocity (nm/s) vs f and vs C_A.
- Optional profilin sweep 0–6 µM at fixed C_A (0.2 µM ATP, or 3 µM ADP) to reproduce the biphasic curve and the tension-switch.
- **Reversibility test:** alternate low↔high tension on the same filament; elongation should toggle slow/depoly ↔ fast (Fig. 6 C,D).

This is a *dev-only single-filament oracle test* — a legitimate validation harness, NOT a production conclusion. Per the native-scale rule, any conclusion about cortex behavior must then be reconfirmed with the formin force-law active inside the native 38000-filament cortex (`--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules`), where the tension f is the emergent bond tension, not an imposed clamp.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **In-vitro isolated fragment, not native cortex.** Constants are for GST-FH1-FH2 (543–1192, autoinhibition removed) on beads, 26 °C, rabbit skeletal G-actin, defined buffer. Living cortex has autoinhibition (Rho-GTPase release), competing cappers (CapZ), Arp2/3 branches, and crowded polydisperse filaments. **Treat Table-1 values as an ORACLE datum for a validation gate, not a runtime magic number.** In production, use the physiological monomer pool + runtime T and let v_elong emerge; only the *functional form* (Eq. 1, d=5.5 nm) and the *sign* (tension accelerates mDia1) are portable as mechanism.
- **Temperature:** fit at 26 °C (kBT=4.13 pN·nm). At physiological 37 °C, kBT=4.28 pN·nm (Bell factor slightly weaker) and k_on/k_off both faster (~2–3× per Q10) — do not import the absolute rates unscaled. The dimensionless p_c⁰ and d are the transferable quantities.
- **Isoform specificity.** mDia1 is ACCELERATED by tension; yeast Bni1p is INHIBITED (Courtemanche 2013). Do not generalize the sign across formins. FF should tag the force-response sign per formin isoform; default to mDia1 (acceleration) since that is our mammalian/MCF7 context.
- **Force magnitude regime.** The law is characterized 0.13–5.5 pN; above 6 pN the cap detaches/filament severs. In the native cortex, per-filament axial tensions are typically sub-pN to few-pN, so the operating point sits in the sensitive band — good — but do NOT extrapolate the exp() acceleration past ~6 pN; clamp with a detachment channel there (§1 M3).
- **Profilin channel is optional and biphasic** — an easy source of a hidden magic number (the "optimal" profilin concentration). If added, derive the biphasic weight from the two additive Bell terms (accelerated poly + repressed depoly), never a fitted peak. Keep profilin off by default so the profilin-free force law is the audited baseline. Physiological-baseline rule: if profilin is ON in production, set its concentration to the real cytosolic value (~tens of µM total, but effective free profilin much lower) — surface to PI if unknown rather than defaulting.
- **Steady-state, no transients.** Eq. 1 is a steady elongation rate; it omits monomer-diffusion depletion at the barbed end. At FF's fine scale with an explicit monomer field, local C_A depletion under fast growth could matter — keep C_A as the *local* field value, not a global constant.
- **Pointed end stabilized in assay.** The dumbbell fixes the pointed end (phalloidin); real filaments treadmill/depolymerize at the pointed end. This law governs only the formin-capped **barbed** end — pair it with the existing FF pointed-end / turnover handling, don't let it stand in for whole-filament turnover.
