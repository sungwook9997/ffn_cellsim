# FF lens — p26

**Paper:** Matsuda, Jung, Sato, Kobayashi, Yamagishi, Kim, Yajima (2024) — *Myosin-induced F-actin fragmentation facilitates contraction of actin networks.* Cytoskeleton 81(8):339–355. DOI 10.1002/cm.21848.

**One-line for FF:** This is a same-family agent-based Brownian-dynamics actomyosin model (Taeyoon Kim lineage — the identical physics stack FF already emulates: serial cylindrical actin segments, Bell's-law ACPs, 8-arm bipolar myosin thick filament, catch-bond parallel-cluster-model heads, harmonic stretch/bend + volume exclusion, Langevin update) plus purified in-vitro validation. Its central new lever — **tension-induced F-actin fragmentation at F_ext > 500 pN** — is a fine-grained mechanistic severing channel FF does **not** yet have, and it directly attacks two open FF problems: network contraction/stress-relaxation and the cortical-tension floor at high connectivity. Because it is the *same model class* at *our scale* (µm filaments, pN forces, s timescales), the port is near-1:1 and the parameter/observable transfer is unusually clean.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Tension-induced F-actin fragmentation (severing) channel  ⭐ primary lever
- **FF module:** `filament` (new per-segment severing rule) + `turnover`.
- **Change:** add a per-segment monitor of the **extensional (tensile) force** carried by each actin bead-rod/bead-spring bond. When `F_ext(segment) > F_frag = 500 pN` at any step, **remove/cut that segment** (split the filament into two independent filaments at that bond; barbed/pointed polarity preserved on each piece; no re-annealing in vitro regime). Deterministic threshold rule (Tsuda 1996 single-molecule datum). Optionally soften to a Bell-type stochastic severing `k_sev = k_sev0·exp(F/F_sev)` later, but the paper's deterministic 500 pN cut is the validated baseline.
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF today can only relax network stress via (a) ACP Bell-Evans unbinding and (b) — if implemented — buckling. The paper's key result is that **for highly connected networks (long filaments + high crosslinker density + high duty ratio) neither ACP unbinding nor buckling can reduce connectivity enough to contract**, and tension-severing is the *only* channel that fires (buckling is suppressed because short inter-crosslink spacing raises the critical buckling force; ACP off-rate is nearly ATP-invariant so it cannot be up-regulated). This is exactly the FF cortex regime (Nc≈266k, densely crosslinked, short mesh) — so this severing channel is a candidate mechanism for **stress relaxation and the γ-floor at native connectivity**, where FF currently saturates.
- **Physiological caveat baked in (§6):** in living cells fast actin turnover pre-empts the huge forces, so this matters specifically in **slow-turnover, densely-crosslinked cortical regions where buckling cannot occur** — precisely the MCF7 cortex FF models. Keep FF's turnover ON at physiological rate; fragmentation is the additional channel for the sub-population of filaments that still accumulate >500 pN.

### M2 — Catastrophic (irreversible) vs. gradual (reversible) stress-relaxation dichotomy
- **FF module:** `crosslinker` (ACP) + `filament` severing, as a *diagnostic behavior*, not new code.
- **Change:** recognize and reproduce two distinct rupture dynamics: **ACP unbinding = reversible → slow/gradual force relaxation and gradual area decrease** (ACPs rebind, force never spikes); **fragmentation = irreversible → force builds uniformly then a few cuts trigger heterogeneous force redistribution → cascade → sharp catastrophic contraction**. FF can use this as a signature to tell whether its cortex is relaxing by the right channel.
- **Why:** gives FF a mechanistic discriminator for "why did contraction stall/jump" that the current single-channel (ACP-only) FF cannot express.

### M3 — Connectivity-gated regime map for which relaxation channel dominates
- **FF module:** scenario/config guidance (crosslinker + filament + myosin densities).
- **Change:** adopt the paper's regime boundary — **ACP unbinding dominates at LOW connectivity** (short ⟨Lf⟩, low RACP, low RM); **fragmentation dominates at HIGH connectivity** (long ⟨Lf⟩≈2.6 µm, high RACP, high RM, low k₂₀=high duty ratio). Use it to decide when FF *must* enable the severing channel to get physical contraction (native cortex = high connectivity → severing likely required).

### M4 — ATP as myosin-head off-rate (k₂₀) proxy, not a lumped "activity" scalar
- **FF module:** `myosin` (parallel-cluster-model head kinetics).
- **Change:** model ATP dependence mechanistically through the **ATP-dependent head unbinding rate k₂₀** in the Erdmann–Schwarz parallel cluster model (higher k₂₀ ⇔ higher [ATP] ⇔ lower duty ratio ⇔ higher walking speed ⇔ lower per-thick-filament force). This reproduces the **biphasic** contraction-vs-ATP curve emergently — beats any lumped "myosin activity" multiplier.
- **Why:** FF's Stam–Hocky/Hill myosin already has the ingredients; this just anchors the ATP axis to a single mechanistic rate and gives a validated biphasic target.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

Map symbols → FF state below.

**(A) Tension-induced fragmentation (the new law):**
```
if  F_ext(bond_i)  >  F_frag   →   cut bond_i (irreversible)
F_frag = 500 pN
```
- `F_ext(bond_i)` = tensile force on actin stretch bond i = `k_s·(|r| − r0)` for extension (only tension, not compression, triggers). Map to FF: the per-bond stretch force already computed in the FF actin extensional potential `U_ext = ½ k_s (r − r0)²`. Add a reduction over all actin stretch bonds each step: flag `F_ext > 500 pN`, cut, re-index the filament into two.
- Deterministic in the paper; the underlying datum is a **rupture force**, so a stochastic Bell-form `k_sev = k_sev0 exp(F·x_sev/kBT)` calibrated to fire ~deterministically near 500 pN is a defensible fine-grained upgrade (surface the x_sev choice to PI as it is not in this paper — do NOT invent a magic number).

**(B) ACP unbinding — Bell's law (already in FF, confirm parameters):**
```
k_off = k_off,0 · exp(F·λ / kBT)          (Bell 1978)
```
- `F` = tensile load on the crosslinker arm bond; `λ` = bond compliance length; `k_off,0` = zero-force off-rate. FF's Bell-Evans crosslinker is the same functional form. Paper's explicit λ, k_off,0 are in Table S1 (NOT in this PDF) — do not port numbers from here, keep FF's Ferrer-anchored values.

**(C) Myosin arm — catch-bond force-velocity via parallel cluster model:**
```
arm = ensemble of 8 myosin II heads;  motor = 8 arms on a bipolar backbone
walk speed ↓ and head-unbinding ↓ as tensile load ↑   (catch-bond, Fig S4 curve)
head unbinding rate = k₂₀  (ATP-dependent)   [Erdmann, Albert & Schwarz 2013; Erdmann & Schwarz 2012]
per-head stall force f_stall = 5 pN
```
- Map to FF myosin: each FF minifilament arm should represent an 8-head ensemble; per-head stall 5 pN; ATP axis enters as k₂₀. FF's Hill (1938) force-velocity is the convenience oracle; the parallel-cluster-model catch-bond is the *mechanistic* head-ensemble law and is preferable per the FF hard rule (catch-bond over slip/linear-stall). The exact PCM curve is Fig S4 (SI, not in PDF).

**(D) Governing dynamics (already FF-native):**
```
Langevin/overdamped:  drag·ẋ = F_ext + F_bend + F_excl + ξ(t)
U_ext  = ½ k_s (r − r0)²        (Eq S1)  — actin stretch, motor backbone/arm, ACP arm
U_bend = ½ k_b (θ − θ0)²        (Eq S2)  — actin semiflexibility (sets ℓ_p), motor/ACP geometry
U_excl ∝ overlap                (Eq S3)  — repulsive volume exclusion between actin cylinders + z-wall
```
- 1:1 with FF's BAOAB integrator + harmonic bonds/angles + LJ excluded volume. Explicit k_s, k_b are Table S1 (not in PDF) — keep FF's literature-anchored stiffnesses.

**(E) Force-accumulation scaling (why 500 pN is reachable) — worked example to reproduce:**
```
⟨Lf⟩ = 2.6 µm ≈ 370 actin monomers
RM = 0.32 → ~60 myosin heads act on one filament
half bound × 5 pN/head → ⟨F⟩ ≈ 300 pN average
Gaussian length distribution + stochastic binding → tail subset exceeds 500 pN → cut
```
- FF check: on a native cortex filament, count engaged heads, verify tail of the per-filament tension distribution reaches 500 pN under physiological myosin density. This is the sanity gate for whether the severing channel will ever fire in FF.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenarios)

**S-p26-A — Reconstituted contractile network (the whole paper's assay):**
- Geometry: thin slab **20 × 20 × 0.1 µm**, periodic x,y, repulsive z-wall (force ∝ z-overlap). This is a clean, small FF validation domain — dev-scale but *purified*, so it is a legitimate mechanism smoke test (label non-authoritative; native cortex is the authoritative basis per hard rule).
- Composition: actin (target ⟨Lf⟩ via slow-nucleation + fast-polymerization assembly, then monomer depletion — no depol), ACPs (transient crosslink at constant on-rate, Bell off), 8-arm bipolar myosin thick filaments (self-assemble, bind/unbind WITHOUT walking during assembly; **walking switched on only after assembly** → clean stress-onset).
- **Assembly-then-activate protocol** is the key structural detail FF should copy for any reconstitution comparison: build the crosslinked network to steady connectivity first, THEN turn on motor walking, so stress onset is well-defined.
- Sweeps to reproduce: RACP 0.01–0.3, RM 0.008–0.4, k₂₀ 1–200 s⁻¹, ⟨Lf⟩ 1 & 2.6 µm.

**S-p26-B — Gelsolin severing trigger:**
- Assemble a *mechanically stable, heavily crosslinked* network (RM=0.33, RANLN/RACP high ≈0.24, ATP 50 µM) that will NOT spontaneously contract. Then inject an external severer (gelsolin, final 0.002–0.02 µM) → contraction emerges. FF analog: apply an external random-severing rate to a stable FF network and confirm it unlocks contraction. Diagnostic that connectivity-reduction (not more force) is the missing ingredient.

**S-p26-C — Single-filament fragmentation tracking:**
- Sparse-label 0.1% of filaments; track a fragment's retraction speed before/after a cut. FF analog: instrument one filament, log its centroid speed across a severing event.

**Isolation switches:** the model can independently disable ACP unbinding and/or fragmentation. FF should keep the same three toggles (`unbind-only` / `frag-only` / `both`) to attribute contraction to a channel.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Purified in-vitro, quasi-2D slab ≠ living 3D MCF7 cortex.** Domain is 20×20×0.1 µm, no membrane/turgor/nucleus, no depolymerization. Do NOT draw cortical-tension conclusions from this scenario — use it only as a *mechanism smoke test* for the severing channel. Every finding must be reconfirmed on the native full cell (`--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules`, Nc≈266k) before it is reported (HARD rule).
- **Rabbit skeletal-muscle actin + non-muscle myosin II + anillin (contractile-ring ACP).** Species/isoform mismatch to MCF7 cortex (α-actinin/filamin crosslinkers, cortical NMII). The *mechanism* (tension-severing at ~500 pN) is a generic actin-bond property (Tsuda 1996), so it transfers; the *crosslinker kinetics* do not — keep FF's α-actinin/filamin Bell-Evans parameters, not anillin.
- **Turnover pre-empts fragmentation in cells (authors' own caveat).** Fast physiological F-actin turnover caps the accumulated tension, so tension-severing is important *only* in slow-turnover, densely-crosslinked cortical regions where buckling is geometrically suppressed. FF must run turnover ON at physiological rate (physiological-baseline rule); fragmentation is an *additional* channel for the residual >500 pN tail, not a replacement for turnover. Over-weighting fragmentation on a no-turnover FF network would over-predict catastrophic collapse — a coarse/stripped-config artifact trap.
- **500 pN is a deterministic threshold, not a rate.** A hard cut can be grid/timestep-sensitive (a single spurious force spike severs irreversibly). Guard: only tensile (not compressive) bonds; consider a short dwell/hysteresis or a Bell-form stochastic `k_sev` so the cut reflects sustained load — but the compliance length x_sev is NOT in this paper, so surface it to PI rather than inventing it (no-magic-number rule). Baseline = paper's deterministic 500 pN.
- **Model omits torsional/undertwist severing** (the real molecular route — tension undertwists the helix, Hayakawa 2011). The 500 pN rule is an *implicit* proxy for that, consistent with FF's fine-grained ethos only insofar as it is bond-force-driven; a future explicit twist DOF would be more mechanistic but is out of scope here.
- **Table S1 (all stiffnesses, rates) is in the SI, not this PDF.** Do NOT KB-register k_s, k_b, k_off,0, λ, PCM curve, or step size from this paper — flag incomplete-provenance and ingest the SI first. Only F_frag=500 pN (Tsuda), 5 pN/head stall, 8×8 geometry, and k₂₀ values are stated in the main text.
- **Sim vs exp magnitudes differ** (authors say match is qualitative — contraction speeds much smaller in sim due to parameter choice). Treat cross-comparison as sign/trend/biphasic-shape, not absolute-number matching. No fitting to close the gap (hard rule).
