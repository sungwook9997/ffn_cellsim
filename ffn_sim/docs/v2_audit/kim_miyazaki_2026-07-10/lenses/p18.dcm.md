# DCM lens — p18

**Paper:** Yim, Slater & Kim (2026), *Durotactic Migration Driven by Anisotropic Matrix Stiffening and Mechanical Feedback*, bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.05.19.726229 (Taeyoon Kim lab, Purdue).

**One-line orientation for DCM.** This is a **2D agent-based durotaxis/ECM-remodeling** paper. The cell is a coarse **two-point agent** (front + rear cell-points on a spring), NOT a deformable surface mesh — so it is a *physics-lineage reference*, not a drop-in. Its single genuine DCM hook is that the **cell–cell interaction law (Eq. 8) is lifted verbatim from SimuCell3D (Runser, Vetter & Iber 2024, ref 40)** — the exact physics ancestry of our DCM engine — plus a **collective-contraction → sustained-radial-tension → subset-escape** story that maps directly onto our open **loose→compact aggregate-compaction-RATE** problem (adhesion sustains contraction and delays escape; a single cell cannot remodel, a cohesive cluster can). Most of the paper (fibrous ECM, contact-guidance, durotactic index) is FF territory, not DCM.

## 1. Mechanisms to adopt

### M1 — Cell–cell contact law cross-check: our Rakshit catch-bond junction vs. this SimuCell3D tent-function (Eq. 8)
- **DCM module:** the cell–cell **cadherin catch-bond junction interface** (Rakshit KU-4.2, load-sharing clusters, contact-age maturation) + the node-face penalty contact.
- **What the paper does:** Eq. 8 is a piecewise **linear repulsion + piecewise-linear "tent" adhesion** with three geometric thresholds `r_{0,int} < r_{max,int} < r_{cr,int}`, taken straight from SimuCell3D. Adhesion strength is a single spring constant `κ_{a,int} = 10⁻⁵ N/m`.
- **Why it does NOT beat DCM, and how to use it:** Our fine-grained catch-bond junction is *more* mechanistic than this lumped tent spring — adopting Eq. 8 as the runtime law would **violate the fine-grained-over-lumped hard rule** (a binary/linear adhesion spring is exactly the "lumped cad_mult" the PI already rejected, see memory `feedback-junction-switch-fine-grained`). **Use it only as a coarse-grained cross-check / analytic oracle**: at contact separations the emergent DCM cadherin cluster should, when coarse-averaged, reproduce a tent-shaped force–separation with a peak near `r_{max,int}=(r_{0,int}+r_{cr,int})/2` and adhesion energy scale set by ~10⁻⁵ N/m. This gives us a second independent anchor (besides Rakshit) for the aggregate σ that our cluster emits.

### M2 — Collective-remodeling gate: single cell cannot compact, a cohesive cluster can (Fig. 4F, Fig. 5)
- **DCM open problem targeted:** loose→compact aggregate-compaction RATE (currently drag-limited via aggregate-σ; the PI has scoped that real 24–48 h compaction needs **cadherin T1 rearrangement escalation**, not aggregate-σ; see memory `project-dcm-compaction-turgor-blocked`).
- **What the paper adds:** a crisp **mechanistic threshold** — a *single* contractile cell produces no sustained matrix contraction (28% radial-bias>0.8), while a **cohesive cluster** (κ_{a,int}=10⁻⁵ N/m) sustains contraction to ~550 h and reaches 82%. The causal chain is *adhesion → slower escape → contraction stays engaged longer → tension builds*. This is the same "cohesion sustains the contractile field" physics our aggregate-σ compaction needs, framed as an **emergent time-integral** rather than an instantaneous σ.
- **Why it beats current DCM handling:** DCM's aggregate-σ compaction is a mechanical *end-state* only (RATE is drag-limited, non-physical timescale). This paper shows the **rate-limiter is escape/turnover kinetics of the cohesive interface**, not the contractile force magnitude — exactly the T1-rearrangement escalation the PI flagged. It argues for making DCM compaction rate governed by **junction lifetime / escape probability** (how long a cell stays load-bearing in the cluster), which is what cadherin maturation + T1 already model. Concrete DCM change: add a **"escape/detachment" observable** to the N=400 aggregate run (fraction of cells that leave the load-bearing core per unit time) and make compaction rate emerge from it, rather than reading a σ-pull equilibrium.

### M3 — Contractility × adhesion are non-additive levers (Fig. 4F)
- **DCM module:** cortex contractility (γ / myosin) and cadherin junction strength.
- **Finding:** +50% contractility alone (83%) ≈ adhesion alone (82%); the two are **non-additive** (dependent mechanisms). Both act by keeping the contractile field engaged long enough for tension to accumulate.
- **DCM use:** when we sweep DCM aggregate compaction over (γ_cortex, κ_cadherin), expect a **saturating, non-additive** response surface — do NOT treat them as independent additive knobs. This is a qualitative validation shape for the DCM γ×adhesion sweep, and a guard against double-counting.

### M4 — (Reference only, likely FF not DCM) Focal adhesion as viscous mechanosensor (Eq. 9)
- Eq. 9 `F_{d,Ii} = −λ(v_I − v_i)` makes the cell sense matrix stiffness purely through **viscous friction λ** at the adhesion (Walcott & Sun 2010). This is a lumped proxy for the molecular clutch. **Do not port into DCM** — it violates the fine-grained rule and FF already has a fine-grained clutch. Listed only so we recognize it as the paper's mechanosensing shortcut when comparing.

## 2. Equations to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

Only Eq. 8 and the tension-decomposition Eqs. 19–20 are DCM-relevant; the rest are agent/ECM equations for the FF lens.

**Eq. 8 — cell–cell interaction (SimuCell3D lineage; coarse-grained oracle for the DCM cadherin junction):**
```
F_int,IJ = κ_r,int (r_IJ − r_0,int) r̂_IJ                                if r_IJ < r_0,int      (repulsion)
         = κ_a,int (r_cr,int − r_max,int − |r_IJ − r_max,int|) r̂_IJ      if r_0,int<r_IJ<r_cr,int (adhesion tent)
         = 0                                                            otherwise
   r_0,int   = R_in,I + R_in,J          (contact onset / repulsion radius)
   r_cr,int  = R_out,I + R_out,J        (interaction cutoff)
   r_max,int = (r_0,int + r_cr,int)/2   (peak-adhesion separation)
```
- **Symbol → DCM state:** `r_IJ` → centroid (or nearest node-face) separation between DCM cells I,J; `R_in,·/R_out,·` → the cell's contact inner/outer shell radii (in DCM, derived from the surface-mesh radius R≈7.5 µm and the node-face penalty band, not free); `κ_r,int` → our node-face penalty stiffness (already present); `κ_a,int` → the coarse-grained equivalent of our summed cadherin-cluster spring constant. **DCM keeps its fine-grained catch-bond as the runtime**; this closed form is the **acceptance oracle** the coarse-averaged junction must match.

**Eq. 19 / 20 — radial vs circumferential tension decomposition (a DCM aggregate observable):**
```
F_r = F cos θ        F_θ = F sin θ        (θ = angle of a contact/edge tension to the radial direction)
```
- **DCM use:** compute per-mesh-edge (or per-junction) tension in a DCM N=400 aggregate, project onto radial/tangential; the **F_r/F_θ ratio** is a directly comparable compaction-anisotropy observable (paper: ≈2, sustained higher with adhesion). This is a clean, cheap DCM diagnostic we don't currently emit.

**Not ported (agent/ECM, → FF lens):** Eq. 1–2 (Mu=f overdamped solve — our DCM already does implicit GPU solves), Eq. 3–7/9–16 (2-point agent + triangular-lattice ECM force laws), Eq. 13 buckling ÷10, Eq. 17 stiffness anisotropy, Eq. 18 durotactic index, Eq. 21 radial migration bias.

## 4. Geometry / protocol to reproduce as DCM scenarios

### P1 — Circular aggregate collective-compaction + escape (Fig. 4) → DCM N=400 aggregate protocol
- **Setup:** seed a cohesive cluster of cells in a central circular region (paper: 100 cells, R_seed=50 µm, grid spacing 17.7 µm) inside a larger domain with **fixed outer boundary**; run long (paper: up to ~1000 h). Two conditions: **±cell–cell adhesion** and **±1.5× contractility** (2×2 matrix).
- **DCM adaptation:** our N=400 aggregate with the cadherin junction ON vs OFF (or weak vs strong maturation), cortex γ at 1.0× vs 1.5×. Measure compaction strain, F_r/F_θ, and **escape fraction** vs time. This is a ready-made 2×2 scenario for the aggregate-compaction-rate work — and directly tests the PI's "T1-rearrangement escalation" hypothesis: does slowing escape (stronger/longer-lived junctions) sustain contraction and speed compaction?

### P2 — Single-cell vs cluster control (Fig. 4F)
- Run ONE DCM cell vs the N=400 cluster under identical contractility. Expectation: single cell produces **no sustained radial tension / no compaction**; only the cohesive cluster does. A clean falsification test that our aggregate-σ / junction mechanism is genuinely *collective*, not a per-cell artifact.

### P3 — Matrix stiffness-probe (Fig. 2, Eq. 17) — **FF, not DCM.** Applying Fext=0.01 nN to a patch and measuring displacement anisotropy is an ECM-network protocol; note it for the FF lens.

## 6. Risks / caveats

1. **Cell is a 2-point agent, not a deformable mesh.** There is no membrane, cortex tension γ, turgor Π, bending κ_m, or nucleus in this model — so **none of its cell-shape physics ports to DCM**, and it says nothing about γ-generation, blebs, division, or membrane–cortex coupling. Its value is purely the *collective interaction/compaction* story and the SimuCell3D Eq. 8 lineage.
2. **2D, not 3D.** Radial/circumferential tension is a 2D decomposition; DCM's spherical aggregate needs the 3D generalization (radial vs tangential over the sphere). The F_r/F_θ≈2 number is a 2D circular geometry — treat the DCM analog as order-of-magnitude/shape only.
3. **Model output ≠ data (physiological-baseline rule).** Reproducing these numbers must **not** become fitting to a non-experimental target. Keep DCM initialized at its own physiological setpoints (γ=0.15/1.5 mN/m, Π₀≈40 Pa, η≈65 Pa·s, MCF7 R=7.5 µm) and check only the *qualitative ordering and non-additivity*, never tune DCM params to hit 0.5 / 82% / F_r/F_θ=2.
4. **Adhesion is a lumped linear spring (Eq. 8), a coarse-graining our engine deliberately rejects.** Using κ_{a,int}=10⁻⁵ N/m as a *runtime* cadherin strength would regress DCM to the lumped cad_mult the PI already vetoed. Keep it strictly as an order-of-magnitude oracle for the coarse-averaged emergent junction.
5. **Permanent crosslinks / no FA maturation (paper's own stated limitations, p24).** The paper has no force-dependent FA maturation and no stress-relaxation/plasticity in the network — our DCM cadherin junction *already* has contact-age maturation, so DCM is more mechanistic here; do not import their simplification.
6. **Different biological framing (cancer invasion/intravasation, low-E-cadherin invasive lines).** Their motivating cells are invasive/EMT breast-cancer clusters; our baseline is cohesive MCF7-like. The "subset escapes" behavior is an *invasion* phenotype — relevant to DCM aggregate-escape questions but not to be conflated with normal compaction; keep the mapping at the mechanism level (adhesion sustains contraction), not the phenotype level.
