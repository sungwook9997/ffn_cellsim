# DCM lens — p30

**Paper:** Sakamoto, Miyazaki, Maeda, *State transitions of a confined actomyosin system controlled through contractility and polymerization rate*, Phys. Rev. Research **5**, 013208 (2023). DOI 10.1103/PhysRevResearch.5.013208.

**One-line for DCM:** A validated continuum active-fluid + phase-field model of a *cell-sized confined* actomyosin cortex/cytoplasm, with a density-dependent active-stress law and a reaction–advection–diffusion F-actin transport equation — a direct template for making DCM's cortical tension γ an **emergent field** (not a lumped constant) and for adding **cortical actomyosin flow** (polarity, ring positioning, bleb healing).

Regime honesty up front: this is M-phase *Xenopus laevis* egg extract in a 200–300 µm quasi-2D water-in-oil droplet with a **nondeformable** boundary — NOT a 7.5 µm deformable MCF7. So it is a **mechanism/equation template + emergent-observable benchmark**, not a MCF7 parameter source. All dimensional constants ((ζμ)₀, D, γ_fric, ρ₀) are left as literals in the paper; only nondimensional simulation values and a handful of experimental observables are numeric.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cortex tension: make γ EMERGENT from an actomyosin density field (biggest lever)
**DCM module:** actomyosin cortex (`dcm/` surface tension γ).
**Change:** replace the lumped scalar cortical tension (γ = 0.15 mN/m interphase / 1.5 mN/m mitotic, or the pressure-borne γ = ΔP·R/2) with a **face-local active surface stress** driven by a cortical F-actin/myosin density field ρ carried on the mesh:
  σ_act(face) = (ζμ)₀ · ρ/(ρ₀ + ρ)   (Eq. 3, Michaelis–Menten saturation in density).
**Why it beats current DCM:** the open γ-generation problem (memory: γ-floor, "force-magnitude limit", ~530× under band; aggregate-σ compaction is drag-limited) has been chasing a *single number*. This paper supplies the missing structural form: cortical tension is not a constant but a **saturating functional of local actomyosin density**, so tension becomes load- and turnover-adaptive and spatially non-uniform. The saturation f(ρ)=ρ/(ρ₀+ρ) is exactly the density-dependence that lets tension self-limit (no runaway) and self-generate from density accumulation — mechanistic, grid-invariant, and NOT tuned to a gate. It directly serves the "cortical-tension γ generation" open problem and is the honest fine-grained-continuum alternative to a hand-set γ.

### M2 — Cortical actomyosin FLOW (advection) on the surface mesh
**DCM module:** cortex + membrane–cortex coupling.
**Change:** carry a scalar surface density ρ per node/face and evolve it by reaction–advection–diffusion (Eq. 4) coupled to an in-surface velocity field v from active-stress-gradient force balance (Eq. 6). Gradients in σ_act drive **cortical flow** (Marangoni-like), which advects ρ and feeds back on tension.
**Why:** DCM's cortex is presently static tension with no in-plane transport. Real cortex flows — this is what centers nuclei, sets polarity, positions the cytokinetic furrow, and heals blebs. All of DCM's listed open problems (division ring, confinement response, bleb, membrane–cortex coupling) need an in-plane cortical velocity field; this paper gives the minimal validated one.

### M3 — Cortical pattern selection as a diagnostic (flow vs. wave vs. rotation)
**DCM module:** cortex dynamics / cell division.
**Change:** the two-knob control map — contractility σ_act vs. polymerization rate k_p — selects steady inward flow vs. periodic contractile-ring waves vs. rotational waves. Use it as a *regime map* to know whether a given γ-and-turnover setting produces a stable uniform cortex (flow, DCM's usual assumption) or a pulsatile/traveling one.
**Why:** DCM currently assumes a quiescent uniform cortex. This paper shows that above a contractility threshold the SAME machinery spontaneously produces periodic contractile rings (T ≈ 1.5 min) — i.e. cytokinetic-ring-like pulses emerge from bulk cortex physics, not a hand-placed ring. That is a mechanistic route to the cytokinetic ring / division open problem, and a warning that high-γ mitotic settings may be in the wave regime.

### M4 — Bulk-vs-surface polymerization contrast (localizing actin assembly to the membrane shell)
**DCM module:** cortex assembly / membrane–cortex coupling.
**Change:** split polymerization source into a surface shell (0.9R<r<R, rate k_p^surf) and bulk (k_p^bulk), with contrast α_p = k_p^bulk/k_p^surf. Membrane-proximal (Arp2/3-driven) assembly dominates.
**Why:** captures that cortical actin nucleates at the membrane (physiological), and the α_p<0.5 criterion is what tips the cortex from steady into pulsatile. It is the mechanistic knob behind "cortex is built at the membrane," relevant to membrane–cortex coupling.

### M5 — Phase-field volume constraint = cross-check for DCM's enclosed-volume/turgor
**DCM module:** cytoplasm turgor / enclosed-volume constraint.
**Change:** their double-well potential U′(φ)=φ(φ−1)[φ−½−α₀(V/V_tar−1)] enforces target volume with stiffness α₀. This is an independent formulation of DCM's enclosed-volume/osmotic constraint.
**Why:** it is a **validation/consistency cross-check**, not a replacement — DCM already does enclosed-volume via the mesh. Confirms the V/V_tar penalty form DCM uses is the standard one; low novelty but reassuring.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port (surface-mesh / continuum mapping)

Symbol map to DCM state: ρ → per-face (or per-node) cortical actomyosin areal density; v → in-surface cortical flow velocity (tangential, per node); (ζμ)₀ → peak active stress per unit F-actin (calibrate so that σ_act at physiological ρ = MCF7 γ); ρ₀ → half-saturation density; η → cortical/cytoplasm dynamic viscosity (DCM η≈65 Pa·s); γ_fric → membrane+cytoplasm drag coefficient (already in DCM's per-node drag); D → cortical actin diffusivity; k_p, k_d → polymerization source / depolymerization sink.

**Eq. 3 — Active surface stress (PORT AS cortical tension law):**
  σ_act = (ζμ)₀ · f(ρ) · I,  f(ρ) = ρ/(ρ₀ + ρ).
  → DCM: per-face isotropic in-plane tension γ_face = (ζμ)₀·ρ_face/(ρ₀+ρ_face). Feeds the existing surface-tension force assembly (replace constant γ with γ_face). Reduces to a constant when ρ is uniform → back-compatible.

**Eq. 1 / Eq. 6 — Force balance (Stokesian, inertialess):**
  Dimensional: ∇·(σ_vis + σ_act) = γ_fric·v   (Eq. 1).
  Nondimensional: ∇²v + λ∇(∇·v) + ∇f = v,  λ = 1 + η_b/η = 1/3   (Eq. 6).
  → DCM: solve for tangential cortical flow v on the mesh; active-stress gradient ∇f = ∇σ_act drives flow against per-node drag (unit friction after nondimensionalization). σ_vis is the Newtonian cortical viscous stress (Eq. 2, η, η_b). This is a surface-Stokes solve — GPU/Warp-friendly, matches DCM's overdamped regime.

**Eq. 2 — Viscous stress:**
  σ_vis = η[∇v + (∇v)ᵀ] + η_b(∇·v)I.
  → DCM: surface viscosity term already conceptually present (membrane surface viscosity); use with λ=1/3 (their η_b/η = −2/3 assumption; flag as their closure, not physiological — see risks).

**Eq. 4 — F-actin transport (PORT AS cortical density evolution):**
  ∂ρ/∂t + ∇·(ρv) = D∇²ρ + k_p − k_d·ρ.
  → DCM: advection–diffusion–turnover of cortical density on the mesh; advection by cortical flow v, diffusion D, turnover source/sink k_p−k_d·ρ. This is the transport that closes the loop σ_act←ρ←flow. Steady state ρ* = k_p/k_d sets the baseline uniform tension.

**Auxiliary — Péclet / effective contractility (regime diagnostic):**
  Pe = (ζμ)₀/(D·γ_fric) = C·l/D, with C=(ζμ)₀/(η·γ_fric)^{1/2}, l=(η/γ_fric)^{1/2}, τ=η/(D·γ_fric).
  → DCM: compute Pe from the cortex config to know if the cell sits in flow (Pe≈38) or wave (Pe≈115) regime. Grid-invariant dimensionless group — a legitimate derived quantity, not a magic number.

Skip for DCM: Eqs. 5,7,8 (phase-field domain + no-flux energy E(ρ,φ)) — these exist only because the paper needs an *implicit* nondeformable circular boundary. DCM already has an **explicit deformable surface mesh**, so the phase field is unnecessary; no-flux at the cortex is native to a closed mesh. Keep only the U′ volume-penalty form (Eq. 5) as the M5 cross-check.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce as DCM scenarios

- **P1 — Cell-sized circular confinement.** Their core setup: actomyosin confined to a fixed circular domain with a friction boundary. DCM analog: a single cell (or its cortex) held at fixed volume/shape, measure whether cortical density/tension goes uniform (flow) or pulsatile (wave) as contractility rises. Note their droplet (200–300 µm) is ~30× a MCF7 radius and quasi-2D — reproduce the *physics* (confined active gel), not the size.
- **P2 — Contractility ramp (CalA analog).** Sweep the effective contractility (raise (ζμ)₀ / Pe) at fixed turnover and watch for the steady-flow → periodic-ring transition. DCM division relevance: does a mitotic-γ cortex spontaneously form periodic contractile rings? Maps to Fig. 1(h) / Fig. 3(f).
- **P3 — Two-knob phase map.** σ_act (contractility) × k_p (polymerization) grid → reproduce Fig. 2(c)/3(g) phase diagram (Wave upper-left, Flow lower-right, reentrant flow at high k_p). Establishes which cortex parameter combos are stable-uniform (DCM's usual assumption) vs. pulsatile.
- **P4 — Rotational-wave via symmetry-breaking delay.** Impose a quadrant time-delay τ_d = 10⁻²·T_sim in contraction onset → rotational cortical wave, stable ONLY at the flow/wave boundary and independent of τ_d magnitude (Fig. 4). A stringent dynamical test of a DCM cortical-flow module.
- **P5 — Membrane-localized assembly.** Nucleate actin in the 0.9R<r<R shell (α_p<1) → test membrane–cortex assembly coupling.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

Emergent-observable checks a DCM cortical-flow module should reproduce (regime-level; cell-type-adapted):

1. **Steady cortical flow speed ≈ 0.7 µm/s** (PIV, control) — order-of-magnitude target for in-plane cortical flow.
2. **Contractile-ring / wave period T ≈ 1.5 min** in the high-contractility regime — period set by *polymerization* (k_p), NOT contractility. A DCM wave should show T tied to turnover, not σ_act.
3. **Flow→wave transition threshold:** exists and is sharp; simulated at Pe ≈ 38 (flow) vs. 115 (wave), onset Pe ≈ 40–80 depending on k_p^surf. DCM should show a contractility threshold for cortical pulsation.
4. **α_p < 0.5 wave criterion** — waves require surface-biased polymerization; α_p ≥ 0.5 → steady flow. Testable structural prediction.
5. **Reentrant flow at high k_p^surf** — very fast polymerization *restabilizes* steady flow (Fig. 3g). Non-monotonic; a discriminating test.
6. **Rotational wave lives only on the flow/wave boundary** and is τ_d-independent (Fig. 4d,e) — a topological/dynamical signature.
7. **Period independent of contractility once in wave regime** (T-plateau ≈ 1.5 min across CalA 3–300 nM).

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Cell type & baseline:** M-phase *Xenopus* egg-extract cytoplasm, not living MCF7. Concentrations, turnover, and (ζμ)₀ are extract-specific. Adopt the **equation forms**; calibrate (ζμ)₀, ρ₀, D against MCF7 γ (0.15/1.5 mN/m) and η (65 Pa·s) so the physiological-baseline rule is honored — never import their nondimensional constants as physiological values.
- **Nondeformable boundary — opposite of DCM.** They use a *fixed* circular phase-field domain (advection term −v·∇φ dropped, bending rigidity neglected) precisely to avoid deformation. DCM's whole point is a deformable surface. Do NOT port the phase field; use only Eqs. 3,4,6 on the explicit mesh. Their own Discussion flags "future deformable droplets" as the open direction — DCM is that.
- **Quasi-2D, huge droplet.** 200–300 µm, 60 µm chamber — a flat disk ~30× MCF7 radius. Confinement geometry and dimensionality differ; the pattern-selection physics transfers, absolute wavelengths/speeds do not without rescaling by cell size.
- **Isotropic active stress, orientation neglected.** σ_act is a scalar pressure-like tensor; no nematic/filament orientation. Fine as a first cortex-tension field, but it is a coarser description than FF's filament-resolved cortex — for DCM (a surface-continuum engine) this is appropriately fine-grained; do not present it as filament-level mechanism.
- **Non-physiological viscosity closure.** λ=1/3 ⇒ η_b/η = −2/3 (negative bulk viscosity) is a modeling closure, not a measured MCF7 value. Flag and re-derive η_b physiologically if the surface-viscous term is used quantitatively; do not inherit −2/3 as a baseline.
- **Friction γ_fric ≠ DCM surface tension γ.** Notation collision: their γ is a *friction/drag coefficient* (drag −γv), NOT surface tension. In DCM, tension is σ_act; their γ maps to DCM's per-node drag. Keep symbols distinct in code to avoid a unit slip (cf. the DCM-meters/FF-microns and ΔP-crush unit pitfalls in memory).
- **k_p as a source term, not filament nucleation.** k_p is a scalar density source; it lumps Arp2/3+formin nucleation. Acceptable at DCM's surface-continuum altitude, but it is a lumped proxy relative to the mechanistic rule — legitimate here because DCM cortex is a continuum layer, not the filament engine (that is FF's job).
- **Adoption cost.** M1+M2 is a genuinely new cortical-transport module (per-face ρ + surface-Stokes solve + turnover), not a one-line change. Worth it for the γ-generation and division/bleb open problems, but scope it as a unit, validate NATIVE + full-compartment, and calibrate against MCF7 before any conclusion.
