# FF lens — p60

**Paper:** Mak, Zaman, Kamm & Kim (2016) *Nat. Commun.* 7:10323, "Interplay of active processes modulates tension and drives phase transition in self-renewing, motor-driven cytoskeletal networks." DOI 10.1038/ncomms10323.

**Why this paper is unusually high-value for FF.** This IS the FF engine's physics class, run by the *same lineage* FF is built to emulate: a 3D Brownian-dynamics network of semi-flexible actin filaments + explicit force-dependent (Bell) crosslinkers + multi-head myosin-II thick filaments (parallel-cluster-model kinetics) + **actin treadmilling turnover** — the Taeyoon-Kim engine (refs 35–39; Kim/Hwang/Kamm 2009-2015). FF already has filaments, Bell off-rates, Stam–Hocky/Hill motors, crosslinkers. The **one FF module this paper directly stresses is actin turnover** (barbed-polym / pointed-depolym treadmilling with spontaneous ACP+motor release on depolymerization) — and its central emergent result (turnover is the *decisive stabilizer* that keeps a motor-driven crosslinked network homogeneous with sustained prestress, versus collapsing into actomyosin foci) is precisely a lever on FF's **known open problems: cortical-tension γ generation/floor, network contraction & stress relaxation, condensation/symmetry-breaking, and pulsed constriction**. Note: all force-law *constants* live in Supplementary Note 1 / Supplementary Table 1, which are NOT in the 12-page PDF — the main text gives the *scheme* + emergent numbers, not the raw rate constants. Those must be fetched to complete parameter anchoring.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

**A. Actin turnover = treadmilling coupled to bound-element release (FF `turnover` / filament module) — the headline lever.**
- Mechanism: polymerization *only* at barbed ends, depolymerization *only* at pointed ends, at *similar* rates → per-filament steady-state "turnover rate" (s⁻¹). On a pointed-end depolymerization event, **any ACP or motor bound to the removed segment detaches spontaneously** (not force-triggered — a topology event). This is the load-relief channel.
- Why it beats current FF: FF has barbed-end growth (KB-3.6 ratchet) and distributed lengths, but the paper shows that **balanced treadmilling with bound-element release is the mechanism that sets whether the cortex sustains prestress or collapses into foci**. FF's known cortical-collapse-vs-artifact debate (the coarse-cell "collapse", the γ-floor) is exactly the homogeneous↔aggregated phase boundary this paper maps. Adding the *pointed-end depolymerization + spontaneous ACP/motor unbinding* channel gives FF an emergent stabilizer it currently lacks, so contraction and stress-relaxation stop being one-way network condensation.
- Concrete: implement a per-filament treadmill where the pointed end loses a segment at rate matched to barbed growth; when a segment is removed, iterate its crosslink/motor bonds and delete them. This is a *mechanistic* release (bond removed because its substrate vanished), fully compatible with the fine-grained-over-lumped rule.

**B. Turnover/motor *ratio* as the controlling variable, not either rate alone (FF scenario design).**
- The paper's master-curve result: peak-stress-vs-turnover curves **collapse onto one curve when turnover rate is scaled by the relative motor walking rate** (Suppl. Fig. 9; motor rate swept 0.5×–2×). i.e. the mechanical phase is set by (turnover rate)/(motor walking rate), not by turnover alone. FF should treat this ratio as the governing knob when sweeping cortex stability — a single dimensionless control instead of two independent sweeps.

**C. Pointed-end release as the physiological load-relief that limits sustained tension (γ-floor connection).**
- Mechanism for why high turnover → low stress: motors bind and walk toward barbed ends building strain, but faster treadmilling disintegrates filaments *before motors have walked far*, so ACPs+motors release and tension relaxes — "at steady state, motors have on average walked a shorter distance before an unbinding event relieves internal tension" (p6). This is a *concrete, mechanistic* prediction for FF's cortical-tension γ: **γ is bounded above by the competition between motor walk-distance and filament lifetime**, not just by motor force magnitude. Directly relevant to the FF γ-floor investigation (memory: γ-floor = force-magnitude limit) — this paper says turnover *lowers* achievable γ, so a FF cortex run at high physiological turnover should show a turnover-suppressed prestress ceiling. Test whether FF's floored γ is partly a turnover artifact.

**D. Permanent-bond limiting case (FF crosslinker: fascin/scruin non-unbinding variant).**
- Suppl. Fig. 10: with *permanent* (non-unbinding) crosslinkers (fascin/scruin-like), turnover takes on a *dual* role — it both destabilizes AND sustains stress. FF's crosslinker module (Bell-Evans α-actinin/filamin) should add a permanent-bond flag to reproduce this and to test bundle-vs-network regimes (filopodium fascin bundles vs cortex α-actinin network) with the *same* turnover physics.

**E. Filament severing → enhanced turnover (FF fragmentation channel).**
- p7 + Suppl. Fig. 11: myosin contractility + ADF/cofilin sever filaments, which *enhances* turnover and *diminishes* stress magnitudes. FF should add a severing channel (cofilin-like fragmentation, or buckling-induced severing) as a distinct turnover mode — mechanistically an internal filament break, not a proxy. This connects to FF's buckling/condensation open problem: severing relieves motor-buckled filaments and caps stress.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

**(i) Bell force-dependent crosslinker off-rate** (ref 41 Bell 1978; parameters anchored to Ferrer 2008 ref 40 — the *same* Ferrer datum FF already uses):
```
k_off(F) = k_off0 · exp( F · x_β / (k_B T) )
```
Map to FF: `k_off0` = zero-force unbinding rate (FF α-actinin currently 0.066 s⁻¹, Ferrer 2008, per project memory); `F` = instantaneous tension on the crosslinker bond; `x_β` = bond characteristic length (transition-state distance); `k_B T` = thermal energy at 37 °C. This is exactly FF's existing Bell-Evans scheme `k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b)` with `F_b = k_B T / x_β`. **Confirms FF's crosslinker law and its Ferrer-2008 anchor are the correct lineage** — no change needed, but the Ferrer rupture-force spectrum is the shared datum; ensure FF's `x_β`/`F_b` matches Kim's Suppl. Table 1 value when fetched.

**(ii) Parallel-cluster-model (PCM) myosin-II thick-filament kinetics** (ref 42 Erdmann & Schwarz 2012):
- Myosin-II TF = backbone + N arms (heads). Each head independently binds/unbinds/walks; the ensemble's *walking, binding, and unbinding rates are force-sensitive* and computed by treating the bound-head cluster in parallel (load shared across currently-bound heads). Processive because multiple heads mean the TF rarely fully detaches.
- Map to FF: FF currently uses Stam–Hocky bipolar minifilament + Hill (1938) force-velocity. **PCM is an alternative/complementary force-sensitive kinetics for the head ensemble** — it gives per-head binding/unbinding + duty ratio under shared load, which Hill's phenomenological f-v curve does not. Recommendation: keep Stam–Hocky/Hill as the FF default (more mechanistic per-head springs), but adopt the **head-count parameter** (see §3) and PCM's *load-sharing across bound heads* as the unbinding rule so motor detachment is emergent from per-head load, matching Kim.
- Head count is a real lever: **64 heads/TF default; 256 heads/TF** used to aggregate networks faster (Fig. 4). FF's minifilament should expose head count as a config and test 64 vs 256.

**(iii) Treadmilling turnover kinetics** (ref 39 Kim, Gardel & Munro 2014):
```
barbed-end polymerization rate  ≈  pointed-end depolymerization rate  ≡  r_turn   [s⁻¹ per filament]
on pointed-end segment removal:  for each ACP/motor bound to that segment → unbind (delete bond)
```
Map to FF: `r_turn` = steady-state per-filament treadmill rate, swept **0, 30, 90, 150, 300 s⁻¹** in the paper. FF should expose `r_turn` and the segment-removal→bond-deletion cascade.

**(iv) Active-kinetic-spring reduced constitutive law** (Suppl. Note 6) — a *validation/analytic-oracle* companion, NOT a runtime mechanism (would violate fine-grained rule if used as runtime):
```
Effective spring constant  k_eff ∝ (# currently-bound ACP-actin springs)
Net wall force  F_wall = Σ_{bound springs} f_contract(spring)
spring population governed by force-dependent binding (Bell) + turnover (disintegration/regeneration), parameterized by k_d / k_u0
```
Use ONLY as an acceptance oracle for FF network-tension emergence (like Cytosim/SimuCell3D oracles), never in the runtime. `k_d/k_u0` = relative turnover rate (swept 0, 0.1, 0.2, 1, 2, 5 → 0–15); spring:actin ratio 0.1; motor:actin ratio 0.05.

**(v) Empirical cluster/decluster sigmoids** (Eqs 1,2 — for the *experimental* validation target, §5):
```
Clustering:   I(t) = A + B / (1 + exp(−(t − t0)/τ_C))     τ_C = 28 ± 5 s
Declustering: I(t) = A + B / (1 + exp(+(t − t0)/τ_D))     τ_D = 155 ± 42 s
```
Fit FF-simulated actin-density-in-box time traces to these to extract τ_C, τ_D for cross-check against live MDA-MB-231.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenarios)

**Scenario FF-p60-A: 3D active-gel phase map (primary).**
- Geometry: 3 × 3 × 3 µm cube, **periodic BC in all 3 directions**. Actin 25 µM. Sweep %ACP × %motor × r_turn.
- Protocol: polymerize a homogeneous well-connected network *with ACPs and motors present but motors OFF*; **switch motor walking on at t=0** (physiological-operating-point note: the network is pre-connected and pre-crosslinked before activation — matches FF's warmup requirement, do NOT drop into cold start). Run to ≥51 s.
- Measure: internal stress = Σ(tension on constituents crossing a cross-section)/area, averaged over multiple cross-sections. Peak stress = max over time averaged over 10 s. Compute S (sustainability) and CF (clustering factor) at t=40 s.
- Output: reproduce the homogeneous↔aggregated phase boundary and critical r_turn.

**Scenario FF-p60-B: 2D cortical slab (larger length scale, cheaper) — closest to FF cortex.**
- Geometry: 8 × 8 × 0.5 µm slab, periodic in x,y only; shallow z forces filaments in-plane → mimics the **cell cortex (few-hundred-nm shell)** and cells on 2D substrates. This is the natural FF-cortex analog and lets FF probe larger length scales at native filament resolution.
- Same activation protocol. Test: increasing filament length and concentration → higher stress + more stability (Suppl. Fig. 5) — a FF cortex-stability prediction directly relevant to the "coarse cell collapses" artifact (more/longer filaments = more stable, matching the native-scale HARD rule).

**Scenario FF-p60-C: pulsed constriction / reversible foci (dynamic).**
- 25 µM actin, 5% ACP, 1% motor, r_turn = 300 s⁻¹, **256-head motors**.
- Pulse protocol: t=100 s disable turnover (→ aggregation/foci), t=200 s re-enable (→ foci dissolve, homogeneous regen), t=300 s disable again (→ foci reform). Reproduce transient stress spikes tied to foci formation, ~100 s pulse period. This is a FF test of pulsed cortical constriction (Drosophila-gastrulation-like) — a genuinely new FF emergent-behavior scenario.

**Scenario FF-p60-D: permanent-bond & severing variants** — as A/B but crosslinker off-rate = 0 (fascin/scruin), or with cofilin/buckling severing enabled; test dual role of turnover (Suppl. Figs 10, 11).

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for FF observables

Emergent-behavior gates (mechanistic + live-cell numbers, KB-registerable; not oracle closed-forms):

1. **Stress-vs-turnover collapse** (Fig. 2a, 3D, 25 µM actin / 5% ACP / 1% motor): peak/steady stress ≈ **300–400 Pa at r_turn=0** → ~230 (peak) at 30 s⁻¹ → ~60–100 at 90 → ~20–40 at 150 → **~0 at 300 s⁻¹**. FF cortex prestress must *decrease monotonically with turnover*.
2. **Critical turnover rate exists**, ↓ with %ACP (→ **0 at ACP:motor ≈ 100**), ↑ with %motor. Reproduce the phase boundary location.
3. **Master-curve collapse**: peak-stress-vs-turnover curves collapse when r_turn scaled by relative motor walk rate (0.5×–2×). Dimensionless-ratio control.
4. **Sustainability S**: ~1 (sustained) vs ~0 (collapse); rate of stress collapse ∝ (1−S). Scales ↑ with ACP & turnover, ↓ with motor.
5. **Clustering factor CF**: ~1 homogeneous vs ~0.7–0 aggregated; same monotonicities as S.
6. **Pulse timescale ~100 s** (Fig. 4) and stress spikes ~10² Pa peaks → ~10¹ Pa.
7. **Stress-fluctuation amplitude √⟨δσ²⟩ ≈ 0–15 Pa·s⁻¹**: ↑ with motor conc, ↓ with turnover, ~flat vs ACP (Fig. 5).
8. **Live-cell time constants (cross-check FF-simulated cluster traces):** τ_C = **28 ± 5 s** (formation, CytoD, R²=0.77), τ_D = **155 ± 42 s** (declustering/washout, R²=0.83). FF pulsed-turnover foci should fall in this ~100 s band.
9. **TFM stress anchor:** physiological traction ~**100 and 1000 Pa** — FF cortical prestress sanity range.
10. **Latrunculin vs Cytochalasin distinction:** CytoD (barbed cap, both polym+depolym off → r_turn→0) → many clusters; LatA (monomer sequestration, polym off but depolym continues → asymmetric fast disassembly) → diffuse, few clusters. FF turnover model must reproduce that *symmetric* turnover-off aggregates but *asymmetric* (depolym-only) disperses.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Constants are in the SI, not this PDF.** Suppl. Note 1 / Table 1 hold the actual bending/stretch stiffnesses, Bell `x_β`/`k_off0`, PCM rates, treadmill kinetics. Fetch them before hard-anchoring any FF parameter; the main text only gives the *scheme* + emergent numbers. Do not invent SI values (no-magic-number rule).
- **Purified/reconstituted-like regime, low density.** 25 µM actin in a periodic box ≠ FF's native pressurized MCF7 cortex with membrane containment, turgor, nucleus, microtubules. Absolute Pa are lower than physiological cortex (authors say so). **Adopt trends/ratios and the mechanism, not the absolute stress numbers**, unless FF explicitly matches 25 µM in a 3 µm periodic box for a targeted parity test. Per the native-scale HARD rule, any conclusion must be reconfirmed on the full 38000-filament cortex with all compartments ON.
- **Periodic BC vs closed cell.** The paper's periodic box has no membrane/turgor/boundary; FF's physiological baseline REQUIRES membrane + resting turgor + gravity from t=0. Run the phase-map scenarios as a *dev-only, labelled-non-authoritative* module test, then port the turnover mechanism into the full closed native cortex before drawing cortical-tension conclusions.
- **PCM vs Stam–Hocky/Hill.** The paper uses PCM motor kinetics; FF uses Stam–Hocky bipolar minifilament + Hill. These are *different* motor models. Do not swap FF's more-mechanistic per-head-spring minifilament for PCM wholesale (that would be a lateral, possibly less-fine-grained move). Adopt only the *head count* (64/256) and the *load-sharing-across-bound-heads unbinding* concept; keep Hill f-v.
- **Turnover release is a topology event, not a force law.** The ACP/motor "spontaneous unbinding on depolymerization" is release because the substrate segment vanished — implement as bond deletion tied to segment removal, distinct from the Bell force-dependent off-rate. Conflating the two would double-count or lump.
- **Species/isoform.** MDA-MB-231 (breast, but a different line than FF's MCF7); Drosophila embryo pulsation is the in-vivo motivation. τ_C/τ_D are drug-perturbation constants (CytoD/LatA), not baseline cortex — treat as perturbation-response cross-checks, not resting-state targets.
- **"Turnover lowers γ" could clash with the γ-floor deficit framing.** Memory says FF γ is *floored ~530× under band* (force-magnitude limited). This paper says high turnover *reduces* achievable tension. Reconcile carefully: run FF at *low* physiological turnover to seek the ceiling, and treat turnover as a modulator that pushes γ *down*, not up — it will not by itself close the γ deficit, but it explains why a high-turnover cortex cannot reach embryonic-scale prestress. Surface to PI rather than tuning turnover to hit a γ target (no-fitting rule).
