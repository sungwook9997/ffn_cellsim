# FF lens — p03

**Paper:** Kim, Gardel & Munro (2014) *Determinants of Fluidlike Behavior and Effective Viscosity in Cross-Linked Actin Networks*, Biophys. J. 106(3):526–534. DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2013.12.031.

**One-line relevance:** This IS the FF engine's ancestral physics family (Taeyoon Kim agent-based Brownian dynamics = Cytosim-adjacent: semiflexible segmented filaments + explicit elastic ACP cross-linkers + treadmilling turnover + overdamped Langevin/Stokes drag + Bell unbinding). It is not "new mechanism to graft" so much as **the canonical validation & parameterization reference for FF's cortex turnover→stress-relaxation loop** — and it hands us a clean emergent constitutive law (η_eff ∝ turnover⁻¹, biphasic yield at σ_th≈1 Pa, 5%/99% load-bearing percolation) that FF should reproduce **natively** and that directly bears on the open γ-generation / network-contraction / stress-relaxation problems.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### 1.1 Turnover-driven cross-link detachment as the PRIMARY stress-relaxation channel (not force-dependent unbinding)
- **FF module:** filament turnover + cross-linker (α-actinin) coupling.
- **Change:** In current FF the cross-link off-rate is Bell-Evans `k_off=k_off0·exp(F/F_b)` and turnover/fragmentation is a separate, largely inactive channel. This paper's default (and central) finding is that **cross-links unbind when the actin segment that contains them depolymerizes** — i.e. treadmilling/turnover, NOT force, drives detachment. When they compared the two head-to-head (Fig S5): the biphasic creep and the critical stress σ_th are *nearly identical*; force-dependent (Bell) unbinding only adds a **slight softening above σ≈10 Pa**. Crucially, η_eff is **4× lower WITH filament turnover** than with pure ACP unbinding, because depolymerization simultaneously detaches the link *and removes its binding partner*, preventing immediate rebinding.
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF today can generate cortical creep/flow essentially only through Bell unbinding + rebind. But in the physiological cortex regime (σ ≲ 10 Pa, most stress on a few pathways) the paper shows force-dependence is *sub-dominant* and turnover is the real relaxation lever. FF must have a **turnover-coupled ACP detachment** channel (link dies when its host segment depolymerizes, with a rebind lockout during the vacated window) as a first-class mechanism, or FF will systematically over-stiffen the cortex and mis-attribute flow to Bell unbinding. This is the mechanistic (not lumped) route to the cortex "fluidize while staying connected" behavior — directly relevant to our **cortical-tension γ generation/floor** and **stress-relaxation / motor-driven-flow** open problems.

### 1.2 Idealized steady-state treadmilling with a single turnover scale factor `n`
- **FF module:** filament (barbed-end growth + pointed-end shrink).
- **Change:** add a **balanced treadmilling** mode — net barbed-end addition + net pointed-end removal at rates that keep ⟨L_f⟩ fixed — parameterized by ONE global scale factor `n` that tunes turnover rate *independently* of length and density (Eq. 5). Add simple nucleation to hold filament count constant.
- **Why:** FF currently has barbed-end growth (ratchet) but not a mechanistically balanced pointed-end disassembly tied to it. Treadmilling is the cleanest knob to sweep turnover at fixed architecture, which is exactly what lets us reproduce ε̇ ∝ n and validate the constitutive law without confounds. It is mechanistic (per-monomer on/off events), not a lumped decay.

### 1.3 Nonaffine, percolation-based load-bearing as an emergent observable (measure, don't impose)
- **FF module:** analysis/observables layer (no new force).
- **Change:** at native scale, instrument FF to extract, at each instant, the **network-spanning percolating pathways** and the per-filament tension distribution. The paper's signature result: **~5% of filaments carry ~99% of the tension** (n=0.1, reference params, Fig 4B) and macroscopic creep = a continuous *cycle of pathway formation → force buildup → turnover → release*.
- **Why:** This is the cortex's true stress-transmission topology. FF's native 38000-filament cortex is exactly the object that can show this natively; verifying the 5%/99% heavy-tail is a strong, non-trivial correctness check that FF's cross-link connectivity + turnover produce the right nonaffine mechanics rather than an artificially affine (over-connected) response.

### 1.4 Biphasic (yield-like) creep from nonlinear subsegment force-extension
- **FF module:** filament + cross-linker serial chains ("subsegments").
- **Change:** ensure FF's filament+ACP serial elements have a **stiffening force-extension** (soft until relative extension L_r = L_ee/L_c → 1, then sharply rigid). The emergent macroscopic consequence is a yield-stress-like creep: ε̇ ∝ σ below σ_th≈1 Pa, ε̇ ≈ const above (over ~2 decades).
- **Why:** This is a genuine emergent constitutive prediction FF should reproduce. Semiflexible filaments already stiffen near full extension (entropic → enthalpic), so if FF's segment stretch modulus + persistence length are right, the biphasic transition should emerge *for free* — a falsifiable native-scale test of FF's single-filament mechanics.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### 2.1 Overdamped Langevin EoM (already FF's regime — confirm parity)
Eq. 1–2:
```
F_i − ζ_i (dr_i/dt) + F_i^B = 0
r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (1/ζ_i)(F_i^B + F_i)·Δt,   Δt = 2.3e−6 s (their explicit Euler)
```
Map: `r_i` → FF particle position (segment endpoint / bead); `ζ_i` → per-segment drag; `F_i^B` → FF thermal (fluctuation-dissipation w/ k_BT); `F_i` → net deterministic (stretch+bend+EV+xlink+motor). FF uses constrained BAOAB, higher-order than their Euler — **FF should NOT regress to Euler**; use their Δt only as a stability sanity anchor (BAOAB should tolerate ≥ this).

### 2.2 Per-segment Stokes drag (Eq. 4) — PORT directly
```
ζ_i = 3·π·η_m·r_c,i·(3 + 2·r0,i/r_c,i)/5
```
Map: `η_m` → medium (cytoplasm) viscosity; `r_c,i` → segment diameter (7 nm actin, 10 nm ACP arm); `r0,i` → segment length (140 nm actin, 35 nm ACP). Length-independent per-segment (they neglect filament-filament hydrodynamics; actin volume fraction <1%). **FF should use this exact cylinder-drag form for its bead-rod segment drag** rather than a bare-sphere Stokes 6πηr — it correctly captures the aspect-ratio factor. NOTE our physiological cytoplasm η≈65 Pa·s (MCF7, Dessard 2024) vastly exceeds their dilute-buffer η_m; FF must use the physiological η_m in this formula (baseline rule), which shifts the viscous/elastic balance — see §6.

### 2.3 Harmonic stretch + bend (Eq. 3) — matches FF
```
U_s = ½ k_s (r − r0)²   →   F_s = −k_s (r − r0)
U_b = ½ k_b (θ − θ0)²
```
Map: `k_s,A`, `k_b,A` chosen so thermal fluctuations give actin persistence length **9 µm** (their value; FF currently uses ℓ_p≈17 µm — see §3, RECONCILE). `k_s,ACP`, `k_b,ACP1` (arm–arm angle θ0,ACP1), `k_b,ACP2` (arm–actin angle θ0,ACP2) → FF α-actinin cross-linker bond + angular stiffness. Numerics live in their Table S1 (SI, not in extracted text — fetch if adopting exact k values).

### 2.4 Treadmilling monomer kinetics (Eq. 5) — PORT
```
k+_B = 6·n   [µM⁻¹ s⁻¹]   (barbed on)
k+_P = 0.6·n [µM⁻¹ s⁻¹]   (pointed on)
k−_B = 0.6·n [s⁻¹]        (barbed off)
k−_P = 6·n   [s⁻¹]        (pointed off)
⇒ steady-state treadmilling rate = 5.4·n  [s⁻¹]
```
Provenance: Fujiwara/Vavylonis/Pollard 2007 (ref 27). Map: FF barbed/pointed on/off events per monomer; `n` = global turnover multiplier (FF config `turnover_scale`). These are **force-independent** — a deliberate modeling choice; the paper flags force-modulated assembly (Courtemanche 2013 formin/profilin tension-modulation; Haviv 2008 myosin-as-depolymerizer) as future work — relevant to FF's formin loose-coupling and myosin channels but NOT parameterized here.

### 2.5 Bell slip-bond unbinding (control only, but FF-canonical)
```
k_ub = k0_ub · exp(λ_ub · F_s / (k_B·T))
k0_ub = 0.115 s⁻¹,   λ_ub = 1.04e−10 m   (Ferrer 2008, single-molecule)
```
Map: FF cross-linker `k_off0` and compliance `x_β = λ_ub` (F_b = k_BT/λ_ub ≈ 4.11e−21 / 1.04e−10 ≈ **39.5 pN**). NOTE: these are the **same Ferrer-2008 datum already in our KB** (per dossier + CLAUDE.md α-actinin re-anchor). Do NOT double-register. Keep FF's Bell channel but treat it as *secondary* to turnover-driven detachment per §1.1.

### 2.6 Toy-model creep scaling (the emergent law to reproduce)
Parallel spring array forming at k⁺, turning over at k⁻, n_L = k⁺/k⁻ elements, E₀ = n_L·K:
```
Linear elements:      ε̇ ~ k⁻·σ / E₀
Nonlinear (Eq. 8, stiffen at ε_c):
   σ < σ_th:  ε̇ ~ k⁻·σ / E₀        (η_eff const)
   σ > σ_th:  ε̇ = k⁻·Δε_avg         (η_eff ∝ σ, stress-independent creep)
   σ_th ~ E₀·Δε_avg
Master collapse (Eq. 6):
   η_eff · n = η_eff⁰ · max(σ, σ_th)/σ_th ,   η_eff⁰≈35 Pa·s, σ_th≈1 Pa
```
In **both** regimes ε̇ ∝ k⁻ (turnover). This is the FF acceptance oracle for cortex creep.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenario)

**FF scenario `cortex_creep_uniaxial`:**
1. **Preassembly:** self-assemble actin monomers + α-actinin ACPs in a thin 3D slab, periodic BC in x,y, until free-monomer conc. → ~1 µM under treadmilling. Reference: C_A=20 µM, R=0.02, domain **5×5×1 µm** → ~1200 filaments, ⟨L_f⟩≈0.9 µm, cross-link spacing ≈0.4 µm. (Use **10×10×1 µm** for length/high-turnover sweeps to avoid finite-size artifacts, per their Fig 3C,D.)
2. **Creep test:** sever + clamp filament ends at the ±x boundaries, deactivate x-PBC, pin −x rigid, apply constant tensile stress σ on +x (default 10 Pa; 0.2 Pa for linear regime). Maintain constant σ via **PD feedback** on the +x boundary position. Monitor ε(t) for **100 s**.
3. **Measure:** ε̇ = slope over quasi-constant window; η_eff = σ/ε̇; decompose σ = σ_e (elastic, network-spanning strain) + σ_v (viscous drag on +x-connected network); frequency moduli E′(f), E″(f) over 0.001–10 Hz.
4. **Sweeps:** σ ∈ 0.1–100 Pa; n ∈ 0.01–1(–10); C_A ∈ 20–100 µM; R ∈ 0.003–0.02; ⟨L_f⟩ ∈ 0.95–3.5 µm; y-PBC on/off; turnover-driven vs Bell unbinding.

**Native-scale adaptation (HARD rule):** This 5×5×1µm slab is a *coarse reduced geometry* (~1200 filaments) — per our native-full-compartment rule it is a **dev-only smoke test**, NON-AUTHORITATIVE. The authoritative FF version runs the same creep protocol on the **native 38000-filament cortex patch** (or a native-density slab at ~100/µm²) with full physiological baseline (turgor, membrane, physiological η_m). The Kim slab is where we *first* confirm FF reproduces η_eff⁰≈35 Pa·s / σ_th≈1 Pa qualitatively; the conclusion is only drawn once reconfirmed native.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for FF observables

Register these as FF cortex-rheology ValidationGates (all provenance-clean):

1. **Master creep law:** `η_eff·n = η_eff⁰·max(σ,σ_th)/σ_th`, η_eff⁰ ≈ 35 Pa·s, σ_th ≈ 1 Pa, collapsing all (n≤0.3, σ∈0.1–100 Pa) data.
2. **Turnover scaling:** ε̇ ∝ n and η_eff ∝ n⁻¹ over n=0.001–0.3 (elastic-dominated). For n>0.1 viscous contribution grows → stronger-than-n⁻¹.
3. **Biphasic stress response:** ε̇ ∝ σ¹ below σ_th; ε̇ ∝ σ⁰ (plateau) above σ_th over ~2 decades. η_eff: constant → ∝σ.
4. **Stress decomposition:** at high σ, σ_v saturates (const) while σ_e keeps rising ∝σ; σ_v/ε̇ const across both regimes; σ_e/ε̇ const→∝σ.
5. **Turnover-required creep:** n=0 → only initial elastic jump, NO long-term creep (Fig 1C red).
6. **Load-bearing percolation:** ~5% of filaments sustain ~99% of tension (n=0.1); heavy-tailed F/50pN distribution; per-filament tension normalizer 50 pN at σ=10 Pa on the reference domain.
7. **Turnover-timescale scaling:** autocorrelation R(τ) of per-filament force decays faster as n↑, slope ∝ n (Fig 4D, S6).
8. **Subsegment nonlinearity:** subsegment tension rises sharply as L_r=L_ee/L_c → 1 at σ=10 Pa (peak ~15–20 pN); minimal buildup at σ=0.2 Pa (never reaches nonlinear threshold). E(σ/ε, no turnover) and η_eff (with turnover) go nonlinear at the SAME σ_th (Fig S7).
9. **Turnover-vs-unbinding:** η_eff 4× lower WITH filament turnover than pure ACP unbinding (matched detachment rate); σ_th nearly identical in both.
10. **Force-dependence secondary:** ε̇(σ) near-identical for Bell vs force-independent up to σ≈10 Pa; only slight softening above.
11. **Generality:** biphasic transition + ε̇∝n persist across C_A(20–100µM), R(0.003–0.02), ⟨L_f⟩(0.95–3.5µm), y-PBC on/off.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **In-vitro dilute vs living cortex viscosity:** their η_m is dilute buffer; the paper explicitly works in the regime where *elastic-turnover relaxation dominates fluid drag*. FF's physiological baseline uses cytoplasm η≈65 Pa·s (MCF7). Higher η_m raises σ_v → shifts the viscous/elastic balance and can push FF *out* of the elastic-dominated regime where the clean master law holds. **Adapt:** run FF creep at physiological η_m (do NOT copy their dilute value to make the law fit — that would be fitting), and report where the elastic-dominated window sits. The master law is the *target the mechanism should produce*, not a curve to force.
- **Short filaments (⟨L_f⟩≈0.9 µm) vs FF native exponential 1–10 µm:** their reference cortex is much shorter and less dense than our native 38000-filament / ~100/µm² cortex. η_eff and σ_th values are architecture-specific. **Adapt:** treat 35 Pa·s / 1 Pa as *reference-architecture* targets reproduced in a matched Kim-geometry scenario; the native cortex will have its own (higher) η_eff — validate the *scaling laws and mechanism*, not the absolute numbers, at native scale.
- **ℓ_p mismatch (9 vs 17 µm):** softer bending (9 µm) lowers the strain at which subsegments stiffen, shifting σ_th. Keep FF's sourced ℓ_p; surface the discrepancy to PI rather than switching to make σ_th match.
- **Uniaxial extensional, single-edge load, 2D-slab geometry:** the cortex in vivo is a closed curved shell under contractile (myosin) prestress, not a pinned slab under external tension. This paper has **no myosin / active stress** — it is purely passive turnover rheology. **Adapt:** this validates FF's *passive* cross-linked-network relaxation; it does NOT address γ generation (which needs active NMII) or contraction. Use it to certify FF's turnover→creep backbone, then add myosin on top — the paper even notes the biphasic insensitivity may *buffer* deformation against active-stress variation, which is the bridge to our contraction/flow work.
- **Force-independent treadmilling assumption:** kinetics (Eq. 5) ignore tension-modulated assembly. FF's formin loose-coupling and myosin-as-depolymerizer (Haviv 2008) channels are precisely the missing force-coupling; do not import Eq. 5 as if turnover were force-blind in the FF production cortex — it is a reference simplification.
- **PD-feedback constant-stress BC:** an experimental-protocol convenience; FF can reproduce via a controlled-stress boundary but must ensure the feedback doesn't inject artifactual work — validate against a strain-controlled variant.
- **No double-registration:** Bell params (k0_ub=0.115 s⁻¹, λ_ub=1.04e−10 m) are the existing Ferrer-2008 KB datum — reference, don't re-add. Treadmilling rates (Fujiwara 2007) and ℓ_p=9µm (Isambert 1995) are the genuinely new registrable parameters; verify their DOIs' `verdict=OK` before KB entry.
