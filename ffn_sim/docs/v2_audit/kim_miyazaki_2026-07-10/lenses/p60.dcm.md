# DCM lens — p60

**Paper:** Mak, Zaman, Kamm, Kim (2016) *Interplay of active processes modulates tension and drives phase transition in self-renewing, motor-driven cytoskeletal networks.* Nat Commun 7:10323. DOI 10.1038/ncomms10323.

**One-line orientation for DCM.** This is a fine-grained 3D Brownian-dynamics actomyosin paper (Kim-lab lineage — it is FF-class physics, not a surface-mesh continuum model, so there is **no direct DCM kernel to port**). Its DCM value is threefold and real: (1) it supplies a **turnover-gated, dynamic constitutive law for cortical tension** (the "active kinetic spring") that DCM's cortex γ term could adopt instead of a static γ; (2) it explains — mechanistically — the **homogeneous↔aggregated phase transition** that is the microscopic analog of DCM's cortex-collapse artifact and of aggregate compaction, with actin turnover as the decisive stabilizer; (3) it delivers **stress-scale (100–1000 Pa) and pulsed-constriction (~100 s, τC=28±5 s, τD=155±42 s) validation numbers** DCM should reproduce for division/apical-constriction scenarios. Honest DCM impact is moderate — a tension-law upgrade + validation anchors, not a new engine kernel.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Cortex tension γ as a **turnover-gated dynamic variable**, not a static constant (active-kinetic-spring reduction)
**DCM module/force:** actomyosin cortex surface-tension term (`cortex.gamma`).
**Change:** Replace the static γ (or the pressure-borne γ=ΔP·R/2 identity used alone) with a tension that is proportional to the *currently-bound, load-sharing crosslinker population* and is **regenerated by turnover** — the paper's active-kinetic-spring constitutive law (Fig. 6, Suppl. Note 6):
> net wall force = Σ over currently-bound springs of the motor contractile force; the bound-spring population is governed by force-dependent binding (Bell) + a turnover appearance/disappearance rate.
So γ_cortex(t) ∝ n_bound(t) · f_motor, with n_bound relaxing toward a turnover-set steady state.
**Why it beats DCM now:** DCM's open problem #1 is *γ generation* — currently γ is imposed. This gives an emergent, turnover-controlled γ whose **magnitude, sustainability, and collapse are physics, not a set-point**. It directly explains the project's live-vs-artifact cortex-collapse finding: below a critical turnover the network aggregates and stress *peaks then collapses* (S→0); above it, stress is *lower but sustained* (S→1). That is exactly the coarse-cell "cortex collapse" the CLAUDE.md HARD rule warns about — this paper says the microscopic cause is insufficient turnover-driven self-renewal, and gives the stabilization knob.

### M2 — Turnover as the anti-aggregation stabilizer for **aggregate compaction rate** and cortex homogeneity
**DCM module:** aggregate surface tension σ / compaction; cortex remesh/turnover.
**Change:** The loose→compact compaction (currently drag-limited, and the PI has scoped aggregate-σ as end-state-only, not rate) has a microscopic rate-limiter analog here: contractile aggregation *proceeds* when turnover is low and *is arrested/homogenized* when turnover is high. The relevant emergent law is the **rate-ratio collapse**: peak-stress-vs-turnover curves collapse onto one master curve when the turnover rate is scaled by the motor walking rate (Suppl. Fig. 9). This says the controlling nondimensional group is **(turnover rate)/(motor walking rate)**, not either alone.
**Why it beats DCM now:** it gives a *derivable, grid-invariant* dimensionless control parameter for how fast/whether contraction aggregates — a principled lever for the compaction-rate problem that is NOT a tuned magic number (it is a ratio of two independently measured rates).

### M3 — Pulsed / reversible constriction for **division (cytokinetic ring) and apical/confinement constriction**
**DCM module:** cytokinetic-ring line tension; apical-constriction scenario; any transient-contraction event.
**Change:** Adopt the turnover-pulse protocol (Fig. 4): toggling turnover across the phase boundary produces **reversible actomyosin foci with transient stress spikes on a ~100 s timescale** (Drosophila-gastrulation pulsed constrictions). For a DCM cytokinetic ring or apical patch, drive the ring line-tension with a turnover-gated pulse rather than a monotone ramp — foci form (stress spike), turnover dissolves them, network regenerates, repeat.
**Why it beats DCM now:** DCM has no mechanistic pulsation; a ring closing under constant tension is a lumped shortcut. This gives *emergent* pulsatile constriction with a lit-anchored timescale and a reversibility mechanism (turnover on/off), matching real ratchet-like apical constriction.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port to the surface-mesh / continuum level

### E1 — Active-kinetic-spring cortex tension (transcribe as a per-face tension law)
Paper's reduced constitutive relation (Suppl. Note 6, Fig. 6a):
```
F_wall(t) = Σ_{i ∈ bound}  f_motor,i           (net contractile force on the boundary)
k_eff(t)  ∝  n_bound(t)                          (effective stiffness ∝ number of bound springs)
dn_bound/dt = k_bind·(n_tot − n_bound) − k_off(F)·n_bound + turnover(regen − disintegration)
k_off(F)  =  k_off0 · exp(F·x_β / k_B T)         (Bell, ref 41; params anchored to Ferrer 2008, ref 40)
```
**DCM mapping (surface mesh):**
- `F_wall` → the cortex line/surface tension γ resolved on each **face** (or the tension transmitted across a mesh cross-section). Cortex γ (N/m) = F_wall per unit boundary length.
- `n_bound` → a per-face (or per-node) scalar state field `phi_cx ∈ [0,1]` = bound-crosslinker fraction, advected/diffused on the surface manifold (`common/surface_manifold`).
- `f_motor` → per-face myosin contractile stress; ties to DCM's mitotic-vs-interphase γ (0.15 → 1.5 mN/m) as f_motor scaling.
- `k_off(F)` → face tension feeds back onto crosslinker unbinding: high local γ ⇒ faster unbinding ⇒ lower n_bound ⇒ softening. This is the force-sensitivity DCM currently lacks in its γ term.
- `turnover(regen − disintegration)` → a relaxation of `phi_cx` toward `phi_ss` at rate `k_turnover` (the same k_turnover already in the FF cortex and in the DCM CFL that must include k_turgor). Set so that `phi_ss` and `k_turnover` reproduce the sustained-vs-collapse behavior.

### E2 — Sustainability / collapse rate (port as γ-decay diagnostic)
Paper (Fig. 3b, 6d): **rate of stress collapse ∝ (1 − S)**, S∈[0,1].
**DCM mapping:** define a DCM cortex sustainability observable S_DCM from a γ(t) trace after a contraction is initiated; require dγ/dt|collapse ∝ (1−S_DCM). Use as a gate on whether a DCM cortex run is in the sustained (homogeneous) or collapsing (aggregated) regime — a *diagnostic*, physiological classifier, not a tuned parameter.

### E3 — Clustering-time sigmoids (port as fitting law for DCM foci/compaction kinetics)
Eqs 1–2 (the only equations printed in the main text):
```
(Eq 1)  I(t) = A + B / (1 + exp(−(t − t0)/τC))     clustering (rise), τC = 28 ± 5 s
(Eq 2)  I(t) = A + B / (1 + exp(+(t − t0)/τD))     declustering (decay), τD = 155 ± 42 s
```
**DCM mapping:** fit the same sigmoids to a DCM aggregate-compaction or foci-formation observable (e.g. local density / contact-patch intensity) to extract DCM τ_cluster and τ_decluster, then compare against 28 s / 155 s. Gives an apples-to-apples validation of DCM contraction *kinetics* against live MDA-MB-231.

### E4 — Rate-ratio nondimensional group (master-curve collapse)
Paper (Suppl. Fig. 9, text p6): peak-stress-vs-turnover profiles collapse when turnover rate is scaled by relative motor walking rate.
```
Π_active = k_turnover / v_motor    (control group; constant Π_active ⇒ same stress profile)
```
**DCM mapping:** DCM cortex must expose `k_turnover` and an effective motor walking rate `v_motor` (or myosin duty cycle) so that Π_active is derivable — the *grid-invariant* control parameter for the homogeneous↔aggregated regime.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce (as DCM scenarios)

- **Turnover-stabilization sweep (Fig. 2–3):** initialize a physiological-baseline DCM cortex (from-resting, full compartments), activate myosin contraction at t=0, sweep `k_turnover` ∈ {0,30,90,150,300 s⁻¹}-equivalents, record γ(t) and cortex morphology. Expect: low turnover → transient γ peak then collapse + surface aggregation; high turnover → lower sustained γ, homogeneous surface. This is the DCM analog of the collapse artifact — run it *native + full compartment* (coarse cells collapse for the unrelated too-few-filaments reason, so only the native cell can distinguish turnover-collapse from filament-count-collapse).
- **Reversible foci pulse (Fig. 4):** toggle turnover off at t=100 s (aggregate/spike), on at t=200 s (dissolve), off at t=300 s (reform). Reproduce ~100 s pulse period and transient γ spikes — a DCM apical-constriction / cytokinetic-ring pulsation demo.
- **Live-cell drug analog (Fig. 7):** CytoD = turnover suppression → clustering with τC≈28 s; washout → declustering τD≈155 s. In DCM, "turnover off" is the CytoD analog; fit Eqs 1–2 to a DCM density observable and compare τ's. (Latrunculin A = monomer sequestration → diffuse actin, few clusters, rounded cell — a *distinct* regime, not just faster CytoD; useful as a negative control that DCM turnover-off should NOT reproduce.)
- **2D cortical slab (8×8×0.5 µm, periodic x,y):** the paper's cheap cortex proxy; conceptually matches DCM's thin-shell cortex — its stress numbers are the ones most directly comparable to DCM cortical tension.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

1. **Cortical stress magnitude:** DCM cortex internal stress must land in **100–1000 Pa** (TFM anchor); the contraction-driven peak should reach **~300–400 Pa** at zero turnover and decay monotonically toward ~0 as turnover rises (Fig. 2a numbers above).
2. **Turnover monotonicity:** γ_sustained is a *decreasing* function of turnover rate; peak γ *decreasing* with turnover, *increasing* with myosin density (Fig. 3a sign-sense).
3. **Sustainability regime classifier:** DCM cortex runs should be classifiable as sustained (S≈1, homogeneous) vs collapsing (S≈0, aggregated); collapse rate ∝ (1−S).
4. **Contraction kinetics:** DCM aggregate/foci formation τ ≈ **28 s** and dissolution τ ≈ **155 s** (Eqs 1–2), matching live MDA-MB-231.
5. **Pulse timescale:** reversible constriction period **~100 s**.
6. **Critical ratio:** above crosslinker:motor ≈ 100, any nonzero turnover keeps the cortex homogeneous (critical turnover → 0).
7. **Master-curve collapse:** stress-vs-turnover profiles collapse under Π_active = k_turnover/v_motor.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Cell line:** MDA-MB-231 (metastatic, mesenchymal breast) — DCM targets MCF7 (epithelial). The τC/τD numbers are a *cross-cell-type* cortex-kinetics reference, not an MCF7 datum. Use as order-of-magnitude anchors, flag the mismatch; do not KB-register as MCF7.
- **2D substrate / in-vitro reconstituted-analog regime:** the simulations are periodic 3×3×3 µm and 8×8×0.5 µm bulk/slab networks with *no closed volume, no membrane, no turgor*, and the cells are on 2D glass. DCM's HARD rule requires the full physiological baseline (enclosed volume, Π₀≈40 Pa, nucleus, gravity). **Do not import the paper's null baseline** — adopt the *tension law* (M1) and *turnover control* (M2) but run them on the pressurized, full-compartment DCM cell, not on a floppy bag.
- **Not a continuum/surface model:** there is no DCM kernel to port; the active-kinetic-spring law (E1) is the only continuum-ready reduction and it is *qualitative* in the paper (matches BD "qualitatively"). Treat γ(n_bound, turnover) as a mechanism to build and validate, not a calibrated closed form.
- **Governing constants are in the SI (Suppl. Table 1), not in this PDF.** Bell x_β, k_off0, PCM motor rate constants, treadmilling rate mapping — must be fetched from Supplementary before any quantitative port. Main text gives only Eqs 1–2 + the stress/timescale numbers above.
- **No magic-number leakage:** the turnover rates (0–300 s⁻¹) and ACP:motor ratios are *sweep axes*, not tuned setpoints — port them as sweep grids and derive Π_active, never fit k_turnover to make a DCM gate pass.
- **Aggregate-σ scope (PI, 2026-07-09):** this paper's aggregation mechanism is *myosin-driven contractile clustering with turnover arrest*, which is a **cortex/foci** mechanism, NOT the *cadherin T1-rearrangement* mechanism the PI scoped as the real driver of 24–48 h loose→compact tissue compaction. Use M2 for **cortex homogeneity / foci pulsation** (its true domain); do NOT reuse it to re-litigate the aggregate compaction-rate problem, which needs junction rearrangement, not actin turnover.
