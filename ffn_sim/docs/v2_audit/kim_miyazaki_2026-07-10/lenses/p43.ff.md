# FF lens — p43

**Paper:** Kim, Hwang, Lee, Kamm (2009) "Computational Analysis of Viscoelastic Properties of Crosslinked Actin Networks," PLoS Comput Biol 5(7):e1000439. DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000439.

**One line:** This is the *direct methodological ancestor* of the FF engine — an explicit-filament + explicit-two-armed-crosslinker 3-D Brownian-dynamics actin network with bending+stretch+excluded-volume potentials, overdamped Langevin with fluctuation–dissipation thermal force. It hands FF (a) a validated potential/parameter set for crosslinker (ACP) mechanics FF currently lumps, (b) two turnkey rheology **measurement protocols** (oscillatory bulk shear → G′/G″; MSD segment-tracking microrheology), and (c) a rich table of network-rheology **validation targets** (G′∝f^0.75 → 0.3 exponent flattening, ~100× prestress stiffening, G′∝τ₀^0.85, threshold prestress ~0.1 Pa, "supportive framework" 25% ACP → 70% stress). It is a **passive** network (no myosin, no crosslink unbinding) — so it validates FF's *passive elastic/viscoelastic backbone* and, by its own stated limitation (permanent crosslinks → stress catastrophe), motivates FF's Bell-Evans crosslinker turnover as the missing physics.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### 1.1 Crosslinker (α-actinin/filamin) — two-armed element with TWO distinct bending stiffnesses [ADOPT — highest value]
Current FF models a crosslinker as an explicit bond with a link stiffness (Ferrer) + Bell-Evans off-rate. Kim shows a crosslinker is mechanically a **two-cylinder-arm element** with THREE independent elastic modes, not one:
- **k_s,ACP** — arm extensional stiffness (stretch of the arm).
- **k_b,ACP,1** — **arm–arm** bending (angle θ₁ between the two arms; this sets bundle vs. orthogonal geometry).
- **k_b,ACP,2** — **arm–filament-axis** bending (angle θ₂ between an arm and the actin filament it grips; equilibrium π/2).

**Why it beats current FF:** Kim's regime analysis (Fig 8, 9) is unambiguous that at intermediate prestrain the crosslinker **bending** stiffness k_b,ACP dominates G′ *more than filament stiffness does* — and it is significant at ALL prestrains. A single-scalar link stiffness cannot reproduce the intermediate-prestrain regime, nor the geometric distinction between a bundler (α-actinin/fascin, θ₁≈0/bundle) and an orthogonal crosslinker (filamin, θ₁≈π/2). FF's cortical-tension γ and network-contraction problems live exactly in this regime (crosslinked, pre-tensioned). Adopting the two-angle crosslinker gives FF the load-transmission path Kim identifies: near-perpendicular filaments carry load **through ACP bending**, not filament stretch (Fig 10A) — a mechanism FF's current bond-only crosslinker cannot express.

### 1.2 Crosslinker binding geometry: bundler (ACPᴮ) vs orthogonal (ACPᶜ) as distinct equilibrium angles [ADOPT]
Two crosslinker *classes* differ only by equilibrium arm–arm angle θ₁,eq and arm length:
- **ACPᴮ (bundling; fascin/fimbrin/α-actinin):** θ₁,eq = π (arms antiparallel ⇒ crosslinked filaments parallel, inter-filament angle 0 ⇒ bundle). Short arm.
- **ACPᶜ (orthogonal; filamin-A):** θ₁,eq = 0.417π (arms V-shaped ⇒ ~70 nm inter-filament spacing, orthogonal mesh). Longer arm.

**Why:** FF's unified architecture already distinguishes CORTEX / FILOPODIUM(bundle) / LAMELLIPODIUM(Arp2/3). This gives the *crosslinker-level* implementation: filopodial bundling = ACPᴮ-type θ₁,eq=π; cortical isotropic mesh = ACPᶜ-type. Kim's key finding — **orthogonal crosslinkers percolate and gel far more efficiently than bundlers at the same R** (Fig 3 vs 4) — is directly relevant to FF cortex γ-generation: a bundled cortex is under-percolated and mechanically weak, an orthogonal-crosslinked cortex forms a "single-body elastic object." This predicts the cortex should be filamin-dominated (orthogonal) to carry tension, not α-actinin-dominated (bundling). FF should set cortical crosslinker to ACPᶜ geometry as the physiological baseline.

### 1.3 Prestress-stiffening is a network *emergent*, not a material parameter [ADOPT as validation philosophy]
Under increasing shear prestrain γ the same network transits three mechanistic regimes (Fig 9): **low-γ = filament bending k_b,A (+thermal fluctuation if l_p≲l_c); intermediate-γ = crosslinker bending k_b,ACP; high-γ = filament+crosslinker stretch k_s,A, k_s,ACP.** FF should NOT hand-tune a stiffening law — it should *emerge* from the explicit elements. This is the mechanistic-over-lumped rule applied to strain-stiffening: reproduce G′∝τ₀^0.85 and the ~100× stiffening as an emergent readout, never as a fitted constitutive term.

### 1.4 Thermal fluctuation is *negligible* under physiological FF conditions [ADOPT as a simplification licence, carefully]
Kim's surprising result (Fig 7C): when l_p ≫ l_c (physiological: actin l_p≈10–20 µm ≫ crosslink spacing l_c≈0.4 µm), removing the Langevin thermal force changes G′ by <few %. Thermal fluctuation matters ONLY at low prestrain AND l_p≲3 µm (l_p~l_c). **Implication for FF:** at native cortex density (l_c sub-µm), the crosslinked network behaves as a *deterministic overdamped elastic solid*; the BAOAB thermal noise is not the load-bearing physics. This does NOT mean turn thermal noise off (FF must keep it for single-molecule binding kinetics, filament undulation spectra, and the l_p≲3 µm filopodial-tip regime) — but it tells FF where the numerical budget matters and gives a clean **falsifiable check**: FF cortex G′ with vs without thermal force should differ <5% at native l_c.

### 1.5 "Supportive framework" — a percolating minority carries the load [ADOPT as an FF observable/diagnostic]
Under prestrain ~25% of ACPs (selected by highest ACP bending force) + ~28% of filaments carry ~70% of network stress (Fig 10). **Why useful to FF:** gives a concrete, computable diagnostic for FF cortex — rank crosslinkers by |bending force|, prune, re-measure stress. If FF's cortical tension is carried by a diffuse majority rather than a sparse percolating backbone, FF's network is wrong (over-connected / wrong crosslinker geometry). This is also the mechanistic bridge to the γ-floor problem: cell G′~10³ Pa is hypothesized (Kim §Discussion) to be the *contractile* supportive framework, i.e. the myosin-pre-tensioned percolating subset — exactly FF's open γ-generation lever.

### 1.6 The paper's OWN stated limitation → FF's differentiator: crosslink unbinding & stress relaxation [ADOPT — this is FF's advantage]
Kim's permanent crosslinks give **stress catastrophe > 100 Pa** where experiments plateau/drop at **1–30 Pa** (Fig 5A too high, slope too low). The authors explicitly flag ACP unbinding + reformation as the missing physics for stress relaxation, network reorganization, plastic deformation. **FF already has Bell-Evans force-dependent off-rate** k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b) on crosslinkers — so FF can do what Kim could not. Concrete FF program: reproduce Kim's G′/G″ with unbinding OFF (parity to Kim), then turn Bell-Evans ON and show the stress-relaxation modes + the 1–30 Pa stress ceiling emerge. This is a clean, high-value FF result the paper itself calls for.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port (symbol → FF state)

All potentials are harmonic; FF already uses the same forms — this maps Kim's symbols to FF and fixes the **crosslinker's missing angular terms**.

**(Eq 1) Overdamped Langevin (drop inertia):**
`0 = F_i − ζ_i(dr_i/dt − v_∞) + F_i^B`, with `⟨F_i^B⟩=0`, `⟨F_i^B(t)F_j^B(t')⟩ = 2 k_BT ζ_i δ_ij δ(t−t')` (fluctuation–dissipation).
→ FF: this IS FF's constrained BAOAB in the overdamped limit. `v_∞` = imposed medium velocity from the applied shear profile (see §4 protocol). Map `ζ_i` → FF per-segment drag; **use the anisotropic cylinder drag below instead of a scalar.**

**(Eq 2) Euler update (Kim uses forward-Euler; FF uses BAOAB — keep BAOAB):**
`r̃_i(t+Δt) = r̃_i(t) + (1/ζ̃_i)(F̃_i^B + F̃_i)Δt + ṽ_∞ Δt`. The `ṽ_∞ Δt` advection term is how the strain rate couples in — FF must add this to driven boundary segments.

**(Eq 3) Stretch + bend harmonic potentials (both actin and crosslinker arms):**
`U_s = ½ k_s (r₁₂ − r₀)²` ; `U_b = ½ k_b (θ − θ₀)²`.
→ FF filament: `k_s`→ segment stretch modulus, `k_b`→ segment bending κ. → FF crosslinker: add `U_s(k_s,ACP)` on each arm + `U_b(k_b,ACP,1, θ₁)` arm–arm + `U_b(k_b,ACP,2, θ₂=π/2)` arm–filament-axis. **This last term is the piece FF is missing.**

**(Eq 4) One-sided harmonic excluded volume (cylinder–cylinder):**
`U_r = ½ k_r (r₁₂ − (σ₁+σ₂)/2)²` if `r₁₂ < (σ₁+σ₂)/2`, else 0. `r₁₂` = minimum distance between the two cylinder axes.
→ FF: this is a WCA-like repulsion on *segment cylinders*, using min-distance between rods, not bead centers. FF currently uses LJ-repulsive on beads; Kim's rod-min-distance form is more faithful for high-aspect-ratio segments and cheaper (harmonic, one-sided). Consider as FF EV option for the cortex.

**(Eq 5) Distribute contact force to segment endpoints by lever arm:**
`F_r,α = F_r·(L−y)/L` ; `F_r,β = F_r·y/L`, where y = distance from endpoint α to the contact point Y along the segment.
→ FF: needed whenever a force acts mid-segment on a rod (EV contact, crosslinker attachment at arbitrary point). Kim confirms ACPs **bind anywhere along a filament in any circumferential direction** — so FF crosslinker attachment must support mid-segment binding with this lever-arm force split, not just node-to-node.

**(Eq 6) Anisotropic cylinder drag (Clift/Grace/Weber):**
`ζ_⊥ = 3πη σ (3 + 2L/σ)/5` ; `ζ_∥ = 3πη σ (4 + L/σ)/5`.
At L/σ=10, ζ_⊥/ζ_∥ = 1.64. Kim used ζ_⊥ isotropically (justified: crosslinked segments move mostly transverse; isotropic-vs-anisotropic G′ 5.07 vs 5.00 Pa, G″ 2.02 vs 2.39 Pa — <1%/16% diff). → FF: adopt these closed forms for per-segment drag from η and segment geometry (supersedes any hand-set drag). **Note the grid-drag / crawl-speed open problem: FF's Σγ∝Nc drag currently destabilizes the solver — Kim's η-derived per-segment ζ is the physically-grounded value to anchor that to.**

**(Eq 9) Fluid shear stress added to network stress:**
`τ_zx = η dγ_x/dt` (instantaneous linear velocity profile; water diffusion time ~10 µs ≪ oscillation period). → FF bulk-rheology measurement: total wall stress = filament-borne stress + this viscous term.

**(Eq 10) Moduli from bulk oscillatory shear:**
`|G*(f)| = |τ_zx|/|γ_x|` ; `G′ = |G*|cosφ` ; `G″ = |G*|sinφ`, where φ = stress–strain phase lag (φ=0 elastic, π/2 viscous), `tan⁻¹(G″/G′) = φ`. → FF: THIS is the FF rheology test harness (§4).

**(Eq 11) Segment-tracking microrheology (modified Mason, cylinder version):**
`|G*(ω)| ≈ k_BT / [π r_b ⟨Δr²(1/ω)⟩ Γ(1+χ(ω))]` ; `G′=|G*|cos(πχ/2)` ; `G″=|G*|sin(πχ/2)`,
with `χ(ω) = d ln⟨Δr²(t)⟩/d ln t |_{t=1/ω}` (local MSD log-slope), `r_b` = effective segment radius from `ζ_⊥ = 6πη r_b`, Γ = gamma function. → FF: a *second, independent* modulus estimator from filament-segment MSD — cross-check against Eq 10. Kim shows the two agree well for homogeneous ACPᶜ networks (Fig 3) but diverge for heterogeneous/prestrained ones (so use bulk rheology when prestressed).

**No motor / no unbinding kinetics in this paper** — Kim gives NO Bell-Evans, NO Hill, NO duty cycle. FF keeps its own for those. But note the polymerization kinetics benchmark: depolymerization time ~100 s for a 1.5 µm filament, polymerization ~1–10 s at C_A=12.1 µM (from ref [49]) — useful FF turnover-timescale sanity numbers.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenarios)

### Scenario A — Oscillatory bulk-shear rheometry (the FF G′/G″ test harness)
Geometry: cubic box, linear dim **2.8 µm** (Kim) — for FF, use a native cortex patch. Protocol:
1. Build/relax crosslinked network at physiological operating point (FF: `--from-resting`, η=65 Pa·s, T=310 K).
2. Fix all segments within one segment-length (70 nm) of the bottom face; drive segments within one segment-length of the top face by an imposed strain.
3. Set prestrain γ by translating top boundary at constant strain rate; then superpose a **small 5% sinusoidal shear** (differential/tangent modulus, Gardel-2006 protocol) at frequency f.
4. Stress = (Σ forces on top-attached ends)/(top area) + fluid term τ_zx=η dγ/dt (Eq 9).
5. G′,G″ from stress/strain amplitude ratio and phase (Eq 10). Sweep f (Kim: ~1–1000 Hz) and γ (0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.55).
**Deliverable:** FF's first quantitative viscoelastic-modulus test — currently FF has no G′/G″ harness. This is a reusable acceptance-oracle scenario.

### Scenario B — Segment-tracking microrheology (independent cross-check)
Divide domain into N_MSD=512 cells, track one segment's center MSD per cell, convert via Eq 11 (χ = local MSD log-slope). Cross-check G* vs Scenario A. Use only for homogeneous/unstressed networks (diverges when heterogeneous/prestrained).

### Scenario C — Bundler vs orthogonal crosslinker comparison
Build two networks identical except crosslinker θ₁,eq: ACPᴮ (π, bundle) vs ACPᶜ (0.417π, orthogonal). Same R. Show ACPᶜ percolates/gels (single-body elastic response), ACPᴮ under-percolates (ladder structures, no long-time elasticity). Maps to FF filopodium(bundle) vs cortex(mesh).

### Scenario D — Supportive-framework extraction
On a prestrained network: sum |bending force| per crosslinker over 0.1 ms, keep top X% (25/50/75), delete the rest + all filaments not bridging two kept crosslinkers, re-measure stress. Target: 25% ACP + 28% filaments → 70% stress. FF cortex diagnostic.

### Scenario E — Bell-Evans extension (FF-only, the paper's requested follow-up)
Repeat Scenario A but turn FF crosslinker Bell-Evans off-rate ON. Show stress relaxation + the experimental **1–30 Pa stress ceiling** emerge (vs Kim's permanent-crosslink >100 Pa catastrophe). This is the headline FF result Kim explicitly calls for.

### In-vitro companion (for absolute calibration)
Kim's matched experiment: rabbit skeletal-muscle actin 10 mg/ml, recombinant filamin-A + gelsolin (caps ⟨L_f⟩≈1.5 µm), AR-G2 rheometer, 0.1–10 Hz, strain <2%. G′,G″ ≈ 0.1–10 Pa rising with f. Overlap with sim 1–10 Hz.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

1. **k_s,A and k_s,ACP are 1/40 of physiological (compute hack).** Do NOT KB-register Kim's dimensional stretch stiffnesses. Real values are ×40. Kim even shows the τ₀-exponent moves 0.85→0.7 as k_s,A is softened, i.e. the exponent is stiffness-dependent — so FF at the *real* (stiffer) k_s,A should land the exponent closer to the experimental ~1, not 0.85. Port ratios/geometry, anchor magnitudes to Ferrer.

2. **Purified in-vitro reconstitution vs living cortex.** Kim is a reconstituted actin+filamin/gelsolin gel: NO myosin, NO turnover, NO membrane, NO turgor, C_A=12.1 µM (dilute, ~0.1% volume fraction), l_c≈0.4 µm. FF native cortex is denser (≈100 filaments/µm²), pre-tensioned by myosin, membrane-bounded. Use Kim as a **passive-backbone unit test** (myosin off, unbinding off, thermostat on) — never as the full-cortex physiological target. The physiological γ-generation requires myosin + turgor that Kim lacks.

3. **Permanent crosslinks → stress catastrophe (Kim's own headline limitation).** Kim's G′ at high prestrain is too high (>100 Pa) and its exponent too low because crosslinks never unbind. **FF must NOT reproduce this.** FF's Bell-Evans off-rate is exactly the fix. Treat Kim's high-γ moduli as an *upper bound artifact*; validate FF against the experimental 1–30 Pa relaxation ceiling instead. This is FF's opportunity, not a target to match.

4. **Water viscosity, not cytoplasm.** Kim uses water-like η with HI neglected (dilute, high aspect ratio). FF physiological baseline is η≈65 Pa·s MCF7 cytoplasm (HARD rule). Adopt Kim's *drag closed-form* (Eq 6) but with FF's η, not water. This changes absolute timescales (Kim's ~1–1000 Hz window will shift) — so port the *exponents and ratios*, recompute absolute G′,G″ at physiological η/T.

5. **Coarse-graining N_C=10 (70 nm segment) + ACP arm +63 nm artifact.** Kim's coarse-graining inflates crosslinker arm length by (L_C,A−σ_C,A)/2 = 63 nm, exceeding real α-actinin/fascin/filamin spacings. FF's native (finer) segmentation avoids this — do NOT inherit Kim's 70 nm segment or the +63 nm arm. Keep FF native scale (this is also the HARD native-scale rule).

6. **2.8 µm periodic box, filaments severed >box, no plateau modulus.** Small-domain finite-size effects: bundled (ACPᴮ) networks are heterogeneous (ladders not bundles), and no entanglement plateau appears (filaments too short + sim too short). FF native cortex patch must be large enough that percolation isn't box-limited; measure over a native-scale patch, not a 2.8 µm cube, before drawing a G′ conclusion (native-scale rule).

7. **Thermal-fluctuation "negligible" is regime-specific.** The <5% claim holds only for l_p≫l_c. FF must keep the BAOAB thermostat ON for: single-molecule binding kinetics, filopodial tips (l_p~l_c at short bundled segments), and low-prestrain sub-µm-spacing regimes. Do not use Kim to justify a global thermostat-off.

8. **Segment-tracking (Eq 11) diverges when heterogeneous/prestrained.** Use bulk-shear (Eq 10) as the primary FF modulus; segment-tracking only as a homogeneous/unstressed cross-check, exactly as Kim did.
