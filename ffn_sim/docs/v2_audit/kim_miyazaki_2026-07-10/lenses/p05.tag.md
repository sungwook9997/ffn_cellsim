# TAG-validation lens — p05

**Paper:** Matsuda, Li, Brumm, Adachi, Inoue, Kim (2019) *Mobility of Molecular Motors Regulates Contractile Behaviors of Actin Networks.* Biophys. J. 116:2161–2171. DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2019.04.018.
**Lab:** Taeyoon Kim (Purdue), agent-based Brownian-dynamics actomyosin model (Cytosim-lineage) — the same mechanistic vocabulary as our FF engine.

## Paper kind

**simulation** (agent-based / coarse-grained Langevin Brownian dynamics; model output, not lab measurement).

Consequence for use (hard rule): the paper's headline numbers — Q_A heterogeneity, per-arm forces, F-actin speeds, biphasic-vs-monotonic trends — are **MODEL OUTPUTS of a peer engine**, so they may be used only as **comparison / cross-check targets for the FF engine**, NEVER as a truth source and NEVER as a fit target. They are *not* experimental overlay data either. The one genuinely validation-grade **experimental anchor** in the paper is the calibration input it inherits from wet-lab refs: the unloaded nonmuscle-myosin-II walking velocity **140 nm/s** (refs 39,40 — Kovács 2003 / Norstrom-type NMII data), which the model was tuned to reproduce at reference k₂₀. That one number is an experimental cross-check; everything else is simulation.

This is a strong **FF** relevance paper (fine-grained filaments + PCM catch-bond myosin + Bell's-law ACP + excluded volume) and only weak/indirect **DCM** relevance (informs how lumped cortical γ could emerge from motor mobility, but not a DCM parameter source).

## Coverage vs existing gates

- **F-actin / motor unloaded velocity (140 nm/s)** → touches **PARAM-v_unloaded** (default 100 nm/s, range 10–100 nm/s, KU-2.4/3.12, "actin retrograde flow / motor unloaded velocity"). The paper's NMII-calibrated **140 nm/s sits just ABOVE the current 10–100 range**. This is a mild **CHALLENGE/TIGHTEN** signal for the retrograde-flow band but note the observables differ: v_unloaded in our KB is retrograde-flow-flavored, whereas 140 nm/s is a *single-motor unloaded gliding velocity*. Flag for PI: register 140 nm/s as a distinct motor-velocity datum rather than overwriting the retrograde-flow parameter.
- **Motor stall force** → touches **PARAM-F_stall_motor** (default 2 pN/motor, KU-2.4/2.18). The paper does not report a single-head stall in pN (it uses stall ∝ 1/k₂₀ and reports *per-arm aggregate* forces of 8–13 pN over N_h=4–8 heads → ~1–3 pN/head, broadly **CONFIRMS** the 2 pN order of magnitude). Not a supersede; a soft consistency check.
- **Biphasic traction / saturating behavior** → conceptually adjacent to **VG-U2-biphasic-traction** (motor-clutch traction vs substrate stiffness, biphasic-but-saturating). Different independent variable (motor *mobility* ζ_M here vs *substrate stiffness* there), so **NOT the same gate**; it is an independent, analogous non-monotonicity worth noting but not merging.
- **Cortex multi-filament reconstruction** (VG-U3-cortex-multifilament, Phase-2 entry gate: N_filaments≥50, Bell-Evans crosslinks, Q_ij measurable) → this paper's Q_A heterogeneity metric and Bell's-law ACP kinetics are directly in scope as a **cross-check for the FF cortex once that gate is exercised**. No band change; it strengthens the mechanistic contract.
- No existing gate covers **motor mobility (drag) → contractility/heterogeneity trend**, per-arm motor force scale, or the one-arm-vs-two-arm connectivity dichotomy. These are genuinely uncovered — but see the caution below.

## New gate candidates

All flagged **candidate, PI-gate review** — and all are engine-vs-engine cross-checks against a peer *simulation*, so they must be framed as "FF reproduces the Kim-model trend," never as truth. Bands below are read off the paper's own output ranges and must not be treated as literature contracts.

1. **[candidate] VG-FF-motor-arm-force-scale** — observable: average tensile force per bound myosin arm in an FF actomyosin patch at physiological motor density. Band (cross-check, not contract): **~1–13 pN per arm** (per-head ~1–3 pN over N_h=4–8), one-arm up to ~13 pN, two-arm up to ~8 pN. Condition: ⟨L_f⟩≈1.5 µm, thin cortex-like network, k₂₀ reference. Engine: **FF**. Source: Fig 2a / Fig 4a. *Rationale:* an order-of-magnitude sanity band for FF motor force generation; consistent with our F_stall_motor=2 pN.
2. **[candidate] VG-FF-motor-unloaded-velocity** — observable: unloaded FF myosin walking velocity. Value (experimental cross-check): **140 nm/s** for nonmuscle myosin II at reference kinetics, scaling up toward ~2 µm/s at high ATP-rate k₂₀. Engine: **FF** (this is a genuine wet-lab-anchored calibration datum, not just model output). Source: p.3, refs 39,40. *Rationale:* strongest single validation datum in the paper; use to calibrate/cross-check FF motor speed. Overlaps PARAM-v_unloaded — see PI note.
3. **[candidate, qualitative] VG-FF-mobility-contractility-trend** — observable: sign/shape of network-heterogeneity (or contractile stress) vs motor drag ζ_M. Expected: **biphasic (peak at intermediate mobility) for one-armed motors; monotonic-increasing-with-mobility for two-armed motors.** Engine: **FF** (requires the fine-grained cortex + explicit one/two-arm motor connectivity). Source: Fig 2d / Fig 4d, Fig 6. *Rationale:* a mechanistic falsifiable *trend* (not a magic number) that our FF two-arm minifilament model should reproduce; qualitative, so defensible without importing a peer engine's numeric output as truth.

I do **not** propose gates from the Q_A absolute values (15–70 a.u.) — that metric is grid/normalization-specific (N_G=20, segment-count units) and not portable as a contract.

## Oracle candidates

**None of contract grade.** The paper gives no new closed-form ground-truth law we would implement as an analytic oracle. The relevant analytic pieces it *uses* are already in our oracle set or KB:
- **Bell's law** ACP unbinding k_off = k_off,0·exp(Fλ/k_BT) → already our Bell-Evans oracle.
- **PCM (Erdmann–Schwarz) catch-bond** walking/unbinding, stall ∝ 1/k₂₀ → this is the mechanistic motor model, adjacent to our **Hill force-velocity** oracle; the PCM 3-state/5-rate cycle could be considered as a *future* fine-grained motor oracle, but it is a full stochastic model (SI rates needed), not a compact closed form → **not** proposed as an oracle now.
- The stall ∝ 1/k₂₀ and F_max = N_h·stall·#arms relations are worth recording as a **cross-check formula** on the FF motor module, but they are model constructs of a peer simulation, not analytic ground truth.
