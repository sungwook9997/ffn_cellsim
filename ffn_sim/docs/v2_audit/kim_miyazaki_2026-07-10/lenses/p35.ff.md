# FF lens — p35

**Paper:** Li, Biel, Lomada, Yu, Kim — "Buckling-induced F-actin fragmentation modulates the contraction of active cytoskeletal networks," *Soft Matter* 2017, 13, 3213–3220 (DOI 10.1039/c6sm02703b).

**Why this is a high-value FF paper:** it IS the FF engine's lineage. Same Taeyoon-Kim agent-based Brownian-dynamics actomyosin model FF draws on — F-actin as serially bonded cylindrical bending-segments, ACP cross-linkers with force-dependent (slip/Bell) unbinding, myosin thick filaments (8 arms × 8 heads, barbed-end walking), F-actin-only volume exclusion, Langevin/Euler integration. The one mechanism FF **lacks and this paper supplies** is **curvature/buckling-induced F-actin severing** (Eq. 1) and the emergent phase behavior it produces (single-focus large contraction ↔ multi-foci fragmentation ↔ buckling-suppressed rigid). This directly attacks FF open problems: **network contraction & stress relaxation, filament turnover/fragmentation, buckling/condensation symmetry-breaking, motor-driven flows to foci, and cortical-tension γ generation** (severing is a real cortical stress-relaxation channel).

---

## 1. MECHANISMS TO ADOPT

### 1.1 Curvature-dependent F-actin severing (PRIMARY — FF currently has none)
- **FF module:** filament turnover / fragmentation channel on the actin filament (`ff/` filament kernel).
- **Change:** add a per-internal-node stochastic severing event whose rate rises exponentially with the local bending angle. When it fires, break the bead-rod chain at that node (remove one segment / split the filament into two shorter filaments, each retaining barbed/pointed polarity). This is a NEW kinetic channel alongside binding/unbinding of crosslinkers and motors.
- **Why it beats current FF:** FF filaments are effectively unbreakable beams. Real cortical actin under myosin compression buckles and severs (Murrell & Gardel 2012 PNAS ref 9; cofilin-enhanced, Schmoller 2011 / McCullough 2011 refs 18/19). Without severing, FF over-predicts network connectivity persistence → it cannot reproduce (a) the *non-monotonic* dependence of contraction on bending rigidity, (b) fragmentation into multiple foci, or (c) buckling-driven stress relaxation. This is the mechanistic (not lumped) route to turnover the CLAUDE.md rule demands — Kim's own roadmap (their Conclusion) calls this the physiological extension.
- **Consequence FF gains:** a genuine emergent stress-relaxation / γ-decay mechanism. Buckled filaments that sever release stored bending + tension energy — a candidate contributor to the cortical-tension γ-floor problem (a stiff un-severing network stores buckling energy that a real cortex would dissipate by fragmentation).

### 1.2 Fragment re-crosslinking (emergent, requires 1.1 + existing ACP dynamics)
- **FF module:** crosslinker (α-actinin/filamin) binding kinetics — already present.
- **Change:** none to the crosslinker code itself; but **run ACP binding continuously through contraction** so that severing-generated short fragments (which locally raise effective actin concentration) get re-crosslinked. The paper's key subtlety (p.6): severing during contraction ≠ starting with pre-fragmented short filaments, because contraction concentrates actin so ACPs *can* re-link fragments and rescue connectivity at high R_ACP.
- **Why:** captures the "high-R_ACP override" — connectivity enhancement can beat severing-induced connectivity loss. FF must have crosslinker rebinding ON (physiological-baseline rule) to reproduce this competition.

### 1.3 Buckling as the contraction motor (validate FF already does this)
- **FF module:** filament bending κ + myosin minifilament.
- **Change:** none — this is a **parity check**. FF's bending-beam + Stam–Hocky minifilament should reproduce that (i) contraction to foci *requires* buckling (asymmetric compressive response of a semiflexible filament), and (ii) too-stiff filaments (high κ) barely contract and instead polarity-sort into aster-like foci (barbed ends to center) — the microtubule-like regime at 64× κ.
- **Why:** confirms FF's semiflexible mechanics produce the compressive-buckling asymmetry that underlies all actomyosin contraction, before adding severing.

### 1.4 Polarity sorting to aster centers (validate)
- **FF module:** myosin walk-toward-barbed-end.
- Emergent at high κ (buckling suppressed): motors carry barbed ends to focus centers → aster. FF's directional walking should reproduce this; it is a clean qualitative gate for motor polarity handling.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES TO PORT

### 2.1 Severing rate (Eq. 1) — the importable law
```
k_s,A = k⁰_s,A · exp( θ_s,A / λ_s,A )
```
- **θ_s,A** = sum of the TWO adjacent bending angles at an internal filament point (the local curvature proxy). Map to FF: at each internal bead `i` on a filament, `θ_s,A(i) = angle(i-1,i,i+1)` — or the sum of the two bond-angle deviations meeting at `i` (match the exact triplet-angle convention of the FF bending potential so κ and severing read the same θ). Units: radians.
- **k⁰_s,A** = zero-angle severing rate constant (baseline severing when straight). Units s⁻¹. Swept 0, 10⁻⁵⁰, 10⁻⁴⁰, 10⁻³⁰, 10⁻²⁰, 10⁻¹⁷ s⁻¹; Figs 4–5 FIX k⁰_s,A = **10⁻³⁰ s⁻¹**. The absurdly small prefactor is the point: severing is essentially OFF for a straight filament and only becomes appreciable once θ_s,A/λ_s,A reaches ~30–115 (i.e. deep buckling), so the exponential is the whole mechanism.
- **λ_s,A** = angular sensitivity (characteristic angle). **Value is in the ESI (not in main text) — must be pulled from ESI / ref 15 (Jung, Murrell & Kim, ACS Macro Lett. 2016, 5, 641) before porting.** This is the one missing numeric.
- **Implementation in FF (Warp, per-step):** for each internal node, `p_sever = 1 − exp(−k_s,A·Δt) ≈ k_s,A·Δt`; draw uniform; on fire, sever the bond. Differentiable-friendly: the rate is smooth in θ; only the discrete cut is non-differentiable (handle like existing bind/unbind discrete events). This is a **first-order kinetic Monte Carlo channel**, exactly the pattern FF already uses for Bell-Evans crosslinker off-rates.
- **NOTE — mechanistic distinction:** this is an *angle*-activated rate, NOT a Bell *force* rate. Do not conflate with crosslinker/integrin `k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b)`. Buckling raises θ → raises k_s. Keep it a separate channel keyed on curvature.

### 2.2 Contraction-extent observable (Eq. 2) — port as an FF diagnostic
```
ξ(t) = 1 − [ Σ_i^N π R_i(t)² ] / [ Σ_i^N π R_i(0)² ]
```
- N = number of auto-detected foci; R_i(t) = mean distance of a focus's member actin endpoints to that focus center. ξ=0 no contraction, ξ=1 maximal.
- **Protocol to replicate exactly (so numbers are comparable):** project actin segment endpoints to x–y → density map → coarsen to histogram → threshold to detect foci (merge adjacent centers) → back-trace endpoints to t=0 → per focus center = mean(x,y), R_i = mean endpoint-to-center distance.
- **Rate/steady-state definitions:** ξ̇(t)=dξ/dt; steady state = first time ξ̇(t) < 0.1·ξ̇(0); t_ss = time to reach it; ξ_ss = ξ there; **average contraction rate = ξ_ss / t_ss**. Port all four as FF network-contraction metrics.

### 2.3 Integration / EOM (already FF-aligned)
- Overdamped Langevin: deterministic (harmonic bending to equilibrium angle + harmonic stretch to equilibrium length + repulsive volume-exclusion between F-actins) + Gaussian thermal force satisfying fluctuation–dissipation; here integrated with **Euler–Maruyama**. FF is STRICTLY BETTER here — FF uses constrained Leimkuhler–Matthews BAOAB, a higher-fidelity integrator than the paper's Euler. Do NOT downgrade; keep BAOAB. Only the severing channel is new.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL TO REPRODUCE (FF scenario)

**Scenario name suggestion:** `ff_severing_contraction` (cortex-like thin-slab reconstitution).

1. **Geometry:** thin quasi-2D slab 20×20×0.1 µm, no periodic BC; actin+ACP+motor self-assemble inside a circular footprint R=8 µm. This is a natural FF *sub-scenario* — a cortex-like thin actomyosin patch, distinct from the full spherical MCF7 cortex, but at the SAME fine-grained fidelity (every filament/crosslinker/motor explicit).
2. **Assembly stage:** nucleate + polymerize F-actin (FF barbed-end growth already exists; ⟨L_f⟩≈2.53 µm, exponential dist. compatible with FF's KB-3.18 1–10 µm); bind ACPs into functional crosslinks; assemble myosin minifilaments with arms bound but **not walking** (motors engaged, zero duty). Reach a percolated pre-stressed network — this satisfies the physiological-baseline rule (start from an assembled, cross-linked, pre-engaged state, not a floppy bag).
3. **Contraction stage:** switch motors to walking → network contracts to foci over ~100–500 s. Record ξ(t), foci count, severing-event count.
4. **Sweeps to reproduce the phase diagram:**
   - Fig. 2 (no severing): R_M × R_ACP grid → the "contraction island" (large ξ needs R_M ≥ R_M^min AND R_ACP^min ≤ R_ACP ≤ R_ACP^max).
   - Fig. 3: κ_b,A(×1..64) × k⁰_s,A(0..10⁻¹⁷) at R_M=0.08, R_ACP=0.02 → non-monotonic ξ_ss(κ), max at intermediate κ.
   - Fig. 4: R_ACP(0.005–0.08) × κ_b,A at k⁰_s,A=10⁻³⁰, R_M=0.08 → optimal κ most apparent at LOW R_ACP.
   - Fig. 5: R_M(0.02–0.32) × κ_b,A at k⁰_s,A=10⁻³⁰, R_ACP=0.02 → optimal κ most apparent at HIGH R_M.

**Native-scale caveat:** per CLAUDE.md, any *conclusion* must be reconfirmed on the native full cortex. Use this thin-slab as a **mechanism-development & parity harness** for the severing kernel (cheap, matches the paper), then port the validated severing channel into the native 38000-filament cortex and re-confirm its effect on γ / stress relaxation there. Label the slab runs non-authoritative.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS (FF observables to reproduce)

1. **Contraction island (no severing):** ξ_ss > 0.50 requires R_M ≥ R_M^min and R_ACP^min ≤ R_ACP ≤ R_ACP^max (Fig. 2C). Below R_M^min: no cooperativity. Above R_ACP^max: buckling suppressed.
2. **Buckling-suppression critical R_ACP ≈ 0.32** (Fig. 2C) — above this, buckling (hence contraction) is inhibited.
3. **Non-monotonic ξ_ss(κ_b,A) WITH severing (the headline):** maximum contraction at *intermediate* κ_b,A, not lowest (Fig. 3D). Very flexible → over-severing → multi-foci low ξ; very stiff → no buckling → low ξ.
4. **Foci count:** 1 (single focus) up to ~10–11, growing with severing frequency (Figs 3C/4C/5C).
5. **Severing-event frequency:** ~20 to ~7000 events, increasing with k⁰_s,A, with R_M, and with lower κ_b,A (Figs 3B/4B/5B).
6. **t_ss ∝ κ_b,A**, nearly independent of k⁰_s,A; larger with higher R_ACP; smaller with higher R_M (Figs S3A/S4A/S5A). Stiffer network resists contraction → slower.
7. **Average contraction rate ξ_ss/t_ss** larger with higher R_M and lower κ_b,A; larger with higher R_ACP and lower κ_b,A.
8. **Critical κ_b,A (buckling cutoff) ∝ R_ACP/R_M**, independent of k⁰_s,A (Fig. 6B).
9. **Optimal-κ conspicuousness scales with LOW R_ACP/R_M** (low crosslink, high motor).
10. **Aster / microtubule-like regime at 64× κ_b,A (ℓ_p=0.58 µm):** motors polarity-sort barbed ends to foci centers; minimal contraction.
11. **High-R_ACP override:** at high R_ACP, single focus + large contraction *despite* frequent severing (connectivity beats severing).
12. **Qualitative anchor to in-vitro:** matches Bendix 2008 actin / skeletal myosin II / α-actinin reconstituted gel (ref 5) — critical motor + intermediate crosslink for contraction.

## 6. RISKS / CAVEATS

### 6.1 Persistence-length mismatch (MOST IMPORTANT)
The paper's *reference* κ_b,A corresponds to ℓ_p ≈ 0.009 µm (since 64× → 0.58 µm), which is **~1900× below the real actin ℓ_p ≈ 17 µm** FF uses. This is a deliberate coarse-grained softness in the Kim agent model (large segments, reduced effective bending) so that filaments buckle at accessible network stresses. **You cannot port k⁰_s,A / λ_s,A verbatim onto FF's stiff physiological ℓ_p=17 µm filaments** — at real stiffness the θ_s,A distribution is entirely different and the exponential would essentially never fire. Correct approach: keep FF at physiological ℓ_p=17 µm (HARD baseline rule), and **re-derive the severing law as a curvature-energy / local-strain criterion** so it fires at the physically correct buckling curvature for real filaments — then treat this paper's phase diagram (non-monotonic ξ(κ), foci count, R_ACP/R_M scaling) as the *qualitative* behavior to reproduce, NOT the absolute k⁰_s,A number. Surface the re-anchoring to PI (no magic number).

### 6.2 In-vitro reconstitution vs living cortex
Purified actin + skeletal myosin II + α-actinin thin gel, no membrane, no turgor, no nucleus, no ATP-turnover-driven treadmilling, no capping. The living MCF7 cortex has all of these. Use the slab as a **mechanism/parity harness only**; the severing channel's effect on γ and stress relaxation must be re-measured in the native full-compartment cortex (membrane + turgor + nucleus + microtubules, `--from-resting`) before any conclusion.

### 6.3 2D quasi-slab vs 3D cortex
20×20×0.1 µm thin slab, no PBC, finite circular footprint. Foci formation and connectivity percolation differ in a curved 3D shell. Do not transfer foci-count numbers to the 3D cortex; transfer the *mechanism* (severing → connectivity loss → fragmentation).

### 6.4 No motor volume exclusion → artificially compact motor aggregates
The paper explicitly has NO excluded volume between motors (only F-actin–F-actin), so motor foci over-compact and the "secondary contraction regime" (Wang–Wolynes ref 17) is suppressed. FF has LJ excluded volume ON (HARD rule) — so FF is MORE physical and may legitimately show behavior the paper cannot (e.g. the secondary regime). Treat divergences here as FF being correct, not a parity failure; do not turn off FF excluded volume to match.

### 6.5 Euler integrator + molar-ratio densities
Paper uses Euler–Maruyama and specifies densities as molar ratios R_M, R_ACP (not absolute counts/areal densities). FF uses BAOAB (keep it) and native areal densities (KB-3.18 ~100/µm² cortex). Map R_M/R_ACP to FF's actual filament/motor/crosslinker counts via the actin count in the slab; don't inherit the ratios as absolute densities. Reconcile against FF native cortex density before quoting counts.

### 6.6 Severing splits a filament — bookkeeping
On a sever event, one filament becomes two, each needing its own barbed/pointed polarity, growth state, bound crosslinkers/motors reassigned, and excluded-volume/neighbor lists updated. In a GPU-resident Warp kernel this is a topology change — plan a compaction/relist step (like FF's existing bind/unbind topology updates) rather than in-kernel array resize. Non-trivial engineering; scope with the FF topology-update path.

### 6.7 ESI-gated numbers
λ_s,A, the reference κ_b,A absolute value, motor stepping rate, ACP on/off rates, viscosity, kT, segment lengths, spring constants all live in the ESI (DOI 10.1039/c6sm02703b) + refs 14/15/16 — NOT in the main text. Fetch the ESI and Jung 2016 (ACS Macro Lett. 5, 641) before finalizing any numeric port; the main text alone is insufficient for a quantitative FF implementation.
