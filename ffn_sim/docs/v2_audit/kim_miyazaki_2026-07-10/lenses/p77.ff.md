## 1. Mechanisms to adopt  (strong for a FUTURE FF microtubule/kinesin extension; the physics is FF-native)
- **Extensile bundle self-organization from motor-driven filament–filament sliding.** Kinesin slides MTs to elongate extensile bundles that connect into a network — the microtubule/kinesin analogue of FF's actin/myosin machinery. FF currently models contractile actomyosin; this paper is the reference for the *extensile* mode (pusher units) and for a **microtubule + kinesin + MAP compartment** as a future FF extension (FF already has microtubules in the native-cell config `--microtubules`).
- **Non-motor crosslinkers (MAPs) build an elastic network that transmits boundary force over long range; bundles buckle under confinement.** FF should model MAP crosslinkers + Euler-buckling of stiff filaments under compressive boundary load (MTs as near-rigid rods, ℓ_p 1–2 mm ≫ contour length).
- **Boundary friction (non-slipping bundle end) generates a moment of force.** A mechanistic boundary-contact friction for filaments pushing on the membrane — relevant to FF membrane-containment + filament-tip forces.

## 2. Force laws / kinetic schemes to port
- No explicit force law (experimental). Concepts: extensile sliding force (kinesin), boundary pushing + non-slip friction, Euler buckling of bundles. C(r,t) correlation for flow organization.

- Confined active MT network (kinesin + MAP) in a cell-sized droplet → extensile bundles push boundary → rotational flow. A FF self-organization benchmark (extensile vs the contractile actomyosin FF already does).
