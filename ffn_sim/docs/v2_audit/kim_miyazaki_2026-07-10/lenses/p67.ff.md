# FF lens — p67

**Paper:** Lee, Pelz, Ferrer, Kim, Lang, Kamm (2009) "Cytoskeletal Deformation at High Strains and the Role of Cross-link Unfolding or Unbinding." *Cell. Mol. Bioeng.* 2(1):28–38. DOI 10.1007/s12195-009-0048-8.

**One line for FF:** This is a **primary filamin-A cross-linker datum** — single-molecule force spectroscopy giving (a) directly-usable Bell/HS off-rate constants for the FF crosslinker module, (b) a *new mechanistic channel we do not yet have* (Ig-domain **unfolding** as a distinct compliance/recovery process separate from unbinding), (c) an **angle-dependent (shear/torsion) rupture** law, and (d) the exact **Kim–Hwang–Kamm 2009 Brownian-dynamics network protocol** that is the philosophical parent of the FF Cytosim-style engine, complete with a bead-drag rheology scenario we can replicate. It is the sister paper to Ferrer 2008 (ref 9), which the project already used to re-anchor α-actinin `k_off0`.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Crosslinker unfolding as a SEPARATE channel (new mechanism; FF currently has only unbind)
FF crosslinkers (α-actinin, filamin) are today explicit bonds with a single fate: force-dependent **unbinding** (Bell-Evans). This paper shows a **second, mechanistically distinct** high-strain fate for filamin-A: **serial Ig-domain unfolding** — a sawtooth in F–extension with period **28 ± 5 nm** per domain (up to 8–17 of the 24 Ig repeats unfold before rupture). The two matter differently for cortex rheology:
- **Unfolding** = *reversible* added compliance + length (refolds when load relaxes) → strain-softening / stress-relaxation channel that **does not disconnect the network**.
- **Unbinding** = *remodeling* (filament rearranges, crosslinker re-attaches elsewhere) → topological turnover.

**Why it beats current FF:** the current single-fate bond conflates "network stays connected but yields" with "network reconnects somewhere else." For the open FF problems of **network contraction & stress relaxation** and **strain-softening / buckling symmetry-breaking**, an unfolding channel gives an emergent, mechanistic softening reservoir (each domain adds ~28 nm of contour at ~57 pN) instead of a lumped viscous term. Implement filamin as a bond with an internal **worm-like-chain / serial-domain state** (N_unfolded ∈ 0…24), each unfolding event at F≈57 pN adding ΔL_c≈28 nm and dropping local stiffness; refolding on unload. This is exactly the fine-grained-over-lumped rule.

### M2 — Angle-dependent (shear/torsion) rupture weakening
Rupture force is ~constant below a **~45° inter-filament pulling angle** for filamin, then drops sharply above it (α-actinin holds to ~70°). FF currently treats a crosslinker off-rate as a function of the **extensional** load only (as does the Kim BD model in this very paper — "only extensional forces on the ABPs are considered"). Adding a **geometric weakening factor** keyed to the angle between the two crosslinked filament segments makes crosslinker rupture *orientation-aware*: shear/torsion in a compressed/buckling cortex ruptures crosslinks earlier than pure tension would predict. **Why it beats current FF:** it is a mechanistic route to strain-softening and to isoform-specific network behavior (filamin=orthogonal/compliant, α-actinin=stiffer bundling) without any fitted softening law. This is the single most novel structural lever in the paper for FF.

### M3 — Two-barrier (two-slope) energy landscape for the off-rate
Rupture force rises with **ln(loading rate)** with **two distinct slopes** — a low-loading-rate regime and a steep high-loading-rate regime — signaling **two energy barriers** (outer barrier suppressed by force). FF's single-exponential Bell law captures only one barrier. Adopting a two-barrier (Evans–Ritchie / dual-Bell) off-rate makes the crosslinker's effective lifetime correct across the cortex's wide range of local loading rates (slow cortical creep vs. fast myosin tugs). **Why:** cortex spans many decades of loading rate; a single Bell slope mis-predicts lifetime at one end.

### M4 — Bead-drag microrheology as an FF validation scenario (network assay + BD model)
The paper's **network assay** (drag a 1 µm bead through a filamin/actin gel, record F vs. displacement) and its **BD reproduction** give FF a ready-made, fully-specified *emergent* test: discrete force-drop transitions (20–56 pN experiment; the drops come from unbinding of crosslinkers in a <500 nm shell around the bead). This is a mechanistic rheology probe FF can run at our fine-grained scale to validate crosslinker kinetics *in a network*, not just single-molecule.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### L1 — Bell slip-bond off-rate (crosslinker unbinding) — *the FF-ready form*
```
k_off(F) = k_off0 · exp( F · x / (k_B T) )
```
Map to FF state: `F` = extensional tension carried by the crosslinker bond (N); `x` = transition-state distance (Bell length); `k_B T` = 4.114 pN·nm at 298 K. Paper's **simulation-input** constants (what Kim's BD used, so directly FF-compatible): `k_off0 = 0.115 s⁻¹`, `x = 0.416 nm`. (Note: this simulation `x=0.416 nm` is ~1.5–2× the HS-fit `x‡`; it is the value tuned to reproduce the *network* response, so it is the value to use for the network scenario; the HS fits below are single-molecule.)

### L2 — Hummer–Szabo (HS) barrier + molecular spring (single-molecule fits)
Free-energy barrier from the two HS observables:
```
ΔG = ½ · κ_m · x‡²
```
`κ_m` = molecular spring constant (crosslinker linear stiffness near the transition state), `x‡` = transition-state distance. Use for setting FF **crosslinker link stiffness** and cross-checking the rupture-force distribution. Values in §3.

### L3 — Dynamic-force-spectroscopy scaling (loading-rate law), two barriers
```
F_rupture  ∝  ln(r) ,   r = dF/dt  (loading rate, pN/s)
```
Two slopes (two barriers). FF map: a crosslinker under loading rate `r` (computable from FF per-step bond-force history) ruptures at a rate whose most-probable force follows this log law; implement as a two-barrier Kramers/Bell escape rather than a single slope. Regimes: low `r` = 5–50 pN/s (Ferrer 2008), high `r` = 400–2000 pN/s (this paper).

### L4 — Ig-domain unfolding (serial WLC sawtooth) — the new M1 channel
Each unfolding event adds contour length:
```
ΔL_c per domain ≈ N_aa · d_aa = 96 aa · 3.5 Å ≈ 34 nm   (theory)
measured period = 28 ± 5 nm
```
Unfolding is itself a force-activated (Bell-type) transition with its own `k_off0`, `x‡`, `κ_m` (see §3). FF map: filamin bond gets an internal serial-domain WLC with up to **24** domains; per-domain unfold event at F≈57 pN, ΔL_c≈28 nm, force-drop 4–10 pN (=6–15 nm bead displacement), stiffness decreases with each unfold; refold allowed on unload (time-scale not measured here → surface to PI, don't invent).

### L5 — Angle weakening (M2), phenomenological threshold to make mechanistic
Paper gives the *observation*: rupture force ≈ const for pull-angle θ < 45° (filamin), falls sharply above. FF implementation: compute θ = angle between the two filament segments joined by the crosslinker; apply the load decomposed into extension + shear/torsion, and let the **shear/torsion component drive an additional off-rate term** (so the 45° threshold *emerges* from the bond's directional compliance rather than being hard-coded). α-actinin threshold ~70° gives the isoform contrast to reproduce.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenarios)

### Scenario A — Single-molecule crosslinker pull (crosslinker force-extension oracle)
One filamin cross-links two actin filaments; one filament fixed, the other pulled at constant velocity via a Hookean trap. FF map: two short filaments, one crosslinker, trap `k = 0.5–0.75 pN/nm`, pull at **5 µm/s** → loading rate 400–2000 pN/s. Observe F–extension; expect rupture at 50–170 pN (mean 70±23) OR sawtooth unfolding (period 28 nm, ~20% of pulls). This is the FF **crosslinker unit test** for both channels.

### Scenario B — Bead-drag network microrheology (the Kim–Hwang–Kamm 2009 BD protocol, our engine's parent)
Fully specified and directly FF-runnable at native fine-grained scale:
- Actin **12 µM**, mean filament length **~1.5 µm**, ABP:actin molar ratio **0.017**, cubic domain **5 µm** width.
- Polymerize + crosslink into an **orthogonal (filamin-like) network**; then **sever & clamp filaments at the top/bottom z-surfaces**, **periodic BC** on x,y.
- Insert a **1.2 µm rigid bead** (replace filaments in a central sphere); force bead in +x at **20 µm/s**.
- Crosslinker unbinding via L1 (`k_off0=0.115 s⁻¹, x=0.416 nm`), extensional force only.
- Record total x-force on bead + each ABP bound/free vs. displacement.
- **Analysis filter** (to isolate the drops-causing events): ABP-to-bead-surface distance <500 nm AND ABP within a +x cone of included angle 2π/3.

Experimental reconstituted-gel variant (for the real-number target): actin **10 µM**, filamin:actin **0.01**, gelsolin sets mean filament length **~5 µm**, 1 µm bead, stage 5 µm/s.

**Value to FF:** Scenario B is an *emergent-rheology* validation that exercises exactly the FF crosslinker off-rate inside a network — and it is the literal BD lineage of the FF engine. It also touches the FF open problem **crawl-speed grid-drag**, because it is a bead-through-network drag problem: reproducing the discrete-drop signature (not a smooth drag) is a check that FF drag is crosslinker-rupture-mediated, not grid-mediated.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **R1 — Purified in-vitro vs. living cortex.** This is reconstituted rabbit-muscle actin + recombinant filamin-A, orthogonal gel, room temperature — NOT a turgor-pressurised MCF7 cortex. Use the numbers as **crosslinker constitutive law + oracle**, never as a cortex-level rheology target. Adopt into FF at the *bond* level; validate the *cortex* separately at native scale + full compartments per the HARD rule. Do not let a 5 µm reconstituted cube stand in for the Nc≈266k native cortex.

- **R2 — Isoform specificity.** These are **filamin-A** constants (V-homodimer, 24 Ig, 2 hinges). They do NOT transfer to α-actinin (short antiparallel dimer, ~70° angle threshold, already anchored to Ferrer 2008 `k_off0=0.066 s⁻¹`). Keep filamin and α-actinin as **separate FF crosslinker types** with separate parameter rows. Do not overwrite the audited α-actinin value.

- **R3 — Shear/torsion is assay geometry, partly.** The large 45° weakening and the large 500–2500 nm extensions include assay compliance (filament bending at attachment, linkage rotation). The molecular-structure origin of the 45° threshold is real (same threshold at both loading rates → structural, not dynamic), but the *magnitude* of extension is not molecular. When porting M2, key the weakening to the **inter-filament segment angle in FF**, not to the assay's bead trajectory; treat the threshold (~45°) as the transferable quantity, the extension magnitudes as assay-specific.

- **R4 — Internal transcription discrepancy on `x‡` unfold.** Running text (p.33) says unfolding transition distance **0.14 nm**; Table 1 says **0.46 nm**. These give very different ΔG=½κx‡² (0.46 nm reproduces the stated 6.8 k_BT with κ=266 pN/nm; 0.14 nm does not). **Trust Table 1 (0.46 nm).** Flag this to PI before it becomes a KB Parameter row; do not silently pick one and bury the conflict.

- **R5 — Simulation forces run high.** The BD model's drop forces (~100–150 pN) exceed experiment (20–56 pN) due to system size / filament length (1.5 µm sim vs 5 µm gel) / bead speed (20 vs 5 µm/s). So the **20 µm/s bead speed is a numerical convenience, not physiological** — if FF reproduces Scenario B, use it as a *qualitative* signature check (discrete drops, near-bead origin), and match absolute force only after correcting for our filament-length distribution (FF uses exponential 1–10 µm, not monodisperse 1.5 µm). Do not tune FF crosslinker `k_off0` to hit the sim's inflated force — that would be gate-fitting.

- **R6 — Loading-rate window.** All the sharp numbers are at 400–2000 pN/s (or 5–50 pN/s low limb). The cortex operates over a wider, often slower band. The two-barrier law (L3) is what lets FF extrapolate correctly; a single Bell slope fit to only the high regime would over-stiffen slow cortical creep. Use both limbs.

- **R7 — Refold time-scale unknown.** M1's reversibility (unfold → refold on unload) is asserted qualitatively; **no refolding rate is measured here.** Implement the unfold channel with a PI-surfaced placeholder for refold kinetics rather than inventing a magic number (no-magic-number rule). Physiological-baseline rule still applies: filamin should be present and bound at t=0 at its real density, not switched on later.
