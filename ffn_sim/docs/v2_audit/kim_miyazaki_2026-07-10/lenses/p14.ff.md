# FF lens — p14

**Paper:** Miyazaki, Laboni & Kim 2025, *Reconstitution of actomyosin networks in cell-sized liposomes* (bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.05.18.654456).
**Why this paper is FF-critical:** the agent-based half (Supplementary Note 2, Tables S1/S2) is **Taeyoon Kim's canonical Brownian-dynamics actomyosin engine — the exact FF paradigm** (overdamped Langevin, explicit cylindrical actin segments, ACP crosslinkers, NM-II minifilament motors via the Erdmann–Schwarz parallel-cluster model, deterministic F-actin severing). It is a fully-specified, citable, native-scale parameter set that FF can adopt kernel-by-kernel, plus a concrete reconstitution scenario (confined shell + gated myosin → cortical tension → bleb) that is an emergent-behavior validation gate. It also supplies a **mechanism FF currently lacks: force-triggered F-actin severing** that lets contraction proceed to network rupture.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### 1.1 (HIGH) Filament turnover — force-triggered F-actin **severing** channel
- **FF module:** `filament` (segment breakage) + `turnover`.
- **Current FF state:** distributed-length filaments + barbed-end growth, but **no fragmentation/severing** channel. FF's "network contraction & stress relaxation" and "filament turnover/fragmentation" are named open problems.
- **Adopt:** deterministic severing — when the **tensile** force on an actin segment exceeds `F_sev`, the segment is cut (bond removed), creating two new barbed/pointed ends. Paper uses `F_sev = 300 pN` (Tsuda 1996, range 200–600 pN).
- **Why it beats current FF:** without severing, a highly-crosslinked contractile network locks up (stress cannot relax; contraction stalls). Severing is the mechanism that (a) lets myosin-generated stress dissipate, (b) drives the **rupture** bleb-initiation mode (successive severing at a stress hotspot → hole in cortex), and (c) provides a *physical, force-derived* stress-relaxation timescale rather than a lumped viscoelastic relaxation time. This is a genuine mechanistic capability FF is missing, directly relevant to the γ-floor / contraction / stress-relaxation open problems.
- **Fine-grained note (mechanistic upgrade over the paper):** the paper uses a *deterministic threshold*. FF's hard rule prefers a rate law — implement as Bell-form stochastic severing `k_sev = k_sev0·exp(F/F_sev*)` so severing is force-*accelerated* not force-*gated*, then verify the deterministic 300 pN limit is recovered as `k_sev0→` sharp. Anchor `F_sev*` to the 200–600 pN literature spread (Tsuda 1996), not tuned to a gate.

### 1.2 (HIGH) Myosin II — **parallel-cluster model (Erdmann–Schwarz)** minifilament kinetics
- **FF module:** `myosin` (Stam–Hocky bipolar minifilament).
- **Current FF state:** Stam–Hocky multi-head bipolar minifilament + Hill (1938) force-velocity + duty ratio + catch-bond unbinding.
- **Adopt / cross-check:** the paper drives each motor **arm** (16 arms/motor, 4 heads/arm = 64 heads ≈ real NM-II 56) with an explicit 3-state parallel-cluster cycle (unbound→weakly-bound→post-power-stroke) with the load-dependent rate scheme in §2.2 below. This is a **mechanistically richer alternative/cross-check to the Hill closed-form**: force-velocity and catch-bond unbinding *emerge* from per-head cluster kinetics rather than being imposed as Hill+Bell closed forms.
- **Why it helps:** FF currently *imposes* the Hill f–v law (a closed-form convenience oracle by our own rule); the parallel-cluster model *generates* f–v and the catch-bond duty ratio from single-head transition rates — strictly more fine-grained, and it is Taeyoon Kim's own motor implementation, so it composes cleanly with the rest of this parameter set. Use it as the myosin sanity oracle at minimum; consider as the runtime motor kernel. Emergent stall ≈ 5.7 pN/arm and unloaded v ≈ 140 nm/s (below).

### 1.3 (MED) Crosslinker — regime-aware **binding-only** ACP with rupture-force justification
- **FF module:** `crosslinker` (α-actinin, filamin; Bell-Evans off-rate).
- **Paper choice:** ACP binds every 7 nm along F-actin at `k_+,ACP = 100 s⁻¹`, **no unbinding from F-actin**. Justification (SNote2 §5): with ≥5–15 crosslinkers per filament (124 in the reference condition, min 6 at lowest density), the α-actinin/F-actin *rupture* force (1.4–80 pN per bond) is swamped by the severing force (200–600 pN), so filaments break before crosslinks let go.
- **Adopt as a validated regime bound, NOT as a replacement for Bell-Evans:** FF should *keep* its mechanistic Bell-Evans `k_off = k_off0·exp(F/F_b)` (our hard rule), but use this paper to (a) set the **per-bond rupture-force scale** `F_b`-neighborhood for α-actinin from the cited 1.4–44 pN (mean 18) / 40–80 pN spectrum (Miyata, Ferrer), and (b) add a **sanity gate**: at native crosslink density, verify FF's emergent crosslink lifetime is long enough that severing dominates unbinding — reproducing the paper's "binding-only is a valid approximation" regime. If FF's crosslinks unbind *before* severing at native density, that flags a mis-set `F_b`/`k_off0`.

### 1.4 (MED) Actin nucleation + barbed-end polymerization with **no depolymerization**
- **FF module:** `filament` growth + `formin`/nucleation.
- **Adopt as scenario knobs:** nucleation `k_n,A = 1.0e-6 µM⁻¹s⁻¹`, barbed-end polymerization `k_+,A = 60 µM⁻¹s⁻¹` (Table S1; text also 60, dossier notes k_+,A=60), no depoly, at fixed `C_A = 10 µM`. Gives a self-assembling cortex from monomer with a **defined assembly-then-contract temporal protocol** (see §4). Not a supersession of FF's formin ratchet — a complementary bulk-nucleation + gated-contraction scenario.

### 1.5 (LOW/context) Membrane–cortex coupling as explicit ACP-membrane bonds
- **FF module:** `clutch` / membrane containment coupling (this is mostly DCM, but FF sees it as the cortex-anchoring boundary).
- **Adopt:** a fraction (`R_C/R_X`) of ACPs carry an attractive harmonic bond to the nearest membrane triangle (normal direction, slides freely tangentially), dissociating beyond `r_0,a` where it feels ~30 pN. This is the fine-grained, tunable cortex-to-membrane clutch — directly usable as FF's cortex anchoring law and the control parameter (`R_C`) that sets cortical tension / detachment.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### 2.1 Overdamped Langevin + FDT noise + cylindrical drag (the FF integrator baseline — cross-check)
- **(S1)** `F_i − ζ_i·(dr_i/dt) + F_i^T = 0` (inertia neglected).
- **(S2)** `⟨F_i^T(t) F_j^T(t')⟩ = (2 k_B T ζ_i δ_ij / Δt)·δ` — fluctuation–dissipation.
- **(S3)** `ζ_i = 3π μ d_i (3 + 2 l_i/d_i) / 5` — **Underhill–Doyle cylindrical per-node drag**. Map: `μ` = medium viscosity, `d_i` = segment diameter, `l_i` = segment length. **Port this drag law directly** — it is the physically-correct per-segment cylindrical drag and is relevant to FF's *grid-drag / crawl-speed* open problem (FF currently sums drag ∝ Nc; a per-segment cylindrical `ζ_i` is the principled form).
- **(S4)** explicit Euler `r_i(t+Δt) = r_i(t) + (1/ζ_i)(F_i + F_i^T)Δt`, `Δt = 1.15e-4 s`. FF's constrained BAOAB is *superior*; use S4 only as a parity oracle, not a downgrade.

### 2.2 NM-II parallel-cluster motor kinetics (Table S2) — port as myosin sanity oracle / kernel
Per-head 3-state cycle with load-dependent transitions (Erdmann–Schwarz):
- states: 0 = unbound, 1 = weakly bound (pre-power-stroke), 2 = post-power-stroke.
- rates (s⁻¹): `k01 = 40`, `k10 = 2`, `k12 = 1000`, `k21 = 1000`, `k20 = 20`.
- binding scales with heads: `k_+,M = 40·N_h s⁻¹`, `N_h = 4` heads/arm, `N_arm = 16`.
- load dependence via force constant `F0,M = 5.04e-12 N` (5.04 pN); step size `s = 7e-9 m`; neck-linker spring `k_m = 1.0e-3 N/m`; free-energy bias to post-power-stroke `E_pp = −60e-21 J`, `E_ext = 0`.
- **Emergent outputs to reproduce:** unloaded velocity `v0 ≈ 140 nm/s`, stall `≈ 5.7 pN per arm`.
- **Map to FF:** `s` → myosin step; `F0,M` → catch-bond force scale; the (k01,k10,k12,k21,k20) set → FF's per-head duty-cycle transition rates; `E_pp` → power-stroke free-energy. Cross-check FF's Hill+Bell closed forms against the *emergent* f–v curve and catch-bond lifetime this scheme produces.

### 2.3 Harmonic filament potentials (extension + bending) — direct calibration
- **(S5)** `U_s = ½ κ_s (l − l0)²`; **(S6)** `U_b = ½ κ_b (θ − θ0)²`, `θ0 = 0`.
- Actin: `κ_s,A = 1.69e-2 N/m`, `κ_b,A = 2.64e-19 N·m` → **persistence length ℓ_p ≈ 9 µm** (via `ℓ_p = κ_b·l0/k_BT`, Isambert 1995). See §6 caveat vs FF's 17 µm.
- **(S7)** actin–actin excluded volume: `U_r,A = ½ κ_r,A (r12 − d_A)²` for `r12 < d_A`, else 0; `κ_r,A = 1.69e-3 N/m`, `d_A = 7 nm`. Map to FF's LJ-repulsive EV as a harmonic alternative / cross-check with the correct actin diameter cutoff.

### 2.4 Bell-kinetics detachment ODE (analytic oracle for cortex-membrane unbinding)
- **(Eq. 4/5)** `dρ_H^b/dt = k_on(ρ_H − ρ_H^b)(ρ_A − ρ_H^b − ρ_N^b) − k_off0·ρ_H^b·exp[(r0/k_BT)(ΔP/ρ_H^b)]`.
  - `ρ_H^b` = surface density of actin-bound His-α-actinin; force per bond `f = ΔP/ρ_H^b`; Bell factor `exp(r0 f/k_BT)`.
- **(Eq. 6)** critical detachment pressure `ΔP* ≃ 0.7·(k_BT ρ_H / r0)·ln(ρ_A / K_m^{2D})`, `K_m^{2D} = k_off0/k_on`.
- **(Eq. 7)** critical cortical tension `γ_C^{D*} ≃ 0.35·(k_BT ρ_H R / r0)·ln(ρ_A / K_m^{2D})`; **(Eq. 8)** `γ_C^{D*} ∝ R_C`.
- **Use as validation oracle** (not runtime): a coupled FF-cortex + membrane run should emergently reproduce `γ_C^{D*} ∝ R_C` (linear scaling of detachment cortical tension with coupling strength). This is a clean, closed-form acceptance target of exactly the kind our architecture sanctions.

### 2.5 Young–Laplace cortical-tension relation
- **(Eq. 2/3)** `ΔP = 2(γ_m + γ_c)/R − 2γ_{m0}/R0 ≃ 2γ_c/R` near threshold (γ_c ≫ γ_m, confirmed in silico).
- FF/DCM already use `ΔP = 2γ/R`; this paper confirms the cortex-tension-dominated regime and quantifies `γ_c` (§5), a lever for the γ-floor problem.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenario)

**Scenario "confined cortex → gated contraction → bleb" (FF⊗DCM coupled run):**
1. **Geometry:** spherical membrane shell, diameter 8 µm (Nc-cheap) or 16 µm validated; FF native target scales to MCF7 R=7.5 µm (Nc≈266k). `V0 = 2.68e-16 m³` (8 µm sphere), triangulated membrane `A0 = 2.12e-15 m²/triangle`.
2. **2D cortex vs 3D bulk (a single toggle):** assemble network in a shell `0.9·r_MB → r_MB` (27.1% of volume, "2D" cortical) — *or* fill the whole interior ("3D" volume-spanning). Experimentally set by methylcellulose (0.2% w/v) presence/absence. **This is a ready FF scenario knob** and it changes emergent behavior (3D bulk myosin randomly oriented → inward antagonistic force → multiple blebs).
3. **Temporal gating (physiological-baseline compliant):** polymerize/crosslink the cortex FIRST (myosin off), THEN activate myosin (mimics ZIPK gating). Membrane frozen during assembly, released at contraction onset. Matches FF's `--from-resting` philosophy: build the physiological cortex, then perturb.
4. **Two independent control parameters:**
   - `R_C = C_H/C_A` = actin–membrane coupling strength (fraction of ACPs bonded to membrane). Primary lever: raising R_C shifts mode No-deformation → Weak → Single-bleb, saturating.
   - `R_X = C_X/C_A` = total crosslinker/actin (network connectivity). Secondary; sets rupture-vs-detachment balance.
   - Reference point: `R_X = R_C = 0.08`, `R_M = C_M/C_A = 0.01`.
5. **Local perturbation:** locally raising/lowering R_C or R_X deterministically positions a bleb (R_X-perturb 100% efficient, R_C 50% at perturbation fraction f_p=0.16) — an FF symmetry-breaking / polarity scenario **without any biochemical cue**, directly relevant to FF's confinement-driven self-organization open problem.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

1. **Purified in-vitro, not living cortex.** Single crosslinker (α-actinin only), single motor (smooth-muscle myosin), no Arp2/3, no formin ratchet, no membrane PIP2 (replaced by engineered His–NiNTA clutch). Adopt the *mechanisms and force laws*, not the minimal composition — FF must keep its full compartment stack (Arp2/3, formin, filamin, microtubules) ON at physiological values. Use this paper's numbers as **calibration/oracle**, not as a stripped-down production config.
2. **Membrane-anchor is engineered (His-NiNTA >100 pN), not native.** The `R_C` coupling maps to FF's integrin/talon cortex-membrane clutch only phenomenologically; keep FF's mechanistic catch-bond clutch, borrow the *tunable coupling-strength* concept and the detachment analytic oracle.
3. **Viscosity mismatch.** Paper's model `μ = 8.6 Pa·s` (their explicit choice, ≈cytoplasm, ~10× the buffer they actually used experimentally). FF physiological MCF7 cytoplasm is ~65 Pa·s — **do not adopt 8.6**; keep 65. The *drag law* (S3 cylindrical) is portable; the viscosity value is not.
4. **ℓ_p = 9 µm vs 17 µm.** Their κ_b,A gives a softer actin than FF's canonical Gittes 17 µm. Keep FF's 17 µm physiological default; log the discrepancy, surface to PI — do NOT retune FF stiffness to match.
5. **Deterministic severing at fixed 300 pN is a proxy for a rate process.** Our hard rule prefers force-*accelerated* stochastic severing (Bell-form) anchored to the 200–600 pN spread, verifying the deterministic limit is recovered — do not hard-code a single magic 300 pN threshold tuned to make a bleb gate pass.
6. **"No ACP unbinding" is a regime approximation, valid only at high crosslink density.** Do not import it as a mechanism — keep FF's Bell-Evans crosslinker off-rate; use the paper only to (a) set `F_b` from the 1.4–80 pN rupture spectrum and (b) add the sanity gate that severing dominates unbinding at native density.
7. **Explicit Euler, Δt=1.15e-4 s** is inferior to FF's constrained BAOAB — use S1–S4 only as a parity/cross-check oracle, never as a runtime downgrade. Confirm FF's larger stable BAOAB timestep still resolves the severing/motor kinetics (CFL must include the fast k12/k21=1000 s⁻¹ motor transitions).
8. **2D-shell "cortex" is 27.1% of volume, an assembly convenience.** FF's native cortex is a true thin shell at R=7.5 µm; map the "2D vs 3D" toggle carefully — the *finding* (bulk-myosin random orientation → multiple blebs) is the transferable physics, not the 27.1% number.
