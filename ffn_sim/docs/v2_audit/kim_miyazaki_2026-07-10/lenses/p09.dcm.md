# DCM lens — p09

**Paper:** Jo et al. 2025, *Reciprocal folding dynamics in cellular networks at the stroma–basement membrane interface*, Acta Biomaterialia 201:360–371, DOI 10.1016/j.actbio.2025.05.069.

**One-paragraph orientation for DCM.** This is a **tissue-scale continuum elastostatics** paper (Ansys FEM, linear-elastic isotropic bodies) coupled to an in-vitro reconstituted three-layer tissue (epithelium / engineered nm-thick collagen-I basement membrane / fibroblast-collagen stroma). Its physics is a **stiffness-contrast-driven elastic buckling instability** — a thin stiff BM on a soft stroma, loaded by cell contractility imposed as a **constant Neumann boundary stress**, evaginates or invaginates depending on E_BM/E_Str. **None of this is a direct DCM parity target**, and its central modeling move (contractility-as-boundary-stress, no explicit FA/cortex) is exactly the *lumped/proxy* option our hard rule forbids. What is genuinely usable at our single-cell fine-grained scale is narrow: (a) three measured stiffness anchors (cell 8.6 kPa, stroma 354 Pa, BM 756 kPa) and a contractile-stress scale (200 kPa) that DCM's cortex/nucleus/ECM moduli must be consistent with; (b) a well-posed **cell-on-stiff-sheet / cell-in-soft-ECM geometry** that DCM could reproduce fine-grained as a spreading/traction scenario; (c) a set of macroscopic validation numbers (evagination 1.91 µm, E_Cell optimum ~14 kPa, condensate ~70 µm, 90 µm coupling threshold). Honest DCM relevance: **low-to-moderal (~2/5)** — conceptual scaffolding and a few anchors, not new mechanism to port. I flag explicitly below where adopting the paper's own method would *violate* our fine-grained principle.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

Ranked by genuine DCM value. Most of the paper's mechanism is either FF-side (traction/clutch/fiber-remodeling) or forbidden lumping. Kept honest.

1. **Cell-on-stiff-membrane / substrate-stiffness-contrast scenario for the contact + spreading modules (conceptual, worth reproducing fine-grained).**
   The paper's core result — a contractile cell body under/on a **stiff thin elastic sheet on a soft substrate** produces an out-of-plane deformation whose *sign and magnitude* are set by the stiffness ratio E_stiff/E_soft — is a clean, well-instrumented test bed. DCM already has a node-face penalty contact (Ericson) and can pressurize/contract a cell. **DCM improvement:** add a **substrate/BM as an elastic boundary with a tunable modulus** (not rigid wall, not lumped stress) and let the cell's *own* cortex-γ + turgor + FA traction deform it — then measure the out-of-plane deflection. This is the mechanistic inverse of the paper: instead of imposing 200 kPa as a BC, we let the fine-grained cortex generate the traction and read whether the emergent deflection lands near the paper's phase behavior. **Why it beats current DCM:** DCM's substrate is effectively rigid; a compliant graded substrate unlocks durotaxis and folding responses the current wall cannot express, and does so without a lumped force.

2. **Stiffness-contrast phase-boundary intuition for the aggregate/confinement modules.**
   The paper gives a concrete map: soft interior + stiff thin shell + internal contractility → out-of-plane buckling. Our aggregate has the same ingredients (soft cytoplasm interior, stiff actomyosin cortex shell, contractile σ). The paper's finding that **evagination peaks at an intermediate shell stiffness (E_BM ≈ 10⁶ Pa) and an intermediate cell stiffness (E_Cell ≈ 14 kPa), and vanishes at zero condensate volume**, is a qualitative sanity constraint for DCM: cortical-shell buckling/folding should be *non-monotonic* in cortex stiffness and *volume-gated*. **Why it helps:** gives an independent, literature-grounded expectation for when a pressurized DCM cell should wrinkle vs. stay smooth — useful as a bleb/wrinkle sanity check, not a fit.

3. **Volume-gating of surface deformation (small but correct addition to the enclosed-volume constraint).**
   Fig S4B: evagination **vanishes when the condensate occupies zero physical volume** — i.e. the deformation is driven by a *volumetric* contractile inclusion, not a surface pinch. This reinforces DCM's physiological-baseline stance that turgor/enclosed-volume Π₀ must be ON: a force-free (zero effective volume) cell cannot fold its shell. No new code — a validation of an existing DCM invariant.

**Explicitly NOT adopting (violates hard rules):**
- **Contractility as a constant boundary stress P_Cell = 200 kPa** (paper's §2.15). This is the lumped/proxy option our PI rule forbids — DCM must generate traction from cortex γ + myosin + FA clutch, never prescribe a uniform Neumann stress. Keep 200 kPa only as an *order-of-magnitude consistency check* on emergent cortical stress, not as an input.
- **Linear-elastic isotropic cell body (E_Cell single modulus).** DCM's cell is a compartmentalized surface-mesh with turgor + cortex + nucleus; collapsing it to one Young's modulus is a coarse-graining we already rejected.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port

The paper writes **no dynamical governing PDE/ODE**; its physics is numerical linear elastostatics plus two image-analysis definitions. Little to port; transcribed for completeness with DCM mapping.

**(Implicit governing physics — linear isotropic elastostatics, paper §2.15.)**
```
∇·σ + f = 0        (quasi-static equilibrium, each body)
σ = C(E, ν) : ε     (Hooke, linear isotropic)
BC:  u = 0 on stroma base (Dirichlet);  σ·n = P_Cell on condensate faces (Neumann)
```
- **DCM mapping:** DCM does NOT solve this continuum system — its analogue is the discrete surface-mesh force balance `Σ F_node = γ-tension + κ_m-bending + K_A-area + Π-turgor + contact + FA = 0` integrated by Warp. The paper's `E` moduli map to DCM **material stiffnesses**: E_Cell ↔ effective cell stiffness set by cortex γ + turgor + nucleus E_nuc; a substrate/BM `E_BM` would be a *new* DCM field (elastic substrate modulus). **Do not** import the Neumann `P_Cell` — replace it with emergent cortex traction.

**(Eq. 1) Alignment percentage** (paper §2.8, an image metric, not a force law):
```
Alignment% = (1 − D_{A−B}/180) × 100 %      D_{A−B} = orientation difference in degrees
```
- **DCM use:** reusable as a *DCM diagnostic* — quantify co-orientation of cortical stress/fold ridges vs. an imposed axis (confinement, substrate anisotropy). Purely a readout, no dynamics.

**(Eq. 2) Focal-adhesion polarity** (paper §2.9):
```
Polarity = (PAX_whole − PAX_DAPI) / PAX_DAPI
```
- **DCM use:** if DCM's FA/junction module tracks per-node adhesion density, this defines a **peripheral-vs-center polarity observable** (leading-edge FA enrichment). Maps to DCM FA state at nodes; a readout, not a mechanism.

**(Wavelength index)** = (fitted fold length)/(image width) — folding wavelength readout; DCM analogue is the dominant spatial wavelength of cortical-mesh wrinkling (FFT of node radial deviation). Diagnostic only.

Net: **0 mechanistic force laws to port.** The paper is force-law-free by construction.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

Two DCM-usable scenarios (both would be **new** DCM scenarios, run at native full-compartment scale, not the paper's continuum shortcut):

1. **Cell / small-aggregate on a compliant stiff sheet ("BM-on-stroma" fine-grained analogue).**
   - Geometry: a DCM cell (or N≈400 aggregate, our scale) adhered on a **thin elastic sheet** (modulus ~E_BM) that itself rests on a **soft elastic substrate** (modulus ~E_Str). Paper's stack: 2-µm BM over a 200×400×600 µm stroma; scale down to one-cell footprint.
   - Load: DCM's **own cortex γ + FA clutch traction** (NOT a prescribed 200 kPa). Base of substrate fixed (Dirichlet), matching the paper's fixed stroma base.
   - Read: out-of-plane deflection of the sheet under the cell (paper's "vertical deformation" probe). Expect sign to flip with the E_stiff/E_soft ratio.
   - Value: gives DCM a durotaxis / substrate-folding capability and a direct comparison to Fig 2F phase behavior.

2. **Pressurized-shell wrinkling / buckling sanity scenario (uses only existing DCM compartments).**
   - Geometry: single native DCM cell, full compartments ON (`--from-resting`, gravity, membrane, nucleus, microtubules), pressurized to Π₀.
   - Protocol: sweep cortex stiffness / γ and cell volume; look for the paper's qualitative signatures — **non-monotonic** surface deformation vs. shell stiffness, and **deformation → 0 as enclosed volume → 0**.
   - No new code; validates DCM against the paper's Fig S4B/S4C shape.

The paper's in-vitro protocol (spin-cast collagen eBM, vitrification, layered tissue, blebbistatin 10 µM contraction-off control) is **not** DCM-reproducible — it is wet-lab reconstitution. The blebbistatin control does map to a DCM "myosin/contraction OFF" run as a mechanism-attribution check (folding should vanish when active cortex tension → passive turgor only).

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Regime mismatch — tissue continuum vs. single-cell fine-grained.** The paper models **multicellular condensates (~70 µm, ~10 cells) as continuum linear-elastic bodies** via Ansys, at tissue scale. Our DCM is a single-cell (R≈7.5 µm) surface-mesh. Its moduli and 1.91 µm deflection are *emergent tissue-scale* quantities; importing them as single-cell parameters is a category error. Use only as bounds/scenario design.
- **Forbidden method — contractility as boundary stress.** The paper's 200 kPa Neumann BC and single E_Cell modulus are precisely the lumped/proxy shortcuts our PI hard rule rejects. Adopting them would violate the fine-grained principle. Keep contractility *emergent* from cortex γ + myosin + FA; use 200 kPa only as a "does our emergent cortical stress land in the right decade" gate.
- **Cell type — 3T3/HaCaT/fibroblast, not MCF7.** E_Cell 8.6 kPa and the stroma/BM moduli come from fibroblast-collagen tissue and epithelial lines. Our physiological baseline is MCF7-like; do not overwrite MCF7 setpoints (γ 0.15/1.5 mN/m, η 65 Pa·s, Π₀ 40 Pa) with these. Surface to PI before any KB parameter change.
- **Static/quasi-static, no time integration.** The FEM is elastostatic — no viscosity, no dynamics, no timescale. It cannot inform DCM's η-driven dynamics or the compaction *rate*; only end-state shape/sign. The 1-h / 24-h timescales come from *experiment*, not the model.
- **In-vitro reconstitution not reproducible in DCM.** eBM fabrication, vitrification, blebbistatin, PIV, FN-FRET are wet-lab; only the blebbistatin "contraction-OFF" logic maps to a DCM control run.
- **How to adapt without breaking the physiological-baseline rule.** If the compliant-substrate/BM scenario is built: initialize the substrate/BM at a *physiological* modulus (a real BM ~0.1–1 MPa, a real stroma ~100 Pa–1 kPa), keep the cell at its full MCF7 baseline with turgor and cortex ON, and let deflection emerge — never prescribe the load. Treat all numbers here as targets/bounds, not fit points; register verified-DOI values as SourceEvidence with the "3T3/tissue-scale, not MCF7" note attached.

**Bottom line for DCM:** a conceptual scaffold (stiffness-contrast folding, volume-gating, an emergent-traction-on-compliant-substrate scenario) plus a handful of order-of-magnitude anchors and one corroborating timescale for the compaction-rate problem. No force law or equation to port; no default to supersede. Relevance ~2/5.
