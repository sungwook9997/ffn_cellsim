# FF lens — p21

**Paper:** Yadav, Banerjee, Tabatabai, Kovar, Kim, Banerjee, Murrell (2019), *Filament Nucleation Tunes Mechanical Memory in Active Polymer Networks*, Adv. Funct. Mater. 29, 1905243. DOI 10.1002/adfm.201905243.

**One-line for FF:** A purified, membrane-nucleated, quasi-2D "model cortex" (mDia1 formin tethered to a lipid bilayer, limiting shared G-actin pool, methylcellulose crowding) shows that **formin nucleation density is a single control knob** that non-monotonically sets filament length, network density, turnover time, and — at an intermediate critical density — traps **polymerization-induced bending energy 100× above thermal**, producing self-templating vortex defects. This is directly on FF's turf: it is exactly the formin-nucleated cortex FF builds, and it gives FF (a) a mechanistic nucleation→length control law we currently fake with a static exponential length distribution, (b) a polymerization-force / growth-pressure buckling channel FF does not yet have, and (c) a stack of in-vitro validation numbers (elongation rates, τ_turnover, bending energies, l_p, mesh-size trend) at OUR native single-cell scale.

Reality check on scope: this is a **purified in-vitro quasi-2D reconstitution with NO myosin as driver and NO explicit crosslinker** (entanglement + MC depletion only). Its stress is **polymerization/growth pressure**, not motor contraction. So it is a validation + mechanism source for FF's *assembly/nucleation/turnover/passive-network* physics, NOT for FF's myosin contraction physics. Adopt it as such.

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

### M1 — Finite shared G-actin monomer pool → emergent length control (HIGH value)
**FF module:** filament nucleation + barbed-end growth kernel (currently `project-ff-filament-length-growth`: exponential length_dist 1–10 µm + barbed-end ratchet, KB-3.18/KB-3.6).
**Current FF mechanism:** filament lengths are drawn from a *prescribed* exponential distribution and grown by a barbed-end ratchet. The length distribution is an **imposed input**, not emergent — a lumped proxy.
**Paper's mechanism (mechanistic replacement):** conserve total actin, `Σ_i L_i·ρ_sub + [G]_free = N_p` (const). Every barbed end's elongation rate is proportional to the *instantaneous free monomer concentration* `[G]_free`, which all filaments share. As the pool depletes, elongation self-limits → mean length and number emerge from `(N_nucleator, N_pool)` rather than being dialed in. **Why it beats current FF:** it removes an imposed distribution (a magic input) and replaces it with a conservation law + a diffusion-limited rate — fully mechanistic, no fitting. It also *automatically* reproduces the biphasic growth (fast R1 while pool is full → slow R2 as pool depletes) and makes filament length respond to nucleator density the way the cell actually controls it. Grid-invariant (conservation of a scalar pool), so it passes the magic-number block.

### M2 — Formin two-mode processive elongation + length-triggered release (HIGH value)
**FF module:** formin (mDia1) processive barbed-end ratchet.
**Current FF:** processive elongation ratchet (v0≈0.623 µm/s from `project-ff-active-movement-pieces`), no explicit release/recycle.
**Paper's mechanism:** a formin bound to a barbed end elongates **fast (~150 subunits/s)** while processive; upon reaching a processivity limit (~100 µm here, but see risk R2) it **releases**, after which that filament elongates **slowly (~10 subunits/s)** as bare profilin-actin, and the freed formin **re-nucleates** a new filament. This is a two-state kinetic scheme (bound-fast / released-slow) + a nucleator-recycling loop. **Why it beats current FF:** (i) it makes nucleator number a *conserved, recyclable* resource that competes for the shared pool — the actual mechanism of cortex filament-number homeostasis; (ii) the fast/slow switch is a real measured kinetic fact FF should encode rather than a single constant velocity; (iii) release-length ↔ membrane-detachment gives FF an emergent "which filaments stay membrane-anchored" rule (longer filaments detach more) that matters for cortex-membrane coupling.

### M3 — Polymerization growth-pressure buckling channel (HIGH value — new physics for FF)
**FF module:** filament bending beam + excluded-volume (LJ) + a NEW growth-pressure term.
**Current FF:** filaments bend elastically (κ, l_p) and repel via LJ, and grow at barbed ends, but growth does **not** generate a mechanical force against the surrounding network — polymerization is kinematic (adds a bead) not dynamic (pushes).
**Paper's mechanism:** continued monomer addition against a sterically hindering network builds a **density-dependent growth pressure P(ρ)** (increasing in local filament density). When P exceeds the filament's Euler bending threshold (set by L and κ), the filament **buckles**; the buckled shape then **templates** further growth (grows while preserving curvature) → stored non-thermal bending energy. **Why it matters for FF:** this is a genuine, mechanistic force channel FF is missing and one of FF's listed open problems is "buckling/condensation symmetry-breaking." It gives FF a route to spontaneous curvature/vortex formation *without myosin* — a clean, motor-free symmetry-breaking test. Mechanistically it is already half-there in FF: FF has LJ excluded volume (the steric hindrance) and bending κ (the buckling resistance); what's missing is a **compressive barbed-end polymerization force** applied when the barbed end is sterically blocked. Implement as: barbed-end growth attempts a monomer insertion; if the target site overlaps (LJ) the growth stalls and instead delivers a longitudinal compressive force f_poly along the filament axis (stall force ~ few pN, see §3), which the bending elastica resolves into buckling. Fully fine-grained, no lumped pressure field needed at our scale.

### M4 — Turnover as assembly-limited (not disassembly-limited) (MEDIUM value)
**FF module:** filament turnover / fragmentation / depolymerization.
**Current FF:** turnover is typically imposed via a depolymerization/severing rate.
**Paper's insight:** with a *constant* basal disassembly rate, the **turnover time τ still varies 7×** (1.7 → 21.8 min) purely because **assembly** is throttled by pool depletion — turnover is set by how fast material can be *renewed*, which is assembly-limited when the monomer pool is finite. **Why it matters:** tells FF that to get realistic turnover-time *variation* across cortex states, it should not tune a severing rate (that would be fitting) — it should let the finite pool (M1) modulate assembly and read τ off the emergent length-renewal rate. Turnover becomes a *derived* observable, not an input.

### M5 — Templating / mechanical memory as an emergent observable (MEDIUM — validation-facing)
Not a new force, but a **regime FF should be able to reproduce and measure**: at intermediate nucleation, bending energy accumulates instead of relaxing (τ_relax decouples from τ_turnover). Once FF has M1+M3, FF should exhibit and quantify this: measure τ_relax (intensity/shape autocorrelation) vs τ_turnover (length-renewal) and confirm the ratio peaks at intermediate nucleation. This is a stringent, motor-free integration test of FF's assembly+bending+excluded-volume stack.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

### L1 — Shared-pool conservation + diffusion-limited elongation
```
Conservation:   Σ_i L_i(t) · ρ_sub + G_free(t) = N_p      (total actin fixed)
Elongation:     dL_i/dt = δ · k_on^eff · G_free(t) · s_i(t)   −  δ · k_off^depoly
  where s_i = 1 (formin-bound, fast) or (v_slow/v_fast) (released, slow)
```
- `ρ_sub` = subunits per unit length = **370 subunits/µm** (canonical F-actin: 2.7 nm rise/monomer, two-start helix). Map to FF: `ρ_sub = 1/(2.7e-3 µm) ≈ 370 /µm`.
- `δ` = subunit length increment = **2.7 nm**.
- `G_free` = FF global scalar state (one reduction over all filament segment counts per step).
- Map `dL_i/dt` to FF barbed-end ratchet: FF already inserts beads at the barbed end; gate the insertion *rate* on `G_free` instead of a constant v0.

### L2 — Formin two-mode elongation (piecewise)
```
v_elong(filament) = 150 subunits/s   if formin-bound (processive)
                  =  10 subunits/s   if released (bare profilin-actin)
Release event:    when L_i ≥ L_release  → detach formin, filament→slow mode,
                  formin freed → nucleate new filament at membrane
```
- 150 subunits/s × 2.7 nm = **0.405 µm/s** formin-bound elongation velocity.
- 10 subunits/s × 2.7 nm = **0.027 µm/s** released elongation velocity.
- Map to FF: `state ∈ {BOUND, FREE}` per filament; a `formin_pool` counter that decrements on nucleation, increments on release.

### L3 — Bending energy of a curved filament arc (already FF-native — use as validation readout)
```
E_bend = (κ / 2) · (L / R²),     κ = k_B T · l_p
```
- FF already has κ per filament (bending beam). This is the SI Eq. S2 estimator. Use it as a **measurement protocol**: from FF filament node positions, fit local radius of curvature R over arcs, histogram E_bend, compare to paper's 10⁻²⁰–10⁻¹⁹ J.
- Sanity: with l_p=15 µm, κ = 4.1e-21 J × 15e-6 m = 6.2e-26 J·m. For L=10 µm, R=2 µm: E = 6.2e-26 × 10e-6/(2e-6)² = 6.2e-26 × 2.5e6 = 1.55e-19 J ✓ (matches the intermediate-nucleation datum — confirms the paper's own numbers and validates FF's κ if reproduced).

### L4 — Frank director-field distortion energy (optional nematic diagnostic)
```
f = ½ K₁₁ (∇·n̂)²  +  ½ K₃₃ ( n̂ × (∇×n̂) )²      [splay + bend]
```
- Not a force to add to FF; a **coarse-grained diagnostic** to compute FROM FF filament orientations to detect vortex/defect organization. K₃₃ ≈ **1.38–4.6 pN** is the target the FF-derived field energy should land in if defects form.

### L5 — Growth-pressure buckling (new FF channel, fine-grained form)
Paper uses a continuum P(ρ); at FF's fine-grained scale, implement mechanistically instead:
```
At each barbed-end growth attempt:
  if insertion site is sterically free (no LJ overlap):  insert bead (grow)
  else (blocked by network):  filament stalls; apply axial compressive
       force  f_poly ≤ f_stall  at the barbed end, directed along filament axis
Euler buckling threshold:   f_crit = π² κ / L²  = π² k_B T l_p / L²
  → buckle when  f_poly > f_crit
Templating: once buckled, continued growth adds beads along the curved
            contour → curvature preserved (emergent, no extra rule needed).
```
- `f_stall` (polymerization stall force) ≈ **1–9 pN per filament** (Footer/Kovar 2007 give ~1 pN single filament; formin bundles / multi-filament can reach higher). Set at physiological single-filament value; do NOT tune to make buckling appear.
- Check: f_crit for L=10 µm = π²×6.2e-26/(10e-6)² = 6.1e-15 N = **0.006 pN** → long filaments buckle at trivially small force (consistent with paper: long floppy filaments curve easily). For L=1 µm: 0.6 pN — comparable to stall force, so short filaments resist (consistent with paper: high-density short filaments stay straight). This monotone-in-L crossover is exactly the paper's non-monotonic-in-nucleation story once length is tied to nucleation via M1. Strong internal consistency → safe to port.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce (FF scenario)

**Scenario "FF-nucleation-sweep" (motor-free cortex assembly):**
- **Geometry:** quasi-2D membrane-tethered layer. FF analog: the cortex shell just inside the membrane, with formin nucleators anchored to the membrane surface (FF already has membrane containment + cortex). Can also run a flat-patch 2D version for direct in-vitro comparison.
- **Components ON at physiological baseline:** membrane, formin nucleators (anchored), finite G-actin pool, filament bending κ, LJ excluded volume, turgor. Myosin and crosslinkers **OFF** for the pure assembly test (this matches the paper and isolates the polymerization-force physics) — but note this is a *diagnostic* scenario, not a full-physiology production run; the full-compartment native run keeps everything on.
- **Control parameter:** nucleator surface density, swept low / intermediate / high (analogous to 10/100/1000 nM), at **fixed total actin pool**.
- **Duration:** ~1 h simulated assembly (the paper's polymerization window); FF should see R1 fast / R2 slow growth crossover (~20 min) emerge from pool depletion.
- **Native scale:** run at the native 38000-filament cortex (Nc≈266k) — the sweep is over *how nucleation partitions the same monomer budget into few-long vs many-short filaments*, which is exactly a native-scale question and must not be judged on a coarse cortex (coarse lacks the density for the buckling/templating transition — would give a phantom).

**Predicted FF outputs to log:** filament count vs t, ⟨L⟩ vs t, ⟨L⟩ vs nucleator number (log-log decreasing), mesh size vs nucleator density, per-arc E_bend histogram, τ_turnover (length-renewal), τ_relax (shape autocorrelation), τ_relax/τ_turnover vs nucleation (expect peak at intermediate).

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **R1 — Purified in-vitro, no myosin driver, no explicit crosslinker.** The paper's stored stress is **polymerization/growth pressure**, and its "network" is entanglement + methylcellulose depletion, not α-actinin/filamin crosslinks. So this paper validates FF's *assembly + passive-bending + excluded-volume* physics, NOT FF's myosin contraction or crosslinker catch-bond physics. Do not use its τ or energies to validate contraction. Adaptation: run the nucleation-sweep scenario with crosslinkers/myosin OFF as a *diagnostic isolate*, then re-add them for the physiological production run and confirm the isolate's mechanisms still behave.

- **R2 — 100 µm formin processivity length is an in-vitro/model number.** In a 7.5 µm-radius MCF7 cortex, filaments cannot be 100 µm. The paper's L_release=100 µm is a purified-mDia1 processivity limit; in the crowded cell, formin processivity and filament length are far shorter (~1–10 µm, matching FF's KB-3.18 exponential 1–10 µm). **Port the mechanism (length-triggered release + recycle) but set L_release to the physiological cell value**, not 100 µm. This keeps the emergent length distribution in FF's already-validated 1–10 µm band.

- **R3 — Quasi-2D membrane assay vs 3D cortex shell.** The vortex/templating defects are a 2D phenomenon (filaments crowded into a plane by MC). FF's cortex is a curved 3D shell. The buckling/templating physics (M3) still applies in 3D, but the specific vortex-lattice organization and Frank-nematic diagnostic (L4) are 2D-flavored — treat the 2D defect morphology as suggestive, and validate the *energy accumulation* (scalar, dimension-robust) rather than the exact defect geometry.

- **R4 — Growth-pressure force is not yet in FF; adding it must not become a magic knob.** f_stall must be set to the literature single-filament polymerization stall (~1 pN, Footer/Kovar), NOT tuned until buckling appears. The Euler threshold f_crit=π²κ/L² is derived (grid-invariant), so buckling emerges from real κ + real f_stall + real steric overlap — passes the magic-number block only if all three are physiological. If buckling won't appear at physiological f_stall, that is a *finding to surface*, not a cue to raise f_stall.

- **R5 — Native-scale mandate.** The intermediate-nucleation templating transition is a **density-dependent collective** effect. A coarse cortex (~250–1000 filaments) will not have the density to buckle/template and will give a phantom (no transition, or a spurious one from too-few filaments). Any conclusion about nucleation→memory MUST be run on the native 38000-filament `--from-resting --microtubules` cell. Use coarse only as a labelled non-authoritative smoke test.

- **R6 — τ measured on a shared finite pool assumes closed actin.** In a living cell the G-actin pool is buffered (thymosin-β4/profilin) and replenished, so absolute τ values differ. Port the *mechanism* (assembly-limited turnover) but expect FF's absolute τ to depend on its physiological pool + buffering model; use the paper's τ as an order-of-magnitude and trend target, not a hard number to fit.

- **R7 — v0 reconciliation.** The formin-bound 0.405 µm/s here differs from FF's recorded piece1 v0≈0.623 µm/s. Both are literature-anchored (different conditions/isoforms). Surface to PI for which to carry; do not silently overwrite a recorded value (cf. the F*=7pN "keep as-recorded" precedent).
