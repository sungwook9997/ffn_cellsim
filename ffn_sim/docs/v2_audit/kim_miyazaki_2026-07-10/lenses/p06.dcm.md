# DCM lens — p06

**Paper:** Li, Kim & Szymanski (2019), "Multi-scale regulation of cell branching: Modeling morphogenesis," *Developmental Biology* 451:40–52. DOI 10.1016/j.ydbio.2018.12.004. **Type: review** (no original model/data).

**Bottom line for DCM: LOW relevance.** This is a *plant* cell-wall / cortical-microtubule review co-authored by Taeyoon Kim. Its mechanical regime — a stiff cellulose-fiber-reinforced cell wall under MPa turgor, growing by irreversible plastic expansion — is categorically different from an animal MCF7 cell (mN/m actomyosin cortical tension, ~40 Pa osmotic turgor, no load-bearing wall). It transcribes **no closed-form governing equations, no force laws, and no animal-cell parameters**. What is transferable is a *modeling pattern* (turgor-shell → relax → remesh → grow) and, weakly, a structural template for anisotropic surface material properties. Nothing here should be registered as an MCF7 validation target, and nothing supersedes a current DCM default. Adopting anything requires re-deriving values at our scale — the paper gives none.

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

Only one is even conceptually applicable; the rest are explicitly out-of-regime and listed to close the door on them.

**(M1) Growth as an iterated `load → viscoelastic-relax → remesh → reset-material` cycle (Yanagisawa 2015 FE loop, Fig. 4b).**
- **DCM module:** the remesh + volume/surface update path (`dcm/` surface-mesh solver + remesh operator).
- **Change:** DCM already has (a) an enclosed-volume/turgor constraint, (b) a node-face contact solver, and (c) remesh. The paper's centerpiece is the *composition order*: apply pressure load, let the shell relax to mechanical equilibrium under viscoelasticity, remesh the deformed geometry, then **reset the local material properties at corresponding surface locations to their reference values**, and repeat. This "material reset after remesh" step is the clean, mechanistic way to emulate *irreversible* (plastic-like) growth without a fitted growth law — the plasticity emerges from repeated relax-remesh, not a tuned strain-rate constant.
- **Why it beats DCM now:** DCM has no first-class surface-growth / cell-enlargement operator (relevant to the *division* and *volume-doubling-before-cytokinesis* open problem). Today DCM would need an ad-hoc rule to grow surface area. This loop gives a magic-number-free recipe: growth increment = whatever the physiological turgor + material relaxation produces per cycle, with remesh preserving mesh quality. **Caveat:** for an animal cell the "material reset" must reset *cortex tension γ and area-elastic rest-area*, not a cellulose-fiber modulus — see §6.

**(M2) [REJECT] Anisotropic fiber-reinforced-matrix surface constitutive law.** The wall model is matrix modulus E0 + anisotropic hyperelastic fibers (modulus k1, angular dispersion κ) aligned by cortical microtubules. This is a genuine mechanistic anisotropy source, but it models a *cellulose cell wall*, which MCF7 does not have. DCM's membrane K_A + cortex γ are isotropic-surface by design and physiologically correct for an animal cell. Do **not** graft fiber anisotropy onto the MCF7 cortex — it would inject a non-physiological load-bearing wall (violates physiological-baseline rule). Retain only as inspiration *if* a future stress-fiber-anisotropy surface term is ever wanted, and only then sourced from an animal SF paper.

**(M3) [REJECT for DCM] Cortical microtubule-array self-organization** (zippering below a critical angle, collision-induced catastrophe above it, katanin crossover severing, boundary/curvature catastrophe). This is filament-scale dynamics — if anything it is an FF concern, not DCM — and it is *plant* cortical-MT physics. DCM's microtubules are a stiff inclusion/scaffold, not a self-organizing cortical array. No adoption.

## 2. EQUATIONS to port

**None are transcribed in the source.** The paper contains no numbered governing equations. The only formal content is order-of-magnitude scaling and an algorithm:

**(E1) Thin-wall pressure-vessel stress (implied, not written): σ_wall ≈ Π·R / t.**
- Symbol map to DCM: Π → enclosed-volume turgor/osmotic pressure Π₀ (≈40 Pa in DCM, vs 0.5–1 MPa here); R → cell radius (7.5 µm); t → shell thickness (cortex thickness ~200 nm in DCM, vs plant wall ~nm–µm); σ_wall → in-plane surface stress, which in DCM is carried by cortex tension γ via the Young–Laplace relation **γ = ΔP·R/2** (DCM already uses this exact pressure-borne-tension law). So E1 is just the wall-analog of DCM's existing Laplace law and adds nothing new — but it confirms DCM's γ = ΔP·R/2 is the correct animal-cell counterpart to the paper's wall-stress scaling.

**(E2) Growth cycle (algorithmic): shape_{n+1} = Remesh( Relax( shape_n | Π ) ), material_props ← reference.**
- Symbol map: `Relax(·|Π)` → DCM implicit/IPC solve to mechanical equilibrium under turgor Π₀; `Remesh(·)` → DCM remesh operator; `material_props ← reference` → reset per-face rest-area (K_A reference) and per-node γ setpoint after remesh. This is a control-flow recipe, not a PDE.

No bending, area-elastic, or viscoelastic potential is given in closed form to port.

## 4. GEOMETRY / PROTOCOL to reproduce

**(P1) Turgor-shell relax-remesh-grow scenario (transferable as a DCM *protocol*, not a validation case).** Set up a single closed surface mesh at the physiological operating point (Π₀=40 Pa, γ interphase 0.15 mN/m, K_A, κ_m all ON), run the E2 loop, and confirm the mesh grows/relaxes stably across many remesh cycles without material drift. This is a useful **numerical-stability / remesh-robustness harness** for DCM's growth+remesh path — directly relevant to the division open-problem (cell must roughly double surface before cytokinesis). Reproduce the *loop structure*, not the plant shapes.

**(P2) [REJECT] Trichome-branch and root-hair-initiation FE setups** (prolate-ellipse shell, softened/stiffened circular patch → circumferential vs radial stress rings). These are plant-shape scenarios with MPa loads; not DCM scenarios. The root-hair "soft patch → circumferential principal-stress ring, stiff patch → radial star" result (Krupinski 2016, Fig. 5a) is a nice qualitative sanity check that *a local surface-stiffness heterogeneity reorganizes the principal-stress field* — DCM could in principle reproduce that pattern with a K_A heterogeneity as a solver sanity test, but there is no MCF7 motivation and the paper gives only relative/abstract stress-colorbar units (−8.0e0…+1.3e3), so it cannot anchor a quantitative gate.

No aspiration, AFM, aggregate-compaction, division-ring, durotaxis, or confinement protocol is present in this paper.

## 5. VALIDATION TARGETS for DCM observables

**None.** Every quantitative result (wall stress 5.0–12.6 MPa; max principal strain 0.023–0.039; new wall material 3.0–5.3 nm/cycle; turgor 0.5–1 MPa; MT L_p ~mm; branch spacing 120°; growth duration 14 h; volume increase 10–100×) is plant-cell-wall physiology in a mechanical regime ~10³–10⁴× stiffer than an MCF7 cortex. None is a tension/shape-index/division-force/ring-closure/compaction/contact-angle/flow-speed target for a single MCF7-like animal cell. **Do not register any as an MCF7 DCM gate or as SourceEvidence for the animal line.** (They would only ever be relevant to a hypothetical, out-of-scope plant/turgor-wall line.)

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **Regime mismatch is total, not partial.** Plant turgor is MPa; the load is borne by a stiff cellulose *wall*, not an mN/m actomyosin cortex. Any numeric transfer would violate the physiological-baseline rule (would set MCF7 pressure/stiffness ~10⁴× too high). The ONLY safe transfer is the *algorithmic* relax-remesh-grow pattern (M1/E2), executed at DCM's own physiological setpoints (Π₀=40 Pa, γ=0.15 mN/m).
- **"Material reset after remesh" must reset the right quantity.** For the plant model it resets cellulose-fiber modulus; for DCM it must reset **per-face reference rest-area (K_A) and per-node γ setpoint** so remeshing doesn't spuriously inject or dissipate elastic/tension energy. Getting this wrong would corrupt DCM's energy balance — treat it as a conservation-invariant sanity gate (surface energy continuous across a remesh, no work injected).
- **It's a review, not a model paper.** No equations, no timestep, no mesh resolution, no E0/k1/κ values — those live in Yanagisawa 2015 / Krupinski 2016. If the relax-remesh loop is ever pursued seriously, go to those primaries, not this review.
- **Anisotropy temptation.** The fiber-reinforced-matrix wall is a clean anisotropy mechanism; resist grafting it onto the MCF7 cortex. An animal cortex is isotropic-surface at baseline; adding fiber anisotropy without an animal-cell source would be a non-physiological magic mechanism.
- **Kim co-authorship ≠ Kim BD-actin physics.** Do not mistake this for one of Taeyoon Kim's agent-based Brownian-dynamics actin-network papers; the actin content here is ARP2/3 as a *genetic regulator* of branch siting, with no filament mechanics, motor force-velocity, or crosslinker kinetics.

**Net:** keep the relax-remesh-grow loop concept on file for the DCM division / surface-growth open problem; ingest nothing numeric.
