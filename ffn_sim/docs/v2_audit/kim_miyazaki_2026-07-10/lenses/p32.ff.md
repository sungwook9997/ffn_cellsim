# FF lens — p32

**Paper:** Jung, Li, Chaudhuri, Kim (2020), *Nonlinear Elastic and Inelastic Properties of Cells*, J. Biomech. Eng. 142(10):100806. DOI 10.1115/1.4046863. **Type: review** (Taeyoon Kim lab; no new data of its own — 184 refs).

**Bottom line for FF:** This is not a data paper; it is the *design brief* for exactly the fine-grained engine FF is. Its closing gap statement (§5.2, §6) says in plain text that no cell-scale discrete model yet incorporates, together, **rapid F-actin turnover + force-dependent crosslinker unbinding + myosin activity** — that combination is the FF thesis. So its value to FF is (a) a prioritized list of the emergent observables FF must reproduce, (b) a handful of hard structural constants to *check* FF filament parameters against, and (c) mechanistic anchors (which primary papers to port force laws from). Almost no transcribable equations or rate constants live in this text — they live in the primaries it cites, which FF should chase separately (Ferrer α-actinin catch bond, Gardel/Koenderink prestress, Lieleg/Bausch fascin unbinding, Latorre superelasticity).

---

## 1. MECHANISMS to adopt

Ranked by FF impact. Each is a mechanism the review flags as *the molecular origin* of a cell-scale behavior FF cares about, mapped to the FF module that must carry it.

1. **Crosslinker (α-actinin/filamin/fascin) — force-dependent unbinding SETS the viscoelastic frequency.**
   Review §4.2 (Fig. 5(f)): the loss modulus G″(ω) of a crosslinked actin network shows a **minimum at the frequency = the crosslinker unbinding rate**; G′/G″ curves at different crosslinker densities collapse onto one master curve after normalization. This is the single most FF-actionable statement in the paper: FF *already* has Bell–Evans off-rate on explicit crosslinker bonds — this says the emergent G″(ω) dip is the **direct, model-free validation observable** for that off-rate. Beats current FF practice because it turns an internal parameter (k_off0) into a measurable network signature: run FF crosslinked-cortex microrheology, extract G″(ω), confirm the dip sits at k_off0. WHY it beats status quo: no new mechanism needed, but it converts a "trust-me" constant into a falsifiable network-level gate.

2. **F-actin turnover + buckling-induced FRAGMENTATION as the stress-relaxation / viscoplasticity channel.**
   §4.2/§4.3 state that (i) F-actin turnover "substantially reduces and stabilizes network stress" [136–139], and (ii) a computational model showed **fragmentation of F-actins facilitated by buckling produces significant stress relaxation** [140], and (iii) crosslinked networks show *plastic* (non-recoverable) deformation via **force-dependent crosslinker unbinding** [144] — the network cannot recover because turnover + unbinding relax the extended state. FF currently has barbed-end growth + Bell–Evans crosslinker unbinding but (per memory) no explicit **severing/fragmentation channel**. ADD: a buckling-triggered severing rule on cortex filaments (sever probability rises when a segment's compressive load / curvature exceeds a threshold). WHY it beats status quo: it is the mechanistic root of two open FF problems at once — **network contraction/stress relaxation** and **viscoplasticity**, and it is fine-grained (per-segment), not a lumped dashpot. This is the review's explicit "missing feature" call-out for discrete models.

3. **Myosin-II prestress as the resting-state operating point (not a perturbation added later).**
   §4.1.1: in vivo F-actin is *already in the extension mode at rest* because myosin-II prestress pulls filaments taut in cortex and stress fibers [119–121]; reconstituted (myosin-free) networks sit in the low-stiffness bending mode. Directly reinforces the FF **physiological-baseline HARD rule**: the native cortex must be initialized with myosin engaged and filaments pre-tensioned from t=0, otherwise FF sits in the wrong (bending-dominated) branch of the K′(σ) curve and under-reports stiffness. WHY it matters: this is the mechanistic justification for the γ-floor work — the cortex tension γ is a *prestress* quantity, and Fig. 3(d) gives the prestress→differential-modulus curve to validate it against (see §5).

4. **Strain-stiffening = emergent bending→extension crossover (do NOT hard-code a stiffening law).**
   §4.1.1: network stiffening is emergent — at low strain filaments deform in bending (soft), at high strain a growing fraction goes into entropic/enthalpic extension (stiff), giving a highly nonlinear σ(ε). FF's semiflexible bead-rod filament with angular bending κ + stretch modulus **already produces this for free**; the review says the correct fine-grained move is to *let it emerge*, not to fit a differential-modulus law. Connectivity matters: **poorly-crosslinked** networks stay in bending mode even at high strain and don't stiffen — so FF stiffening is a joint test of filament mechanics AND crosslinker density/connectivity.

5. **Microtubule-facilitated stiffening (compression-bearing struts that suppress actin bending).**
   §2.2, §4.1.1: MTs embedded in a crosslinked actin network **suppress F-actin bending and force them into extension → network stiffens** [118]; and actin/IF pinning shortens the MT buckling unit-length, raising MT compressive capacity. FF has microtubules in the native config (`--microtubules`); this says the actin–MT volume-exclusion / cross-link coupling is a real stiffening lever, not decorative. Keep MTs as rigid compression struts (L_p 1–5 mm ≫ cell radius) coupled to actin.

Lower priority / DCM-leaning (note but don't build in FF): superelasticity via cortex fluidization + IF backstop (§4.1.2), poroelastic cytoplasm fluid–solid coupling (§4.4), nuclear lamin-A stiffening crossover at ~3 µm (§4.1.1). These are compartment/continuum behaviors better carried by DCM; FF's role is to supply the cortex actomyosin sub-model.

## 2. FORCE LAWS / KINETIC SCHEMES to port

The review **prints no numbered equations** — it describes forms verbally and points to primaries. What is transcribable as a scheme:

- **Crosslinker off-rate, catch/slip (named, §4.2, refs [141,142]):** ACPs unbind *faster* (slip) or *slower* (catch) with force. FF already uses Bell–Evans slip form `k_off(F) = k_off0 · exp(F/F_b)`. The review's addition is the **catch branch** for α-actinin — FF should use the full two-pathway catch–slip (Rakshit-style, as already used for cadherin) rather than slip-only for load-bearing ACPs. Map: `F` = tension on the explicit crosslinker bond; `k_off0`, `F_b` per-ACP-type config; catch pathway lowers k_off over an intermediate force window then slip takes over.
  - **Validation coupling (Fig. 5(f)):** the *network* observable is `ω_min(G″) ≈ ⟨k_off⟩` — the loss-modulus minimum frequency equals the mean crosslinker unbinding rate. This is the port target: an FF microrheology sweep must land the G″ dip at the configured k_off0.

- **Entropic spring / semiflexible force–extension (conceptual, §4.1.1, ref [93]):** force diverges as end-to-end distance → contour length. FF's bead-rod bending (persistence length L_p) + finite extensibility already realizes the Marko–Siggia-type form; the review just certifies the qualitative shape. No new constants — set L_p from §3 below.

- **Buckling→fragmentation stress-relaxation scheme (§4.2, ref [140]):** verbal — "fragmentation of F-actins facilitated by buckling results in significant stress relaxation." Port as a per-segment severing rate that switches on above a compressive/curvature threshold; couple to turnover so severed ends re-nucleate/re-grow (FF barbed-end growth already present). No numeric rate given — pull the actual k_sever from the primary [140] before use; do NOT invent one (magic-number rule).

- **Reversible softening via buckling (§4.2, ref [22], Chaudhuri 2007):** branched Arp2/3 network softens *reversibly* under compression — buckling, not rupture. FF Arp2/3 branch (angle-harmonic 70°±35°) + LJ excluded volume + bending should reproduce reversible compressive softening with no plastic offset; contrast against the fragmentation channel (which gives an *irreversible* offset). Good paired validation.

## 4. NETWORK ARCHITECTURE / PROTOCOL to reproduce

Three concrete FF scenarios, each a whole-cortex or reconstituted-network run at native scale.

- **A. Prestressed crosslinked cortex — differential modulus vs prestress (Fig. 3(d) protocol).**
  Reconstituted-style: F-actin + filamin crosslinkers (fixed, = actin amount) + myosin at 3 ratios (0.02/0.005/0.001) + a passive (myosin-off) control. Apply a static prestress σ_ext via bulk shear, superimpose small oscillatory strain, measure differential modulus K′(σ_ext). FF port: cortex patch under controlled shear prestress; myosin minifilament count set by the ratio; read K′. Target curve in §5.

- **B. Crosslinked-network microrheology — G′(ω), G″(ω) master curve (Fig. 5(f) protocol).**
  Fascin- (or α-actinin-) crosslinked actin at several crosslinker densities; drive oscillatory shear over a frequency sweep; normalize; confirm collapse + G″ minimum at the unbinding rate. FF port: cortex patch, active microrheology (oscillatory boundary strain or embedded-probe drag), sweep ω across k_off0.

- **C. Whole-cell stress relaxation / creep with turnover (Fig. 5(a) / §4.2 protocol).**
  Constant applied force/strain on the full native cell; measure stress decay (relaxation) or strain growth (creep) over ~10²–10³ s; turnover + crosslinker unbinding + fragmentation should give power-law (not single-exponential) decay. FF port: native `--cortex-fil 38000 --from-resting --microtubules` cell under microplate-style boundary load; measure σ(t). This is the scenario that exercises all three "missing" mechanisms at once.

All three must be run at NATIVE scale + full compartments per the HARD rule — a coarse cortex will fluidize/collapse on its own and confound the relaxation signal.

## 6. RISKS / caveats

- **It's a secondary review — never cite it as a datum source.** Every number here is quoted from a primary; KB registration must point to the primary (Koenderink/MacKintosh [115], Lieleg/Bausch [96], Latorre [8], Fernandez [108], Ferrer/Rakshit for ACP kinetics), with this review only as a pointer. Enforce the citation-integrity gate before any FF constant is claimed "from p32."
- **L_p = 9 µm conflicts with the ℓ_p ≈ 17 µm FF anchor.** The review's 9 µm [27] is a real literature value but the low end; Gittes ~17 µm is equally standard. Do NOT swap silently — surface to PI as a parameter-choice, and note the network-level observables (K′, G″) are fairly insensitive to which within the 9–17 µm band, so this is not blocking.
- **Reconstituted vs living cortex regime gap.** Figs. 3(d) and 5(f) are *purified in-vitro* networks (fixed crosslinker, controlled myosin ratio, no membrane/turgor/turnover). The living MCF7 cortex FF simulates has membrane containment, turgor prestress, continuous turnover, and heterogeneous filament lengths. So use A/B (reconstituted protocols) as **mechanism-isolating unit tests** (validate off-rate, validate prestress→K′ in isolation), and only then reconfirm the emergent behavior on the native full cell (scenario C). Don't compare a purified-network K′ number directly to a turgor-pressurized cortex tension.
- **No rate constants in-text → fragmentation channel needs a sourced k_sever.** The buckling-fragmentation mechanism (§4.2 [140]) is verbal; do not add a severing rate by fitting to make relaxation match — pull k_sever (and its buckling/curvature threshold) from the primary [140] or surface to PI. Magic-number rule applies.
- **Power-law rheology is a fingerprint, not a fitted target.** The review stresses cells have *no single relaxation time* (power-law creep, [126,127]). FF must **emerge** power-law relaxation from the distribution of turnover + unbinding timescales; do not insert a fractional/lumped element to force the exponent. If FF gives a single-exponential decay, that is a finding (missing turnover-timescale spread), not a bug to paper over.
- **Superelasticity/poroelasticity/lamin are DCM territory.** Tempting to build cortex-fluidization or fluid–solid poroelastic coupling into FF; keep FF to the actomyosin sub-model and hand the cortex tension γ (target 1–3 mN/m, up to interphase 0.15 / mitotic 1.5 for MCF7) to DCM. Don't smuggle continuum poroelasticity into the fine-grained filament engine.
- **Species/cell-type spread.** Numbers span fibroblasts, myoblasts, chondrosarcoma, epithelial domes — not MCF7. Treat magnitudes as order-of-magnitude bands, not point targets, when validating the MCF7 native cortex.
