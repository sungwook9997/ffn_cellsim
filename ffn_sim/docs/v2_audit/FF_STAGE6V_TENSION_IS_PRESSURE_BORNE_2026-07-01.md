# FF Stage 6V — cortical tension is osmotic-pressure-borne; the γ-floor was a mis-comparison

**Date:** 2026-07-01  **Engine:** FF (Warp, A5000)  **Branch:** dcm/main
**Culmination of the PI's insight chain:** density → DCM-assembly → "measure by pressing (AFM)" →
native → V0-bug → volume regulation → myosin study. This stage resolves the γ-floor.

## The resolution in one line

**Cortical tension = the Young-Laplace tension of the osmotically-pressurised cell, γ = ΔP·R/2, where ΔP
is set by OSMOREGULATION (not myosin contraction). The FF "γ-floor" compared myosin's DIRECT material
tension (γ_myo) to the TOTAL, pressure-borne cortical tension — the wrong quantities.**

## Task 1 — volume regulation reproduces both tension states + confinement-independence

Real cortical tension (Fischer-Friedrich 2014 parallel-plate, HeLa) is **confinement-INDEPENDENT** (±10%
over 10–40% compression) — the signature of a liquid drop of constant surface tension. Our fixed-osmolyte
turgor did the opposite (ΔP spiked under compression). Adding **volume regulation** (`pressure_setpoint`:
the cell holds ΔP at a setpoint via water/ion flux — the perfect-Lp limit; real finite-Lp is between this
and fixed-osmolyte) fixes it. NATIVE A5000 (γ_apparent = ΔP·R_eq/2):

| mode | strain 0% | 5% | 10% | 15% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fixed osmolyte (no regulation) | 0.14 | 3.86 | 14.4 | 31.3 (explodes) |
| **regulated ΔP=40 Pa (interphase)** | **0.150** | **0.150** | **0.150** | **0.150** |
| **regulated ΔP=400 Pa (mitotic)** | **1.503** | **1.503** | **1.503** | **1.503** |

→ regulated turgor gives **confinement-INDEPENDENT γ**, and **ΔP=40 → γ=0.150 ≈ experimental interphase
0.17**, **ΔP=400 → γ=1.503 ≈ experimental mitotic 1.6**. The model reproduces BOTH cortical-tension states
via the Laplace relation with the regulated osmotic pressure.

## Task 2 — myosin can neither generate the material tension nor drive the pressure ramp

Loaded shell (fixed osmolyte, no plates), sweep f_myo:

| f_myo [pN] | ΔP_final [Pa] | R_mean | V/V0 | γ_myo [mN/m] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 32.7 | 7.500 | 1.000 | 0 |
| 5 (physiological) | 32.7 | 7.500 | 1.000 | 6e-5 |
| 30 | 32.8 | 7.500 | 1.000 | 3e-4 |
| 100 | 33.1 | 7.500 | 1.000 | 4e-4 |
| **300 (60× physiological)** | **33.6** | 7.500 | 1.000 | 3e-4 |

- **Myosin cannot drive the pressure ramp:** even 60× physiological force moves ΔP only 32.7→33.6 Pa
  (V/V0=1.000, R unchanged) — the osmotic modulus (~7e5 Pa) is far too stiff for myosin to compress the
  volume. The mitotic ΔP ramp (40→400) is NOT myosin-driven; it is **osmoregulation** (ion pumps; Stewart
  2011: "the rounding force is generated by an osmotic pressure").
- **Myosin's material tension γ_myo = 6e-5..4e-4 mN/m** even at 60× force — ~10³–10⁴× below the band.

## What myosin actually does (real cell) vs our model

- **Real cell:** the mitotic ΔP ramp is osmotic; myosin is REQUIRED to CONTAIN it (cortex integrity/tension
  bears ΔP via Laplace; blebbistatin → cortex fails → tension drops ~75%). Myosin = container + modulator,
  co-regulated with osmoregulation via mitotic RhoA — NOT the primary tension generator.
- **Our model:** the pressure is set externally (osmoregulation setpoint); the (over-stiff) passive cortex
  bears the Laplace tension without needing myosin → myosin is irrelevant to the tension (can't generate,
  can't drive the pressure). Our over-stiff cortex MASKS the myosin-containment role.

## The γ-floor, resolved

The ~10³× "floor" (γ_myo vs the band) was **the wrong comparison**: it measured myosin's direct material
surface tension, but cortical tension is not generated that way — it is the **Laplace tension of the
osmotically-pressurised cell** (osmoregulation sets ΔP; the cortex bears it). Our model, with volume
regulation, reproduces the measured cortical tension in BOTH states (0.15/1.50 ≈ 0.17/1.6) and its
confinement-independence. The floor is real *for γ_myo* but γ_myo is not the cortical tension.

## Remaining honest model gaps (not bugs; PI-roadmap)

1. **Cortex mechanically over-stiff:** even in the regulated case the plate force is ~6–54 µN (~10³× the
   real nN-scale) — the inextensible reshape makes the shell resist area-change too strongly; the real
   cortex is soft / liquid-drop (actin turnover). So our cortex is not a clean liquid drop and a
   force-derived γ (the experiment's actual extraction) would disagree with the turgor-Laplace γ.
2. **Osmoregulation as an explicit dynamic driver** (finite Lp + ion-pump ΔP setpoint), co-regulated with
   myosin — currently the setpoint is imposed.
3. **Myosin containment role** is masked by (1); needs the soft-cortex regime to surface.
4. No direct MCF7 cortical-TENSION datum exists (only modulus) — the target is poorly grounded.
