## 3. Why source-derived porting beats clean-room

**It does not, and nothing was ported.** Both laws here are textbook and both are forced by
operators this repository already owns:

* Anisotropic slender-body drag is `f = -Z v` with `Z = L (zeta_par t t^T + zeta_perp (I - t t^T))`.
  That decomposition is the definition of a transversely isotropic resistance tensor; there is no
  design freedom in it once the two coefficients are named.
* The exterior traction is `f = -R V` with `R` the grand resistance operator that
  `aleph/vertical/medium.py` already factorises. This connector computes no Stokes solve of its own
  and must not: a second exterior solve living in a connector would be the same viscosity twice,
  which is precisely what `ExteriorDragChannel` exists to refuse.

Per `ports/TEMPLATE.md` §3 that is a reason to write clean-room.

## 4. Physical or mathematical law represented

### 4.1 Anisotropic immersed drag — `mt_cytosol_transfer`, `if_cytosol_transfer`

At each quadrature point `i` with unit tangent `t_i` and quadrature length `L_i`:

```
Z_i     = L_i * ( zeta_par * t_i t_i^T  +  zeta_perp * (I - t_i t_i^T) )
f_i^(a) = -Z_i . v_i                    # on the solid
f_i^(b) = -Z_i . (-v_i)                 # on the fluid, the SAME law at the reversed relative velocity
```

`v_i = v_solid,i - v_fluid,i` is the relative velocity at the site. `zeta_par` and `zeta_perp` are
drag coefficients **per unit length** [pN·s/µm²]; multiplying by `L_i` is what makes the integral
resolution-independent, so refining the quadrature does not change the total drag.

**Why the tensor and not a scalar.** The registered `mt_cytosol_transfer` contract says a microtubule
"is stiff and long, so its drag is strongly anisotropic and **sets the rate at which the filament can
reorient at all**. Without this edge a microtubule reorients against nothing, and a buckling or
sliding time scale computed from it is meaningless." An isotropic `zeta` reproduces every force
magnitude on axis and still deletes the quantity the contract names, because with `Z = zeta I` the
drag is exactly antiparallel to `v` for every orientation and there is **no transverse component and
no reorienting couple at all**. That failure is invisible to force closure, to the energy books and
to any magnitude check: it is a missing term, and a missing term conserves everything perfectly.

The transverse component is the physics, and it has a closed form. With `v` at angle `theta` to the
tangent, the component of `-f^(a)` perpendicular to `v` is

```
|f_perp| / (L |v|) = (zeta_perp - zeta_par) * sin(theta) * cos(theta)
```

which is exactly zero for every `theta` iff `zeta_perp == zeta_par`. That is the oracle in §7.2 and
the negative control in §9.

**`if_cytosol_transfer` uses the same element** with a ratio the caller supplies. The IF contract
does not claim anisotropy — it says the network "is dense and space-filling, so it is the population
whose omission most changes the effective permeability the other immersed bodies see" — so the
honest statement is that this connector *admits* a ratio of exactly one for IF and does not impose
one. `aleph/vertical/intermediate_filament.py` publishes `quadrature_weight_um` and **no local
tangent**, which is recorded in §14 as a limit rather than papered over: an IF site's tangent must
come from the caller.

**Slender-body theory fixes the ratio, not the magnitudes.** To leading order in `1/ln(L/a)` the
resistance coefficients of a slender rod satisfy

```
zeta_perp / zeta_par -> 2          as L/a -> infinity
```

This is a *dimensionless* asymptotic result, so quoting it violates nothing in `ALEPH-PORT-1803`,
which forbids a porosity, permeability or viscosity **magnitude** in code. It ships as
`SLENDER_BODY_ANISOTROPY_LIMIT` with the derivation in its docstring, is **not** a default — every
constructor requires the ratio explicitly — and the controls check the *ratio* the connector
reproduces rather than any coefficient.

### 4.2 Exterior Stokes traction — `membrane_medium_traction`

```
f^(a) = -R V            # the medium's resistance on the moving surface
f^(b) = -R (-V) = R V   # the SAME law at the reversed surface velocity
```

`R` is the grand resistance operator `aleph/vertical/medium.py` already factorises from the
regularised single-layer mobility. **This connector owns no solve.** It reads `resistance_force_pn`
from the exterior solve it was constructed against, and refuses at `accumulate` time if the endpoint
it is handed reaches a *different* medium object — the same refusal `publish_endpoint` makes on the
medium's own side, for the same reason: applying one cell's exterior solve to another cell's surface
loads that body with this one's resistance and every individual force still looks right.

**This is a different kind of connector from §4.1 and the difference is not presentational.** An
immersed drag is a local, per-site, diagonal law inside a porous medium. An exterior Stokes
resistance is a **dense operator coupling every surface node to every other**, on an unbounded
exterior domain, and it is the only object in the registry that loads the six rigid-body modes. The
registered contract's `implemented=False` was load-bearing exactly because no such solve existed;
`ALEPH-PORT-3206` supplied it, and this entry is the connector that makes it reachable.

### 4.3 Why `force_b` is measured and not assigned

`AdjointPair`'s contract (`aleph/runtime/participant.py:310`) forbids `force_b := -force_a`, because
that turns force closure into a tautology. Both connectors here therefore do two separate things:

1. They compute the fluid/medium side **by evaluating the same law at the reversed relative
   velocity**, not by negating the solid side. For a linear resistance the two agree to round-off;
   they are still two evaluations, and a resistance that had become state-dependent, asymmetric or
   non-linear would separate them.
2. They report as `force_b` the **measured delta of the receiving owner's own array** —
   `CytosolField.body_force_resultant_pn()` for §4.1, `ExteriorStokesMedium.surface_traction_pn` for
   §4.2 — read before and after the scatter.

So a connector that computes a drag and delivers none reports `force_b == 0` and fails closure, and
a connector that delivers *part* of it reports a `force_b` with the right sign and direction and the
wrong magnitude, which also fails. Those are the two negative controls in §9.

### 4.4 The drag-channel declaration

`CytosolField.declare_drag_channel` refuses one owner holding both a lumped nodal drag and a resolved
Darcy drag, because they are the same viscosity twice and the error grows with speed. The
`AnisotropicImmersedDragConnector` declares `resolved_darcy` for its solid endpoint's owner on every
`accumulate`, so an owner that had already declared a lumped channel is refused by the shipped guard
rather than by anything this module invents. The guard is respected, never worked around.

| Quantity | Aleph unit | SI | Domain |
|---|---|---|---|

| anisotropy ratio `zeta_perp / zeta_par` | 1 | — | `>= 1`, finite |

| grand resistance (rotation) | pN·µm·s | — | positive definite |

Singular and boundary cases, every one refused rather than clamped:

- **No relative velocity set.** Refused. Returning zeros would make "nothing was evaluated" and
  "nothing is moving" the same answer — the defect `PLAN.md` §6.1 records, where a cold cache made a
  deliberately broken strut look innocent.
- **No quadrature set** (tangents and lengths). Refused, same reasoning: a drag integral over an
  unset quadrature is not a small drag, it is an absent one.
- **A non-unit tangent.** Refused. Normalising silently would rescale `zeta_par` by `|t|^2` and
  present it as physics.
- **A non-positive or non-finite quadrature length.** Refused. A site standing for zero length
  transmits nothing and would pass every closure check vacuously.
- **`zeta_par <= 0`, non-finite, or an anisotropy ratio `< 1` or non-finite.** Refused. A zero
  coefficient is not a weak coupling, it is an absent one.
- **Zero sites.** Refused; do not register an empty connector rather than registering one.
- **A configuration block of the wrong size.** Refused — a connector handed a configuration of the
  wrong size is being asked about a body other than the one it holds state for.
- **A stencil count that disagrees with the site count.** Refused: one stencil per quadrature point,
  because the reaction has to be returned where the velocity was read.
- **An exterior stencil reaching a different medium object.** Refused (§4.2).
- **A phase other than `ACCUMULATE_COUPLING`.** Refused. A coupling crosses an ownership boundary.
- **A stale endpoint handle.** Not this module's guard: the owners refuse it
  (`MicrotubuleStaleHandleError`, `IFStaleHandleError`, `MediumStaleHandleError`) and the connector
  lets the refusal through unmodified. `TransferStencil` still stamps no epoch — see §14.

Invariants, each with the control that asserts it:

- **I1.** The drag tensor is transversely isotropic about the tangent: an eigen-decomposition of
  `Z_i / L_i` gives `zeta_par` once along `t_i` and `zeta_perp` twice in the plane normal to it.
- **I2.** Resolution independence: splitting one quadrature site of length `L` into `k` sites of
  length `L/k` on the same tangent, at the same relative velocity, gives **exactly** the same total
  force. Asserted to round-off, not to a tolerance chosen to pass.
- **I3.** The transverse response: the angle between the drag force and the relative velocity is
  non-zero for an oblique rod iff the ratio is not one, with the closed form of §4.1.
- **I4.** `force_a + force_b` closes against the **constituent** scale, with `force_b` read from the
  receiving owner's own array.
- **I5.** Dissipation is strictly positive for any non-zero relative velocity (`v^T Z v > 0`,
  `Z` positive definite), and exactly `0.0` at rest.
- **I6.** The exterior connector reproduces `6*pi*mu*a` in translation and `8*pi*mu*a^3` in rotation
  **through the connector's own reported pair**, not by re-reading the medium's accessors.
- **I7.** Isotropy is exactly inert in the transverse channel: with the ratio at exactly one, the
  perpendicular force component is `0.0` — the number, not a small number.

## 7. Independent oracle or derivation

1. **Transverse isotropy by eigen-decomposition.** `Z/L` must have eigenvalue `zeta_par` with
   eigenvector `t` and eigenvalue `zeta_perp` with multiplicity two in the normal plane. This is an
   oracle on the *structure* of the tensor and is independent of both coefficients' values.
2. **The slender-body ratio.** Sliding a rod along its own tangent and then broadside at the same
   speed gives two total forces whose ratio is `zeta_perp / zeta_par` exactly — a **ratio**, so every
   magnitude, every length and the viscosity cancel. Checked against
   `SLENDER_BODY_ANISOTROPY_LIMIT = 2` when the connector is built at that ratio, which is the check
   the brief asks for: the ratio, not a magnitude.
3. **The transverse-force closed form** of §4.1, checked at several oblique angles.
4. **Resolution independence** of the quadrature integral (I2), which no rescaling of a coefficient
   can reproduce.
5. **The two sphere limits**, `6*pi*mu*a` and `8*pi*mu*a^3`, measured **through the connector's
   `AdjointPair`** — the resultant of `force_a` under a uniform translation and the moment of
   `force_a` under a rigid rotation. `ALEPH-PORT-3206` §4.4 derives both from the exact surface
   tractions of the translating and rotating sphere. The rotational one is the sector the
   translational limit cannot see: an isotropic per-node drag tuned to reproduce `6*pi*mu*a` exactly
   gets `8*pi*mu*a^3` wrong by a factor of two.

## 10. Numerical and precision envelope

float64 throughout; no reduced-precision path.

Closure is asserted against the **constituent** scale, never the resultant. `PLAN.md` §2.5 records a
resultant-based tolerance rejecting 40,000 consecutive steps of a correct relaxation, and both
connectors here have exactly that shape: a body immersed in a fluid, and a closed surface in an
exterior medium, both deliver large per-site forces whose *resultant* can legitimately be small. The
sphere case is the sharpest instance — under a rigid rotation the resultant force is zero by symmetry
while every node carries a large traction — so `constituent_force_pn` and `constituent_moment_pn_um`
are supplied on every pair.

I2 and I7 are asserted with `==` rather than a tolerance, because both are exact statements:
splitting a quadrature site redistributes an identical sum of identical products, and an isotropic
tensor's transverse component is an algebraic zero. A tolerance on either would let a term that had
quietly become active pass as round-off.

The sphere limits are asserted against `ALEPH-PORT-3206`'s own measured convergence behaviour: the
scheme is first order in the mean node spacing, so the control fixes a resolution and a band derived
from that entry's measurements rather than asserting a tight tolerance a coarser quadrature could not
meet. The bands used are recorded with the measured values in §8.

"would polymerise from an infinite reservoir — growth with no monomer cost and no transport limit,
which is the most flattering error a protrusion model can make"; the filopodial one says monomer
transport "rather than polymerisation kinetics is what limits its length. Declaring drag alone would
remove that limit entirely." `aleph/vertical/filopodium.py` ships
`assert_monomer_sink_is_declared_with_drag`, which refuses a bundle that publishes the mechanics and
drops the chemistry — a guard, not a convention.

**Wiring only the drag half is wiring half a connector**, and this lane declines to claim either. The
monomer half cannot be written honestly today, and it is blocked in three independent places:

1. `aleph/vertical/cytosol.py:1039` — `species_content` "is allocated, committed, rolled back — and
   **nothing advects or diffuses it**. The G-actin channel that two of the nine connectors name does
   not exist." There is no depletion API to deliver into.
2. `aleph/vertical/filopodium.py:716` — `MonomerSinkEndpoint.elongation_rate_um_per_s` **refuses**:
   tip elongation under load is on the registered contract's evidence-owed list.
3. `aleph/vertical/lamellipodium.py:745` — the owner "cannot report a monomer count, because
   converting µm of F-actin into monomers needs a constant nobody here has sourced, and inventing one
   would be the same failure as inventing a rate."

A connector wired against those three absences would import cleanly, pass force closure on its drag
half, and silently supply the infinite reservoir both contracts name as the error to avoid. An absent
row reports an honest count; a premature one reports a number nothing can feel.

**What would unblock them**, stated so the next lane does not have to rediscover it: a transport
operator for `species_content` in `cytosol.py` (its owner's lane), plus either a sourced
subunits-per-µm constant or a sourced elongation rate. Both are evidence questions, not plumbing.

### 14.3 The membrane side of `membrane_medium_traction` publishes no endpoint

`aleph/vertical/membrane.py`'s `HelfrichMembrane` has no `endpoint_sink`, no `transfer_endpoint` and
no published surface quadrature. The connector is therefore written against the `CoupledSiteEndpoint`
*protocol* and is exercised against a `MeshSiteEndpoint` over a surface block, which is what
`endpoints.py` exists for and is the same construction `tests/vertical/test_connectors_fluid.py` uses
for the solid side of `sf_cytosol_transfer`.

Stated as an absence rather than implied: **nothing here is evidence that the live membrane surface
is the surface this medium resolves.** `ALEPH-PORT-3206` §14 already records that nothing in Aleph
publishes a live membrane quadrature. Joining the two is the membrane owner's lane.

### 14.4 The intermediate filament publishes no tangent

`intermediate_filament.py` publishes `quadrature_weight_um` and no local tangent, so the tangent for
an IF quadrature site must be supplied by the caller. The microtubule owner publishes both, on extra
attributes of its sink (`quadrature_length_um`, `tangent_unit`) that the declared
`CoupledSiteEndpoint` protocol has no room for — `ALEPH-PORT-3202` §14 already reports that gap. This
entry does **not** widen the protocol: widening a declared seam to fit one owner is a decision above
this lane, and the connector takes the quadrature explicitly instead. That is recorded here as a
finding, and it is the reason `set_quadrature` is a separate call rather than something read off an
endpoint.

### 14.5 What no control here establishes

- **Nothing here is evidence that a coupled solid–fluid system converges.** It gives momentum a place
  to go. `SOLVE_COUPLED` has not been run by this project.
- **Nothing here is evidence that a microtubule reorients on the right time scale.** The controls
  check that the reorientation channel *exists* and has the structure slender-body theory gives it.
  No magnitude is a microtubule number.
- **No kinetics.** Family `[C]`: nothing binds, unbinds or turns over, and nothing commits.
- **The momentum reaches the skeleton through the pressure field, not directly** — inherited
  unchanged from `ALEPH-PORT-3101` §14. `force_a + force_b = 0` is a statement about what the
  *connector* exchanged.
- **The stale-`TransferStencil` hole is pre-existing and stays open.** `_install_classification`
  reallocates the cytosol's sink arrays, and `TransferStencil` stamps no topology epoch, so
  `endpoints.EPOCH_NOT_STAMPED` applies and the staleness guard cannot fire for it. Reported, not
  fixed: it is a change to an owner's transaction discipline.
- **The exterior connector does not run the medium's own `accumulate`.** If an assembly ran both the
  medium's `ACCUMULATE_FIELD` phase *and* this connector in one step, `surface_traction_pn` would
  carry the medium's record of the force on the surface **and** this connector's reaction to it, and
  the two net to zero. That is arguably the intended reading of "the medium's own resistance is
  already there, and a connector's reaction joins it", but **no assembly exists to check it against**,
  so it is named as an open question for whoever schedules the two together rather than settled here.
  The connector's own `force_b` is a *delta* measured across its own scatter and is unaffected either
  way.

## 4a. Mutation testing

Mutants applied one at a time to `aleph/vertical/connectors_transfer.py`, the full control file run
against each, then restored and the file verified **byte-identical by SHA-256** — the file is
untracked while this lane runs, so `git diff` would show nothing either way and a hash is the check
that actually holds. Before and after:
`b986f95cbaadbfb39b6c7877fbd17a8c4bb43c9cc9fbc358ca9006a797d79646`.

| # | Mutant | Result | Killed | Survived? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the anisotropic excess set to `0.0`, so `zeta_perp` silently becomes `zeta_par` | 5 failed, 48 passed | 5 | no |
| 2 | the quadrature length `L_i` dropped from the drag tensor | 6 failed, 47 passed | 6 | no |
| 3 | the **drag** connector's `force_b` assigned as `-force_a` | 1 failed, 52 passed | 1 | no — but see below |
| 3' | the **traction** connector's `force_b` assigned as `-force_a` | 3 failed, 50 passed | 3 | no |

**Mutant 3 is the finding, and it survived the first pass entirely** — 50 passed, nothing failed. The
reason is not a gap in the controls; it is a proof. The immersed-drag law is exactly linear, and in
IEEE-754 `-(Z v)` and `Z (-v)` are not merely equal but **bitwise identical**: negation is exact, so
every partial product and every partial sum negates exactly. Verified rather than argued — 2000
random `(Z, v)` draws gave zero bitwise-different results. **No measurement can distinguish the two
expressions for a linear resistance**, so no numerical control can kill this mutant on that class.

What was done about it, in order of strength:

1. The **injected-law control** `TestTheTwoSidesAreEvaluatedIndependently` was added. The exterior
   connector reads its law from an object it is handed, so a stub whose resistance is deliberately
   **not odd** in the velocity — `f(V) = -(V + 0.5 max(V, 0))` — separates `f(-V)` from `-f(V)`. Two
   behavioural controls then bite: the two sides are no longer each other's negation, and the pair
   the connector reports is **open**, so the ledger's closure fails instead of passing an identity.
   That is what took mutant 3' from surviving to killed by three controls.
2. A **source-level** control asserts the module does not contain the assignment, and it is labelled
   in its own docstring as textual rather than physical. It is the instrument that kills mutant 3,
   and it is a weaker instrument than a measurement. Saying so is the point.

Recorded as a limit rather than closed: **for a linear immersed-drag law, "evaluated twice" and
"assigned once" are observationally identical, and the discipline is maintained by reading the code.**
The same statement would apply to `ALEPH-PORT-3101`'s `ImmersedDragConnector`, which is not this
lane's file and was not touched.
